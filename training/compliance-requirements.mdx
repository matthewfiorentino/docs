Everyone who works on a study must have documented training before being delegated a study task or doing any task related to the study. The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) count adequate training as essential to good clinical practice (GCP), regulatory compliance, data integrity and participants' rights, safety and well-being. Some investigators also need [Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status](#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status). Each certificate must stay valid for the whole study. Renew it before it expires.

## Who needs training

Everyone listed on a study's <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> must hold current training for their role and for the study's risk level, including:

- The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC.
- In a drug trial, the qualified investigator, the person responsible to the sponsor for the trial at the site.
- Sub-investigators.
- Research coordinators and research nurses.
- Every other team member who performs study tasks.

Training is required of every team member, regardless of their eligibility for Privileges or Researcher Status.

At the MUHC, the principal investigator, the sponsor-investigator or, in a drug trial, the qualified investigator is responsible for obtaining the level of institutional training the study requires before submitting the study to the Research Ethics Board in Nagano.

### Who cannot work with participants

The following people may not contact or recruit research participants or perform procedures on them at the RI-MUHC or the MUHC, because they are not covered by professional or academic liability insurance:

- Volunteers.
- Students without valid academic insurance, that is, students not covered by the institutional policy of a nursing or medical program.
- Non-affiliated people, who are not employed by or appointed at the RI-MUHC or the MUHC.

The people in this list may not be listed on protocols, access identifiable patient health records or take part in study submission either.

Once added to the Task Delegation Log, [volunteers](/roles/volunteer-observer#know-what-volunteers-and-observers-may-do) may do administrative and other tasks that do not involve participants, such as filing, entering de-identified data and handling logistics. Volunteers may not interact with participants.

[Students](/roles/trainee#what-you-can-be-delegated) covered by academic insurance may take part, including interacting with participants, if they meet the training requirements.

## Training requirements by study level

The highest-risk study you work on sets your training level, from Level I (highest risk) to Level V (lowest risk). Everyone on a study should hold the study's level or a higher one before signing its Task Delegation Log. Training at a high-risk level covers low-risk studies too.

| Level | Kind of research | SOP Reader and Assessment in TalentLMS | Other mandatory training |
|---|---|---|---|
| Level I (high risk) | Drug studies, including biologics, gene and cell therapies, blood products, vaccines and radiopharmaceuticals | SOP-CR Level I, renewed every 2 years | GCP training on the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) guideline E6, and Health Canada Division 5 training, both on [CITI](/training/external-certifications) and renewed every 2 years |
| Level II (high risk) | Medical device and surgery studies | SOP-CR Level II, renewed every 2 years | For medical device studies, GCP training on the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) standard [ISO 14155](/training/external-certifications#iso-14155-gcp-for-medical-devices), or ICH E6 GCP training on CITI if the sponsor or sponsor-investigator cannot provide ISO 14155 training or the document |
| Level III (high risk) | Natural health product studies | SOP-CR Level III, renewed every 2 years | ICH E6 GCP training on CITI, renewed every 2 years |
| Level IV (low risk) | Prospective studies that Health Canada does not regulate and that involve no surgery, such as educational and non-invasive interventions (psychotherapy, physiotherapy, diet, exercise and massage) | SOP-LR Level IV, renewed every 5 years | None beyond the SOP Reader and Assessment |
| Level V (low risk) | Retrospective studies, including studies of samples already held in a biobank | SOP-LR Level V, renewed every 5 years | None beyond the SOP Reader and Assessment |

Training with a provider other than CITI is accepted if it meets [the RI-MUHC's conditions for other providers](/training/external-certifications#using-another-training-provider).

## SOP Reader and Assessment

[TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) has one SOP Reader and Assessment course for each level: SOP-CR Level I, Level II and Level III, and SOP-LR Level IV and Level V. Each course covers the SOPs that apply to the risks and the kinds of activity of studies at its level.

In the course for your level, open each SOP's unit, read the SOP and acknowledge it. Once you have acknowledged every unit, complete the course's assessment, a scenario-based quiz on applying the processes and procedures in the RI-MUHC's SOPs to practical, real-life situations.

### Read revised SOPs

[Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) launches revised and new SOPs in improvement cycles, and each launch is announced by RI-Mailer. Renew your SOP Training within three months of each launch: read each revised or new SOP in the SOP Reader and pass its quiz. The RI-Mailer asks that the training be completed by the SOP's effective date, typically three months after the launch. File the certificate with your study training records.

Reading revised SOPs on time matters most in studies regulated by Health Canada. A Health Canada inspector may compare your training records with SOP effective dates and record a lapse in SOP knowledge as a finding in their inspection report.

### Move to a higher or lower level

You move up a level when you join a study at a higher risk level than your training covers, such as a drug trial when you hold Level IV. Start by writing to the RI-MUHC's [Training Advisor](mailto:training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca).

The Training Advisor assigns you the new level's course and marks the units it shares with your current course as complete, so you complete only the units specific to the new level. The new certificate runs for its full period and replaces the old one.

If you move down a level, for example from Level I to Level IV because you no longer work on drug trials, your SOP-CR certificate stays valid until it expires. At your next renewal, you are reassigned to the SOP-LR course, and the new certificate runs for its full period.

## Human Research Privileges and Researcher Status

Leading a study takes a credential as well as training. Quebec's health ministry requires people who conduct research under the auspices of an establishment in its network to hold research privileges or researcher status. The requirement is in the 2020 [framework for research with human participants](https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/document-002696/) (French only), which the ministry issued under its former name, the Ministère de la Santé et des Services sociaux. The ministry's name is the Ministère de la Santé, de la Prévention et des Aînés.

The MUHC applies the requirement in its [*Regulatory Framework for Clinical Research at Santé Québec – McGill University Health Centre: Organization and Good Practices*](https://rimuhc.ca/conduct-research/policies#framework). Anyone who acts as principal investigator at the MUHC must first hold Human Research Privileges, Researcher Status or a written institutional authorization. An RI-MUHC appointment as a Scientist or Investigator does not meet the requirement.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-500#2-0-scope) require these people to hold Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status:

- The principal investigator.
- A sub-investigator delegated the principal investigator's backup tasks.
- A sponsor-investigator.

Other team members on the Task Delegation Log do not need them. [The RI-MUHC's SOP on adverse events](/sops/cr-012#5-1-1) requires Privileges of every investigator who will assess adverse events in an interventional study.

## Validity and the 90-day rule

Privileges and Researcher Status are [valid for 3 years](/sops/cr-500#5-2-1). When a study is submitted, no team member's training certificate, Privileges or Researcher Status may be within 90 days of expiry. Renew any certificate that close to expiry before submission, or it will not count in the institutional feasibility review.

TalentLMS sends an automatic reminder 90 days before a certificate it holds expires. If you have active studies in Nagano, renew as soon as the reminder arrives. For an SOP Reader and Assessment certificate, TalentLMS sends a second reminder 30 days before it expires.

A study cannot receive [MUHC Authorization](/sops/cr-007#5-4-2) until its principal investigator, its sponsor-investigator or, in a drug trial, its qualified investigator has the required training and holds Privileges or Researcher Status. Lapsed Privileges or Researcher Status may also halt active studies. Questions about MUHC Authorization go to the office of the [Personne mandatée](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca), the person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research.

## Find your training records

Where a training record is kept depends on the credential:

- [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) holds your SOP Reader and Assessment certificate and your Privileges or Researcher Status certificate. Certificates can be downloaded from your profile.
- Your Researcher Administrative Profile in the RI-MUHC Portal shows your Privileges or Researcher Status and your SOP Reader and Assessment records, for viewing only. Certificates cannot be downloaded from it.
- Your external training provider, such as CITI, holds your GCP and Health Canada Division 5 certificates. TalentLMS does not hold them.
