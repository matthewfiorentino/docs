Everyone involved in clinical research at the RI-MUHC must hold current institutional training before being delegated any study task. Training level depends on study type — from a single SOP course for retrospective chart reviews to full GCP certification and Health Canada Division 5 training for interventional drug trials.

This section covers what's required, how to complete it, and where to go for ongoing learning.

## Training levels at a glance

Training level is determined by the study type — not your role. Everyone on a study's Task Delegation Log must be trained at the level required for that study.

| Level | Study type | Key certifications |
|---|---|---|
| Level I | Interventional drug, biologic, gene/cell therapy, radiopharmaceutical | GCP (ICH E6), Health Canada Division 5, SOP Reader (all SOPs), Competency Assessment |
| Level II | Medical device, surgical intervention | GCP (ICH E6 + ISO 14155), SOP Reader (all SOPs), Competency Assessment |
| Level III | Natural health product | GCP (ICH E6), NHP regulations, SOP Reader (all SOPs), Competency Assessment |
| Level IV | Prospective observational, non-invasive interventions | SOP Reader (18 SOPs), Competency Assessment |
| Level V | Retrospective, secondary data, anonymized biosamples | SOP Reader (9 SOPs), Competency Assessment |
| Sponsor-Investigator | Any study where the PI also acts as Sponsor | Levels I–III plus [SOP-CR-100](/sops/cr-100) through 107 series |

<Note>
  Training must be complete and current **before** a person appears on the Task Delegation Log and **before** any study task is performed. TalentLMS sends renewal reminders for SOP training; CITI sends reminders every two years for GCP.
</Note>

## TalentLMS — the RI-MUHC training platform

All institutional SOP training and Competency Assessments are completed through TalentLMS. Your completion records in TalentLMS are what reviewers check during feasibility review and what auditors and inspectors check during monitoring visits.
