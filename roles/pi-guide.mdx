As <Tooltip tip="The person responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a drug or natural health product trial at a clinical trial site, who is entitled to provide health care under the laws of the province where the site is located." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip> or <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>, you carry ultimate legal and regulatory accountability for your study at the RI-MUHC site. Tasks can be delegated — accountability cannot. This guide maps what is yours personally, what your team handles, and your obligations at each stage.

<Note>
**A note on terminology.** Health Canada's drug-trial regulations use _Qualified Investigator_ (QI) for a physician (or a dentist, for a drug used only for dental purposes) entitled to provide health care in the province and a member in good standing of their professional association. ICH <Tooltip tip="A standard for the design, conduct, performance, monitoring, auditing, recording, analysis and reporting of clinical studies that provides assurance that the data and reported results are credible and accurate, and that the rights, integrity and confidentiality of participants are protected." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> uses _Principal Investigator_ (PI). At the RI-MUHC, QI is the precise term for Division 5 drug trials; PI is used more broadly. Whichever term applies, [the sponsor or sponsor-investigator ensures](/sops/cr-002#5-2-1) that there is no more than one QI or PI at each study site.
</Note>

## Understanding the role

### Training requirements

Levels I–III follow the SOP-CR path on a 2-year SOP Reader and Assessment cycle, with ICH-GCP required for Levels I and III, Health Canada Division 5 for Level I and ISO 14155 GCP for Level II medical device studies (or ICH-GCP, if the sponsor or sponsor-investigator cannot provide ISO 14155 training or the document). Your training level is set by your **highest-risk active study** across all protocols. For the full matrix and the level summary, see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements) and the [Investigator Orientation](/roles/investigator#institutional-training).

## Before you begin

Your personal prerequisites — Research Privileges or Researcher Status, institutional training, and the 90-day expiry rule — are covered in the [Investigator Orientation](/roles/investigator). The signature table there shows what only you can sign at each lifecycle phase. This guide covers what happens operationally once those prerequisites are in place.

<Note>
  **Study design support.** The [Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)](/kb/design-support/bcu) provides sample size calculations, randomization plans, and Statistical Analysis Plans. [CORD](/kb/design-support/cord) builds REDCap databases and reviews CRFs. Engage both before finalizing your protocol — statistical and data design decisions are costly to revise after enrollment begins.

## Study submission

All human research studies at the RI-MUHC are submitted through Nagano. The F11 submission triggers the triple review — scientific, ethics (REB), and institutional feasibility — that leads to MUHC Authorization from the Personne mandatée.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Before you open Nagano">
    Confirm your Privileges or Researcher Status and institutional training are both current and not within 90 days of expiry. Have a finalized protocol, the current <Tooltip tip="A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip> or product monograph, draft <Tooltip tip="The written form that gives a participant the information essential to an informed decision about taking part in a study." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip>(s) in English and French, and any planned recruitment materials.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contracts, agreements, and budget">
    All contracts must be submitted via My Contracts on the Portal and fully executed by the Research Agreements Office before MUHC Authorization is issued. Start early — contract negotiation can take weeks.

    **Industry-sponsored studies:** The Sponsor proposes a Clinical Trial Agreement (CTA); the Research Agreements Office reviews and negotiates on behalf of the institution. Your responsibility is to confirm the budget covers all institutional costs — pharmacy, nursing, laboratory, overhead, archiving, and REB billing fees — and that the Sponsor will provide a Certificate of Insurance naming the MUHC.

    **Investigator-initiated studies:** You build the budget — staff salaries, CORD data management, BCU biostatistics, pharmacy, monitoring (an independent, dedicated monitor, which [the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-013#5-2) require of a sponsor-investigator), participant reimbursement, overhead, archiving, and contingency. Submit inter-institutional agreements if multi-site. For grant-specific budget preparation — CIHR, FRQ, and foundation programs — see [Preparing Funding Applications](/kb/funding/funding-applications).

    See [Study Budgets](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) for overhead rates and the REB fee schedule, and [Research Agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#know-when-the-study-needs-an-agreement) for the types of agreement. The Research Agreements Office negotiates CTA terms, but you should understand the clauses that bind the site — indemnification, publication rights, data ownership, and termination. See [Research Agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements).
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
**The MUHC Authorization letter is the green light — not REB approval alone.** REB approval and a favourable institutional feasibility review must be in place before the Personne mandatée issues MUHC Authorization; where the trial needs Health Canada's authorization, the Personne mandatée also waits for its letter.

Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization. Where the trial needs Health Canada's authorization, or the sponsor issues a go letter, do not begin any study procedure, including screening and consent, until you have it too. Recruitment should also wait for the site initiation visit. Keep the printed REB approval letter in your ISF. The MUHC Authorization letter should also be filed there.
</Warning>

### Have Health Canada authorization in place before the study starts

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

For a drug trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, a <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application</Tooltip> (CTA) must be submitted to Health Canada and a <Tooltip tip="A letter that Health Canada issues when it has found a Clinical Trial Application satisfactory." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nol">No Objection Letter</Tooltip> (NOL) obtained before the study starts, so start the application as early as possible.

Health Canada does not issue a formal "approval" for clinical trials — it issues a letter confirming no objection, or an authorization to proceed. What the sponsor submits, and what Health Canada issues, differ by study type (in a Sponsor-Investigator study you are the sponsor; for a device, the manufacturer or importer applies):

- **Drug, biologic, vaccine, radiopharmaceutical, or gene/cell therapy:** a <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip>; HC issues a <Tooltip tip="A letter that Health Canada issues when it has found a Clinical Trial Application satisfactory." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nol">No Objection Letter (NOL)</Tooltip>.
- **Basic clinical research study of a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical:** [the sponsor's application under Division 3 of the Food and Drug Regulations](/paths/index#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study), instead of a CTA. The Minister authorizes the sponsor to sell or import the radiopharmaceutical.
- **Natural health product:** an NHP Clinical Trial Application; HC issues a Notice of Authorization (NOA).
- **Medical device, Class II, III or IV (IVDDs included):** when the study needs an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)</Tooltip>, the manufacturer or importer applies for it.

The HC letter must be in hand before the study starts, and the Personne mandatée waits for it before issuing MUHC Authorization. The RI-MUHC's SOPs have the sponsor obtain Health Canada's authorization first. The sponsor should send its letter to the site, which includes it in the Nagano submission, although the order can differ for natural health products and some devices. See [Health Canada authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization) for what Health Canada requires for each type of trial and who files the application.

### Multi-site studies

- **If the MUHC is the lead site:** your Nagano submission covers all participating Quebec <Tooltip tip="Quebec's public health and social services network, the réseau de la santé et des services sociaux, whose establishments include the MUHC." headline="RSSS" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#rsss">RSSS</Tooltip> institutions. You are the REB Lead. Each participating site still completes its own institutional feasibility review and obtains its own institution's authorization from its Personne mandatée.
- **If the MUHC is a participating site (MEO):** the reviewing REB is the lead institution's REB. Your Nagano file is a participating-site submission. You still require RI-MUHC feasibility review and MUHC Authorization. Contact the Research Facilitator before starting a MEO submission.
- For industry-sponsored multi-site trials, CATALIS Québec's FAST TRACK Evaluation Service may apply; the RI-MUHC is one of its partner institutions.

### Sponsor-Investigator studies

If you are both Sponsor and QI, you hold dual obligations: applying for Health Canada's authorization where a drug or natural health product trial needs it, arranging independent monitoring, managing the investigational product supply chain, including compliance with [good manufacturing practices (GMP) where they apply](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#investigational-product-and-gmp), and fulfilling all QI site responsibilities. For a device that needs an Investigational Testing Authorization, only the manufacturer or importer can apply. In a drug trial, Health Canada's regulations do not differentiate between a commercial and a non-commercial sponsor, so the same requirements apply to you as sponsor-investigator. You must also register the trial in a publicly accessible registry before the first participant is recruited; ask Quality Assurance for access to the MUHC's ClinicalTrials.gov account ([SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007)).

You must fund independent monitoring (you cannot monitor your own study), IP supply and management, the Trial Master File, SUSAR reporting to Health Canada, and — for multi-site studies — oversight of all participating sites. The insurance framework is also more complex than for industry-sponsored studies; discuss with the Research Agreements Office early in planning, not at submission. See [Research Agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#cover-the-study-as-its-sponsor) and [Sponsor-Investigator Studies](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator). For the step-by-step startup checklist — CTA filing, insurance, safety reporting infrastructure, monitoring plan, and DSMB — see [Start a Trial You Sponsor Yourself](/kb/planning/iit-startup).

## Building and maintaining your team

### Setting up the delegation log

- The Task Delegation Log must be set up before any study tasks begin — including your own. Name every team member, their role, their specific delegated tasks, and their start date. You countersign each entry; team members sign to accept delegation.
- Every person on the log must have documented training before signing it, and should hold the study's training level or a higher one. You are confirming this with your countersignature.
- A Sub-Investigator should be appointed as your backup. The backup must hold the same authorization (Privileges or Researcher Status) and institutional training as you, or better. Fellows and residents cannot serve as your backup.
- If you use CIM services, a CIM service agreement must be in place before CIM staff are added to the delegation log. Pharmacy maintains its own Task Delegation Log — obtain it and file it in the ISF before close-out.

<Tip>
  **Running a Phase I, oncology, or IV/infusion study?** The [Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)](/cim/planning) provides clinical research infrastructure — nursing, coordination, monitoring, regulatory support, and technical platforms. CIM offers both turnkey and à la carte service models. Engage early: CIM availability is limited and a service agreement must be in place before CIM staff are added to your delegation log.
</Tip>

### Training requirements for your team

Your entire team trains at the same level as the study requires. File every team member's current CV and all training certificates in the ISF before the <Tooltip tip="A monitoring visit conducted by a monitor as a final evaluation of the site, and to train the site team on the study protocol and its associated documents and processes." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip>. Your own CV must be [initialled and dated every 2 years](/sops/cr-002#5-3-5), and the other team members' CVs must be current. A team member whose training certificate lapses mid-study may not perform delegated tasks until they renew it. You are responsible for monitoring your team's certificate expiry, not just your own.

### Staff changes during the study

- **When a team member joins:** confirm their training is current, add them to the delegation log with your countersignature, notify the Sponsor, and update the study contact list.
- **When a team member leaves:** record their end date on the delegation log with your countersignature confirming undelegation. Notify the Sponsor. Submit a Nagano amendment (F1) for any change to listed investigators.
- **If you leave the study:** a new principal investigator should be named, the sponsor must be informed, and the contract must be revised and signed by you and the incoming principal investigator. The change should be documented on the [Study Team Member Change form](/sops/cr-002#appendix-5). Inform the REB without delay through an amendment in Nagano (Form F1). Before you leave, you must delegate all your tasks to the incoming principal investigator.

For the full QI/PI change process (five steps: TDL, Sponsor, REB amendment, QIU, backup), leave-of-absence retraining rules, and service department delegation, see [Team Management](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management).

For hiring research staff and the agreement each team member needs, see [Hire staff and put agreements in place](/kb/team/building-your-team#hire-staff-and-put-agreements-in-place). For inclusive team composition and recruitment strategies, and for sex and gender considerations in your research design, which CIHR expects when appropriate, see [EDI in Research](/kb/team/edi).

### Sub-Investigator responsibilities

A Sub-Investigator (Sub-I) is a physician or qualified professional who assists the QI/PI and can serve as PI backup when you are unavailable. The Sub-I is not a regulatory title in Canadian law — it is defined by what you delegate on the Task Delegation Log. Understanding the boundaries matters, because monitors and inspectors will check whether the Sub-I who performed a task was actually qualified and delegated to do it.

**What a Sub-I can do** (when delegated and documented on the TDL):

- Eligibility assessments and enrollment decisions
- Obtaining informed consent (if appropriately trained per [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008))
- Clinical assessments and medical evaluations during study visits
- AE/SAE causality and severity assessments
- IP prescription (drugs, biologics) — requires medical licensure
- Protocol deviation assessment and documentation
- Serving as PI backup during absences (if they hold the required credentials — see below)

**What a Sub-I cannot do:**

- Sign as QI/PI on regulatory documents (CTA submissions, REB applications, Nagano forms) — these require your signature specifically
- Act as your backup without the same authorization ([Human Research Privileges](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status) or Researcher Status) and institutional training as you, or better. Researcher Status alone is not sufficient for research that requires medical oversight, including any Health Canada-regulated study with an investigational product.
- Override your medical judgment on eligibility, causality, or clinical significance determinations

**Credential requirements for Sub-I backup:** the same authorization (Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status) and institutional training as you, or better. Privileges are required for studies that need medical oversight or are Health Canada-regulated with an investigational product.

## Ongoing obligations during the study

### What direct supervision means in practice

Delegating tasks does not mean stepping away from the study. Direct supervision means you are available and reachable during study activities, you review all data and safety documents in a timely manner, and you document that review with your initials. You do not need to be physically present for every procedure — but if a team member performs a delegated task requiring medical oversight, you must be contactable.

Before the Site Initiation Visit (SIV), confirm with your CRC that the ISF is set up, study supplies and IP storage are arranged through pharmacy, and consent forms match the REB-approved version. Team members are delegated tasks on the delegation log only after their protocol training, which may take place at the SIV. Your presence at the SIV is expected, and the sponsor's CRA will confirm these items with you directly.

During active conduct, ensure your team has a documented escalation path for urgent safety issues: who they contact, by what channel, and the expected response time. If you are unavailable, your Sub-Investigator backup must be reachable and the team must know to redirect.

The most common cause of study halts at active sites is a PI becoming unavailable without an adequately qualified Sub-I backup named on the delegation log. Make sure that [the Sub-Investigator who acts as your backup during a vacation, an absence or a sabbatical](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#name-a-backup-for-the-principal-investigator) holds the same authorization and institutional training as you, or better.

A clinical trial should ideally [list at least one sub-investigator as a co-investigator in Nagano](/kb/submission/preparing#name-the-principal-investigator), so that medical oversight continues if you become unavailable.{/* Sources: SOP-CR-500_06, section 3.0 (the Investigators are responsible for "Ensuring that a Sub-Investigator qualified to be a QI/PI backup (during vacation, absences, sabbaticals) has also obtained the same authorization (Privileges/Researcher Status) and institutional training as the QI/PI or better"); the co-investigator in Nagano: FACTS only ("Submission tips (CC #7)": the Centre for Applied Ethics' Community Connect #7, Nov. 27, 2024, clinical trials "should ideally have at least ONE co-investigator listed in Nagano"), stated on its home, Preparing Your Submission. b290 second reader (Nov. 19, 2026; MF, ledger row 330): "co-investigator" became "sub-investigator as a co-investigator", as on the home, and "is continuous" became the slide's reason ("Medical oversight needs to be continuous even if PI becomes unavailable"). ### Medical oversight

You retain medical oversight of all study activities at the site even when tasks are delegated. For every AE and SAE, your CRC documents and reports to you — you assess causality, determine seriousness, and document your assessment. Your initials are required on every SAE report before it is filed.

When a team member identifies a potential adverse event, you assess causality (relationship to the investigational product), severity, seriousness, and expectedness. You may delegate this assessment to a physician Sub-Investigator. Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline. If you disagree with the Sponsor's or CRA's causality assessment, document your rationale — your clinical judgment as QI takes precedence at the site level. Post-study SAEs remain your responsibility for the follow-up period defined in the protocol.

SAE reporting timelines are fixed — see [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012) for the full schedule. Each SUSAR report the sponsor distributes is reviewed by you or by a physician Sub-Investigator delegated SUSAR review. Document your medical oversight of each one by initialling and dating it. In Quebec, any medication given to a patient, including an investigational drug, must be dispensed by a pharmacist, and all drugs and some natural health products used in research must be managed by the MUHC pharmacy. You delegate these tasks to the pharmacy but remain responsible for them, including making sure the IP is used per the approved protocol ([SOP-CR-010](/sops/cr-010)). For the full IP management framework — the Quebec pharmacy dispensing requirement, storage and temperature monitoring, and accountability documentation — see [Investigational Products](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products).

### Informed consent obligations

See also: [Consent in Quebec](/kb/recruitment/consent) | [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) | [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006)

You are responsible for ensuring informed consent is obtained from every participant before any study procedure — including screening blood draws, eligibility questionnaires, or medical record review for screening. Document the consent process in the source record: who conducted the discussion, when, that the participant had adequate time to consider, that questions were answered, and that they signed voluntarily. The ICF signature alone is not sufficient documentation.

- **Re-consent:** once the REB approves an amended ICF, and the sponsor's go letter, where there is one, has arrived, re-consent each affected participant on it at their earliest visit to the site, unless the sponsor, the REB or a study or department SOP has a different requirement.
- **Withdrawal:** document the withdrawal, confirm whether already-collected data may be retained (per the original ICF), and follow the protocol's withdrawal procedures.
- **Quebec-specific rules:** substitute consent for adults who cannot consent, pediatric consent and assent, the treating-physician restriction and the requirement for consent forms in English and French, unless the REB accepts a justification, all apply. [Consent in Quebec](/kb/recruitment/consent) covers each in depth; see also [Pediatric Research](/kb/recruitment/pediatric-research) and [Research with Participants in Vulnerable Circumstances](/kb/recruitment/vulnerable-populations). Always use the current REB-approved ICF version.

If the protocol requires pre-screening before consent, you need the REB's approval to use the records without consent, MUHC authorization to access them and the short EFVP form, which you fill in; ask for them in your Nagano submission.

### Recruitment and enrollment

Recruitment should begin only after all of these are in place: Health Canada's authorization, if the study needs it; the REB approval letter; the MUHC Authorization letter; the sponsor's site initiation visit; documented training; documented task delegation on the Task Delegation Log; and the sponsor's go letter, where there is one. The Screening Log, Enrollment Log, and Participant ID Log are essential documents. For the full recruitment authorization sequence, screening procedures, and Quebec compensation rules, see [Recruitment, Screening, and Enrollment](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment).

### Protocol deviations

All deviations must be documented. Major and critical deviations go to the REB within its deadlines with the corrective and preventive action (CAPA) already taken, recorded on the CAPA form, which should also be sent to QA; any sponsor-approved waiver of an eligibility criterion, any change to the approved consent process, any deviation that leads to a serious adverse event and any other deviation that, in your judgment, jeopardizes participants' safety, the efficacy of the research or data integrity, or could otherwise affect participants' rights, safety or well-being also go to the REB within its deadlines, whatever their tier. Other minor deviations are reported at the annual continuing review. If your team identifies a potential deviation, they should notify you before acting — not after. 

Report [the other events the REB lists](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#report-participant-safety-and-ethical-concerns) to the MUHC REB in Nagano, such as a change to the research's risks or potential benefits, an unanticipated problem that could significantly affect the conduct of the research at the site, or a concern a participant raises about their rights or about ethical issues in the research. Report each one within the REB's deadlines in [Key reporting timelines](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines). A change made to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants is reported to the REB immediately. [Report a privacy breach](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person) to the REB as soon as you become aware of it. As applicable, also report it to the appropriate organizational officials. For classification guidance on ambiguous cases, root cause analysis, and the CAPA workflow, see [Deviation Management and CAPA](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management).

### Conflict of interest

You disclose conflicts on **two independent tracks**, both required, every year to McGill and, for each study, to the REB in the initial application, at each continuing review and whenever a new conflict arises (and, when it applies, to participants in the ICF). If a COI arises mid-study, disclose it promptly via Nagano amendment — do not wait for annual renewal. See [Conflict of Interest](/kb/governance/conflict-of-interest) for what counts as a conflict and how the REB manages one.

### Research integrity

You are responsible for honest, rigorous, and transparent conduct by your entire team. A concern about conduct within your team goes first to the MUHC's [process for events and allegations](/kb/governance/research-integrity#raise-a-concern-about-how-research-is-conducted). McGill University also requires anyone who has reasonable grounds to believe that research misconduct is occurring or has occurred at McGill or at an institution affiliated with it, such as the MUHC, to report it immediately, in writing; [Research Integrity](/kb/governance/research-integrity) explains where to report it and what counts as a breach of the responsible conduct of research.

### Annual renewal

Track the REB approval expiry date from day one. Submit the renewal via Nagano before approval lapses. A lapse in REB approval voids MUHC Authorization, and the renewal is not retroactive; [If REB approval lapses](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#if-reb-approval-lapses) says what to do. MUHC Authorization is revalidated with each timely REB renewal. The renewal submission includes a progress report, updated participant counts, any new safety information, and a current list of study personnel ([SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007)).

### At close-out

Review and sign off on the completed ISF before the Sponsor's <Tooltip tip="The last monitoring visit, arranged by the sponsor or sponsor-investigator and conducted by a monitor at the site, to make sure that every required aspect of closing the study has been addressed before database lock." headline="Close-Out Visit (COV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cov">Close-Out Visit</Tooltip>. ### Publications and results disclosure

ICMJE authorship criteria apply. For CIHR grants funded from Jan. 1, 2022, summary results must be public by [CIHR's deadline](/kb/close-out/publications#cihr-funded-trials), and publications of the results must be open access from the date of publication. For CTA publication clauses, authorship policies, and disclosure timelines, see [Publications, Authorship, and Results Disclosure](/kb/close-out/publications).

### Health Canada inspections

As PI, you are present for the opening and closing meetings with the inspector. You provide access to the site, the ISF, and study personnel. You should inform QA promptly when an inspection notice arrives; QA helps prepare and host the inspection as required. With QA, make sure every study file is complete, up to date and available for review ([SOP-CR-017](/sops/cr-017)).

For the deadline to inform QA, inspection frequency, notice periods, the GUI-0043 risk classification, and a full preparation walkthrough, see [Hosting a Health Canada Inspection](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/hc-inspection-guide).

### Archiving

The RI-MUHC's SOPs set **15-year retention**, counted from the study's completion, for Health Canada-regulated studies (drug, device and natural health product) in which at least one participant signed a consent form, screen failures included. The ISF must remain complete, secure, and retrievable for the full retention period. Do not destroy any study documents without written authorization from you and the Sponsor. If you change institutions before the retention period ends, notify the Sponsor and ensure storage transfer arrangements are made. You retain personal accountability for the integrity of the archived file ([SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015)).

For downloadable ISF templates, delegation logs, and archiving checklists, see [SOP Appendices and Templates](/sops/appendices).
