Level IV studies — prospective observational, educational, and non-invasive interventions — and Level V studies — retrospective chart reviews and studies using previously anonymized biobanked samples — follow a simpler regulatory pathway than interventional studies. There is no Health Canada submission, no investigational product, no sponsor monitoring, and (for Level V) usually no participant contact. Your personal accountability as PI for consent decisions, team oversight, and data integrity is unchanged.

<Info>
  This guide covers day-to-day operations for Level IV and V studies. For your personal accountability map — prerequisites, what only you sign, and training levels — see the [Investigator Orientation](/roles/investigator).
</Info>

<Tip>
  **Documents requiring your personal signature** — QI/PI signature obligations (Nagano commitment, delegation log countersignature, amendment sign-off, annual renewal) apply at every level. See [What only you sign](/roles/investigator#what-only-you-sign) on the Investigator Orientation.
</Tip>

## Understanding the role

As <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> at Level IV or V, your compliance path is lighter — the SOP-LR series, no external certifications — but the [four-layer regulatory framework](/kb/governance/regulatory-framework) still applies: federal (TCPS2), provincial (Civil Code consent rules, EFVP, MSSS framework), [institutional](/kb/governance/institutional-structure) (MUHC Authorization, RI-MUHC SOPs), and professional obligations. Quebec privacy law and institutional requirements are not covered by standard GCP training.

The difference between the two levels is where consent and data risk sit:

- **Level IV (prospective observational):** participants are contacted and consented; your primary accountability areas are the consent process, team oversight, and data integrity.
- **Level V (retrospective):** there is usually no participant contact; your primary accountability areas are the EFVP consent waiver decision, data access authorization, and REB maintenance.

### Training requirements

Both levels follow the SOP-LR path on a 5-year SOP Reader and Assessment cycle, with no external certifications required. For the full matrix, see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements).

<Info>
**If you also lead interventional studies:** your training level is set by your highest-risk active study. A Level IV observational alongside a Level I drug trial means you hold Level I certification. See the [Investigator Guide — Interventional](/roles/pi-guide).
</Info>

## Prerequisites

- **[Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status)** — Researcher Status is valid for Levels IV and V since no medical oversight is required. CPDP members may hold either. Apply via [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms). Valid for 3 years. The 90-day rule applies at submission.
- **SOP Reader and Assessment** — SOP-LR path, 5-year renewal. One course in [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms). Certificate must not be within 90 days of expiry at submission.

## Study submission

<Note>
See also: [Site Activation and Study Setup](/kb/site-activation/site-setup) | [Preparing Your Submission](/kb/submission/preparing) | [Submitting via Nagano](/kb/submission/submitting-via-nagano)
</Note>

- Submit via <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> (F11-H or the appropriate form type). No Health Canada submission is required at either level.
- Answer the F11 accurately. Service departments (nursing, laboratory, DPS) conduct feasibility reviews based on your answers. For Level V, the F17/F18 forms trigger the EFVP if identifiable data is involved — the most consequential feasibility review for a retrospective study.
- **Contracts and budget:** Contracts and agreements, where applicable, must be executed via My Contracts before <Tooltip tip="Institutional authorization allowing a study to proceed at the RI-MUHC. Issued after ethics approval and feasibility review." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>. Most studies at these levels are grant-funded or unfunded — budget for staff time, data management (CORD/REDCap), biostatistics (BCU), Data Warehouse access, and participant reimbursement as applicable. If data will be shared outside the MUHC — including with collaborators at other institutions — a Data Transfer Agreement is required before any data leaves the site. See [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) for overhead rates and REB fees.
- MUHC Authorization from the <Tooltip tip="Individual formally authorized to issue the final written authorization for each research project at a Quebec RSSS institution." headline="Personne mandatée (PFM)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> is required before any participant contact (Level IV) or before your team accesses any patient records for research purposes (Level V). REB approval alone is not sufficient.
- For Level V, the REB approval should explicitly state whether a consent waiver has been granted and specify the authorized data sources and patient population.

## Consent

### Level IV — prospective consent

<Note>
See also: [Informed Consent](/kb/recruitment/consent) | [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008)
</Note>

The same Quebec [consent obligations](/kb/recruitment/consent) apply to observational studies as to interventional ones. You are accountable for ensuring consent is obtained and documented before any study procedures.

- Consent forms must be available in French (Bill 96). Use only the current REB-approved version.
- **Incapacitated adults (Art. 21 CCQ):** substitute consent from the person with legal authority AND authorization from an MSSS-designated REB.
- Re-consent when amendments change what participants experience.

Some Level IV studies may qualify for alteration of consent — for example, observational studies where full disclosure of the research question would bias participant behavior. Alteration of consent is not automatic; it requires REB approval and must satisfy TCPS2 criteria (minimal risk, no adverse effect on welfare, research could not practicably be carried out without the alteration, and participants will be debriefed afterward if appropriate). If you believe your study qualifies, discuss with the REB before submission.

### Level V — the consent waiver decision

<Note>
See also: [Privacy and Confidentiality in Research](/kb/recruitment/privacy)
</Note>

Most retrospective studies operate under a consent waiver, but under <Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act to modernize personal information protection law (in force Sept 2023). Requires EFVP before sharing personal data without consent." headline="Law 25 / Loi 25" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#law-25">Loi 25</Tooltip>, whether a waiver is granted depends on the <Tooltip tip="Required under Quebec Law 25 before communicating personal information without consent for research. Non-compliance: up to $25M or 4% of worldwide turnover." headline="EFVP — Privacy Impact Assessment" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#efvp">EFVP</Tooltip> (privacy impact assessment).

<Warning>
**The legal default is always to obtain consent.** Access to identifiable data without consent requires approval from the EFVP committee. A waiver is granted when the committee determines that obtaining consent is not feasible — for example, a chart review of 500 patients from 2000 to 2010, where many patients may have died, been lost to follow-up, or have outdated contact information.

In contrast, if you are reviewing records for a small number of recent patients — say, five patients from the last year — the committee may determine it is reasonable to contact those patients and obtain consent, even though the research is retrospective.
</Warning>

The EFVP committee evaluates whether obtaining consent is infeasible — not merely inconvenient. Factors include: whether participants can be located, whether contacting them would reveal their health status to third parties, and whether the research could practicably be conducted with consent. A large cohort size alone does not establish infeasibility; the committee assesses the specific circumstances.

**Timeline:** Allow 4–6 weeks for the EFVP review. The outcome is communicated to the REB, which makes the final consent waiver decision. If the EFVP determines consent is feasible, you must obtain consent from each participant — following the Level IV consent process above — even though the study remains Level V in every other respect. Consider whether this changes the study design or feasibility, and confirm the consent requirement before your team begins any data extraction.

## Data access and privacy

<Note>
See also: [Privacy and Confidentiality in Research](/kb/recruitment/privacy) | [Research Data Security](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security)
</Note>

Privacy is the highest-risk area for retrospective studies, and the same obligations apply to identifiable data collected prospectively. You are responsible for ensuring your team accesses only the data authorized by the REB and handles it in compliance with Quebec law — the EFVP may also be required for a Level IV study that involves identifiable personal health information or uses software platforms for data collection.

- **OACIS access** must be authorized specifically for the study. For data beyond OACIS (lab results at scale, pharmacy dispensing, administrative data), the [MUHC Data Warehouse](/kb/design-support/data-warehouse) may be required — for cohort identification and feasibility assessment, it is often the starting point.
- **De-identification is not optional.** The research database must contain participant IDs only. Remove or code all direct identifiers (name, health insurance number, date of birth, address, phone number); indirect identifiers may remain only if they cannot reasonably re-identify an individual in context. The identification key must be stored separately from the research database — encrypted, access-restricted, and with a documented disposition plan. When in doubt, consult the CORD team.
- **Data transfer agreements** are required if data is shared outside the MUHC. Cross-border transfers (data leaving Quebec or Canada) have additional requirements under Loi 25.

**Data extraction (Level V):** Define the extraction plan before beginning — which variables, from which systems, for which date range. Have a second team member verify a sample (e.g., 10%) of extracted records against source for accuracy, and document the verification in the study file. If the extraction scope changes mid-study, submit an amendment before implementing the change.

<Info>
CORD can build and test REDCap databases for your study. Contact them early — database architecture decisions are costly to change after extraction begins. See [CORD](/kb/design-support/cord). For statistical methodology, sample size, and analysis planning, consult the [Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)](/kb/design-support/bcu).
</Info>

## Your team

- Set up the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> before any study tasks — including data access — begin. You countersign each entry. See [Building Your Research Team](/kb/team/building-your-team).
- Appoint a Sub-I backup who holds Privileges or Researcher Status — for these levels, Researcher Status is sufficient.
- Your team trains at the study's level on the SOP-LR path. File CVs and training certificates in the study file and monitor expiry dates.

For hiring research staff, see [Hiring and HR Support](/kb/team/hiring-hr-support). All team members need an RI-MUHC affiliation before performing any study tasks. For inclusive recruitment and GBA+ consultation (required by CIHR), see [EDI in Research](/kb/team/edi).

## Ongoing obligations

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Data oversight (Level IV)">
    Review collected data periodically — at minimum quarterly for multi-year studies. Check for completeness, consistency, and outliers that could indicate data entry errors or protocol non-compliance. Document each review (date, scope, findings, actions taken). Without a Sponsor's CRA checking your work, this self-monitoring is your primary quality assurance mechanism.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Safety (Level IV)">
    If a participant reports a health concern during a study interaction, assess whether it constitutes an unanticipated problem — an event that is unexpected, related or possibly related to the research, and suggests increased risk to participants. Not every health concern is an unanticipated problem; the assessment requires your clinical judgment. Document your assessment even if the conclusion is that no reporting is required. Report unanticipated problems to the REB regardless of causality.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Deviations">
    Document all deviations — including, at Level V, unauthorized data access or extraction outside the approved scope. Report major deviations to QA and the REB. See [Reportable Events](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Amendments">
    If what participants experience changes (Level IV), or the data extraction plan changes — new variables, expanded population, new data sources (Level V) — submit an amendment before implementing the change.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Annual renewal">
    Submit via Nagano before REB approval lapses. Confirm your credentials and your team's are current, and include any changes to data extraction scope. See [Tracking and Responding to Submissions](/kb/submission/tracking-responding).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Conflict of interest">
    COI disclosure operates through two independent tracks — McGill institutional (annual disclosure to your supervisor) and REB study-level (at Nagano submission and whenever a new conflict arises). Both are required. If a COI arises mid-study, disclose promptly via Nagano amendment. See [Conflict of Interest](/kb/governance/conflict-of-interest).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Research integrity">
    You are responsible for honest, rigorous, and transparent conduct by your entire team. Data fabrication, falsification, or selective omission of records in a chart review is research misconduct; report suspected misconduct through McGill's structures. See [Research Integrity](/kb/governance/research-integrity).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Close-out

- Confirm all data collection or extraction is complete and queries resolved, and the database is final. See [Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out/close-out).
- Submit the Study Completion Report to the REB via Nagano.
- Record end dates on the delegation log for all team members.
- Execute the data disposition plan — destroy or archive the identification key as documented.
- Archive the study file. The 15-year Division 5 retention requirement does not apply to Levels IV–V; the minimum for non-regulated studies is 7 years from study closure (see [Data Integrity](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity)). Confirm requirements with QA based on the funding source and institutional policy.
