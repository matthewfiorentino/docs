## Understanding the role

For your role definition, scope, and training requirements, see the [CRC Orientation](/roles/coordinator). This section covers the operational context specific to interventional studies (Levels I–III).

### Study types and what changes for your work

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Industry-sponsored — Sponsor's CRA monitors your site">
    A Sponsor's CRA monitors on a Sponsor-set schedule; monitoring letters must be answered and QI/PI-initialled. The Sponsor controls the protocol and timelines. The CRA is not your supervisor — if a request conflicts with your SOPs or protocol, escalate to your PI and QA before acting.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Investigator-initiated — PI initiated the study independently">
    There may be no commercial Sponsor providing a CRA — monitoring arrangements fall to the PI, and you often take on more ISF setup, monitoring coordination, and Health Canada safety reporting.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sponsor-Investigator — PI is both Sponsor and QI">
    Your PI holds dual [Sponsor and QI obligations](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator) — CTA filing, independent monitoring, IP supply, GMP compliance — so as CRC you may manage more <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip> infrastructure than usual. Confirm with your PI which Sponsor-level tasks are delegated to the site team.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Single-site — MUHC is the only site">
    A single F11 submission through the MUHC REB covers all three review streams — scientific, ethics, feasibility. Your PI is the responsible investigator; the Personne mandatée reviews all three and decides on MUHC Authorization.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Multi-site, lead — MUHC REB reviews for all Quebec RSSS sites">
    Your PI is the responsible investigator and the Nagano submission covers all participating Quebec RSSS sites — you may coordinate with CRCs at other sites and track authorization across institutions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Multi-site, participating — MEO submission; another institution leads">
    The reviewing REB is the lead institution's, not the MUHC REB. Your Nagano submission is a participating-site (MEO) file; MUHC feasibility review and Authorization are still required locally. Contact the Research Facilitator for guidance.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Your training requirements

Levels I–III follow the SOP-CR path on a 2-year SOP Reader and Assessment cycle, with GCP required for Levels I–II, Health Canada Division 5 for Level I, and ISO 14155 for Level II. If you work on studies at different levels, your training is set by the highest-risk active study. For the full matrix, see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements) and the [CRC Orientation](/roles/coordinator).

### On the study team

- **QI/PI:** carries ultimate legal and regulatory accountability for the study. You execute; they oversee. Their countersignature on the delegation log confirms your qualification for your tasks, but accountability does not transfer either direction.
- **Sub-Investigator (Sub-I):** assists the PI and can serve as PI backup if they hold RI-MUHC Human Research Privileges — or Researcher Status where the study does not require medical oversight; see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status).
- **Research Nurse / CRC-Nurse:** performs clinical procedures (blood draws, vital signs, medication administration) reserved under Quebec's _Code des professions_ — these cannot be delegated to non-nurse CRCs regardless of the TDL. If you hold an OIIQ licence, the [Research Nurse guide](/roles/nurse) covers the nursing-specific operational layer.
- **Research Assistant:** may support data entry, specimen processing, filing, or scheduling, typically under CRC or PI supervision with a narrower TDL scope.
- **Data Manager / Biostatistician:** on larger or investigator-initiated studies, may handle database design, query management, or analysis.
- **Sponsor's CRA (monitor):** an external representative who verifies data and GCP compliance — not your supervisor, and cannot instruct you to deviate from the protocol or SOPs. Monitoring letters must be responded to, queries resolved, and filed in the ISF; your PI initials every letter.

### Institutional contacts and service departments

- **QA department:** contact QA when uncertain whether a task is compliant — before a deviation occurs, not after.
- **Research Facilitator:** your entry point for RI-MUHC processes, the Nagano workflow, and study-specific questions.
- **Research Agreements Office:** reviews contracts and agreements; no study can begin without a signed agreement where one is required.
- **Training and Learning Solutions Advisor:** questions about [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms), the SOP Reader and Assessment, or training records — **training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca**.
- **MUHC service departments** conduct their own feasibility reviews during Nagano submission — answer the F11 accurately, as underreporting delays Authorization when the gap surfaces during review. **Pharmacy** handles IP storage, dispensing, and accountability, issues its own agreement and Task Delegation Log (file at close-out). **Nursing services** and **Laboratory (OPTILAB-MUHC)** trigger their own feasibility reviews and agreements when required. **DPS** approves use of MUHC resources (beds, operating rooms, imaging, medical records). **Privacy and information security** trigger the <Tooltip tip="Required under Quebec Law 25 before communicating personal information without consent for research. Non-compliance: up to $25M or 4% of worldwide turnover." headline="ÉFVP — Privacy Impact Assessment" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#efvp">ÉFVP</Tooltip> via the F17/F18 forms when identifiable PHI, software, or platforms are involved.

### Your role in Nagano submissions

CRCs frequently build Nagano submissions on behalf of the PI — creating the project, completing the F11, and uploading supporting documents — but the submission is not complete until the PI's final commitment and signature step, which you cannot substitute for. See the [Nagano User Guide](/apps/nagano/overview).

## Part 1: Study lifecycle

A chronological walkthrough from pre-activation through close-out.

## Pre-activation

See also: [Site Activation and Study Setup](/kb/site-activation/site-setup) | [The Investigator Site File](/kb/site-activation/isf) | [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015)

Everything that must be in place before the first participant is approached. Pre-activation work is the foundation every subsequent monitoring visit, audit, and inspection will evaluate.

<Warning>
**Four conditions must be confirmed in writing and filed in the ISF before the first participant is approached.** [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006) §5.3.8 is explicit: REB approval, MUHC Authorization from the Personne mandatée, the Site Initiation Visit completed with a signed Task Delegation Log, and — for sponsored studies — the Sponsor's Go Letter. REB approval alone does not authorize participant contact, and no protocol activity, including screening, may begin until all four are in hand. A consent obtained before all four conditions are met is a protocol deviation. See [Site Activation and Study Setup](/kb/site-activation/site-setup).
</Warning>

### ISF setup

Set up the ISF structure per [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015) before the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">Site Initiation Visit</Tooltip> — the SIV reviews an ISF already in progress, not the starting point. File the MUHC Authorization letter as soon as received, along with the signed protocol, approved ICF, Investigator's Brochure or product monograph, REB approval letter, and — for drug studies — the Health Canada No Objection Letter and QIU form signed by your PI; for device studies, the ITA. MUHC investigators should **not** sign the US FDA Form 1572 — if a US sponsor requests it, use the RI-MUHC ICH GCP attestation form (Appendix 4 of [SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002)) instead. Confirm the executed clinical trial agreement is filed (no agreement, no Authorization), and any confidentiality agreement or NDA is signed before materials or data are shared ([SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019)).

### Team, training, and delegation

The Task Delegation Log must be complete — every team member named, signed, and PI-countersigned — before any study task begins ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002)). File current CVs and training certificates (not within 90 days of expiry) for everyone on the log. Confirm the Sub-Investigator PI backup is named and holds RI-MUHC Human Research Privileges — or Researcher Status where the study does not require medical oversight; see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status). Fellows and rotating residents cannot serve as PI backup. If pharmacy is involved, obtain and file its own Task Delegation Log.

### Site Initiation Visit

The SIV, led by the Sponsor's CRA, covers protocol training, IP procedures, EDC access, source document templates, and an ISF completeness review. Document all training on the Training Log (a separate document from the delegation log) and file the attendance sheet and slides in the ISF. Confirm the trial is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov before the first participant is enrolled — a Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator obligation ([SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007)).

## Consent and enrolment

See also: [Consent in Quebec](/kb/recruitment/consent) | [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) | [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006)

The highest-risk phase for protocol deviations. The rules here are specific, some are Quebec-specific, and violations are among the most common findings at inspections.

### Before you approach anyone

- No screening, recruitment contact, or consent activities before the MUHC Authorization letter is in the ISF. Not "pending" — received and filed.
- All recruitment materials must be REB-approved before use. If materials are to be displayed on MUHC walls or screens, MUHC Communications approval is required after REB approval.
- Always use the current REB-approved version of the ICF. Version control is your responsibility.

### The consent process

Your obligation is to ensure consent is free, informed, and documented — not merely signed. Provide the French version of the ICF under Bill 96, give participants adequate time to read and ask questions, and document the process (not just the signature) in the source record. Confirm who conducts the discussion against your Task Delegation Log before leading a consent process — reserved nursing acts (blood draws, medication administration, patient assessment) require licensed personnel regardless of what the log says. For incapacitated adults, Art. 21 CCQ requires consent from the person with legal authority AND authorization from a Quebec MSSS-designated REB; an incapacitated person who understands and objects cannot be enrolled regardless of substitute consent. For vulnerable populations and pediatric consent, see [Vulnerable Populations](/kb/recruitment/vulnerable-populations) and [Pediatric Research](/kb/recruitment/pediatric-research).

### Eligibility and enrolment documentation

- Eligibility must be confirmed against every inclusion and exclusion criterion. Document each criterion in the source record.
- Record every screened participant on the Screening Log — including screen failures and reasons for ineligibility. This log is reviewed at every monitoring visit.
- Record every enrolled participant on the Enrolment Log with their participant ID. Never use participant names in study documents — use the assigned ID and maintain the Subject Identification Log separately in a secure location.
- File a copy of the signed ICF in the ISF and provide a copy to the participant. Document in the source record that a copy was given.

## Active conduct

See also: [Data Integrity](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity) | [Reportable Events](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events) | [Monitoring, Audits and Inspections](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits)

The longest and most operationally complex phase. Three areas dominate: source data and <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> integrity, safety reporting, and monitoring visit management.

Under E6(R3), procedures that receive the most attention at the SIV and monitoring visits are the study's critical-to-quality factors (CTQs). When competing tasks conflict, CTQs come first. See [GCP and Quality by Design](/kb/foundations/gcp-quality-by-design).

### Source data and CRF integrity

CRF entries must match source documents (hospital charts, lab reports, nursing notes, diaries) — discrepancies are a finding at every monitoring visit. Complete CRFs promptly, note the reason for any late entry, and never back-fill or reconstruct data undocumented. Corrections follow GDP: single line through the error, correct value, sign or initial, and date (never correction fluid); eCRF corrections follow the system's audit trail. All QI/PI-delegated review of data — lab results, safety reports, medical assessments — must be initialled and dated by the QI/PI.

### Safety reporting

<Warning>
**<Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">AE</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reporting timelines are fixed and non-negotiable.** Missed timelines are among the most cited findings at Health Canada inspections. When in doubt, report and then clarify — do not wait for certainty before notifying your PI.
</Warning>

Your role in safety reporting is to identify, document, and escalate promptly — the QI/PI performs the medical assessment and determines reportability.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Document the event in the source record" />
  <Step title="Notify the QI/PI immediately" />
  <Step title="Complete the SAE report form" />
  <Step title="Support the QI/PI in meeting Sponsor and REB reporting timelines" />
</Steps>

External SUSARs from the Sponsor must be filed in the ISF and initialled and dated by the QI/PI to document review. See also [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012).

Participant safety issues, informed consent problems, or undue risk should be reported to the MUHC REB immediately — even if no formal protocol deviation has occurred. Do not wait for the next reporting cycle.

### Monitoring visits

Your obligation spans the full cycle: before the visit, resolve outstanding queries from the previous letter and ensure source documents, CRFs, and the ISF are current and accessible; during, provide access to records and answer questions while the monitor verifies source data, the ISF, and delegation and training logs; after, sign the Monitoring Log, file the follow-up letter, and chase the QI/PI's initial and date on it before the next visit. Respond to all queries and <Tooltip tip="Actions taken to resolve a compliance issue and prevent recurrence: identify, correct, prevent." headline="Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#capa">CAPA</Tooltip> requests within the stated timelines. See [Monitoring, Audits and Inspections](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits).

### Ongoing team and document management

Update the delegation log immediately on staff changes, notify the Sponsor, and update the contact list — new members need institutional and protocol training first (see [Team Management](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management) for onboarding, QI/PI changes, and leave rules). When an SOP is revised, complete the reset [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) Reader unit before your next task it governs. For REB-approved amendments, document training on the Training Log and update the ISF before implementing — both Health Canada and REB approval are required first, and implementing early is a deviation (see [Study Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments)). A lapsed GCP or SOP Reader and Assessment certificate means removal from the delegation log until renewed; leave of more than 6 months requires training renewal and a documented trained backup before return ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002)).

## Annual renewal

See also: [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007) | [SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026)

REB approval is valid for up to one year. A lapse in approval halts the study. Track the expiry date from day one and treat renewal as a recurring calendar event.

- Submit the annual renewal via Nagano before REB approval expires. The MUHC Authorization is automatically revalidated with each REB renewal — but only if the renewal is timely.
- The annual renewal submission includes a progress report, updated participant counts, any safety information, and a current list of study personnel.
- File the new REB approval letter in the ISF as soon as it is received.
- Watch your own training certificate expiry alongside the REB renewal date. QA will flag expired or near-expiry credentials and the submission will not pass feasibility review.

## Close-out

See also: [Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out/close-out) | [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016)

Close-out is CRC-heavy work. The Sponsor's monitor conducts a <Tooltip tip="Final monitoring visit to verify all study closure requirements have been addressed before database lock." headline="Close-Out Visit (COV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cov">Close-Out Visit</Tooltip> to verify the ISF is complete and data are clean before the site is formally closed.

### Before the close-out visit

Confirm the last participant has completed their last visit and all CRFs are complete and accurate, review the ISF for completeness against the [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015) essential document checklist, resolve outstanding queries and CRF corrections before the monitor arrives, ensure every monitoring visit has a corresponding QI/PI-initialled follow-up letter filed, and notify pharmacy, lab, and other involved departments of the visit date.

### Delegation log and team close-out

Record end dates for all team members on the Task Delegation Log as they conclude study activities, with the QI/PI's signature confirming undelegation. The CRC and QI/PI remain on the log to respond to post-close-out queries.

### After the close-out visit

File the Close-Out Visit Report and Close-Out Notification letter in the ISF, submit the Study Completion Report to the REB via Nagano ([SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026)), and update the ClinicalTrials.gov registration. See also: [Publications, Authorship, and Results Disclosure](/kb/close-out/publications).

### Archiving

**15-year retention** applies to Health Canada regulated studies (drug, device, NHP) under Division 5 (C.05.012). Do not destroy or transfer any study documents without written authorization from both the QI/PI and the Sponsor, and obtain the Essential Document Destruction Request form signed by both before any destruction at the end of the retention period ([SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015)).

<Warning>
**Inspections after close-out.** Health Canada may inspect a site after study completion — sometimes years later. The ISF must remain complete, retrievable, and intact for the full retention period.
</Warning>

## Part 2: Domain reference

Operational guidance organized by competency domain. These sections cover areas of CRC work that span the full study lifecycle. Each includes practical learning activities you can do with your supervisor or an experienced colleague.

## Recruitment and screening

See also: [Recruitment, Screening and Enrolment](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment) | [SOP-CR-009](/sops/cr-009)

### Identifying potential participants

Building a candidate pool depends on your study and patient population. Most interventional studies start with OACIS chart review to pre-screen candidates against key criteria (confirm your OACIS permissions first) — pre-screening identifies candidates to approach, while formal screening happens after consent. Referrals from clinical teams are another major source; a one-page eligibility summary shared with the team works better than the full protocol. All recruitment materials (posters, ads, letters) must be REB-approved before use, with MUHC Communications approval added if displayed on MUHC walls or screens. Maintain a screening log from the start, recording every candidate approached, consent status, and reason for any screen failure.

### Applying eligibility criteria

Every inclusion and exclusion criterion must be assessed and documented in the source record for every participant, not just a summary checkbox. Criteria requiring clinical judgment must be assessed by the QI/PI or a delegated Sub-I, not the CRC independently, unless your delegation log authorizes it; criteria depending on timing need careful window tracking; criteria referencing local standards need lab-confirmed reference ranges before screening begins. Document the clinical reasoning and QI/PI sign-off for any borderline case.

### Screen failures

Record the screen failure on the Screening Log with the specific reason, complete any required screen-failure CRF pages, and document any procedures performed before the failure was identified along with any needed safety follow-up. Do not re-screen a participant unless the protocol explicitly permits it.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Recruitment and screening">
    **Observe:** Sit with an experienced CRC during a pre-screening session. Watch how they review OACIS records, apply eligibility criteria, and decide who to approach.

    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - How do you build referral relationships with clinical teams in your department? What has worked and what hasn't?
    - How do you handle borderline eligibility — when a participant is close to an exclusion criterion but not clearly out?
    - What are the most common reasons for screen failure on our current studies?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Investigational products

See also: [Investigational Products](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products) | [SOP-CR-010](/sops/cr-010)

### Receipt, storage, and dispensing

On receipt, verify the shipment against the packing list and temperature monitors, and record it in the Drug Accountability Log before transferring it to storage — an out-of-range temperature reading must be reported to the Sponsor before use. Store IP per the protocol and Investigator's Brochure, check and document conditions per your site's SOP, and keep it separate from commercial medications and labelled "For Clinical Trial Use Only." Quarantine expired or damaged product and notify the Sponsor; never destroy or return without written Sponsor instructions. For drug studies, pharmacy typically dispenses — your role is to coordinate the visit, confirm dosing eligibility, and record every dispensing event and participant compliance in the accountability log.

### Return and destruction

At close-out, reconcile the Drug Accountability Log against actual inventory — every unit received must be accounted for as dispensed, returned, destroyed, or remaining. Never destroy IP without written Sponsor authorization.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Investigational products">
    **Observe:** Accompany the research pharmacist during an IP receipt and dispensing session. Note how they verify shipments, log inventory, and handle temperature excursions.

    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - Walk me through the IP accountability flow for one of our active studies — from arrival to final reconciliation at close-out.
    - What happens if a temperature monitor shows an excursion during shipping?
    - How do we handle unblinding in an emergency? Who has the codes, and what's the procedure?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Biological specimens

See also: [Management of Biological Specimens](/kb/conduct/study-operations/biological-specimens) | [SOP-CR-011](/sops/cr-011)

### Collection, labelling, and shipping

Follow the Lab Manual for tube types, draw order, fill volumes, and processing windows — deviations from the window are reportable, and if you are not a licensed nurse or phlebotomist, you cannot perform blood draws regardless of the delegation log. Label every container with protocol-specified identifiers (participant ID, visit, date/time, sample type — never names) and verify against the source record before it leaves the room. Package for transport per IATA TDG regulations — most specimens are UN3373, Category B, requiring triple packaging — using a Sponsor-approved carrier; IATA TDG training may be required. Maintain a Specimen Inventory Log tracking collection, processing, shipping, on-site storage, and final disposition at close-out.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Biological specimens">
    **Observe:** Watch an experienced CRC or lab technician process and package a specimen shipment. Pay attention to the timing of processing steps, the labelling procedure, and how the shipping package is assembled.

    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - What are the most common specimen handling errors on our studies, and how do we prevent them?
    - What happens when a specimen is accidentally mislabelled or processed outside the window?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Budgets, contracts, and study finances

See also: [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts)

### Participant reimbursements

- Under Quebec's Civil Code (Art. 25 CCQ), participant compensation is limited to reimbursement of actual expenses, pro-rated to actual participation — not payment for time or inconvenience. The amount and method are specified in the protocol and REB-approved ICF. See [Recruitment, Screening and Enrolment](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment).
- Maintain a Participant Reimbursement Log documenting every payment: date, participant ID, amount, and reason.

### Expense tracking and invoicing

- Study budgets are managed through cost centres on the RI-MUHC Portal (MyAccounts).
- For industry-sponsored studies, the Sponsor pays based on milestones. Your role may include tracking when milestones are met and flagging them for invoicing.
- For investigator-initiated studies, track spending against the approved budget and alert your PI if expenditures are trending above projections.

### Contracts

- No study activity can begin before the clinical trial agreement is signed and filed. Contracts are managed via My Contracts on the Portal.
- If amendments to the study scope, budget, or timeline are negotiated, ensure the contract amendment is executed before implementing changes.
- Data Transfer Agreements (DTAs), Material Transfer Agreements (MTAs), and confidentiality agreements may also be required.
- While the Research Agreements Office negotiates CTAs, you should understand the key clauses — especially publication restrictions, termination provisions, and when study activities can begin relative to contract execution. See [Reviewing Clinical Trial Agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements).

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Budgets and finances">
    **Review:** Ask your supervisor to walk you through the budget for one of your active studies. Identify the per-patient payment structure, which milestones trigger invoicing, and how participant reimbursements are processed.

    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - How do I process a participant reimbursement — what forms are needed and where do I submit them?
    - What is our invoicing process for sponsor milestones, and who is responsible for tracking them?
    - How do I check the study's financial status on the Portal?
  </Accordion>

## Privacy and data protection

See also: [Privacy and Confidentiality](/kb/recruitment/privacy) | [Data Security](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security) | [SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019)

**Loi 25** (Quebec Law 25) governs the protection of personal information. If your study collects, accesses, or uses identifiable personal health information (PHI), the institution may be required to complete a privacy impact assessment (ÉFVP) before data collection begins.

### Day-to-day implications

- **Access to medical records.** Your access to OACIS and other clinical systems must be authorized and limited to what is necessary for the study.
- **Data storage.** Participant data must be stored on secure, institutionally approved systems. Do not store identifiable data on personal devices, USB drives, or unapproved cloud services.
- **Data sharing.** If identifiable or coded data is shared outside the MUHC, confirm that the appropriate data agreements and privacy assessments are in place. Cross-border transfers have additional requirements under Loi 25.
- **De-identification.** Use participant IDs, not names, in all study documents. The Subject Identification Log must be stored separately in a secure location with restricted access.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Privacy and data protection">
    **Review:** Read the data management plan for one of your studies. Identify what personal health information is collected, where it is stored, and whether any data leaves the MUHC.

    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - Has an ÉFVP been completed for this study? What were the conditions?
    - What systems am I authorized to access, and where is that authorization documented?
    - If I accidentally access a record outside the study population in OACIS, who do I report it to?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Protocol deviations

See also: [Reportable Events and Ethical Concerns](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events) | [SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026)

### What counts as a deviation

A protocol deviation is any departure from the approved protocol, ICH GCP, institutional SOPs, or REB approval conditions — missed or out-of-window visits, eligibility issues, consent timing, incorrect dosing, late or incorrect CRF entries, missed safety timelines, or procedures performed by someone not on the delegation log or whose training has lapsed. Severity follows the standard minor / major / critical classification (by impact on participant safety or data integrity).

### Your role

- **Identify.** Deviations are often caught during routine work — the earlier, the better.
- **Document.** Record what happened, when, why, and the corrective action, in the source record and any Sponsor tracking form.
- **Report.** Notify your PI immediately; major and critical deviations must also reach the Sponsor, QA, and the REB within required timelines.
- **Correct.** Implement CAPAs, and if the cause is systemic, fix the process with your PI and QA, not just the instance. See [Deviation Management and CAPA](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management) for root cause analysis and the CAPA workflow.

### Common CRC-related deviations

Untracked visit windows, consent timing gaps, incomplete screen-failure documentation, IP accountability logs not updated at dispensing, and monitoring letter responses missed. See [Deviation Management](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management#preventing-common-deviations) for targeted prevention strategies.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Protocol deviations">
    **Review:** Ask your supervisor for an example of a deviation report from a previous study (with identifying details removed). Trace how it was identified, documented, classified, and reported.

    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - What are the most common deviations on our active studies, and what has been done to prevent them?
    - When does a deviation need to be reported to the REB vs. just documented internally?
    - Walk me through a CAPA you implemented — what changed as a result?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Data management beyond CRFs

See also: [Data Integrity](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity) | [SOP-CR-014](/sops/cr-014)

### EDC systems

Most industry-sponsored studies use a Sponsor-provided EDC system (e.g., Medidata Rave, Oracle InForm, Veeva Vault CDMS); investigator-initiated studies most often use [REDCap](/apps/redcap). Complete EDC training (Sponsor-provided, or via [CORD](/kb/design-support/cord) for REDCap) before entering data, and enter data promptly — most systems track timeliness.

### Query resolution

Respond to queries promptly — open counts are reviewed at every monitoring visit. Check the source document first; if the CRF entry was wrong, correct it following GDP, but never change the source to match the CRF. See [EDC Systems and Query Management](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/edc-query-management) for effective query responses, SAE reconciliation, and audit trail practices.

### Database lock

At the end of the study, the database is locked — no further changes without formal unlocking. Before lock: all CRFs must be complete and clean, all queries resolved and closed, and the QI/PI must sign the data review confirmation. Missing the database lock deadline can delay the entire study.
