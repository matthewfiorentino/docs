A <Tooltip tip="A nurse, health professional or other qualified member of the research team who handles most of the administrative responsibilities and day-to-day activities of a clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">clinical research coordinator</Tooltip> runs a study day to day with the <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip>, who leads the study at the MUHC. [The coordinator works with the principal investigator and a multidisciplinary research team](/sops/cr-002#5-4-1) to keep the study to rigorous standards, and acts as an advocate for the participants. A coordinator is listed on the <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> before carrying out any study task, and carries out only the tasks the principal investigator has delegated to them there.

The role has no regulatory definition in Canadian law: no national licence, no regulated title and no statutory scope of practice. What a coordinator may do is what has been delegated to them on the log, and what their own professional credentials allow.

Research nurse and protocol nurse are among the other names for a clinical research coordinator. The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) [delegate the collection and reporting of adverse events to coordinators and nurses](/sops/cr-012#5-1-3). A coordinator who holds a nursing licence has the same tasks and rules as any coordinator, and [Research Nurse](/roles/nurse) gives the duties that come with the licence.

A coordinator does the role's tasks [alone or with the help of other members of the research team](/sops/cr-002#5-4-3). A research assistant may be delegated some of these tasks, and [what a research assistant may do](/kb/team/building-your-team#fill-the-core-roles) depends on their qualifications.

## Know which tasks can be delegated to you

The principal investigator delegates study tasks on the Task Delegation Log, and [keeps oversight of every person given a task](/sops/cr-002#5-3-5). [Sign and date the log yourself to accept the tasks delegated to you](/sops/cr-002#5-1-2). [Your training for each task must be documented](/sops/cr-002#5-6-3) before you and the principal investigator sign the log. The principal investigator's signature on the log confirms that you are qualified and trained for the tasks delegated to you.

The signature doesn't move the responsibility for the study to you. Every member of the team stays responsible for carrying out and documenting their own role. The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) sets ISO 14155, the good clinical practice standard for devices. In a medical device study, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-015#3-0-responsibilities) read ISO 14155 as delegating the responsibility along with the task.

The tables below list the coordinator's main tasks by stage of the study. The second column says what stays with the principal investigator. [Investigator](/roles/investigator#know-what-you-are-responsible-for-as-principal-investigator) gives the principal investigator's side in full, with what only the principal investigator may do.

### During the study

| What you may do, once delegated | What stays with the principal investigator |
|---|---|
| Give participants the information they need about the study, and [obtain their consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#choose-who-obtains-consent) if you are qualified by education and experience, have documented training on the consent SOPs and the protocol, know the study procedures, and are delegated the task on the log. | The principal investigator must ensure that each participant's consent is obtained and documented. The principal investigator may be present to answer questions. |
| Coordinate participants' appointments, and carry out the study procedures the principal investigator authorizes. | Medical care and medical decisions in the study are under the principal investigator's supervision. |
| Complete the case report forms (CRFs) in a timely fashion, consistent with the source documents, and [answer the data queries](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/edc-query-management#answer-queries). [Record and correct data](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#record-and-correct-data-properly) as the SOPs require. | The principal investigator should sign and date the CRF where indicated. |
| Coordinate the sponsor's monitoring visits. [Prepare for each visit](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#prepare-for-a-monitoring-visit), including a monitoring log for you and the monitor to sign on the visit date. Sign the monitoring log with the monitor before the monitor leaves. | The principal investigator should be available at the time of the visit. The principal investigator [initials and dates each monitoring letter](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#respond-to-the-monitoring-letter) to document medical oversight. |
| [Renew the monitor's ID for OACIS](/apps/oacis#requesting-access-for-external-monitors), the MUHC's electronic medical record system, each year by sending the study's REB annual renewal approval to Medical Records. When the monitor no longer works on the study, notify Medical Records and ask them to remove the monitor's access to OACIS. | The RI-MUHC's SOPs give the renewal to the coordinator or a delegate. [The monitoring procedure remains the ultimate responsibility](/sops/cr-013#3-0-responsibilities) of the sponsor, the sponsor-investigator or the principal investigator. |
| Liaise with the hospital departments involved in the study, such as the laboratory, the pharmacy and radiology, with third parties and with the REB. | At the principal investigator's request, [the specialists of the other parties involved, or the supervisors of a team on rotation, must be added to the log](/sops/cr-002#5-5-1). |
| Tell the principal investigator about each protocol deviation as soon as you identify it, and document what happened. [Every deviation is reported to the sponsor, and some also to the REB](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations). [Work through a corrective and preventive action (CAPA)](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management#working-through-a-capa) where the deviation needs one. | Assessing, addressing and documenting every deviation can be delegated. The responsibility rests with the principal investigator. |
| Submit amendments, reportable events and renewals to the REB in Nagano. | The responsibility for complying with the REB's requirements rests with the principal investigator. An amendment is not implemented until [every approval it needs](/roles/investigator#during-the-study) is in place. |
| When a team member is on [leave for more than 6 months](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#leave-of-absence), make sure that the Task Delegation Log shows them as undelegated and replaced by a backup with appropriate qualifications and training. Update the study's contact list, and notify the sponsor of the change in staff. | The principal investigator signs the log to delegate tasks, and to undelegate them. |

## Know what you may not do

[A coordinator does not perform acts reserved to a licensed professional](/kb/team/building-your-team#fill-the-core-roles), unless licensed. Quebec's [Nurses Act](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/I-8) reserves activities to nurses, among them applying invasive techniques and administering prescribed medications. Only a nurse may engage in them, unless the law expressly gives that right to another professional or a regulation made under paragraph h of section 94 of the Professional Code allows it.

The [Professional Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/C-26) reserves some activities to the members of other professional orders, within the scope of their profession. Drawing blood is among them. Medical technologists may perform phlebotomies, licensed practical nurses may introduce an instrument into a peripheral vein to take a specimen, and respiratory therapists may introduce an instrument into a peripheral vein, each according to a prescription.

No item of the lists of reserved activities in the [Medical Act](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/M-9) and the Nurses Act names taking vital signs. Both Acts reserve the clinical monitoring of the condition of persons whose state of health is problematic, each in its own terms.

<Warning>
A coordinator whom no law authorizes cannot perform blood draws, administer prescribed medication or carry out any other reserved activity, whatever the Task Delegation Log or the protocol says. Reserved activities must be delegated to a nurse or another professional the law authorizes.
</Warning>

Some tasks stay with the principal investigator, or with an investigator, whatever is delegated to you:
- Completing the Principal Investigator's Commitment and Signature step of a Nagano submission.
- Assessing adverse events and reviewing external SUSARs, which the RI-MUHC's SOPs delegate to physician investigators.
- The final medical decision on a participant's eligibility.
- Prescribing medication in a study with an investigational product.

[Investigator](/roles/investigator#know-what-you-are-responsible-for-as-principal-investigator) lists the principal investigator's responsibilities in full.

Each person who signs a consent form should personally date it. [A coordinator must not date the form for a participant](/sops/cr-006#appendix-1).

The sponsor's monitor verifies the study's data and its compliance with good clinical practice, and is not your supervisor. A monitor cannot instruct you to deviate from the protocol or the SOPs. If a monitor's request conflicts with the protocol or the SOPs, consider raising it with the principal investigator and [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you act.

## Follow the critical rules

Each of these rules applies to the tasks a coordinator carries out:

- Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
- All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes before all the required signatures are on the form is a deviation.
- Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.
- Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.
- If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk.

Every Knowledge Base page applies to your studies as its "Applies to" strip shows, and a section with its own strip applies to the studies that strip names.
