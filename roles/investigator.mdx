The <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip> leads a study at the MUHC and is responsible for its overall conduct at the site. In a drug or natural health product trial, the principal investigator is also the <Tooltip tip="The person responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a drug or natural health product trial at a clinical trial site, who is entitled to provide health care under the laws of the province where the site is located." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">qualified investigator</Tooltip>, the person responsible to the sponsor for the trial at the site.

Students, trainees and residents may not conduct research activities independently, so none of them can be a principal investigator. A trainee who runs their own project does it under a supervisor, and the supervisor is the project's principal investigator. Research acts that involve direct interaction with participants may still be delegated to the trainee, under the institution's policies. What a trainee may be delegated is on [Trainees](/roles/trainee).

## Know what you are responsible for as principal investigator

The principal investigator keeps ultimate responsibility for the conduct of the study at the site, however its tasks are shared out. [The principal investigator keeps oversight of every person or party given a task](/sops/cr-002#5-3-5), and remains responsible for supervising them.

Most tasks can be delegated on the <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> to a team member who is qualified and trained for them. The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) give some responsibilities to the principal investigator by name. A <Tooltip tip="A qualified member of the study team, designated and supervised by the qualified or principal investigator at a site, who performs critical investigator tasks as described on the Task Delegation Log or makes important trial-related decisions." headline="Sub-Investigator" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sub-investigator">sub-investigator</Tooltip> named as the principal investigator's backup [temporarily replaces the principal investigator if needed](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#name-a-backup-for-the-principal-investigator).

### Before you submit the study

| Responsibility | Who carries it out |
|---|---|
| Hold [Human Research Privileges, Researcher Status or a written institutional authorization](#hold-the-credential-and-the-training), and the level of institutional training the study requires. | The principal investigator, who must hold them personally. |
| [An assessment of whether the study can be done at the site](/kb/planning/feasibility), before agreeing to conduct it. | The principal investigator or a delegate should do it. |
| [A sub-investigator named as your backup](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#name-a-backup-for-the-principal-investigator). The backup must hold the same Privileges or Researcher Status, qualifications and institutional training as you, or better. | The principal investigator or a delegate should name the backup. A fellow, a resident or a medical student cannot be the backup. |
| [Delegate each task on the Task Delegation Log](/kb/team/building-your-team#delegate-every-task-on-the-task-delegation-log), and sign each team member's entry to confirm that the person is qualified and trained for the tasks delegated. | Only the principal investigator signs to confirm the delegation. Each team member signs and dates the log to accept their tasks. |
| Make sure each team member has [the level of institutional training the study requires](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#add-a-new-team-member), with their training documented, before tasks are delegated to them. | The principal investigator must ensure it. |
| [Submit the study in Nagano](/kb/submission/preparing#check-credentials-and-training). | The principal investigator, or a team member delegated the submission on the log. If a delegate submits it, the Principal Investigator's Commitment and Signature form must be completed. |
| To check eligibility in health records before someone consents (pre-screening), request [MUHC Authorization, which includes access to the records](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent), in the Nagano submission. | [Declare your own conflicts of interest to the Research Ethics Board (REB)](/kb/governance/conflict-of-interest#declare-a-conflict-to-the-reb-that-reviews-your-study), and identify the conflicts of every member of your team. | Every investigator and team member declares their own conflicts. Identifying the team's conflicts is the principal investigator's responsibility. |

### During the study

| Responsibility | Who carries it out |
|---|---|
| [Supervise every person and party given a study task](/kb/team/building-your-team#delegate-every-task-on-the-task-delegation-log). | The principal investigator keeps oversight of them and the responsibility for supervising them. |
| Supervise medical care and medical decisions in the study, and [document every study-related medical decision](/kb/team/building-your-team#fill-the-core-roles). | Medical care and medical decisions are under the principal investigator's supervision. Medical decisions can be delegated to a qualified and trained sub-investigator, once the delegation is documented on the log. |
| Make sure [each participant's consent is obtained](/kb/recruitment/consent#choose-who-obtains-consent) as the RI-MUHC's SOPs require, and that the consent process is documented. | The principal investigator must ensure it. Obtaining consent can be delegated to a team member who is qualified, trained on the consent SOPs and the protocol, and delegated the task on the log. |
| [Initial and date the documents that need medical oversight](/kb/site-activation/isf#document-medical-oversight-by-the-principal-investigator), such as test results, safety reports, monitoring letters and new versions of the Investigator's Brochure. | The principal investigator. |
| Make sure the site has [a written process](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#review-and-report-external-susars) describing how documents that need medical oversight, such as reports of suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs), are reviewed and how they reach the team members who need them. | The principal investigator must ensure it. |
| Make sure that [fellows' and residents' medical assessments are co-signed](/kb/team/building-your-team#fellows-and-residents-on-a-research-team). | The principal investigator, or a qualified sub-investigator with Privileges or Researcher Status, co-signs them. |
| Make sure that [the people delegated tasks keep the study's level of training](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#check-the-log-at-every-update) while the study runs. | The principal investigator must ensure it. |
| [Assess, address and document every deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations). Report every deviation to the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, and report to the REB the ones it requires. A major or critical deviation should have a <Tooltip tip="A series of actions taken to resolve a failure to comply with the study's requirements, good clinical practice or the applicable regulatory requirements, and to prevent it from happening again." headline="Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#capa">corrective and preventive action (CAPA)</Tooltip>, captured on the [CAPA form](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management#working-through-a-capa). | The tasks can be delegated. The responsibility rests with the principal investigator. The CAPA form has lines for the reporter, the principal investigator, the study nurse or coordinator and the clinical research manager to sign and date. |
| Tell the REB about [complaints about the research and other events it lists](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#report-participant-safety-and-ethical-concerns), and about [any unauthorized collection, use or disclosure of personal information](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person). | The tasks can be delegated. The responsibility rests with the principal investigator. |
| [Keep REB approval current](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#if-reb-approval-lapses). [Submit each amendment](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#know-which-changes-need-an-amendment), and do not implement it until you have [the REB's written approval](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#wait-for-every-approval-before-you-implement-the-amendment) and, where it applies, the sponsor's go letter or confirmation. A change needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants may be made before the REB's approval, and must be reported to the REB immediately. In a sponsor-investigator study, the sponsor-investigator should also be notified as soon as possible afterwards. | The principal investigator is responsible. Submitting in Nagano can be delegated on the log. |
| [When you are notified of an audit or an inspection](/kb/close-out/close-out#obligations-that-continue-after-close-out), inform the sponsor. For a regulatory inspection, also inform the REB and Quality Assurance. | The principal investigator must inform the sponsor, and should inform the REB and Quality Assurance. |
| Prepare the site for an audit or an inspection with the team, using [Hosting a Health Canada Inspection](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/hc-inspection-guide). | Parts of the preparation can be delegated. In a drug study, the ultimate responsibility stays with the sponsor or sponsor-investigator and the principal investigator. In a medical device study, responsibility is delegated with the study tasks. |
| If you leave the study or the MUHC, [hand the study to a new principal investigator](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#change-the-principal-investigator). | A new principal investigator should be named. The outgoing principal investigator delegates all the principal investigator's tasks to the incoming one, and both sign the revised contract. |

## Hold the credential and the training

Before acting as principal investigator at the MUHC, a person must hold [the credential required for their profession and study](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status), or a written institutional authorization. Researcher Status covers only research that does not require medical oversight.

Holding Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status [commits you](/kb/governance/research-integrity#meet-your-commitments-as-a-principal-investigator) to declaring any investigation or sanction you may be subject to in connection with your research, and the creation of a business or your incorporation as a researcher where it relates to research involving human participants, in accordance with the RI-MUHC's policies. Privileges and Researcher Status also commit you to obtaining institutional authorization when you take part in research not conducted under the auspices of Santé Québec-MUHC.

Obtain [the level of institutional training your study requires](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) before you submit the study to the REB in Nagano. Renew any credential or training that is [close to expiry](/training/compliance-requirements#validity-and-the-90-day-rule) before you submit.

## Follow the critical rules

The principal investigator is ultimately responsible for each of these rules on every study they lead:

- Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
- All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes before all the required signatures are on the form is a deviation.
- Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.
- Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.
- If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk.

Every Knowledge Base page applies to your studies as its "Applies to" strip shows, and a section with its own strip applies to the studies that strip names.
