### Study types at a glance

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Phase I drug studies">
    Phase I drug studies are the CIM's primary clinical mandate. These typically require intensive participant monitoring, frequent blood sampling (PK), IV drug administration, overnight stays in negative pressure pods, and close medical oversight during dosing visits. CIM's dedicated Phase I capacity at the Glen site is specifically designed for this. Capacity is limited and pre-booking is common.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Oncology trials">
    Trials involving IV chemotherapy, biotherapy, and immunotherapy as study drugs are a core part of the CIM's portfolio. The CIM has dedicated oncology nursing staff with competency-based certification for hazardous drug administration. Both the Glen and Montreal General sites support oncology studies.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Industry-sponsored trials requiring IV administration or infusion">
    Any trial where the protocol requires IV drug administration, IV infusions, or access to a dedicated infusion centre. This includes biologics, monoclonal antibodies, and other infused study drugs outside oncology.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sponsor-Investigator studies">
    CIM can provide both clinical execution and monitoring infrastructure for Sponsor-Investigator studies. CIM CRAs conduct site initiation, routine monitoring, close-out visits, and IP accountability monitoring for SI studies that lack a commercial sponsor.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Studies requiring functional testing">
    Studies requiring cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET), pulmonary function testing (PFT), ECG, Holter, body composition, imaging, or polysomnography may be a fit for the CIM Functional Testing Platform at the Glen site.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Studies requiring specimen processing or biobanking">
    Studies can use the CIM's Containment Level II Processing Laboratory at the Glen or Montreal General sites.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Paediatric studies">
    Paediatric studies are supported on the paediatric side of the Glen site (Block B). Contact the CIM to confirm availability for your patient population and study type.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Observational or device studies">
    If your study is observational but includes procedures the CIM specializes in — functional testing, specimen collection, imaging — a service-only arrangement may be possible. Medical device studies depend on the nature of the device and procedures involved. Contact the CIM to discuss.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

For a full catalogue of services, platforms, and team contacts, see [CIM Services](/cim/services).

## How to engage

CIM involvement must be identified and confirmed during the feasibility phase — before your Site Agreement is signed and before your Nagano submission. This ensures CIM service costs are in the budget from the start. CIM services are available to any researcher at The Institute — you do not need to be formally affiliated with CIM to use them.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Contact CIM early">
    Reach out to [clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) as soon as you know your study may need CIM facilities, staffing, or platforms. Phase I studies in particular require early discussion — CIM capacity is limited and pre-booking is common.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Feasibility review">
    CIM will review your protocol and confirm what can be supported and at which site (Glen or MGH). This feeds into your site feasibility assessment under [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Budget preparation">
    CIM budget specialists prepare a cost estimate for the services required. This is incorporated into the Clinical Trial Agreement and reviewed by Research Finance before execution.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Study activation">
    Once Nagano approval is confirmed and the Site Agreement is signed, CIM staff are formally onboarded. The Site Initiation Visit includes CIM staff for any protocol procedures they will perform.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ongoing conduct">
    CIM staff operate under the same RI-MUHC SOPs as the rest of your team. All study-related activities at CIM are subject to the same GCP, Health Canada, and institutional requirements as any other site activity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Budget and timing

CIM has finite capacity and a budget process that must happen early. Understanding the timing constraints before you commit to a protocol timeline can save significant delays later.

<Warning>
  The most common source of delay is late engagement. If you are reading this page while your study is already in REB review or your Site Agreement is being negotiated, contact CIM immediately. A budget amendment is always possible, but it costs time.
</Warning>

- **Phase I capacity is limited and pre-booking is common.** If your Phase I study has a tight activation timeline, contact CIM during protocol development — not after REB submission.
- **Oncology and infusion capacity varies by period.** Availability depends on concurrent study volume. CIM will confirm capacity during the feasibility review.
- **CIM costs must be in your initial budget.** Adding CIM after the Site Agreement is signed requires a budget amendment, which delays activation. The feasibility conversation and budget estimate must happen in parallel with protocol review — before REB submission and before sponsor confirmation.
- **The Site Initiation Visit gates participant recruitment.** CIM staff are formally onboarded at the SIV. Recruitment cannot begin until the SIV is complete and Nagano approval is confirmed.

## What to prepare for your first meeting

To make the most of your initial feasibility discussion with CIM, prepare the following before you reach out:

- A draft or near-final protocol — or a study synopsis describing the key procedures and visit schedule
- A clear description of which procedures the CIM would be responsible for performing
- Your anticipated study timeline, including target submission and activation dates
- An indication of whether the study is industry-sponsored, PI-initiated, or Sponsor-Investigator
- Any site-specific constraints — patient population, required equipment, or Phase I requirements
