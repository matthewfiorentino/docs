The Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM) is the RI-MUHC's clinical research facility, in the MUHC's hospitals at the Glen site and the Montreal General Hospital. Plan the CIM's part in your study before you submit the study. The CIM confirms whether it has the capacity for your study, and the cost of its services goes into the study budget.

Any researcher at The Institute can use CIM services. No formal affiliation with the CIM is required. The CIM can run an industry-sponsored study from start to finish, or provide individual services for a study initiated by its <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip>.

The CIM's facilities are prioritized for clinical research: trials the CIM leads, sponsor-led trials that the CIM does not run, and grant-funded trials initiated by a principal investigator. Examples are the research lab tests and infusions previously done in the Oncology Day Centre (ODC).

## Check whether your study needs the CIM

Not every study at the RI-MUHC needs the CIM. A study may need it for its infrastructure, its staff or its technical platforms. Find your study in this table:

| Your study | What the CIM offers | When to contact the CIM |
|---|---|---|
| A Phase I drug study, in oncology or not | Phase I studies are the CIM's primary clinical mandate. Phase I studies typically need intensive monitoring, frequent blood sampling for pharmacokinetics, intravenous drug administration, overnight stays and close medical oversight during dosing visits. The CIM's Phase I unit at the Glen site has 8 negative-pressure pods for prolonged monitoring and overnight observation. | During protocol development, before you submit the study to the Research Ethics Board (REB). Phase I capacity is limited, and pre-booking is common. |
| A study in the [CATALIS FAST TRACK](/kb/design/multi-centric#catalis-fast-track) service | Since June 2026, CATALIS FAST TRACK studies are conducted through the CIM. The CIM team can help with every step, from the pre-study feasibility review through submissions, protocol amendments and annual renewals to study close-out. Resources are already set aside at the CIM for the start-up regulatory work and the budget negotiation. | The CIM team is contacted at the same time as the sponsor approaches CATALIS. |
| A trial that gives intravenous chemotherapy, biotherapy or immunotherapy as study drugs | Trials of these drugs are a core part of the CIM's portfolio. Nurses who give chemotherapy and biotherapy as study drugs have completed a competency-based training program on giving them. Both the Glen and the Montreal General sites support oncology studies. | During feasibility. |
| A trial whose protocol requires intravenous drug administration or infusions, or a dedicated infusion centre, outside oncology | Infusion of study drugs such as biologics and monoclonal antibodies, at the CIM Infusion Centre. | During feasibility. |
| A study that needs functional testing | Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET), pulmonary function testing (PFT), electrocardiograms (ECG), Holter monitoring, body composition, imaging and sleep studies (polysomnography), on the CIM's technical platforms at the Glen site. | To book access to a platform. |
| A study that needs specimen processing or biobanking | The CIM's Containment Level 2 processing laboratory, at the Glen and the Montreal General sites. | To book access to the laboratory. |
| A sponsor-investigator study that needs monitoring | The CIM can provide both the clinical work and the monitoring for a sponsor-investigator study. The CIM's monitors conduct the site initiation, routine monitoring and close-out visits, and monitor the accountability of the investigational product, for sponsor-investigator studies with no commercial sponsor. | During feasibility. |
| A pediatric study | Space on the pediatric side of the Glen site (Block B). | To confirm availability for your patient population and type of study. |
| An observational study or a medical device study with specialized procedures | For an observational study that includes procedures the CIM specializes in, such as functional testing, specimen collection or imaging, a service-only arrangement may be possible. For a device study, what the CIM can offer depends on the device and the procedures. | To discuss whether the CIM can support the study. |

If none of these applies to your study, it likely does not need the CIM.

A sponsor-investigator who delegates monitoring to the CIM keeps the [ultimate responsibility for it](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#monitoring-independence). The sponsor-investigator is responsible for ensuring that the duties the CIM carries out for the study are [specified in writing](/sops/cr-002#5-2-1), and for overseeing them.

Each service, the site that offers it and its contact are on [CIM Services, Facilities and Platforms](/cim/services).

## Contact the CIM before you submit

Contact the [CIM](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) during feasibility, before you submit the study in Nagano and before the research agreement is signed. If your study will use CIM services, contact the CIM before you submit, to determine whether the CIM can provide them. The institutional feasibility review in Nagano still takes place after that.

Nagano's feasibility questions ask you to consider whether some services could be provided outside hospital resources, for example at the CIM. If you plan to use the CIM without contacting it, the institutional feasibility review can fail when the CIM does not have the capacity. A study the CIM runs has already been through the CIM's own feasibility review. If the CIM covers the study's nursing, [answer "No" to MUHC nursing services](/sops/cr-007#5-4-9) in Nagano.

The steps, from first contact to the conduct of the study:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Contact the CIM">
    Write to the [CIM](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) as soon as you know your study may need its facilities, staff or platforms. The general inbox sends your question to the right manager.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The CIM reviews the protocol">
    The CIM reviews your protocol, and confirms what it can support and at which site, the Glen or the Montreal General. The CIM's services and their costs are part of [the site's feasibility assessment](/kb/planning/feasibility#feasibility-checklist).
  </Step>
  <Step title="The budget is prepared">
    The CIM's budget specialists prepare a cost estimate for the CIM services the study needs. Once the principal investigator approves the budget, it is written into the study's agreement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The study is activated">
    Once the study's approvals are confirmed and its agreement is signed, the CIM's staff are formally onboarded. The site initiation visit includes the CIM's staff for any protocol procedures they will perform.
  </Step>
</Steps>

[If your study uses CIM services, a CIM agreement must be in place](/sops/cr-002#5-5-3). For services used since June 1, 2026, the principal investigator signs a CIM service agreement. The service agreement sets out the study's details and the CIM's roles and responsibilities.

The CIM's staff work under the same RI-MUHC standard operating procedures (SOPs) as the rest of your team. Every study activity at the CIM is subject to the same good clinical practice, Health Canada and institutional requirements as any other study activity.

## Plan the budget and the timing

The CIM's capacity is finite, and its costs belong in the budget from the start. If your study is already in REB review, or its agreement is already being negotiated, consider contacting the [CIM](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) now. Late engagement is the most common source of delay. A budget amendment is always possible, but it takes time.

Oncology and infusion capacity varies over time, with the number of studies under way. The CIM confirms its capacity during its feasibility review.

Consider putting the CIM's costs in the [first budget](/kb/funding/budget-negotiation#avoid-the-common-mistakes), before the agreement is signed. Adding the CIM after the agreement is signed requires a budget amendment, which delays activation. The feasibility conversation with the CIM and its budget estimate run in parallel with the protocol review, before the study is submitted to the REB and before the sponsor confirms the site.

The CIM's staff are formally onboarded at the site initiation visit. If the study holds a site initiation visit, recruitment cannot begin until that visit is complete.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

## Prepare for your first meeting with the CIM

Bring these to your first feasibility discussion with the [CIM](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca):

- A draft or near-final protocol, or a study synopsis that describes the key procedures and the visit schedule.
- A description of the procedures the CIM would perform.
- The study's expected timeline, including the target dates for submission and activation.
- Whether the study is industry-sponsored, initiated by its principal investigator, or a sponsor-investigator study.
- Any site-specific constraints, such as the patient population, the equipment the study needs or its Phase I requirements.
