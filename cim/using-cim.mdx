## When you need CIM

CIM involvement is appropriate when your study requires infrastructure, staffing, or procedures that go beyond what your department can support independently. Common triggers include:

- Overnight participant stays (Phase I, vaccine studies, nutrition studies)
- IV drug administration or infusion procedures
- Specialized functional testing — cardiopulmonary, imaging, muscle biopsy, sleep studies
- Dedicated research nursing or coordination support
- Project management for a multicentre trial
- Ethics submission support (REB startup, amendments, renewals)
- Budget negotiation for an industry-sponsored study

CIM services are available to any researcher at The Institute. You do not need to be formally affiliated with CIM to use its services.

<Note>
For a full description of all available services and technical platforms, see [CIM Services](/apps/cim/services).
</Note>

## How to engage

CIM involvement must be identified and confirmed during the feasibility phase — before your Site Agreement is signed and before your Nagano submission. This ensures CIM service costs are in the budget from the start.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Contact CIM early">
    Reach out to [clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) as soon as you know your study may need CIM facilities, staffing, or platforms. Phase I studies in particular require early discussion — CIM capacity is limited and pre-booking is common.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Feasibility review">
    CIM will review your protocol and confirm what can be supported and at which site (Glen or MGH). This feeds into your site feasibility assessment under SOP-CR-004.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Budget preparation">
    CIM budget specialists prepare a cost estimate for the services required. This is incorporated into the Clinical Trial Agreement and reviewed by Research Finance before execution.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Study activation">
    Once Nagano approval is confirmed and the Site Agreement is signed, CIM staff are formally onboarded. The Site Initiation Visit includes CIM staff for any protocol procedures they will perform.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ongoing conduct">
    CIM staff operate under the same RI-MUHC SOPs as the rest of your team. All study-related activities at CIM are subject to the same GCP, Health Canada, and institutional requirements as any other site activity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Budget and timing

The most common source of delay when working with CIM is late engagement. If CIM is identified after the Site Agreement is signed, adding their costs requires a budget amendment — which takes time and can delay activation.

<Warning>
**Budget amendment risk.** CIM service costs must be included in the initial budget submitted for Research Finance review. Do not wait until after REB approval or sponsor confirmation to contact CIM — the feasibility conversation should happen in parallel with protocol review.
</Warning>
