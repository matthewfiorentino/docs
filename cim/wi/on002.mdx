## 1 · Purpose and scope

This Work Instruction (WI) describes the instructions for the safe handling of hazardous drugs for clinical trials conducted within the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM) of the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre (RI-MUHC).

This WI applies to Qualified Trained Staff at the CIM of the RI-MUHC who administer intravenous chemotherapy and other hazardous drugs.

---

## 2 · Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Qualified Trained Staff (QTS)** | Hazardous drugs can only be administered and manipulated by QTS who have successfully completed the training on this WI. QTS is responsible to: know the limits and extent of their practice as it relates to the manipulation of hazardous products; follow safe handling practices when handling hazardous drugs; verify that a spill kit is available and not expired before manipulating any hazardous drugs. |
| **Oncology Pharmacy** | Responsible for informing QTS about hazardous drugs by identifying all hazardous products delivered to the CIM with a "Cytotoxic" hazard symbol label. |

## 4 · Instructions

<Warning>
Hazardous intravenous drugs must be prepared in the oncology pharmacy under a biological safety cabinet.
</Warning>

### 5.1 Transport

5.1.1 Hazardous drugs will be placed in a closed, leak-proof plastic bag by the pharmacy and will be transported in a rigid cytotoxic transport container to the treatment/infusion room of the CIM.

5.1.2 The transport container must be identified with the "Cytotoxic" hazard symbol and the bottom should be covered with an absorbent, plastic-backed cloth.

5.1.3 Hazardous IV push agents will be dispensed in a syringe with a closed connector system.

5.1.4 Hazardous drugs should **NEVER** be transported via mechanical means (e.g., pneumatic conveyors).

5.1.5 Never use rigid cytotoxic transport containers for other purposes.

### 5.2 Use of Treatment/Infusion Room

5.2.1 Access to the treatment/infusion room is restricted to:
- QTS
- Subjects
- One family member, only if necessary (they should be informed of the risks involved before entering the room)
- Pregnant visitors and children must not be allowed in the treatment/infusion room

5.2.2 Workers and family members must not eat, drink, chew gum, apply makeup, or store food in the treatment/infusion room. Subjects may eat and drink if treatments are long.

5.2.3 Subjects have access to a washroom reserved exclusively for their use. They should be instructed to flush twice after using the toilet and wash their hands. Men should be instructed to sit to urinate. If diapers are used, these must be discarded in a cytotoxic waste container.

5.2.4 All subject linens can be placed in regular linen hampers, even if soiled.

5.2.5 Workers, subjects, and family members must wash their hands with water and soap every time they leave the room.

5.2.6 Cytotoxic waste (empty chemotherapy bags/tubing and used PPE) must be disposed of in a cytotoxic waste container. A rigid cytotoxic waste container must be used for sharps.

5.2.7 A Spill Kit is available at the infusion room of the CIM. Refer to Appendix 1 (Spill Kit Content) and Appendix 2 (Chemo Spill Algorithm) for instructions on how to manage spills.

### 5.3 Administration

#### 5.3.1 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

All QTS manipulating and administering hazardous drugs must use PPE as described below. The QTS who administer hazardous drugs must wash their hands, put on the gown, then put on the gloves (ensuring gloves cover the gown cuff), after which they should put on face protection if necessary. When two pairs of gloves are required, put on the first pair before putting on the gown.

**Gloves**
- Gloves can be worn for a maximum of 30 minutes for the same subject.
- QTS must wear one (1) pair of long-cuffed chemotherapy-resistant gloves to handle and administer hazardous drugs, from the time they remove the drugs from the Ziploc® type plastic bag.
- QTS must wear two (2) pairs of long-cuffed chemotherapy-resistant gloves during procedures involving an increased exposure risk (e.g., topical application of a hazardous drug). The first pair of gloves must be worn under the gown cuff, while the second pair must be worn over the cuff.
- QTS must take the gloves off immediately after the procedure is finished and wash their hands with soap and water every time they remove their gloves.

**Gown**
- QTS who administer hazardous drugs must wear one (1) chemotherapy-resistant gown to handle and administer the drugs from the time they are removed from the Ziploc® type plastic bag.
- One gown may be worn for a period of no more than 3.5 hours, after which the gown is to be disposed of in a cytotoxic waste container.
- When keeping the gown for reuse, hang it outside traffic areas, with the contaminated side inward.
- Avoid touching the outside of the gown without protection. The outside of the gown must not come into contact with the clothes worn by workers or subjects.

**Face Protection**
- The face shield can be re-used by the same QTS for one shift.
- When there is a risk of splashing (e.g., direct IV push), QTS who administer the drugs must wear face protection. Working at a level lower than the face will limit the risk of facial exposure.

#### 5.3.2 Preventive Measures During Administration

<Warning>
Never prime the IV tubing with the hazardous drug — always use the retrograde technique with a compatible solution (see Figure 1).
</Warning>

*Figure 1 — Retrograde priming technique. Source: ASSTSAS, Prevention Guide, Safe Handling of Hazardous Drugs.*

5.3.3 Check the integrity of hazardous drug containers throughout the administration process.

5.3.4 Place a disposable, absorbent, plastic-backed pad for the containers on the work table or under the subject's arm when applicable, and particularly during drug administration by peripheral IV.

5.3.5 Wrap the tubing connection site with a 10 × 10 cm sterile compress when administering hazardous drugs by direct IV push and when removing the infusion tubing, to contain any potential leaks.

5.3.6 For IV infusion, once drug administration is complete, rinse the primary tubing with the primary solution, leave the secondary tubing attached to the primary tubing, and discard all in the cytotoxic waste container.

5.3.7 For oral administration: wear one (1) pair of gloves (a gown is not necessary) when handling and administering solid form hazardous drugs. For liquid formulations, both a gown and gloves must be worn.

5.3.8 Tablets of hazardous drugs should not be crushed or cut outside the pharmacy. The pharmacy should provide these drugs in an oral syringe, in ready-to-administer, liquid oral form.

5.3.9 Wear two (2) pairs of gloves and a gown when handling and administering topical hazardous drugs (creams, ointments, etc.).
