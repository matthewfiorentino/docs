# Safe Handling of Hazardous Drugs

<Badge color="gray">WI-CIM-ON002_01</Badge> <Badge color="gray">Version 01</Badge> <Badge color="green">Effective 10 May 2022</Badge> <Badge color="blue">CIM Oncology Staff</Badge>

<Note>
This work instruction describes the safe handling practices for hazardous drugs used in clinical trials at the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM), RI-MUHC. It applies to all Qualified Trained Staff who administer intravenous chemotherapy and other hazardous drugs, and is referenced throughout WI-CIM-ON001.
</Note>

## Key points

- Hazardous IV drugs must be prepared in the oncology pharmacy under a biological safety cabinet; they must never be prepared at the bedside.
- Hazardous drugs must be transported in a closed, leak-proof plastic bag inside a labelled rigid cytotoxic transport container with an absorbent, plastic-backed cloth at the bottom — never via pneumatic conveyors.
- Access to the treatment/infusion room is restricted; pregnant visitors and children must not be allowed in; workers must not eat, drink, or apply makeup in the room.
- Full PPE is required when handling hazardous drugs: one pair of long-cuffed chemotherapy-resistant gloves (two pairs for high-exposure procedures), a chemotherapy-resistant gown (maximum 3.5 hours use), and face protection when splashing is possible (e.g., direct IV push).
- Never prime IV tubing with hazardous drug — always use the retrograde technique with a compatible solution.
- Cytotoxic waste (empty bags, tubing, used PPE) must be discarded in a cytotoxic waste container; sharps require a rigid cytotoxic waste container.
- A Spill Kit must be verified as present and unexpired before manipulating any hazardous drug. Refer to Appendix 1 (Spill Kit Content) and Appendix 2 (Chemo Spill Algorithm) for spill management.
- Tablets of hazardous drugs must not be crushed or cut outside the pharmacy; oral liquid hazardous drugs require both gown and gloves.
