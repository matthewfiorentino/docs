## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}

This work instruction describes the procedures for preparing for and managing a Code Blue (cardiac or respiratory) emergency at the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM), including pre-visit safety preparation, emergency response, documentation, and post-event debrief. It applies to all personnel on CIM premises.

---

## 2 · Responsibilities {#responsibilities}

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Safety Team Lead** | Oversees the maintenance of UBECs in accordance with applicable work instructions. |
| **CRC-N** | Must be trained to recognize and manage Code Blue situations according to the scope of their qualifications or field of practice and have completed CPR training. |
| **QI** | Must ensure awareness of the investigational product and its side effects; develop a treatment plan for adverse drug reactions if necessary; ensure availability and readiness of qualified staff and emergency equipment before each visit. |
| **QTS (Qualified Trained Staff)** | Must be trained to recognize Code Blue situations within their field of practice and have completed CPR training. Completes Appendix 1 Safety Checklist prior to visits with IP. May be assigned as Pointer or Runner during emergencies. |
| **Study personnel** | Ensure Appendix 1 Safety Checklist is completed prior to each visit with IP administration; document all aspects of a medical emergency; notify applicable personnel. |

## 4 · Instructions {#instructions}

### 4.1 Managing a medical emergency at the CIM {#managing-a-medical-emergency-at-the-cim}

**Step 1 — Evaluate the situation**

- Ensure the person is not left alone.
- Evaluate the person for precursors potentially leading to a Code Blue Event: shortness of breath, decreased blood pressure, tachycardia, diaphoresis/clammy skin, sudden pallor.
- A Code Blue Event is identified by (not limited to): absence of or difficulty breathing (e.g., gasping), loss of consciousness, irregular pulse, chest pain, absence of pulse for more than 10 seconds.

**Step 2 — Call for assistance**

- Before launching a Code Blue, if unsure of the person's state, call for immediate assistance within the CIM. State: "Attention! Attention! Assistance required in room XYZ" (name the room, not just the number). Repeat twice, then hang up.
- If you hear this message and can make yourself available, you must offer assistance.
- Bring the UBEC and Automated External Defibrillator (AED) close to the patient if applicable. Refer to Appendix 2 CIM Floor Plan with UBEC and AED Locations.
- If the individual continues to be unwell and interventions are not improving their state, transfer them to the emergency department.

**Step 3 — Code Blue Activated**

- If a Code Blue is identified, activate the Code Blue button (if in a patient room) AND call specifying: Code Blue (adult or pediatric); MUHC site (Glen); precise location (pavilion/block, floor, room number); department: RI-MUHC CIM; caller name; callback number.
- Someone must stay with the individual at all times.
- Once a Code Blue Event is activated, only the Code Blue Team can deactivate it — do not press the button again or press "Annuler."
- If the individual is conscious, keep them conscious and safe (head away from a door; do not change posture before a medical professional assesses them).
- If unconscious or pulseless and you are trained, initiate CPR until the Code Blue Team arrives, using appropriate precautions.
- Ensure the area is free of clutter before the Code Blue Team arrives.
- Appoint a **Pointer** to meet and direct the Code Blue Team inside the CIM.
- Appoint a **Runner** to obtain additional supplies as required.

### 4.2 Preparing for medical emergencies during a clinical study {#preparing-for-medical-emergencies-during-a-clinical-study}

- The QI must review the protocol, investigator brochure, and pharmacy manual for all possible IP side effects; develop a treatment plan for adverse drug reactions if necessary; ensure availability and readiness of qualified staff and emergency equipment before each visit.
- Study personnel must plan and prepare for known complications of the patient population (e.g., hypoglycemia in a diabetic patient).
- The QI or delegated personnel must ensure patient IV access during a visit if the study presents an elevated risk of allergic reaction.
- QTS must complete **Appendix 1 — Safety Checklist Prior to Study Visit** before any visit involving IP administration.
- During visits, QTS must assess the patient regularly and report to the QI as appropriate.
- For patients remaining overnight or after regular hours, at least two QTS must always be available.

### 4.3 Documenting medical emergencies occurring on a study {#documenting-medical-emergencies-occurring-on-a-study}

- Document all aspects of the emergency in **Appendix 3 — Code Blue Emergency Event**. If the initial witness is not a member of assigned study personnel, ask that person to assist with documentation and have them co-sign.
- Add pertinent details to study source documents (time of incident, interventions, personnel advised).
- Document and report an AE or SAE as applicable.
- Notify the Sponsor as soon as possible and determine next steps in collaboration with the QI; document all communications and decisions within study records.
- Report to the patient's treating physician and/or in OACIS if required.
- Notify the Safety Team Lead if the UBEC was used so contents can be verified and replenished.

### 4.4 Post-emergency event debrief {#post-emergency-event-debrief}

- Conduct a post-event debrief with all staff involved (study personnel, QI, treating physician) to review the event trigger, interventions taken, the process followed, lessons learned, and next steps.
- Document the debrief in a report as appropriate (e.g., CAPA).
