# Management of Study Briefings and Delegation of Nurses on Studies for General Nursing Tasks

<Badge color="gray">WI-CIM-GN005_01</Badge> <Badge color="gray">Version 01</Badge> <Badge color="green">Effective 20 May 2022</Badge> <Badge color="blue">CIM Research Staff</Badge>

<Note>
This WI describes the process for creating and maintaining Study Briefings (nurse feasibility documents), tracking their currency against REB-approved source documents, and delegating Registered Nurses and Auxiliary Nurses (including external nurses) to assist on clinical studies for general nursing tasks (e.g., blood draw, vitals, ECG) — for nurses who are not on the main study delegation log.
</Note>

## Key points

- A Study Briefing (also called Nurse Feasibility or Nursing Alert) is a version-controlled document summarizing key information from REB-approved study documents used to train nurses on general nursing tasks; it is **required** when external nursing resources are used.
- Study briefings must be QC reviewed by someone other than the preparer before approval by the main/lead CRC/CRC-N, and must include a revision history.
- Currency of each study briefing must be tracked using Appendix 2 Study Briefing Tracking Log, which records the briefing version, source document and version reviewed, and whether changes were required — even if the answer is "N" (no changes required).
- Assigned nurses must review the study briefing (or, if unavailable, review the study protocol sections on schema, schedule of events, drug description, and adverse events) and sign Appendix 3 General Nursing Task Delegation Log.
- Before continuing with any study interventions, the main CRC/CRC-N must confirm that the Investigator has reviewed any flagged laboratory values.
- External nurses must additionally sign Appendix 4 (External Nurse Study Briefing/Protocol Specific Tasks & GCP Training Record) or Appendix 5 (REB Nursing Feasibility Review — Cancer Care Mission Sample Prescription), plus provide an updated CV (every 2 years) and license (annually).
- CIM staff cannot transfer full responsibility for nursing tasks to external resources; if overnight interventions are required, a CIM nurse must also be present.
- All necessary materials (work instructions, study package, nursing briefing, recording templates, nursing supplies) must be readily available to the replacing nurse — e.g., at the front of the patient binder.
