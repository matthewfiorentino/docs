## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}

**Purpose:** This Work Instruction (WI) describes the instructions for preparing medications other than study drugs, from a vial or a glass ampule, to be administered in clinical trials within the Centre of Innovative Medicine (CIM) of the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre (RI-MUHC).

**Scope:** This WI applies to nurses at the CIM of the RI-MUHC, who administer intravenous medications via a peripheral intravenous (PI) or a Central Venous Access Device (CVAD).

---

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Nurse | Follow aseptic non-touch techniques (ANTT) while manipulating medication for intravenous use. Know the limits and extent of their practice as related to the particular protocol indications. |

## 4 · Instructions {#instructions}

### 4.1 General Principles {#general-principles}

**4.1.1** Perform hand hygiene and follow ANTT throughout the procedure.

**4.1.2** When possible, prepare IV medication in a low circulation area (such as medication room) to reduce risk of contamination.

**4.1.3** To minimize the risk of introducing dust particles into the syringe when filling with air, ensure that there is no open construction-renovation project within the working vicinity or aerosolization of dust activities.

**4.1.4** Medication prepared outside of the laminar airflow hood of the pharmacy clean room is to be administered as detailed in the protocol and/or pharmacy manual. If not detailed in the protocol and/or pharmacy manual, administer within one hour of preparation and document the time in the source documents (e.g., nursing notes).

### 4.2 Identification of the Preparation {#identification-of-the-preparation}

**4.2.1** Label syringe with protocol number, subject number, subject initials, medication name and dosage, date and time prepared, and initials and date of the QTS who prepared medication.

### 4.3 Filter Use Principles {#filter-use-principles}

**4.3.1** The filter needle must be used when preparing medication from a glass ampule or from a vial (if required by manufacturer or pharmacy procedures, or protocol and/or pharmacy manual).

**4.3.2** A 5-micron filter needle has inner space of approximately 0.07 mL; if the vial or ampule does not contain overfill, a second vial or ampule may be necessary to obtain the complete dosage.

**4.3.3** The same filter needle cannot be used to prepare and to inject.

### 4.6 Withdrawing Medication From a Vial — General Principles {#withdrawing-medication-from-a-vial-general-principles}

| Principle | Rationale |
|---|---|
| After removing cover from medication vial, always disinfect the rubber septum using vigorous friction with a new alcohol swab; allow drying completely. If more than one vial requires disinfection, use one (1) alcohol swab per vial. | The vial top is covered with a "dust cover" and is not sterile. Disinfection is required to prevent bacteria or particles from entering the solution. |
| Always identify the syringe containing the medication: label with protocol number, subject number, subject initials, medication name and dosage, date and time prepared, and initials/date of the nurse who prepared medication. | — |
| Vial must be punctured at an angle of 45° if accessed with a needle to avoid coring. | — |
| Avoid touching the inside of the plunger when injecting air and while withdrawing the medication. | — |
| If using a needle, recap needle safely: (1) Place cap on a solid surface; (2) Slide needle into cap using one-hand technique; (3) Secure cap; (4) Discard needle in sharp container after use. | To avoid accidental needle stick injury. |
| After use, discard the vial if empty or if the leftover medication is preservative free. A multi-dose medication vial (e.g., Insulin) can be kept. In this case, the first puncture date and time, the discard date and the nurse initials must be clearly written on the vial. Store medication according to requirements (e.g., refrigerator, room temperature in medication room/cart). | — |

### 4.7 Withdrawing Medication From a Vial That Requires Reconstitution {#withdrawing-medication-from-a-vial-that-requires-reconstitution}

| Technique | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **1.** Attach a regular 21G needle (or larger for viscous medication) onto syringe. | — |
| **2.** Remove cover from diluent container. Scrub diluent rubber septum with an alcohol swab and allow drying completely. | To prevent contamination of the needle and the solution. |
| **Withdrawal of diluent:** | — |
| **3.** Fill syringe with volume of air equal to the volume of diluent to be withdrawn. | To ease withdrawal of the medication and equalize pressure in the vial once the syringe is filled. |
| **4.** Using a needle, puncture diluent rubber septum at a 45° angle, within the central circle of the septum, orienting bevel of needle upward. Rapidly straighten syringe to a 90° angle. | To minimize the risk of introducing rubber pieces into the vial, the tip of the needle and the shorter part of the bevel must enter the rubber stopper at the same place. This will prevent coring of the rubber stopper. |
| **5.** Inject air and withdraw appropriate volume of diluent. | — |
| **Reconstitution of medication:** | — |
| **6.** Use same technique to pierce the rubber septum of the medication. Following manufacturer or pharmacy recommendation, mix medication until completely dissolved and withdraw medication. If administering with a cannula, withdraw a larger volume and replace needle with a syringe cannula. Ensure complete expulsion of air and accuracy of dose prior to administration. | — |

### 4.8 Withdrawing Medication From a Vial That Does Not Require Reconstitution {#withdrawing-medication-from-a-vial-that-does-not-require-reconstitution}

**1.** Attach a regular 21G needle or access cannula (or larger for viscous medication) onto syringe.

**2.** At all times, avoid touching any part of the needle, the needle hub and shaft, or disinfected rubber septum.

**3.** Fill syringe with volume of air equal to the volume of medication to be withdrawn.

**Using a needle:** Puncture medication rubber septum at a 45° angle, within the central circle of the septum, orienting bevel of needle upward. Rapidly, straighten syringe to a 90° angle.

**Using an access cannula:** Insert at 90°. For multi-dose vial: insert an interlink system prior to using the cannula.

**4.** Inject air and withdraw appropriate volume of medication. *(To ease withdrawal of the medication and equalize the pressure into the vial once the syringe is filled.)*

### 4.9 Withdrawing Medication From a Vial That Requires Use of a Filter Needle and Reconstitution {#withdrawing-medication-from-a-vial-that-requires-use-of-a-filter-needle-and-reconstitution}

**1.** Attach a regular 21G needle (or larger for viscous medication) onto syringe.

**2.** Remove cover from diluent container. Scrub diluent rubber septum with an alcohol swab and allow drying completely. At all times, avoid touching any part of the needle, the needle hub and shaft, or disinfected rubber septum. *(To prevent contamination of the needle and the solution.)*

**Withdrawal of diluent:**

**3.** Fill syringe with volume of air equal to the volume of diluent to be withdrawn. Using a needle, puncture diluent rubber septum at a 45° angle, within the central circle of the septum, orienting bevel of needle upward. Rapidly, straighten syringe to a 90° angle. *(To ease withdrawal of the medication and equalize pressure in the vial once syringe is filled.)*

**4.** Inject air and withdraw appropriate volume of diluent.

**5.** Reconstitution of medication: Use same technique to pierce the rubber septum of the medication. Following manufacturer or pharmacy recommendation, mix medication until completely dissolved.

**6.** Withdrawing of medication: Remove needle and attach filter needle onto syringe and withdraw content. The filter needle inner space is approximately 0.07 mL. If vial does not contain overfill, a second vial may be necessary to obtain complete dosage. *(To minimize the risk of introducing rubber pieces into the vial, the tip of the needle and the shorter part of the bevel must enter the rubber stopper at the same place. This will prevent coring of the rubber stopper.)*

### 4.10 Withdrawing Medication From a Vial That Requires Use of a Filter Needle and Does Not Require Reconstitution {#withdrawing-medication-from-a-vial-that-requires-use-of-a-filter-needle-and-does-not-require-reconstitution}

**1.** Attach a filter needle onto syringe. At all times, avoid touching any part of the needle, the needle hub and shaft.

**2.** Fill syringe with volume of air equal to the volume of medication to be withdrawn.

**3.** Puncture medication rubber septum at a 45° angle, within the central circle of the septum, orienting bevel of needle upward. Rapidly, straighten syringe to a 90° angle. *(To ease withdrawal of the medication and equalize the pressure into the vial once the medication has been removed.)*

**4.** Inject air and withdraw appropriate volume of medication. Using a cannula: a larger volume must be withdrawn.

**5.** Remove capped filter needle and replace with:
- A new needle to complete preparation, or
- A plastic cannula if injecting into a split septum connector.

> **WARNING:** The same filter needle cannot be used to aspirate and then inject because this would lead to re-injecting particulates that were filtered.

During the aspiration, hold syringe upright and tap to ensure complete expulsion of air. *(In order to ensure accurate volume following expulsion of air; to prevent injection of air.)*

### 4.11 Withdrawing Medication From a Glass Ampule {#withdrawing-medication-from-a-glass-ampule}

| Technique | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **1.** Remove medication trapped in the neck of the ampule by moving it in a circular motion to create centrifugal force. | — |
| **2.** Disinfect ampule neck with a new alcohol swab and allow drying completely. | Ampules have been found to contain bacteria after breakage. |
| **3.** With sterile gauze, hold the glass ampule neck and break the ampule away from the body. Discard neck in sharp container. | To prevent small particles of glass entering the face or eye of the health care provider. |
| At all times, avoid touching any part of the needle, the needle hub and shaft. | — |
| **4.** Attach filter needle onto syringe and withdraw content, ensuring that tip or shaft of needle does not touch the rim of the ampule. The filter needle inner space is approximately 0.07 mL. If ampule does not contain overfill, a second ampule may be necessary to obtain complete dosage. | To prevent contamination of the needle and the solution. Ampules have been found to contain glass particles after breakage. The filter used to aspirate the solution does not filter bacteria, it filters particles of glass. |
| **5.** Remove capped filter needle and replace with: a new needle to complete preparation, or a plastic cannula if injecting into a split septum connector. | — |
| > **WARNING:** The same filter needle cannot be used to aspirate and then inject because this would lead to re-injecting particulates that were filtered during the aspiration. | — |
| **6.** Hold syringe upright and tap to ensure complete expulsion of air. | To prevent injection of air. |
| **7.** Discard sharps (ampule and filter needle) in sharp container even if ampule content is not used completely. It cannot be used later. | — |
