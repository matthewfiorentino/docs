# Preparing Immediate-Use Intravenous Medications Other Than Study Drugs From a Vial or a Glass Ampule

<Badge color="gray">WI-CIM-GN002_01</Badge> <Badge color="gray">Version 01</Badge> <Badge color="green">Effective 10 May 2022</Badge> <Badge color="blue">CIM Nursing Staff</Badge>

<Note>
This WI describes the step-by-step procedures for preparing immediate-use IV medications other than study drugs from a vial or a glass ampule, covering aseptic non-touch technique (ANTT), filter needle use, reconstitution, and safe disposal — for nurses at the CIM who administer medications via peripheral IV or central venous access device.

## Key points

- Perform hand hygiene and follow ANTT (aseptic non-touch technique) throughout the entire procedure; prepare medications in a low-circulation area (e.g., medication room) where possible.
- Medication prepared outside the pharmacy laminar airflow hood must be administered within one hour of preparation; document the time in source documents.
- Label every syringe with: protocol number, subject number, subject initials, medication name and dose, date and time prepared, and the QTS's initials/date.
- A filter needle must be used when drawing from a glass ampule; a filter needle is also required from a vial when specified by the manufacturer, pharmacy, or protocol.
- The same filter needle cannot be used to aspirate and then inject — replace with a new needle or plastic cannula before administration.
- A 5-micron filter needle has an inner space of approximately 0.07 mL; if the vial/ampule contains no overfill, a second container may be needed to complete the dose.
- When puncturing a rubber septum with a needle, always approach at 45° within the central circle of the septum (bevel upward), then straighten to 90° — this prevents coring of the rubber stopper.
- After breaking a glass ampule, always attach a filter needle before withdrawing content — glass particles are present even if not visible; discard the ampule and filter needle in a sharps container after use.
