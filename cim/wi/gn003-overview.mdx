# Accessing TIVAD — Insertion and Removal of a Non-Coring Needle on Adult Subjects

<Badge color="gray">WI-CIM-GN003_01</Badge> <Badge color="gray">Version 01</Badge> <Badge color="green">Effective 10 May 2022</Badge> <Badge color="blue">CIM Nursing Staff</Badge>

<Note>
This WI describes the step-by-step instructions for inserting and removing a non-coring needle from a Totally Implantable Vascular Access Device (TIVAD, e.g., Port-a-Cath, BardPort, PowerPort) and for maintaining port patency in adult research subjects at the CIM.

## Key points

- Before accessing the port, verify subject identity per WI-CIM-CR001, inspect the skin overlying the TIVAD for redness, swelling, erosion or edema, and palpate to confirm port position.
- Always select the smallest gauge and shortest needle length available; a 20G ¾ inch needle is adequate for the majority of subjects.
- Skin disinfection uses a chlorhexidine 2% / isopropyl alcohol 70% swab stick applied with back-and-forth horizontal friction for 15 seconds, then vertical friction for 15 seconds; allow at least 3 minutes to dry completely.
- Use the "C-grip" with the non-dominant hand (middle finger over catheter-chamber junction, index and thumb stabilizing the chamber) to locate the septum centre before puncturing perpendicularly with a single continuous motion.
- After insertion, confirm blood return by pulling back on the syringe, then flush the full 10 mL of NaCl 0.9% using turbulence or stop-and-go technique before clamping.
- To lock the port for patency maintenance, flush with 10 mL NaCl 0.9% (turbulence technique), then lock with a Heparin pre-filled syringe using positive pressure technique (clamp while injecting the last 0.5 mL).
- Needle removal requires a Heparin prescription on file; stabilize the platform with the non-dominant hand, lift the safety arm with the dominant hand index until a click is heard, then discard in sharps container.
- Document date and time of insertion, patency verification, Heparin lot number/expiry, and initials/date in source documents.
- Technique photos for each step are available on the full WI page.
