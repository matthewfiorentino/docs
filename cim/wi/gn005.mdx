## Key points

- A Study Briefing (also called Nurse Feasibility or Nursing Alert) is a version-controlled document summarizing key information from REB-approved study documents used to train nurses on general nursing tasks; it is **required** when external nursing resources are used.
- Study briefings must be QC reviewed by someone other than the preparer before approval by the main/lead CRC/CRC-N, and must include a revision history.
- Currency of each study briefing must be tracked using Appendix 2 Study Briefing Tracking Log, which records the briefing version, source document and version reviewed, and whether changes were required — even if the answer is "N" (no changes required).
- Assigned nurses must review the study briefing (or, if unavailable, review the study protocol sections on schema, schedule of events, drug description, and adverse events) and sign Appendix 3 General Nursing Task Delegation Log.
- Before continuing with any study interventions, the main CRC/CRC-N must confirm that the Investigator has reviewed any flagged laboratory values.
- External nurses must additionally sign Appendix 4 (External Nurse Study Briefing/Protocol Specific Tasks & GCP Training Record) or Appendix 5 (REB Nursing Feasibility Review — Cancer Care Mission Sample Prescription), plus provide an updated CV (every 2 years) and license (annually).
- CIM staff cannot transfer full responsibility for nursing tasks to external resources; if overnight interventions are required, a CIM nurse must also be present.
- All necessary materials (work instructions, study package, nursing briefing, recording templates, nursing supplies) must be readily available to the replacing nurse — e.g., at the front of the patient binder.

## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}

**Purpose:** This Work Instruction (WI) describes the process for managing Study Briefings and the delegation of Nurses to assist on studies for general nursing tasks who are not on the main study Delegation Log.

**Scope:** This WI applies to Registered Nurses and Auxiliary Nurses at the Center for Innovative Medicine (CIM) of the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre (RI-MUHC) or external nursing resources on contract. Specifically, to Nurses that will be assisting on a study to perform general nursing tasks, but are not part of the main study team and do not sign the main study delegation log.

This WI excludes protocol-specific clinical trial tasks (e.g., drug administration) where the Nurse must be delegated on the main study delegation log and must follow the process for study personnel delegation and training.

## 4 · Instructions {#instructions}

### 4.1 Management of Study Briefings {#management-of-study-briefings}

**4.1.1** Using Appendix 1 Study Briefing Template, the main CRC or CRC-N will prepare the study briefing, which will include information on the medication/investigational product and instructions for nursing care in accordance with the effective REB-approved protocol and other study documents (e.g., manuals). The study briefing must be version controlled, including a revision history providing a summary of changes, and must be QC reviewed by an individual other than the person preparing the document prior to approval by the main/lead CRC/CRC-N.

**4.1.2** Study briefings are required when using external nursing resources (i.e., external to the CIM).

**4.1.3** Study briefings must remain current based on the effective REB-approved version of study documents (e.g., Protocol or Protocol Amendment, Laboratory Manual, Investigator's Brochure). This will be tracked using Appendix 2 Study Briefing Tracking Log, which will document the study briefing version number, source document and version reviewed, and documenting whether changes are required. Even if the source document revision did not affect the study briefing, this must be documented as "N" (no) in the Requires Changes column to document that the source document was reviewed to ensure it did not affect the study briefing.

**4.1.4** Ensure all pertinent materials are readily available prior to delegating any nursing, for example a trend chart for laboratory values or vitals of interest, requisitions, sample tubes and labels, etc.

### 4.2 Required Training and Documenting Assignment of Delegation on a Study {#required-training-and-documenting-assignment-of-delegation-on-a-study}

**4.2.1** In addition to basic training and qualifications (e.g., RI-MUHC SOPs, GCP, role-specific training, professional license, etc.), assigned nurses performing general nursing tasks on a study (e.g., blood draw, taking vitals, ECG) must review the study briefing, if available. This will provide the nurses with key information such as awareness of safety information on the investigational product (i.e., potential AEs expected). Training will be documented using study-specific training logs. For external nurses refer to section 4.2.5 Requirements for External Nursing Resources.

**4.2.2** If a study briefing is not available, assigned nurses must refer to the study protocol and review the following sections listed below as a minimum (study briefings are required when using nursing resources external to the CIM). Note that part of a nurse's tasks defined by their profession and as holders of a valid license is to medicate patients, provide standard of care and not to perform any tasks they are uncertain of, as they remain responsible for their own knowledge, experience and training before providing care. As such, if they encounter a potential adverse event, a clinical assessment of the research patient would be performed and the event would be documented on the study adverse event log to be assessed by the Investigator.

Minimum protocol sections to review (if no study briefing is available):
- Schema or similar study flow chart overview, if available
- Schedule of Events/Activities/Assessments or similar table summarizing the required interventions for each visit
- Study medication/drug description
- Adverse events section and other pertinent sections summarizing safety aspects related to potential reactions and how to manage them

**4.2.3** The main CRC/CRC-N is responsible for ensuring they review the laboratory analysis report and have the Investigator review any flagged values prior to continuing with any interventions. This should be confirmed by the delegated nurse prior to continuing with their delegated tasks.

**4.2.4** Assigned nurses must sign Appendix 3 General Nursing Task Delegation Log. To account for unexpected absences, any qualified research nurse (CRC-N) may sign Appendix 3 during the course of the study. The main CRC-N assigned to the study will be documented on the main study delegation log signed by the Investigator, with the understanding that any of the qualified nurses appearing on the general nursing task delegation log (Appendix 3) may be further delegated by the CRC-N.

### 4.2.5 Requirements for External Nursing Resources {#requirements-for-external-nursing-resources}

**4.2.5.1** When using external nursing resources, they are required to document training on the study briefing, be informed of who to contact in case of emergency, be briefed on adverse events of special interest, and complete other appropriate training for the tasks being delegated (e.g., Code Blue training).

**4.2.5.2** External nurses must sign Appendix 4 External Nurse Study Briefing/Protocol Specific Tasks & GCP Training Record or Appendix 5 REB Nursing Feasibility Review — Cancer Care Mission Sample Prescription, as applicable. Appendix 4 or 5 must be updated accordingly to include any study-specific details.

**4.2.5.3** The necessary materials, such as work instructions, should be provided in a study package, including Nursing Briefing, templates necessary to record all interventions to document the tasks being covered, and applicable nursing supplies. The required documents should be readily available to the replacing nurse (e.g., front of patient binder).

**4.2.5.4** Additional trainings may be required depending upon the tasks being delegated. For example, nurses must be trained on study-specific source documentation and this must be documented on a study-specific training log.

**4.2.5.5** The main CRC-N must review any documentation completed by the external nurse (e.g., completed correctly, signed and dated, etc.) prior to their departure, if possible.

**4.2.5.6** The team manager must request the necessary training record documentation so they can be filed in the ISF (e.g., license, CV). The main CRC-N must ensure they are saved in the applicable study folder of the ISF, including their contact information should it be necessary to follow-up with them.

**4.2.5.7** The main CRC/CRC-N cannot give full responsibility of nursing tasks on a study to external resources (i.e., unsupervised). For example, if interventions are being performed overnight, a CIM nurse must also be present.
