# Administration of Intravenous Chemotherapy and Biotherapy as Study Drugs in Clinical Trials

<Badge color="gray">WI-CIM-ON001_01</Badge> <Badge color="gray">Version 01</Badge> <Badge color="green">Effective 10 May 2022</Badge> <Badge color="blue">CIM Oncology Staff</Badge>

<Note>
This work instruction describes the full procedure for administering intravenous chemotherapy and biotherapy as study drugs in clinical trials at the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM), RI-MUHC. It applies to all Qualified Trained Staff (QTS) who administer these agents, and must be read in conjunction with WI-CIM-ON002 Safe Handling of Hazardous Drugs.
</Note>

## Key points

- Only Qualified Trained Staff (QTS) who have completed the nursing competency training program may administer hazardous study drugs.
- An Independent Double Check (IDC) of the dose calculation is mandatory for every antineoplastic agent; two QTS must verify each drug against the prescription and protocol before administration.
- Laboratory values (ANC, platelets, renal/liver function, electrolytes, haemoglobin) must be reviewed and within parameters — confirmed by the Qualified Investigator (QI) — before treatment begins.
- Hypersensitivity reaction (HSR) rescue medications must be prescribed and physically available at the infusion room before any study drug is given.
- IV access patency must be confirmed by blood return and saline flush before every antineoplastic agent; peripheral vesicants require site checks every 5–10 minutes throughout the infusion.
- Vesicant infusions of more than 60 minutes must use a central venous access device (CVAD); never use a volumetric pump for vesicants via peripheral IV (exceptions: Paclitaxel, Docetaxel, Nab-Paclitaxel, Oxaliplatin).
- All study drug administrations — drug, dose, route, start/stop times, rate changes, vital signs, and adverse events — must be documented in source documents.
- Full PPE (chemotherapy gown, long-cuffed chemotherapy-resistant gloves, face protection) is required when manipulating any hazardous drug; refer to WI-CIM-ON002 for PPE requirements.
