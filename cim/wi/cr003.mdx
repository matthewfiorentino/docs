## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}

**Purpose:** This Work Instruction (WI) describes the instructions for preparing and conducting monitoring visits and sponsor audits at the Center for Innovative Medicine (CIM) of the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre (RI-MUHC).

**Scope:** This WI applies to all CIM personnel hosting or assisting with monitoring visits or sponsor audits. This WI **excludes** management of regulatory agency inspections. Should you receive a request for an audit from a regulatory agency (e.g., Health Canada), forward the email to the RI-MUHC QA Manager and CIM Director before responding.

---

## 2 · Responsibilities {#responsibilities}

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| CIM Audit Lead | Qualified individual responsible for managing and hosting the monitoring visit or sponsor audit, including: coordinating with the auditor; ensuring all study personnel and required subject matter experts (SMEs) are aware of the agenda and schedule; ensuring requested records have been reviewed, scanned, verified, and uploaded to the CIM research shared folder (H drive); ensuring confidential information is redacted and study blinding is not breached; ensuring required confidentiality and security agreements have been signed prior to granting access; and ensuring MUHC platform access is removed at the end of the audit. |
| Study Personnel | Every member of the research team assisting with the audit is responsible for ensuring data is adequately protected and for being familiar with the MUHC Guidelines referenced in section 6.0. |

## 4 · Instructions {#instructions}

<Note>
Following the practices within this WI to access data remotely will not require prior approval by the evaluating REB. However, any measures that deviate from the instructions within this WI are considered as increasing the risks to study participants and will require prior REB approval before being implemented.
</Note>

### 4.1 Scheduling the Audit {#scheduling-the-audit}

**4.1.1** Upon receiving a request for an audit, acknowledge the email by requesting the following information, as applicable, and confirm the availability of appropriate team members before accepting any proposed dates:

- **4.1.1.1** The proposed dates and duration of the audit (maximum of three (3) days for sponsor audits; exceptions may be granted on a case-by-case basis).
- **4.1.1.2** The first and last name of the auditor(s).
- **4.1.1.3** The scope of the audit (e.g., routine or for cause), including Investigator name and study number/title.
- **4.1.1.4** List of subject numbers that will be reviewed.
- **4.1.1.5** An agenda identifying the proposed start and end times, along with proposed times for personnel interviews and documentation reviews, including a list of documents required for the record review component and, if applicable, a list of non-study related records (e.g., software validation, equipment calibration, laboratory certificates, organizational charts, SOPs).
- **4.1.1.6** Confirm with whom the auditors would like to meet or interview and whether they want to tour the facilities (e.g., pharmacy).

**4.1.2** For sponsor audits, inform CIM management (i.e., study team manager and CIM Director) by forwarding the request and including them in subsequent communications. Confirm whether there was a prior audit from the same sponsor and review the report to ensure there are no outstanding issues. Provide CIM management an update on the previous report, if applicable.

**4.1.3** Any communications with the sponsor, monitor, or auditor must assure the protection of research subjects' privacy and confidentiality.

**4.1.4** Once all applicable information is received, notify the personnel involved to coordinate the date(s) of the audit. Include non-study personnel as appropriate (e.g., Biomedical team for equipment questions).

**4.1.5** Ensure the audit is scheduled sufficiently in advance to allow the study team to prepare — for example to set up required accounts and access rights, perform a QC review of records within the scope, and to scan records if the audit is remote. IT requests to set up access to applicable platforms must be sent at least **5 business days** prior to the scheduled audit (see section 4.2).

**4.1.6** Once a date has been agreed upon internally, contact the designated sponsor/CRO audit representative to inform them of the proposed dates.

**4.1.7** For remote audits, discuss the available sharing platforms (see section 4.2) and confirm whether the auditor has any specific requirements. Request the following information from the auditor(s) in order to request access to required MUHC remote sharing platforms:
- Confirmation letter from the lead auditor including the charts/research subject records (e.g., screening or randomization numbers) and the date access will be required (to be submitted to Medical Records when requesting OACIS access).
- For all auditors requiring access to OACIS or sharing platforms: first and last name, date of birth (unique identifier for IT, kept confidential), company name and **institutional** email address (never a third-party email such as gmail), and mobile phone number (for two-factor authentication).
- Signed Appendix 7 Security Pledge — Confidentiality and Integrity of MUHC Information and Information Systems.

**4.1.8** Ensure a signed confidentiality agreement is on file by contacting the contracts team. If not, request that each auditor requiring access sign Appendix 8 OACIS Confidentiality and Security Agreement and return a copy so it can be sent to Medical Records for OACIS access.

**4.1.9** If the auditor has not done so, create meeting invite(s) based on the proposed agenda. Include dial-in information for the communication platform used (e.g., Zoom, WebEx, Microsoft Teams).

**4.1.10** You may provide the instructions in Appendix 1 (Accessing CITRIX/IRIAS3 and OACIS) and Appendix 2 (Accessing CIM Study Shared Folder) to the auditors prior to the scheduled audit.

**4.1.11** Study personnel must ensure the signed and dated REB-approved informed consent form permits access to the research subject's records (if not, research subjects may need to be re-consented). The ICF should include wording to state that research or health records and other source records identifying them may be audited or inspected in the presence of the Investigator or delegated study personnel for the purpose of monitoring or auditing the research. No information or records that disclose identity must be shared or released without consent unless required by law. Ensure study blinding requirements are met throughout the audit.

**4.1.12** One week prior to the scheduled audit, confirm the scheduled date(s) and ensure there are no changes to the planned agenda.

**4.1.13** For remote audits, schedule a connectivity test with the lead auditor a few days prior to the audit. Ensure the signed Appendix 7 Security Pledge is available — access cannot be granted unless this agreement is signed.

### 4.2 Approved Technology Platforms and Access Requirements {#approved-technology-platforms-and-access-requirements}

**4.2.1** Book a conference room and ensure the required equipment is available (computer, camera for remote audits).

**4.2.2** Only secure and approved tools must be used. Approved technology platforms at the RI-MUHC include:

- **4.2.2.1 WebEx:** To provide access to OACIS and the certified copies of study records (i.e., `\\wnetvmri54\cim_study` folder). The CRC or audit lead must assist with setting up a WebEx account for each participating auditor. Refer to Appendix 1. Auditors must be informed that records are not to be downloaded, saved, or printed. Any copies must be requested so they can be logged and appropriately redacted if necessary. Log all record requests using **Appendix 3 Copies of Study Records Provided Log**.

- **4.2.2.2 File share:** `\\wnetvmri54\cim_study` — Refer to Appendix 2. A study-specific folder must be created using the naming convention: `SponsorName_studynumber_YYYY-MM-DD` (e.g., `CIM_123456_2022-10-15`).

- **4.2.2.3 Video conferencing tools** (Microsoft Teams, Zoom, or WebEx): Permits screen sharing for remote audits only — uploading records using these platforms is prohibited. Records are shared by screen share only. When sharing screens or presenting on camera:
  - Conduct calls/videoconference in a private location.
  - Do not allow non-authorized personnel to overhear conversations.
  - Do not grant control of your desktop.
  - Restrict attendees to intended personnel only (e.g., using a password or specific link).
  - Secure the work environment before turning on webcam or sharing screen (no personally identifiable information visible in background; blank webcam background recommended in Zoom).
  - Do not include personal information in meeting titles or descriptions (e.g., no participant initials or names).
  - Sessions are not to be recorded.
  - Follow proper desktop hygiene — remove or file documents, close unnecessary programs, disable notification popups, set Teams status to "Do Not Disturb."

**4.2.3** Any sponsor tools proposed must comply with regulatory requirements and guidelines, including the study ICF (jurisdiction of information storage, level of identifiable records leaving the RI-MUHC). Contact QA management for additional guidance on external sharing platforms.

### 4.3 Steps for Requesting Access to Technology Platforms {#steps-for-requesting-access-to-technology-platforms}

**4.3.1** For auditors who have previously been granted access, certain steps below will not be required, unless there is a need to re-activate their account (e.g., expired password, disabled due to inactivity, or mandatory password change).

**4.3.2** Ensure all steps below are performed at least **five business days** prior to the audit.

**4.3.3 CITRIX and MUHC network (AD) account:**
- **4.3.3.1** Submit an IT C2 Request selecting "Access request (network, Email, Citrix...)" requesting: (1) creation of a network AD account, and (2) access to CITRIX/IRIAS3, providing for each auditor: first and last name, date of birth (unique identifier, kept confidential), company name and email address, and mobile phone number (for two-factor authentication).

**4.3.4 CIM Study folder access:**
- **4.3.4.1** Submit an IT Rescue request to create the audit-specific folder in `\\wnetvmri54\cim_study` and grant appropriate access (CIM staff: read/write; external auditor: read-only).

**4.3.5 OACIS Electronic Medical Records access:**
- **4.3.5.1** Submit to Medical Records at least one week in advance: signed Appendix 8 OACIS Confidentiality and Security Agreement; confirmation letter from the auditor (including subject MRNs and dates of access); copy of the initial MUHC authorization or annual renewal from Nagano; and Appendix 5 Investigator Approval for OACIS Access.
- **4.3.5.2** A login/password for OACIS will be emailed to the CIM requestor and must be forwarded to the auditor before the first day of the remote audit.
- **4.3.5.3** OACIS access must be limited to **read-only** access to selected records within the scope of the audit. Access will only be granted for research subjects who have consented to participate in the clinical trial and authorized disclosure of protected health information for audit purposes.

**4.3.6** Forward pertinent login information to the auditors once received from IT. Each user will be required to create a unique password at initial login.

**4.3.7** If it is the first time using a technology platform with the auditor, schedule a meeting to test the platform as soon as possible to allow time to seek alternative arrangements if necessary.

### 4.4 Preparing Records for Uploading to CIM Study Shared Folder {#preparing-records-for-uploading-to-cim-study-shared-folder}

**4.4.1 QC Review of study records:**

- **4.4.1.1** Study personnel must ensure compliance with established SOPs, FDA requirements, sponsor's protocol, and local regulatory authorities — ensuring that data generated during the study reflects protocol requirements (source data vs. protocol or applicable amendments).
- **4.4.1.2** Verify the source data versus entries in the electronic case report form (eCRF) for accuracy.
- **4.4.1.3** The checklist in **Appendix 6 Study Record Review Checklist** may be used to facilitate the review and may be customized to suit the specific study. The completed checklist should be maintained within the essential documents binder.
- **4.4.1.4** Verifications to consider include but are not limited to:
  - ALCOA principle respected (e.g., clear who performed each task)
  - Delegation of authority by PI documented appropriately, including applicable training record requirements
  - Verification of protocol adherence
  - Study Investigational Product accountability and dispensation documentation
  - Communications between sponsor and PI
  - REB documentation, communications, and approvals
  - Signed, REB-approved consent forms for each subject with correct version
  - Accurate and complete reports of adverse events followed to resolution
  - CRF verification with source documentation and other records
  - Monitoring reports addressed
  - Record retention consistent with GCPs and sponsor requirements

**4.4.2** Guidelines for scanning records:
- **4.4.2.1** Ensure all pages have been scanned.
- **4.4.2.2** Ensure the file is readable (PDF can be opened and is not corrupt).
- **4.4.2.3** Ensure the scan is clear and the contents are legible (margins and any headers and footers intact).
- **4.4.2.4** Ensure any characteristics necessary to reconstruct the activities are present (e.g., if the original paper document has color, the scan must preserve this metadata).
- **4.4.2.5** When saving the scan, name the file by adding the date the file is saved as the suffix (e.g., `12345-DelegationLog_DDMMMYYYY`). The date must correspond to the date when the verification log (Appendix 4) is signed.
- **4.4.2.6** Document the verification on **Appendix 4 Certified Copy Verification Log** to attest the scanned copy is a true copy of the original source.

**4.4.3** Once scanning, reviewing, and filing is complete, the ISF from the H drive used for the study must be copied directly into the shared folder used for the audit. Any changes or modifications should be made in the original source ISF on the H drive and then copied over to the shared drive.

**4.4.4** If any paper documents will be shown by holding up for viewing via camera, ensure all required records are available prior to the audit in the appropriate tabs of the study binders. Ensure they have been redacted if necessary.

**4.4.5** Ensure a log is maintained to track all copies of records provided to the auditor (Appendix 3).

### 4.5 Conducting the Remote Audit {#conducting-the-remote-audit}

**4.5.1** Initiate the WebEx meeting with screen sharing off — only audio and video should be on prior to verifying credentials. Take virtual meeting attendance.

**4.5.2** Review the agenda and adjust as necessary. The CIM audit lead should not begin sharing the screen for review of records unless both parties are clear on the processes and goals for that day.

**4.5.3** Remind the auditor that screenshots during the review of the EMR or any documents presented or viewed is strictly forbidden according to the signed confidentiality agreement, including keeping copies or printing records from the CIM Study folder. Copies must be requested and, if appropriate, will be redacted and logged using Appendix 3.

**4.5.4** Share the screen only as necessary and avoid sharing the entire desktop. If sharing the desktop, stop sharing when navigating to different locations to avoid inadvertent breaches. Stop screen sharing at any point in the meeting where active showing of records is not required.

**4.5.5** Ensure only documents requested are provided. If additional materials need to be reviewed that were not part of the originally agreed list, stop screen sharing, locate the records prior to sharing the screen again, and update any agendas or logs accordingly.

**4.5.6** If the remote audit occurs over several days, confirm the agenda for the next day to ensure the required records are ready. It is recommended to have a daily debrief so any issues noted can be shared and possibly addressed prior to the conclusion of the visit.

**4.5.7** It is recommended to take notes of discussions, potential issues raised, or findings and communicate this to the team manager and CIM Director as a daily summary for sponsor audits.

**4.5.8** In the event of an inadvertent breach of data confidentiality and patient privacy or unauthorized access to records shared remotely, report the incident to the Investigator, Sponsor, and REB. Document the event in accordance with RI-MUHC SOP007 Research Ethics Board Submissions and Ongoing Communication and REB-SOP 405A.001 Ongoing REB Review Activities.

### 4.6 Conducting Onsite Audits {#conducting-onsite-audits}

**4.6.1** Refer to **Appendix 10 Audit Preparation Checklist** for steps to follow for onsite audits, including setting up the meeting room, required materials, process for providing certified copies of records, and managing daily meetings.

### 4.7 Concluding the Audit {#concluding-the-audit}

**4.7.1** Schedule a time for the closeout meeting and invite appropriate personnel (e.g., Investigator, QA, team manager/supervisor).

**4.7.2** Request when and how the report with observations will be communicated. It is recommended to request that the monitor provide a follow-up letter with a table listing the observations, recommended corrective actions, and a column for the CRC to enter their responses — to facilitate tracking findings and follow-ups for subsequent visits.

**4.7.3** Any observations should be agreed upon by all parties before the conclusion of the closeout meeting.

**4.7.4** Retain documents created or completed for the audit and file them with the study regulatory file.

**4.7.5** Submit an IT Rescue request to remove user access to the CIM Study share folder and request IT to update the folder name with the suffix `_ARCHIVED` (e.g., `CIM_2022-10-15_ARCHIVED`). Once archived, no further changes are permitted — the folder and its contents must reflect what was used at the time of the audit.

**4.7.6** If the auditor is not expected to return, request IT to disable the external user's MUHC network username via a C2 Request or by emailing `ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca`.

**4.7.7** Remove OACIS access at end of audit (unless there are planned future visits, e.g., monitoring visits).

### 4.8 Follow-ups for Monitoring Visit Reports {#follow-ups-for-monitoring-visit-reports}

**4.8.1** Once the report has been received, the CRC must ensure the Investigator acknowledges receipt of the follow-up letter by signing/dating and maintaining it in the ISF.

**4.8.2** The CRC should address the report as soon as possible to ensure observations are addressed prior to the next monitoring visit, unless the monitor requests updates prior to their visit. The timeline for addressing the reports should be agreed upon between the CRC and monitor.

### 4.9 Follow-ups for Sponsor Audits {#follow-ups-for-sponsor-audits}

**4.9.1** Once the report has been received, the CIM audit lead must coordinate the responses by sending the appropriate stakeholders their assigned finding and requesting them to provide a response.

**4.9.2** Responses must include the following information (supported with evidence such as documentation as attachments):
- **4.9.2.1** The observation/finding
- **4.9.2.2** The root cause
- **4.9.2.3** Immediate corrective action taken, including date of correction
- **4.9.2.4** Preventive actions that will be implemented to prevent recurrence, including target implementation date

**4.9.3** All sponsor audit reports, including responses and associated evidence, should be saved on the H drive in a central location and appropriately filed (by sponsor and date of audit). File audit documents in appropriate confidential files, separate from the investigator site files.

**4.9.4** It is recommended that management maintain a tracking log of sponsor audits (including audit dates, observations, classification — major/minor/critical — observation type/category, responses, personnel responsible for corrective and preventive action, and target dates for implementation). The CIM audit lead or designee should follow-up with assigned personnel prior to due dates. If an extension is required, the revised due date must be agreed upon, documented, and added to the tracking log.
