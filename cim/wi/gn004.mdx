## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}

**Purpose:** The purpose of this Work Instruction (WI) is to standardize the practice of administration of intravenous infusions for clinical trials within the Centre of Innovative Medicine (CIM) of the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre (RI-MUHC), and to reduce the risk of infection and medication precipitation by administering incompatible solutions.

**Scope:** This WI applies to Qualified Trained Staff at the CIM of the RI-MUHC who administer intravenous medications as study drugs in clinical trials.

**Note:** The sponsor is responsible for providing instructions for specific pump infusion and filters, and providing study-specific instructions for IV infusions.

## 4 · Instructions {#instructions}

### 4.1 Principles of Aseptic Non-Touch Technique (ANTT) when Manipulating Intravenous Equipment {#principles-of-aseptic-non-touch-technique-antt-when-manipulating-intravenous-equipment}

**4.1.1** Use aseptic techniques and follow routine precaution.

**4.1.2** Prepare work surfaces by clearing of equipment and supplies; clean work surface with disinfectant wipe and allow drying.

**4.1.3** ANTT is used to protect critical parts (catheter material, hub, insertion site) from beginning to end of procedure.

**4.1.4** ANTT aims to prevent micro-organisms on hands, surfaces or equipment from being introduced into or onto a susceptible site. The overriding and basic principle is that the critical site should not come into contact with any item that is not sterile.

**4.1.5** Disinfect bag injection port (or vial septum) before accessing (if necessary to access the bag).

**4.1.6** Once open, critical sites should be exposed to air for the least amount of time possible.

### 4.2 Identification of the Preparation {#identification-of-the-preparation}

**4.2.1** Label syringe/bag with protocol number, subject number, subject initials, medication name and dosage, date and time prepared, and initials and date of the QTS who prepared medication.

### 4.3 Required Equipment/Supplies {#required-equipment-supplies}

| Infusion of secondary medication in a MINI-BAG | Infusion of a secondary medication in a SYRINGE |
|---|---|
| 1 primary infusion set with at least 1 port above the pump segment | 1 primary infusion set (if dilution is required) with at least one port close to patient (below pump) |
| 1 bag of compatible solution (0.9% sodium chloride, D5%, etc.) | 1 microbore tubing 60" |
| 1 secondary infusion tubing (or set) | Chlorhexidine-alcohol swabs |
| Medication bag | Syringe |
| Red-cap to maintain sterility of tubing | — |
| Chlorhexidine-alcohol swabs | — |

### 4.4 Preparation of an IV Tube for Infusion {#preparation-of-an-iv-tube-for-infusion}

#### 4.4.1 Primary IV Line

**Step 1.** Select appropriate IV solution, as per protocol and/or pharmacy manual. Inspect bag for damage, inspect solution for particles/deposits and verify expiration date. Document the lot and expiry in the source documents (e.g., nursing notes).

**Step 2.** To remove the overwrap, tear the package at the notch away from your working surface (condensation may be found on the bag).

**Step 3.** Select your primary infusion set and document the lot number/expiry in the source documents (e.g., progress notes). Open package and remove tubing from package. Do not discard packaging (use packaging to collect priming solution).

**Step 4.** Ensure tubing is not kinked. Close roller clamp.

**Step 5.** Open vent for a bottle. Keep vent **CLOSED** for a bag.

**Step 6.** Remove infusion set port cover. Remove cover from IV tubing spike.

**Step 7.** Hold IV bag or IV bottle with port facing upward and spike at waist level. Twist while pushing the spike through the diaphragm of the port.

**Step 8.** Fill drip chamber 2/3 full by squeezing the chamber.

**Step 9.** Do not write directly on the bag with a pen/marker. Use a label to identify the date and, if applicable, additive solution.

**Step 10.** Proceed with priming.

**Priming through a volumetric pump:**
- a) Insert IV tubing using the specific pump instructions.
- b) Use the "prime tubing function" if the infusion is to be done through a pump that has this capacity.
- c) Remove cap at tip of IV tubing. Place tip so fluid is emptied into IV tubing package. Do not empty over garbage or sink.
- d) To remove air bubbles, tap Y ports upside down while tubing is being primed.
- e) Cover the end of the tubing with a sterile end-cap once priming is complete.

**Priming by gravity:**
- a) Remove the anti-siphon valve at the end of the tubing (if in place) and make sure to keep end of the tubing sterile by not touching the distal end of the tubing while priming.
- b) If there is no anti-siphon valve at the end of the tubing, remove the cap protecting the end of the tubing in place during priming.
- c) Open roller-clamp partially and prime with gravity over empty package.
- d) Ensure to remove all air bubbles at the Y ports by holding the ports upside down and tapping the tubing while the tubing is priming.
- e) If solution is not to be connected immediately, cover the end of the tubing with a sterile end-cap once priming is complete.

#### 4.4.2 Secondary Solution MINI-BAG

**Step 1.** Identify subject using CIM work instruction WI-CIM-CR001 Double Identification of Research Subjects and Independent Double Check of Medication Prior to Administration.

**Step 2.** Check that the secondary infusion is compatible with the primary solution and change primary infusion or set-up if necessary (e.g., Lexicomp).

**Step 3.** Ensure that the primary infusion set has a Y port in the segment **ABOVE THE PUMP**. Use this Y port.

**Step 4.** Open mini-bag with compatible solution; verify and document expiry date and lot number; inspect solution for particles/deposits.

**Step 5.** Open medication to be added to the mini-bag; verify expiry date; validate with prescription and subject identification the five rights.

**Step 6.** Follow WI-CIM-GN002 Preparing Immediate-Use Intravenous Medications Other Than Study Drugs From a Vial or a Glass Ampule to prepare the secondary medication to be administered.

**Step 7.** With a chlorhexidine-alcohol swab, clean the injection port of the mini-bag.

**Step 8.** Add the medication to the mini-bag.

**Step 9.** Use a label to identify the date and the medication added, as well as the name of the person who prepares.

**Step 10.** Remove secondary tubing from package.

**Step 11.** Close roller clamp. If your secondary set has a vent, ensure that the vent is **OPEN**.

**Step 12.** Remove infusion set port cover. Remove cover from IV tubing spike.

**Step 13.** Hold IV bag port facing upward and spike at waist level. Twist while pushing the spike through the diaphragm of the port.

**Step 14.** Scrub the Y port on the primary tubing with a 2% Chlorhexidine and 70% Isopropyl Alcohol swab. Let dry.

**Step 15.** Secure secondary tubing onto Y port above the pump by screwing it in place using ANTT principles.

**Step 16.** Back prime secondary tubing by lowering the secondary bag below the primary bag. Once tubing is primed, squeeze drip chamber so that at the end of the priming, it is 2/3 full.

**Step 17.** Hang secondary medication bag/bottle higher than the primary bag — at least 8 inches. Use hook provided with the secondary tubing, placing it on the IV primary solution.

**Step 18.** Verify subject's identity by following CIM work instruction WI-CIM-CR001.

**Step 19.** Proceed with administration as per protocol and pharmacy manual, as applicable (refer to Appendix 1 Possible Medication Set-up With Infusion Devices):
- **Pump Infusion:** Program pump for piggyback or secondary medication infusion.
- **Gravity Infusion:** Use the primary tubing roller clamp to adjust the drip rate of secondary infusion.

> **Important:** Always verify that the clamp of the secondary line is open, in order to ensure that the medication is infusing. Otherwise only the primary solution will infuse.

**Step 20.** Once infusion is completed, back prime secondary tubing as per protocol and pharmacy manual instructions. If not listed, fill secondary bag with approximately 25 mL of your primary fluid. Administer this diluted solution.

**Step 21.** Once primed, close roller clamp. Invert and squeeze medication drip chamber to move excess fluid into the bag/bottle. The line is now ready to infuse or to connect a new bag/bottle.

> **Note:** If a secondary medication is to be administered at a rate greater than 300 mL/hour, it is recommended to clamp (using a slide clamp) the primary tubing above the Y port where the secondary line is connected.

**Using an add-on or inline filter:**
- a) Determine adequate filter size according to clinical information provided by protocol (pharmacy manual).
- b) If priming 0.2 micron filter tubing, do not invert filter during priming unless specified differently on package instructions.
- c) If priming 1.2-micron filter tubing, do not invert filters during priming unless specified differently in protocol instructions.
- d) If administering solution as a primary solution, add the filter at the end of the primary solution or use primary tubing with a built-in filter.
- e) If administering solution as a secondary solution, add the filter at the end of the secondary tubing and connect this set up at the Y port above the pump segment.

#### 4.4.3 Administration by Syringe

**Step 1.** Verify prescription and choose compatible diluent.

**Step 2.** Prepare the medication following the protocol instruction and/or WI-CIM-GN002 Preparing Immediate-Use Intravenous Medications Other Than Study Drugs From a Vial or a Glass Ampule.

**Step 3.** Use the compatible diluent to complete the final volume needed (usually 10 mL, 20 mL or 50 mL).

**Step 4.** Identify the medication with protocol number, subject number, subject initials, medication name and dosage, date and time prepared, and initials/date of the QTS who prepared the medication.

**Step 5.** Confirm Subject ID following CIM work instruction WI-CIM-CR001 Double Identification of Research Subjects and Independent Double Check of Medication Prior to Administration.

**Step 6.** Proceed with administration as per protocol or standard of care, documenting the start time in the source documents (e.g., nursing notes). Refer to Appendix 1 Possible Medication Set-up With Infusion Devices.

**Administration by pump:**
- a) Follow steps 1 to 5.
- b) Connect syringe to the syringe IV administration set and prime it by gently pushing the plunger.
- c) Follow pump instructions to install the syringe and to program the dose and the time according to the protocol and/or prescription.
- d) At the end of the infusion, rinse using the primary line with a compatible solution.

**Administration by IV Push via Primary Line:**
- a) Follow steps 1 to 5.
- b) Scrub the Y port closest to the subject with a 2% Chlorhexidine and 70% Isopropyl Alcohol swab. Let dry.
- c) Connect syringe to the Y port.
- d) Clamp the IV line on the pump side.
- e) Verify blood return by gently aspirating. If good blood return, start administration, documenting the start time in the source documents. If no blood return, install a new IV catheter.
- f) Inject medication according to the protocol and/or prescription.
- g) Rinse using the primary line or with a 10 mL syringe with compatible solution.

**Administration by Direct IV Push:**
- a) Follow steps 1 to 5.
- b) Scrub the needleless connector with a 2% Chlorhexidine and 70% Isopropyl Alcohol swab. Let dry.
- c) Connect syringe with medication to the Y port.
- d) Open clamp.
- e) Verify blood return by gently aspirating. If good blood return, start administration, documenting the start time in the source documents. If no blood return, install a new IV catheter.
- f) Inject medication according to the protocol and/or prescription.
- g) Rinse with a 10 mL syringe with compatible solution. Rinse at the same rate as the drug administration.
- h) Close the clamp while administering the last mL of rinsing solution.
- i) Dispose all needles and syringes in the appropriate container.

### 4.5 Frequency of Replacement of Infusion Sets {#frequency-of-replacement-of-infusion-sets}

**4.5.1** Infusion set (tubing and connectors) must be completely replaced when:
- Sterility has been compromised (e.g., end of the tubing touched a non-sterile surface such as hand of healthcare provider, linens)
- Dropped on the floor
- Tubing is found hanging without a sterile cap covering the end
- IV access is replaced, or
- According to the frequency of Replacement of Infusion Sets (Appendix 2)

**Appendix 2 — Frequency of Replacement of Infusion Sets**

| Type of Infusion Set or Therapy (Continuous or Intermittent) | Frequency of Replacement |
|---|---|
| Primary infusion set | Every 96 hours |
| Secondary infusion set | Every 96 hours |
| Microbore extension for syringe driver | Every 96 hours |
| Set used for Lipids | At every bag change, minimally every 24 hours |
| Add-on devices with or without filters (e.g., 3-way stopcocks, extension tubing) | Every 96 hours or more frequently if special solution is infused through the device (i.e., Lipids, blood products, Propofol) |
| Injection cap — lumen in use | Every 96 hours or more frequently if visible debris or blood |
| Injection cap — lumen not in use | Every 7 days, at the time of weekly flush |
| Infusion sets used for contrast injection in radiology (2 spikes — Contrast media and Saline) | Every 4 hours — except catheter extension to change with each patient |
| IV bag (no additive, premixed by company or prepared by MUHC pharmacy) | Every 96 hours unless otherwise indicated. Refer to expiration date written by pharmacy on medication label. |
| IV bag (with medication added by MUHC health care provider — outside of laminar flow hood in pharmacy) | Every 24 hours |
