## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}

The purpose of this Work Instruction (WI) is to describe the instructions for the administration of intravenous chemotherapy and biotherapy as study drugs in clinical trials within the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM) of the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre (RI-MUHC).

This WI applies to Qualified Trained Staff at the CIM of the RI-MUHC who administer intravenous chemotherapy and/or biotherapy as study drugs in clinical trials.

---

## 2 · Responsibilities {#responsibilities}

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Qualified Investigator (QI)** | Prescribing study drugs according to protocol requirements, including all pre-medications and hypersensitivity reaction medications. |
| **Qualified Trained Staff (QTS)** | Know the limits and extent of their practice as it relates to this WI. Follow WI-CIM-ON002 Safe Handling of Hazardous Drugs while handling hazardous drugs. |
| **Oncology Pharmacy** | Prepare and dispense all medications related to study drugs, including pre-medications (anti-emetics, hypersensitivity prevention medications, hydration). Communicate any change or delay on study medications to the infusion nurse. |

## 3 · Terminology and definitions {#terminology-and-definitions}

| Term / Acronym | Definition |
|---|---|
| AEs | Adverse Events |
| Biotherapy | Drugs which boost or restore the ability of the immune system to fight cancer |
| BSA | Body Surface Area |
| Chemotherapy | Hazardous drugs that inhibit or prevent the growth and/or spread of malignant cells |
| CRC-N | Clinical Research Coordinator-Nurse |
| CVAD | Central Venous Access Device |
| ECOG | Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group |
| Hazardous Drugs | Drugs capable of causing toxicity to those who come in contact with them, classified as hazardous when possessing any of: Carcinogenicity, Genotoxicity, Teratogenicity, Reproductive toxicity, or Organ toxicity at low doses |
| HSRs | Hypersensitivity Reactions |
| Independent Double Check (IDC) | Occurs when two clinicians perform medication verification steps separately and independently (alone and apart from each other to reduce the risk of bias) and then compare results |
| Irritant | An agent which may produce irritation or inflammation along the vein when injected; in the event of infiltration, may produce a transient tissue reaction |
| IV | Intravenous |
| MRN | Medical Record Number |
| PIV | Peripheral Intravenous |
| PPE | Personal Protective Equipment |
| PRN | As Needed |
| Nurses | Infusion nurses, CRC-Ns, and Research Nurses who have successfully completed a competency-based educational program about the administration of chemotherapy and biotherapy and who regularly administer it |
| SAEs | Serious Adverse Events |
| Vesicant | An agent which, in the event of extravasation, has the potential to cause tissue damage which may lead to blistering, tissue necrosis, and damage to underlying structures |

## 4 · Instructions {#instructions}

<Warning>
Before manipulating and administering any hazardous drug, the Qualified Trained Staff must complete the nursing competency training program.
</Warning>

### 5.1 Gathering Required Supplies for Infusion {#gathering-required-supplies-for-infusion}

This can be done days before the administration of chemotherapy/biotherapy. A verification of all infusion room supplies must be done at a minimum of once per week.

The following supplies are essential to start chemotherapy infusion (not limited to):

- Personal protective equipment (PPE): chemotherapy gown, long cuffed chemotherapy resistant gloves, face protection (face shield)
- 10 cm × 10 cm (4 inches × 4 inches) compress (for direct IV push)
- Cytotoxic waste container
- Spill kit
- Extravasation kit if administering vesicant drugs
- Antiseptic swabs
- Infusion pumps
- Tubing and filter if required as per study protocol or standard of care

### 5.2 Subject Chart Review {#subject-chart-review}

The verification of the subject's chart may be done the day before the treatment. The QTS must verify:

#### 5.2.1 Prescription

a) Subject information: first and last name, date of birth, subject number, and study number.

b) Verify the cycle, day and scheduling of the study medication.

c) Verify the **5 rights** of all medications prescribed: the right person, the right drug, the right dose (see dose IDC calculation in section 5.2.1 d)), the right route, and the right time — against protocol and/or study briefing.

<Warning>
Never administer study treatment drug before receiving drug information training from the nursing alert/study briefing.
</Warning>

d) Perform an Independent Double Check (IDC) of the dose calculation(s):

1. Height (cm) and weight (kg) must be measured at the start of a new treatment and entered in OACIS. The system will calculate the BSA automatically.
2. Weight (kg) must be measured at the start of each new cycle of treatment. An IDC of the weight must be done at the first cycle or if changed by more than 10% against the previous visit.
3. Most chemotherapy doses are calculated using body surface area (BSA) mg/m²; many biotherapies use weight-based dosing mg/kg.
4. IDC of the dose calculation: Multiply BSA (mg/m²) × dose. Divide calculated dose by prescribed dose.
5. A 5% variance (+ or −) on the dose calculation is acceptable; physicians may choose to round off a dose. Note: by exception, the dose of Carboplatin is calculated by the physician and pharmacist only using area under the curve (AUC).
6. Any discrepancies in the dose calculation must be brought to the attention of the pharmacist and resolved prior to administration of the study drug.

e) Ensure all medications required for the management of hypersensitivity reactions are prescribed as per protocol requirements and/or standard of care, and that the medication is available at the infusion room.

<Warning>
Never administer a study drug if these medications are not prescribed and available.
</Warning>

f) Ensure that the prescription is dated and signed by the QI.

g) After verification, the QTS puts initials and date on the prescription. The QTS will apply the prescription in the appropriate study source document.

#### 5.2.2 Laboratory Tests

- Blood tests must be done within the previous 24–72 hours unless otherwise determined by the QI/oncology pharmacy or by protocol.
- Results must be within the parameters below unless otherwise stipulated in the protocol or determined by the QI:
  - Absolute neutrophil count (ANC) ≥ 1.5 (10⁹/L)
  - Platelets ≥ 100 (10⁹/L)
  - Renal and liver functions within normal range (dependent on specific agent)
  - Electrolytes within normal range
  - Haemoglobin ≥ 80 mg/L
- Document verification of blood test results on the nursing notes.
- Any abnormal laboratory value must be brought to the attention of the QI. No treatment should be started without QI confirmation.

#### 5.2.3 Nursing Notes

a) The QTS reads prior nursing notes to notice any reactions to study drug, previous and ongoing side effects, problems with catheters, history of allergies, etc.

### 5.3 Pre-Treatment Subject Assessment {#pre-treatment-subject-assessment}

5.3.1 Verify subject identity according to CIM work instruction WI-CIM-CR001 Double Identification of Research Subjects and Independent Double Check of Medication Prior to Administration.

5.3.2 Verify that the subject still consents to continue on the clinical trial.

5.3.3 Assess side effects and performance status using ECOG since the last treatment (refer to Appendix 4).

5.3.4 Assess subject and family understanding, learning needs, and address any concerns.

5.3.5 Assess concomitant medications/conditions.

5.3.6 Measure vital signs.

### 5.4 Venous Access and Tubing Set-Ups {#venous-access-and-tubing-set-ups}

#### 5.4.1 IV Access Assessment

a) Subjects must be assessed to ensure that the most appropriate IV access — peripheral vs. central venous access device (CVAD), including type of CVAD — is selected for treatment.

b) Ensure the subject has a patent IV access.

- **CVAD:** Assess patency prior to administration of every antineoplastic agent by checking for blood return by aspirating with a 10 ml syringe, then flushing with normal saline; note any resistance to flushing. Assess the site every hour during infusion and PRN.
- **Peripheral IV:** Use a 22G or 24G catheter to minimize trauma and provide better flow. When administering vesicants, select a vein in the forearm as first choice; avoid the hand, wrist, or antecubital fossa. Peripheral IV must be assessed minimum every hour or more frequently if required (in the case of vesicants) or if a subject complains of pain, tenderness, or discomfort at the IV insertion site.

<Warning>
Never use an IV access if there is resistance to flushing and/or no blood return.
</Warning>

#### 5.4.2 Infusion Pump

Ensure a certified, calibrated infusion pump(s) is/are available. Document pump ID in source documents (e.g., nursing notes).

a) Prepare tubing set-up:

- Mainline tubing can be luer-locked directly to the catheter hub or through an injection cap.
- If needed, prime second primary tubing with compatible solution and connect to the lowest Y port of the mainline. This is needed when:
  - a. Simultaneous infusion of IV fluids is required (example: hydration, infusion of a vesicant or irritant via PIV).
  - b. Agents with a significant risk for hypersensitivity reactions (HSRs) are administered — allows for a rescue IV line if needed.
  - c. The agent to be administered requires a filter: prime IV tubing with filter with compatible solution and connect to the lowest Y port of the mainline, or connect a filter extension piece to the secondary IV tubing. See Appendix 1.

### 5.5 Administration of Study Medication {#administration-of-study-medication}

#### 5.5.1 Pre-Infusion

a) Ensure that laboratory test has been reviewed by the QI.

b) Ensure the QI or Sub-QI is present at the time of infusion and obtain written permission to dose prior to administering the medication (see QI progress notes in OACIS).

c) Verify if all medications have been received from pharmacy.

d) Follow the prescription and administer pre-medications (e.g., anti-emetics) or hydration, if required.

e) Put on PPE (for hazardous drug only).

f) Verify product integrity while medication is in the inner transport bag. If a leak is observed, leave everything inside and contact the pharmacy and follow instructions.

g) Verify the study treatment medication received from the pharmacy against the prescription and study-related protocol.
   - Two QTS must do an IDC for each single antineoplastic agent (one of the QTS must be the administering nurse).
   - The IDC includes: check the label of each bag to ensure that the subject name, date of birth, MRN, drug, dose, and route are correct. Check the expiry date and solution type, volume and clarity. Also note any additional labels such as vesicant/irritant, need to protect from light, or refrigerate.
   - The verifying QTS must initial and date the label provided by pharmacy and the source documents.

h) Confirm subject identification in accordance with WI-CIM-CR001 Double Identification of Research Subjects and Independent Double Check of Medication Prior to Administration.

#### 5.5.2 Continuous IV Infusion

a) Take the study treatment medication out of the bag (clear zip-lock bag); keep the bag until the end of infusion to put biohazard waste in it.

b) Use a chlorhexidine and alcohol swab to disinfect the CVAD connector for 15 seconds with mechanical friction. Let dry for 30 seconds.

c) Do IV assessment as described in section 5.4.1.

d) Connect the primary solution.

e) Make sure the IV tubing is clamped if already connected to the bag; otherwise connect an IV tubing to the bag.

f) Disinfect the distal Y port (closest to the pump) of the chemotherapy line with a chlorhexidine and alcohol swab, then connect the secondary tubing.

g) Open the roller clamp. If secondary tubing is not primed, back-prime to fill the tubing chamber.

h) Make sure all roller clamps are closed and study drug is hung higher than the primary solution (see Appendix 1).

i) Take the gloves off and discard in cytotoxic waste container; wash your hands with soap and water.

j) Set the rate of infusion so that the study drug will infuse over the prescribed length of time as per the related protocol and/or QI instructions.

k) A second QTS performs an IDC to verify pump programming.

l) Open the roller clamp and start the infusion. Document start time in the source documents.

m) **If vesicant infused through PIV:**
   - Infusions of vesicant drugs with a duration of 60 minutes or less may be administered through a peripheral IV catheter; longer infusions require a central venous catheter.
   - Do not use volumetric pump to infuse by PIV (except: Paclitaxel, Docetaxel, Nab-Paclitaxel, and Oxaliplatin).
   - Run mainline hydration by gravity at the same time as the chemotherapy drug infusion. This dilutes the vesicant and the flow rate provides a sign of patency (a reduced flow rate may be an early sign of extravasation).
   - If a peripheral IV has been in place for 24 hours or more, select a new site. If a vesicant drug is administered on subsequent days, a new IV is to be inserted before each administration.
   - Visually monitor the site for signs and symptoms of infiltration and inflammation; check for blood return prior to starting the infusion, every 5 to 10 minutes throughout the infusion, and at the end of the infusion.

<Warning>
Note that extravasation can also occur in the absence of symptoms.
</Warning>

n) **If vesicant infused by CVAD:**
   - Use of volumetric pump is permitted.
   - Run mainline hydration by gravity.
   - Check for blood return prior to the infusion, at the end of infusion, and PRN for any subject discomfort or suspected problem.
   - During infusion, visually monitor CVAD every hour and PRN.
   - Never use CVAD if no blood return is observed.

o) Take PPE off and discard in cytotoxic waste container (the gown and face shield may be re-used and only discarded at end of shift) — see WI-CIM-ON002 Safe Handling of Hazardous Drugs.

p) Wash your hands with soap and water.

q) Monitor subject during infusion for (but not limited to): signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reaction, vital signs, AEs and SAEs. Document all findings during infusion in source documents.

r) Verify blood return before starting each new antineoplastic agent. During the perfusion, verify site minimum every 30 minutes for peripheral IV and every hour for CVAD to detect early signs of extravasation.

s) Encourage the subject to report any discomfort or any new symptom.

t) At the end of infusion, document stop time in source documents.

u) To give the complete dose of study drug and avoid residual volume:
   - Rinse as per protocol requirement, or with 35 ml of primary solution at the same rate as the medication administration.
   - Clamp the mainline IV tubing just below the drip chamber at the beginning of the rinse — this allows fluid from the secondary IV tubing to infuse only. When it is almost empty, open the clamp on the mainline IV tubing and close the clamp on the secondary tubing.
   - Remain with the subject or the mainline IV tubing may fill with air. Allow the rinse to finish.

v) Put on PPE — for hazardous drug only. Disconnect the mainline from the CVAD or by removing the catheter from the peripheral IV (secondary line), and dispose of the empty bag(s), IV line tubing, and any other waste in the large delivery bag (for hazardous drug only); discard in cytotoxic waste container.

<Warning>
Never unspike tubing from an IV bag which contained a hazardous drug.
</Warning>

w) Take PPE off and discard in cytotoxic waste container.

x) Wash your hands with soap and water.

#### 5.5.3 Direct IV Push

a) Follow steps for pre-infusion process in section 5.5.1.

b) Put a disposable waterproof drape on the work zone.

c) Take the syringe with the study drug out of the bag. Put it on the disposable waterproof drape. Prepare a 10 cm × 10 cm compress.

d) Do IV assessment as described in section 5.4.1.

e) Program the pump to infuse the primary solution of the chemo line at 500 ml/h.

f) Use a chlorhexidine and alcohol swab to disinfect the Y connector for 15 seconds with mechanical friction. Let dry for 30 seconds.

g) Remove the protective cap from the syringe underneath the disposable waterproof drape. Avoid pressing the plunger.

h) Hold the syringe with one hand; never hold the hub towards the face; never prime the syringe.

i) Surround the proximal Y port with a 10 cm × 10 cm compress and connect the syringe.

j) Verify blood return by pulling back the plunger of the syringe.

k) Inject medication according to the prescription. Document the time in source documents (e.g., nursing notes).

l) Encourage the subject to report any discomfort or pain.

m) During the injection, continuously verify IV patency to detect extravasation early.

n) Keep the 10 cm × 10 cm compress in the Y port during the injection until flushing.

o) Once administration is complete, leave the syringe on the tubing, bend the tubing below the Y site and fill with at least 3 ml of primary solution. Push this diluted solution and pull a second 3 ml. This ensures a disconnection with fluid rather than cytotoxic medication. Document end of administration in source documents (e.g., nursing notes).

p) Gently withdraw the syringe. Discard the syringe; put the compress and waterproof drape in the clear zip-lock bag, then discard in cytotoxic waste container.

q) Disinfect the secondary Y port from the primary tube and connect a 10 ml syringe containing a compatible solution.

r) Inject 10 ml of the compatible solution to eliminate all residual medication from the IV tubing.

s) Discard the syringe in the cytotoxic waste container.

t) Take off PPE and discard in cytotoxic waste container (the gown and face shield may be re-used and only discarded at end of shift).

u) Wash your hands with soap and water.

#### 5.5.4 Elastomeric Pump

a) Follow steps in section 5.5.1 for pre-infusion process.

b) Put a disposable waterproof drape on the work zone.

c) Take the elastomeric pump with the study drug out of the bag. Put it on the disposable waterproof drape. Prepare a 10 cm × 10 cm compress.

d) Use a chlorhexidine and alcohol swab to disinfect the CVAD connector for 15 seconds with mechanical friction. Let dry for 30 seconds.

e) Ensure all clamps are closed (e.g., Hubert needle, elastomeric pump).

f) Connect a 10 ml prefilled syringe; verify blood return.

g) Remove the protective cap from the pump underneath the disposable waterproof drape.

h) Connect the pump infusion line to the CVAD.

i) Secure the connection between the CVAD and the pump, as well as the flow regulator, by putting a transparent dressing fixing it to the skin.

j) Open all clamps. An IDC of the pump installation must be performed.

k) Make sure the subject can move freely without pulling the line. This prevents disconnection of the infusion system and spill of medication.

l) Put the elastomeric pump in a transport bag that keeps the infusion system no lower than waist level and protected from light.

m) Teach subject and family regarding precautions to be taken with the infuser at first treatment and/or upon subject request.

n) Fax the request sheet with the date and hour of pump discontinuation to the subject's local CLSC; ensure a DSIE (demande de services interétablissements) has been previously sent and accepted.

### 5.6 Documentation {#documentation}

5.6.1 Register drug name, dose, route, date and time of administration, and duration of infusion of all study medications administered on the source documents. Document the type of IV fluids administered, as well as the rate or total volume over time infused.

5.6.2 Register the time of start, end, and rate modifications of the infusion (if any) in source documents (e.g., nursing notes).

5.6.3 Document all assessments (vital signs, pertinent laboratory values, AEs, SAEs, IV or CVAD patency, and tolerance to drug infusion), interventions, and outcomes in source documents.
