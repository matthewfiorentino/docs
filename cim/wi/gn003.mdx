## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}

**Purpose:** This Work Instruction (WI) describes the instructions for insertion and removal of a non-coring needle, as well as the dressing change on implantable vascular access devices for clinical trials conducted within the Center of Innovative Medicine (CIM) of the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre (RI-MUHC).

**Scope:** This WI applies to nurses at the CIM of the RI-MUHC who care for and maintain implantable vascular access devices.

## 3 · Terminology and definitions {#terminology-and-definitions}

| Term / Acronym | Definition |
|---|---|
| Totally Implantable Vascular Access Device (TIVAD) | The catheter of these devices passes from the cannulated vein beneath the skin and attaches to a subcutaneous infusion port or reservoir that is placed into a subcutaneous pocket. Examples: Port-a-Cath, BardPort, PowerPort, Infuse-a-Port. (Chopra, 2019) |
| Qualified Investigator (QI) | Also referred to as Principal Investigator (PI) or Investigator. |

## 4 · Instructions {#instructions}

### 4.1 Gathering the Required Supplies {#gathering-the-required-supplies}

The following supplies are essential to access a TIVAD, but not limited to:

- Impermeable sterile field
- One (1) 10 mL Sterile NaCl 0.9% prefilled syringe
- One (1) Isopropyl Alcohol 70% and chlorhexidine 2% swab stick
- One (1) pair of sterile gloves
- Regular masks for nurse, subject, and family
- One (1) or two (2) non-coring needle(s) depending on the number of chambers of the port
- One (1) or two (2) needleless injection cap(s) — if required
- One (1) 500 units Heparin prefilled syringe — if required
- One (1) semi-permeable dressing (e.g., Tegaderm or IV 3000) with hypafix to reinforce borders
- If sensitive skin: liquid skin protectant barrier film (e.g., Cavilon™)
- Disinfecting wipes (to clean working area)

### 4.2 Assessment {#assessment}

**4.2.1** Verify subject's identity by following CIM work instruction WI-CIM-CR001 Double Identification of Research Subjects and Independent Double Check of Medication Prior to Administration.

**4.2.2** Explain the procedure to subject and family.

**4.2.3** Visually inspect skin over and around TIVAD for redness, swelling, skin erosion, skin breakdown, leaking, poor healing of the incision and/or edema.

**4.2.4** Palpate the site to confirm port position and junction between catheter and chamber. Make sure the skin is mobile over the septum surface and rotate puncture site to avoid puncturing in a previous hole.

**4.2.5** Ask the subject if there have been any problems with the TIVAD such as pain or lack of blood return.

**4.2.6** Consider approaches to decrease anxiety such as distraction and/or pain management; an individual order of topical anesthetic cream may be obtained.

### 4.3 Selection of the Non-Coring Needle {#selection-of-the-non-coring-needle}

**4.3.1** Always select the smallest gauge available to infuse therapy.

**4.3.2** Always select the shortest length available so that the needle wings rest flat on the skin and the tip of the needle reaches the bottom of the chamber.

**4.3.3** Usually, a 20G ¾ inch is adequate for the majority of subjects.

### 4.4 Preparing the Environment {#preparing-the-environment}

**4.4.1** Close the door or curtain and turn off electric fans to minimize air movement.

**4.4.2** Prepare work surface by clearing of equipment and supplies. Clean work surface with disinfectant wipe and allow drying.

**4.4.3** Nurse and subject need to wear a mask. Long hair must be tied up.

**4.4.4** Perform hand hygiene.

**4.4.5** Open dressing trays and add sterile supplies (non-coring needle, dressing, sterile NaCl 0.9%, etc.). Use aseptic non-touch technique to protect critical parts (catheter material, hub and insertion site) from beginning to end of procedure.

### 4.5 Procedure {#procedure}

#### 4.5.1 Non-Coring Needle Insertion

**4.5.1.1** Perform skin disinfection covering an area larger than the size of the dressing. With one side of a swab stick, disinfect the skin using a back-and-forth friction motion horizontally for 15 seconds. With the second side of the swab stick, disinfect the skin using a back-and-forth friction motion vertically for 15 seconds. *(This technique allows for greater penetration of the antiseptic into the epidermis as friction is applied.)*

**4.5.1.2** Allow disinfectant to dry completely. Isopropyl Alcohol 70% and chlorhexidine 2% takes at least 3 minutes to dry.

**4.5.1.3** Consider application of skin barrier (e.g., Cavilon™) if sensitive skin or subject is sweating profusely to prevent contact dermatitis.

**4.5.1.4** Put on sterile gloves.

**4.5.1.5** Connect needleless injection cap onto the extension tubing of the non-coring needle.

**4.5.1.6** Prime with Normal Saline, leave syringe attached to the cap, clamp tubing.

**4.5.1.7** Place sterile field near TIVAD. This will be used to place your syringe during puncture of the chamber.

**4.5.1.8** Grasp the non-coring needle with your dominant hand. Hold needle using the white upper part of the needle.

**4.5.1.9** Remove protective tube covering the lower part of the needle and place syringe on the sterile field while holding on the needle.

**4.5.1.10** Using the non-dominant hand, make a "C" with your middle finger, your index and your thumb. Place your middle finger over the connection between the catheter and the chamber. Stabilize chamber with your index and your thumb.

**4.5.1.11** To locate the middle of the septum, imagine lines crossing from the tip of your index (1) to the junction between your middle finger (2) and your thumb (3) to the junction between your middle finger and your index (4).

**4.5.1.12** Before puncturing, as much as possible, orient the bevel of the needle away from the catheter. This allows the complete flushing of the chamber.

**4.5.1.13** Using a continuous motion, puncture the septum at the crossing of the lines, perpendicularly to the septum, until the needle tip comes in contact with the bottom of the chamber. **DO NOT** follow the curvature of the needle as this will damage the membrane.

**4.5.1.14** You should feel the bottom of the chamber when the needle is completely inserted. Sometimes it is possible to hear it too.

**4.5.1.15** Unclamp tubing, flush 1 or 2 mL of NaCl 0.9%, then pull back to confirm presence of a blood return into the tubing, then flush the full 10 mL of NaCl 0.9% using turbulence or stop-and-go technique.

**4.5.1.16** Clamp tubing.

**4.5.1.17** If accessing:
- **For maintaining patency only:** See section 4.5.2 Maintenance of Patency.
- **For medication administration:** Remove the clip, pinching it and sliding it to the end, lift and discard.

**4.5.1.18** Proceed with application of transparent dressing:
- Place the middle of the window on top of the needle. Apply the window part first then apply the remaining of the dressing.
- Ensure that the extension tubing falls under the notch of the dressing. Cross each side of the notch under the extension.

**4.5.1.19** Apply closure piece of the dressing by slipping it under the tubing and over the edge of the dressing.

**4.5.1.20** Connect IV tubing and start infusion. Document the time in the source documents (e.g., nursing notes).

**4.5.1.21** Remove gloves and wash hands.

**4.5.1.22** Document date and time of insertion, patency verification, and initials and date.

**4.5.1.23** At the end of the infusion, before removal of the needle, flush and lock the TIVAD as described in section 4.5.2 below.

#### 4.5.2 Maintenance of Patency

**4.5.2.1** Make sure you have an individual prescription for Heparin; obtain the prescription or ensure the prescription is already on the subject's chart.

**4.5.2.2** Gather equipment:
- Non-sterile gloves
- Isopropyl Alcohol 70% and chlorhexidine 2% wipe
- NaCl 0.9% 10 mL pre-filled syringe (Posiflush™)
- Heparin pre-filled syringe according to prescription

**4.5.2.3** Wash hands and don gloves.

**4.5.2.4** Ensure all the clamps are closed and disconnect IV line.

**4.5.2.5** Clean the needleless connector port with an Isopropyl Alcohol 70% and chlorhexidine 2% wipe; let dry.

**4.5.2.6** Flush with a 10 mL pre-filled syringe (Posiflush™) using the turbulence technique. Clamp tubing and remove syringe.

**4.5.2.7** Lock with one pre-filled Heparin syringe using positive pressure technique (follow QI's prescription). For positive pressure technique: close the clamp while injecting the last 0.5 mL of solution.

**4.5.2.8** Proceed to needle removal.

#### 4.5.3 Non-Coring Needle Removal

**4.5.3.1** Gather equipment:
- Non-sterile gloves
- Sterile 2×2 inches compress
- Fabric bandage

**4.5.3.2** Put on gloves and remove dressing. Note if drainage, redness or swelling. If so, notify the investigator. Discard dressing.

**4.5.3.3** With your non-dominant hand, stabilize the platform of the needle with your thumb and your index.

**4.5.3.4** With your dominant hand index, lift the safety arm; you will hear a click once the needle is completely removed. Discard in sharps container.

**4.5.3.5** Apply pressure at puncture site until bleeding stops (if any bleeding). Cover with a diachylon (bandage).

**4.5.3.6** Document time of flushing, dose and lot number/expiry of Heparin used to lock, and initial/date.
