## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}

**Purpose:** This Work Instruction (WI) describes the instructions for the double identification of research subjects and performing an independent double check of medication prior to administration at the Center for Innovative Medicine (CIM) of the Research Institute of the McGill University Health Centre (RI-MUHC).

**Scope:** This WI applies to all qualified trained staff (QTS) involved in clinical research studies at the CIM, RI-MUHC.

|---|---|

## 3 · Terminology and definitions {#terminology-and-definitions}

| Term / Acronym | Definition |
|---|---|
| Double Identification of Research Subjects | The process of verifying the identity of an individual prior to performing an assessment. |
| Independent Double Check (IDC) | A series of verifications performed every time a medication is administered, which includes ensuring the following is performed with the correct individual (also known as "5 Rights"): right patient, right medication, right dosage/concentration, right route, right time, and with the correct assessment. Occurs when two clinicians perform medication verification steps, comparing medication label and syringe/container (if applicable) to the prescription, separately and independently (alone and apart from each other to reduce the risk of bias) and then compare results. |
| MRN | Medical record number |
| Qualified Trained Staff (QTS) | Personnel with the appropriate training, experience, and if applicable certification and/or license to perform study related activities based on their role (e.g., Qualified Investigators (QIs), Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC), Clinical Research Coordinator Nurse (CRC-N), Infusion Nurses). |
| Study Records | Includes source documents or labels (e.g., medication, blood test tubes). |
| Study Record Verification | The process of verifying the accuracy of study records (e.g., source documents, a printed document such as a prescription or medication label) using two identifiers from approved sources. |

## 4 · Instructions {#instructions}

### 4.1 General Principles {#general-principles}

**4.1.1** Always bring the relevant study forms and labels to be used to verify positive patient identification before starting any intervention.

**4.1.2** A failure to properly identify the subject puts both the subject and institution at risk. A Double Identification of Research Subjects and Independent Double Check must be performed in order to avoid medical error and improve subject safety.

**4.1.3** Extra precautions should be taken when there are research subjects on a study with similar or identical names (e.g., flagged with an alert or caution sign on the subject's chart/binder). They must be carefully identified using at least two (2) unique identifiers such as the MRN, date of birth and/or first name.

**4.1.4** Note that with the exception of year, all elements of dates directly related to an individual (such as birth date, admission date, discharge date, date of death) are considered direct identifiers that may lead to the identification of a specific patient; caution should be taken not to disclose this information (e.g., to the sponsor). These identifiers may be replaced in the study records with age or a default day and month when these fields are required (e.g., 01-Jan in the eCRF), if appropriate. The actual dates will be maintained confidentially within the study records.

### 4.2 When to Perform Double Identification of Research Subjects {#when-to-perform-double-identification-of-research-subjects}

**4.2.1** At a minimum, QTS must verify the identification of all new research subjects they are unfamiliar with prior to any study-related activities taking place including, but not limited to:
- Signing consent
- Administering treatment (confirming correct patient, medication label, and prescription)
- Laboratory specimen collection such as blood sample, fluid sample, tissue samples, swabs (confirming correct patient, tube labelling, and requisitions)
- Exams (e.g., ECG, EEG, Doppler)
- Medical imaging and nuclear medicine examination or intervention

### 4.3 Approved Sources for Performing Double Identification of Research Subjects {#approved-sources-for-performing-double-identification-of-research-subjects}

| Approved Identifier | Approved Sources for Confirming Identification |
|---|---|
| **First Identifier (mandatory)** | |
| Full legal name | Verbally with research subject; or ID listed below |
| **Second Identifier (any one of the following)** | |
| Medical Record Number (MRN) *(Note: MRN is unique while names and birthdates may not be)* | Hospital issued identification card |
| Date of birth | Government issued photo identification (e.g., driver's license, Medicare card); or verbally with research subject |
| Home Address | Government issued photo identification (e.g., driver's license, Medicare card); or verbally with research subject |

**4.3.1 Research Subjects as Source of Identification**

**4.3.1.1** When performing the double identification with the subject as the source (i.e., verbally with research subject) the QTS must explain to the research subject why the double identification is being performed, for example: *"I am performing this verification because we want to ensure your safety and that you are receiving the care intended for you."*

**4.3.1.2** When administering treatment (e.g., infusion), the first identifier must be confirmed verbally with the research subject.

**4.3.2 Approved Sources Other than Research Subjects**

**4.3.2.1** Check that the verbal identification provided is consistent with the research subject's identification (e.g., MRN, Medicare card), matches identifiers on all relevant sources/forms, and labels.

### 4.4 Steps for Performing the Double Identification of Research Subjects {#steps-for-performing-the-double-identification-of-research-subjects}

**4.4.1** Bring relevant documentation and labels with you to be used to verify the subject's identification before starting any intervention.

**4.4.2** If unfamiliar with the research subject, ask them (or if applicable their family or legal guardian) to confirm their full name and another approved identifier, for example:
- *"For your safety I need to ask you to state your full name and date of birth."*
- *"Hello, I am Jane, your nurse today. As I verify the medication label, can you confirm your first and last name? Also, what is your date of birth?"*
- Note: do not ask to confirm the information such as *"Is your name Mr. John Doe?"* but ask them to state the information.

**4.4.3** Check that the verbal identification provided by the research subject is accurately reflected on the applicable approved source of identification, and that it matches the identifiers on all relevant records/forms and/or labels.

**4.4.4** If the research subject is unable to identify themselves (e.g., pediatric), you may perform the verification with their family member or legal guardian. If a family member or guardian is unavailable, refer to the subject's MRN, Medicare card, and/or driver's license. If necessary, ask for an interpreter to verify the information.

**4.4.5** After having performed the subject's identification the QTS must verify the study records before performing any study procedures (e.g., prior to IV catheter insertion, drug administration, drawing blood). Any pre-filled documentation and/or pre-printed labels must be verified (e.g., confirming the drug label before administration, confirming the blood tube labels).

**4.4.6** Always label samples before leaving the research subject's side. Never remove an unidentified sample from the presence of the research subject.

### 4.5 When and How to Perform an Independent Double Check {#when-and-how-to-perform-an-independent-double-check}

**4.5.1** Prior to administering medication the following verifications must be performed:

- **Right Research Subject:** At each medication administration, the study nurse must compare the patient's identification (if individual is unfamiliar, using two patient identifiers per section 4.1 General Principles) to the prescription and pharmacy label, to ensure the right patient receives the correct medication. When applicable, the patient can be asked to identify their name on the label of the medication.

- **Right Medication:** Independent verification of the medication label and prescription against the current treatment protocol and regimen, verification of the expiry date, if medication is a solution verify volume and clarity of the preparation, any additional labels such as vesicant/irritant, special precautions (e.g., need to protect from light or refrigerate) are also noted.

- **Right dosage/concentration:** Check that the dosing formula (e.g., mg/kg) used to obtain the final dose is correct, ensure that dosage is safe for weight and/or body surface area (BSA) (for the pediatric population), check programming of the infusion pump including rate of infusion against the prescription.

- **Right route of administration:** Ensure that all distal infusion lines and infusion pumps are labeled. For multiple infusions, trace tubing back to their source, ensure line patency at site and that all clamps are open and connections are secure. Use dedicated pumps and dedicated tubing (e.g., yellow striped dedicated tubing for epidural administration), and ensure that all infusions are connected to the right route.

- **Right time (date and frequency of administration):** Ensure the frequency/timing is according to the protocol requirements.

- **Correct nursing assessment,** including review of lab values, physical assessment, as well as protocol specific needs: The nurse is aware of the specific patient assessment and monitoring required prior to, during, and following the administration of the medication. Ensure appropriate monitoring and guidelines are followed (e.g., verification of serum potassium prior to electrolyte replacement or diuretic therapy; verification of the partial thromboplastin time (PTT) while the patient is on a heparin infusion, verification of blood glucose prior to insulin administration).

**4.5.2** Retain the IP label(s) and attach to source documents (an additional label may need to be requested from pharmacy).

**4.5.3** For IDC of the dose calculation refer to WI-CIM-ON001 Administration of Intravenous Chemotherapy and Biotherapy as Study Drugs in Clinical Trials.

### 4.6 Error Management and Reporting {#error-management-and-reporting}

**4.6.1** Errors in subject identification (refer to section 4.4 Steps for Performing the Double Identification of Research Subjects) or any of the "five rights" when performing an independent double check (refer to section 4.5 When and How to Perform an Independent Double Check) must be investigated and rectified immediately. If applicable, measures to prevent recurrence must be implemented (e.g., deviation, corrective and preventive action).
