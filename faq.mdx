## Types of study and Health Canada

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Which type of study is mine, and which training level does it need?">
    What a study tests and how it gets its data decide its kind, and each kind has its own training level.

    Ask in this order whether the study tests a drug (Level I), a medical device or a surgical procedure (Level II), or a natural health product (Level III), and stop at the first yes. A study that tests none of these and only uses records, data or samples that already exist is a retrospective study at Level V. Any other study is a prospective study not regulated by Health Canada, at Level IV.

    If the study will interview or survey participants about past events, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) which level applies.   <Accordion title="What does review time depend on?">
    Review time depends on the study's level of risk, how complete your submission is, whether revisions are needed and the REB's workload. There are no submission deadlines, so enter your study in Nagano as soon as it's ready. Before the REB reviews a submission, staff of the [Centre for Applied Ethics](https://muhc.ca/cae/research-ethics) check it for completeness and the quality of its documents, and assess its level of risk.

    Full-board review is the default. Studies that involve no more than minimal risk are [eligible for delegated review](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops), which runs continuously. A new project that could interfere with the integrity of a minor or of an adult who can't consent cannot go through delegated review at its first review. Studies with more than minimal risk are reviewed at a full-board meeting of one of the REB's panels, whose meeting calendar is in Nagano.

    Incomplete submissions are a common cause of delay, so check yours against [Preparing Your Nagano Submission](/kb/submission/preparing) before you submit.   <Accordion title="Which REB reviews my study: the MUHC REB, McGill's, or another?">
    The MUHC REB reviews a study whose only site in Quebec is the MUHC. A study at more than one public institution in Quebec is reviewed by [one Quebec evaluating REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/multicentre-study), and each institution still does its own feasibility review and authorization. In a pan-Canadian study with sites outside Quebec, submit for those sites under each jurisdiction's rules, and submit the Quebec sites in Nagano.

  <Accordion title="What's the difference between de-identified and anonymized data?">

  <Accordion title="Where may I keep and send my study's data?">
