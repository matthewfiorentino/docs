Before you spend time planning a study, answer one question first: **does this project need REB review at all?** Most clinical research does. Some projects that look like research, such as chart reviews, process audits and evaluations of standard care, qualify as **quality assurance or quality improvement (QA/QI)** and are exempt from REB review, as long as they're used only for assessment, management or improvement. Submitting a QA/QI project for ethics review costs time and creates the wrong file in the wrong place. Skipping ethics review for what is actually research is far worse — the work cannot be published, cannot be funded, and may need to be redone.

The MUHC Centre for Applied Ethics (CAE) publishes a [Research vs QA/QI Screening Tool](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/templates-consent-forms) (Word document) that is the canonical version of this decision aid. Its questions and its rule are set out under [Screening questions](#screening-questions).

## The distinction

**Research** is defined by TCPS2 as *"an undertaking intended to extend knowledge through a disciplined inquiry and/or systematic investigation."* The aim is **generalizable knowledge** — findings that can withstand the scrutiny of the relevant research community and inform practice beyond the local setting. Research involving human participants requires REB review and approval at the MUHC.

**Quality assurance / quality improvement** projects aim to **introduce changes that improve patient outcomes, system performance, or professional development at the local institution**. They often implement already-established best practices using methods like Plan-Do-Study-Act cycles, and place no additional burden on participants beyond standard care. For researchers following [TCPS 2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html), QA/QI projects used only for assessment, management or improvement are **exempt from REB review**.

<Note>
  Research and QA/QI sit on a continuum. A single project can carry features of both. The CAE's screening tool was designed to help with this exact ambiguity. **The MUHC REB retains the right to make the ultimate determination** regardless of what the tool indicates.
</Note>

A few common questions before you start:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can a retrospective chart review be QA/QI?">
    Sometimes. The objective and intent of the project are the determining factors. Many chart-review projects are in fact **Health Records Research (HRR)** and must be submitted to the REB via Nagano using the [CAE HRR protocol template](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/templates-consent-forms). Complete the screening tool to decide.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I automatically need REB review if I intend to publish?">
    No. Per TCPS2, *"intent or ability to publish findings are not factors that determine whether an activity is research requiring ethics review."* Some QA/QI projects are publishable; some research projects are not. Publishability is independent of the research-versus-QA/QI question.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who is ultimately responsible for the decision?">
    The TCPS2 instructs the REB to be consulted *"when in doubt."* You are responsible for the accuracy and completeness of your answers. The REB makes the final determination if your project is submitted.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if my project starts as QA/QI and evolves into research?">
    You are responsible for obtaining REB review at the point of change. An exemption letter for the original QA/QI scope does not extend to the new research aspects.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Special considerations for QA/QI projects">
    Even when exempt from REB review, QA/QI projects must respect three additional obligations:
    - **Patient health information without consent** requires a **Privacy Impact Assessment (EFVP / évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée)**. Initiate directly with [efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca).
    - **Hospital resources** require authorization from the appropriate responsible authorities. Initiate directly.
    - **Eeyou/Eenou (Cree) populations**: declare the project to the Cree Board of Health and Social Services of James Bay at [18tcr.research.committee@ssss.gouv.qc.ca](mailto:18tcr.research.committee@ssss.gouv.qc.ca).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Screening questions

These are the CAE's screening questions, in three groups. A YES to any question in the first group means the project should be submitted to the REB. In the other two groups, YES answers suggest a research project or a QA/QI project.

### If any answer is YES, submit the project to the REB

- Does the project involve the use of an experimental medical device, drug, or natural health product that requires approval from Health Canada?
- Does the project involve the off-label use of an existing drug?
- Is the project funded by, or being submitted to, a funding agency, a research grant, or an award that requires ethics review?
- Is the goal of the project to generate new generalizable knowledge by answering a question or testing a hypothesis using methods recognized by the scientific community?

### YES answers suggest a research project

- Does the project seek to control for variables or confounders to promote generalizability?
- Will you determine the number of participants via formal statistical justifications, power calculations, or expected thematic saturation levels?
- Is the project a pilot study or proof of concept designed to support a future, larger research project?

### YES answers suggest a QA/QI project

- Is the project the result of a department-wide initiative at the MUHC (e.g., with concerted support from colleagues)?
- Is the immediate goal of the project any of the following?
  - To improve a process, program, or system at the MUHC
  - To improve MUHC performance as judged by accepted practice standards
  - To evaluate or implement a best practice at the MUHC
- Do you have reasons to expect that the results of your project will be quickly integrated into local practices at the MUHC?
- Would the project still be done even if there were no opportunity to publish the results or if the results might not be applicable anywhere else?

## Authoritative source

These questions come from the MUHC Centre for Applied Ethics **Research vs Quality Assurance/Improvement (QA/QI) Screening Tool**. The canonical Word document is published on the [CAE Templates page](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/templates-consent-forms) and is the version the REB references when issuing exemption letters. If you need to submit a completed form to the REB — for an exemption letter or a formal determination — download and complete the CAE document directly.

For questions about whether a project requires REB review, contact the REB at extension 36077 or [reb@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca). Ethical concerns related to QA/QI projects (not requiring REB review) should be directed to the [Centre for Applied Ethics](mailto:cae@muhc.mcgill.ca).
