Before you spend time planning a study, answer one question first: **does this project need REB review at all?** Most clinical research does. Some projects that look like research, such as program evaluations, performance reviews and some chart reviews, are quality assurance or quality improvement (QA/QI) projects. They are exempt from REB review when they are used only for assessment, management or improvement. Submitting a QA/QI project for ethics review costs time and creates the wrong file in the wrong place. Skipping ethics review for what is actually research is far worse — the work cannot be published, cannot be funded, and may need to be redone.

The MUHC Centre for Applied Ethics (CAE) publishes a [Research vs QA/QI Screening Tool](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/templates-consent-forms) (Word document) that is the canonical version of this decision aid.

## Screening questions

The CAE's screening tool helps you determine whether your project is research that requires REB review or a QA/QI project. Read its questions carefully, answer them accurately and act on the tool's instructions.

### The project should be submitted to the REB if any of these is yes

The tool opens with these four questions:

- Does the project involve the use of an experimental medical device, drug, or natural health product that requires approval from Health Canada?
- Does the project involve the off-label use of an existing drug?
- Is the project funded by, or being submitted to, a funding agency, a research grant, or an award that requires ethics review?
- Is the goal of the project to generate new generalizable knowledge by answering a question or testing a hypothesis using methods recognized by the scientific community?

### What the other answers suggest

Yes answers to the tool's other questions are suggestive of a research project or of a QA/QI project, as the second column shows:

| Question | A yes suggests |
|---|---|
| Does the project seek to control for variables or confounders to promote generalizability? | Research |
| Will you determine the number of participants via formal statistical justifications, power calculations, or expected thematic saturation levels? | Research |
| Is the project a pilot study or proof of concept designed to support a future, larger research project? | Research |
| Is the project the result of a department-wide initiative at the MUHC (e.g., with concerted support from colleagues)? | QA/QI |
| Is the immediate goal of the project any of the following: To improve a process, program, or system at the MUHC? To improve MUHC performance as judged by accepted practice standards? To evaluate or implement a best practice at the MUHC? | QA/QI |
| Do you have reasons to expect that the results of your project will be quickly integrated into local practices at the MUHC? | QA/QI |
| Would the project still be done even if there were no opportunity to publish the results or if the results might not be applicable anywhere else? | QA/QI |

<Note>
  Research and QA/QI sit on a continuum. A single project can carry features of both. The CAE's screening tool was designed to help with this exact ambiguity. The MUHC REB retains the right to make the ultimate determination regardless of what the tool indicates.
</Note>

## The distinction

**Research** is defined by TCPS2 as *"an undertaking intended to extend knowledge through a disciplined inquiry and/or systematic investigation."* The aim is **generalizable knowledge** — findings that can withstand the scrutiny of the relevant research community and inform practice beyond the local setting. Research involving human participants requires REB review and approval at the MUHC.

At the MUHC, research involving human participants, their data or their biological material is [clinical research](/kb/foundations/clinical-research-101#tell-research-apart-from-clinical-care). Clinical research includes chart reviews and other research studies that use only existing records, data or samples. Research studies that develop or test software, such as an app or an algorithm, with participants or their data are clinical research too.

[The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) require REB approval for a retrospective study](/sops/cr-008#5-4-1). A retrospective study also needs MUHC Authorization before any research activity begins, as every study does. A study that uses identifiable health information without the participants' consent also needs an approved [privacy impact assessment](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent), the EFVP (évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée), before MUHC Authorization.{/* Sources: b277 (Nov. 19, 2026; MF, ledger rows 315 to 322: "often ppl don't think retrospective studies using data is 'clinical' research and don't realize they'll need privacy impact assessment or ethics review"): the definition, the MUHC's Regulatory Framework, section 1.2.1 ("clinical research activities (i.e., research involving human participants, their personal information and/or their biological material)"), in the Hub's words for its scope as on kb/governance/institutional-structure ("human participants, their data or their biological material"; STYLE-HUB A7); TCPS 2 (2022), article 2.1 ("those individuals whose data, biological materials, or responses to interventions, stimuli or questions by the researcher, are relevant to answering the research question(s)"), through its home, kb/foundations/clinical-research-101; the software sentence is the Hub's reading of article 2.1 (b277 record, Q-D2); SOP-CR-008_08, section 5.4.1 ("REB approval is required for a retrospective study."), named as the RI-MUHC's SOPs because TCPS 2, article 2.4, exempts research that relies only on anonymous information (STYLE-HUB A9), as on paths/retrospective; critical rule 1's two approvals (SOP-CR-007_08, section 3.0); SOP-CR-007_08, section 5.4.3, and FACTS "MF, Nov. 12, 2026 (batch 79's questions)", through kb/governance/privacy (the EFVP before MUHC Authorization). */}

The Centre for Applied Ethics sets **quality assurance and quality improvement (QA/QI)** apart from research by its aim. QA/QI projects aim to introduce changes that lead to improved patient outcomes, system performance or professional development.

The Centre describes QA/QI projects as projects that often seek to implement established best practices and that may use established methods, such as Plan-Do-Study-Act cycles. The Centre adds that QA/QI projects generally place no additional burden on participants beyond what is expected in standard care or practice.

Quality assurance and quality improvement studies used exclusively for assessment, management or improvement purposes [are not research for the purposes of TCPS 2, and fall outside REB review](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html).{/* Sources: b414 (Nov. 23, 2026, second reader; the conditions credited to the body that sets each, as kb/foundations/glossary, "TCPS 2 Article 2.5 (QA/QI exemption)", does since b408): the Centre for Applied Ethics' Research versus QA/QI Screening Tool (May 16, 2025; tools/_sources_text/Unsorted__QI vs Research screening tool_2025-05-16_ENG.docx.txt), question 2 ("Quality assurance/improvement projects aim to introduce changes that will lead to improved patient outcomes (health), system performance (care) and/or professional development."; "Often, QA/QI projects seek to implement already-established best practices."; "QA/QI projects may use established methods (e.g., Plan-Do-Study-Act cycles) and generally do not place any additional burdens on participants other than what is expected during standard care or practice."); TCPS 2 (2022), article 2.5 ("Quality assurance and quality improvement studies, program evaluation activities, and performance reviews, or testing within normal educational requirements when used exclusively for assessment, management or improvement purposes, do not constitute research for the purposes of this Policy, and do not fall within the scope of REB review."; saved, evidence/web/tcps2-2022-chapter2_read-2026-11-08.html), in the words of the glossary's "Quality Assurance and Quality Improvement (QA/QI)" entry. A few common questions about the distinction:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can a retrospective chart review be QA/QI?">
    Sometimes. The objective and intent of the project are the determining factors. Many chart-review projects are in fact **Health Records Research (HRR)** and must be submitted to the REB via Nagano using the [CAE HRR protocol template](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/templates-consent-forms). Completing the screening tool helps you decide.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I automatically need REB review if I intend to publish?">
    No. Per TCPS2, *"intent or ability to publish findings are not factors that determine whether an activity is research requiring ethics review."* Some QA/QI projects are publishable; some research projects are not. Publishability is independent of the research-versus-QA/QI question.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who is ultimately responsible for the decision?">
    When in doubt about whether your project is QA/QI or research that needs REB review, [you should consult the REB](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html), at [reb@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca). You are responsible for the accuracy and completeness of your answers. The REB makes the final decision on exemption from research ethics review.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if my project starts as QA/QI and evolves into research?">
    If you substantially change your project and you think it is moving towards research, you are responsible for obtaining REB review. An exemption letter will not apply to the new aspects of the project.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Authoritative source

The screening questions come from the MUHC Centre for Applied Ethics **Research vs Quality Assurance/Improvement (QA/QI) Screening Tool**. The canonical Word document is published on the [CAE Templates page](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/templates-consent-forms) and is the version the REB references when issuing exemption letters. If you need to submit a completed form to the REB — for an exemption letter or a formal determination — download and complete the CAE document directly.

For questions about whether a project requires REB review, contact the REB at extension 36077 or [reb@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca). Ethical concerns related to QA/QI projects (not requiring REB review) should be directed to the [Centre for Applied Ethics](mailto:cae@muhc.mcgill.ca).
