<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span></div>

You want to test a natural health product in people, such as a vitamin or mineral, a herbal medicine, a homeopathic preparation or a probiotic. A company may have asked the RI-MUHC to take part in a trial of its product. Or the question may be your own, and you would run the trial yourself.

The product may already hold a product licence from Health Canada. A trial of a natural health product is still a clinical trial that Health Canada regulates, under Part 4 of its Natural Health Products Regulations, including a trial within the product's licence.

Natural health product studies are at the RI-MUHC's [training Level III](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs), one of the three high-risk levels. Some natural health products must be managed by the MUHC Pharmacy. Every serious adverse event in a participant is reported to the sponsor, which reports some of them to Health Canada.

A natural health product study goes through the stages every clinical study does: feasibility, design and planning, submission and review, site activation, recruitment, conduct, close-out and archiving. Before any research activity begins, it needs the approval of the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, as every study does.

Several people share the work. The sponsor [takes responsibility for initiating, managing or financing the trial](/paths#who-sponsors-the-study). In an industry-sponsored trial, a company is the sponsor, and it applies to Health Canada where the trial needs it.

An investigator who both initiates and conducts a clinical trial is its <Tooltip tip="An individual who both initiates and conducts, alone or with others, a clinical trial, and under whose immediate direction the investigational product is administered to, dispensed to or used by a participant." headline="Sponsor-Investigator" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sponsor-investigator">sponsor-investigator</Tooltip>, with the obligations of both a sponsor and an investigator. When you sponsor the trial yourself, no company holds the protocol above you, and you do the sponsor's work too.

Health Canada's rules call the person responsible to the sponsor for the trial at the site the <Tooltip tip="The person responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a drug or natural health product trial at a clinical trial site, who is entitled to provide health care under the laws of the province where the site is located." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">qualified investigator</Tooltip>. In a natural health product trial, Health Canada requires the qualified investigator to be a physician, or a physician or a dentist for a product to be used for dental purposes only. The principal investigator leads the study at the MUHC, and the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) often join the two as "QI/PI".

The research team, such as coordinators and nurses, does the tasks the principal investigator delegates on the <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>, and the principal investigator keeps the overall responsibility for them.

A study that tests a drug follows [Drug Studies](/paths/level-i). A study that assigns participants to an intervention Health Canada does not regulate, such as a diet or an exercise program, follows [Prospective Studies Not Regulated by Health Canada](/paths/level-iv). If you can't tell which type of study yours is, [Types of Study](/paths) explains each kind, and the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help you place it.

## Place the study

Health Canada authorizes the sale or import of a natural health product for a clinical trial. If the trial needs that authorization, the sponsor applies for it, and the <Tooltip tip="The person whom an establishment of Quebec's health network formally mandates to authorize the research conducted under its auspices." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> waits for it before issuing MUHC Authorization.

A natural health product trial needs Health Canada's authorization, unless the trial is for a recommended use or purpose for which the product holds a product licence. [Check which applies to your study](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study).

The sponsor of a natural health product trial within the product's licence must still ensure that the trial follows good clinical practices, and must still meet the Natural Health Products Regulations' requirements on labelling and records.

The sections of the Hub tagged <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span> apply to every natural health product trial. The sections tagged <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span> apply to a trial that needs Health Canada's authorization. [Studies Regulated by Health Canada](/paths/hc-regulated) lists every section with either tag, by stage of the study.

## Plan an industry-sponsored trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Industry-sponsored studies</span></div>

In a company's trial, the site's research team conducts the study here. The team follows the sponsor's protocol, and its members keep their own professional and institutional obligations.

Most industry-sponsored trials reach the RI-MUHC through a company, or a contract research organization working for it, that has already picked the RI-MUHC as a possible site. [Answer the feasibility questionnaire](/kb/planning/sponsored-trials#answer-the-feasibility-questionnaire) the sponsor sends, and prepare for its [site feasibility visit](/kb/planning/feasibility#the-site-feasibility-visit). If you are considering having the [Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) run your study, contact it before you answer the questionnaire.

Before the team receives confidential study documents, such as the protocol, a [confidentiality agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#know-when-the-study-needs-an-agreement) between the sponsor, the institution and the investigator must be signed and dated. The [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) reviews and approves it before it is signed. The investigator must then submit the signed and dated agreement in Nagano.

The sponsor writes and owns the protocol, provides the <Tooltip tip="A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip> and applies to Health Canada for the trial's authorization where the trial needs it. The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, [negotiates the budget with the sponsor](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#negotiate-the-budget-with-an-industry-sponsor) while the Research Agreements Office negotiates the [clinical trial agreement's clauses](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#read-the-clauses-of-a-clinical-trial-agreement).

The RI-MUHC is a partner institution of CATALIS Québec's [FAST TRACK Evaluation Service](/kb/design/multi-centric#catalis-fast-track) for industry-sponsored clinical trials. Ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) whether your study is eligible.

## Plan a trial you sponsor yourself

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

When the trial is yours, no contract research organization monitors it and no company's regulatory affairs department prepares Health Canada's applications. Investigators sometimes discover the full scope of the sponsor's obligations only once they are committed to a study, often after a grant has been awarded.

Before you commit to a trial you initiate yourself, weigh [the sponsor's obligations](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#what-the-two-roles-mean) that you will hold as well as the investigator's. None of the sponsor's obligations is reduced or waived because the sponsor is also the investigator. Consider writing to [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) when you start planning the trial.

[Develop the protocol](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#what-the-two-roles-mean) in compliance with all the applicable requirements. For the RI-MUHC's insurance to cover the trial, the RI-MUHC should be [named as its sponsor](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#cover-the-study-as-its-sponsor) on the agreement, on regulatory documents and on ClinicalTrials.gov.

Develop a risk-based monitoring plan and arrange an independent monitor. You cannot monitor your own study. Keep the trial master file as well as the investigator site file.

If you create electronic case report forms in an electronic data capture system, [ensure that the system has been validated](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#what-the-two-roles-mean), is password protected, has an integrated audit trail and has a backup. Give the REB an acceptable [plan for monitoring safety, efficacy or effectiveness (where feasible) and validity](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html).

In a natural health product trial, [ensure that the product bears the label](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#investigational-product-and-gmp) the Natural Health Products Regulations set, in both official languages.

## Check that the site can carry out the trial

Feasibility is the earliest point at which serious problems can be caught, before money and time are committed. A protocol submitted without a realistic feasibility assessment often raises questions during review that delay authorization.

Once you have the protocol, assess its feasibility to find out what the site will need. Secure, adequate and temperature-controlled space for storing the investigational product [should be identified](/kb/planning/feasibility#before-the-site-feasibility-visit). A qualified person responsible for managing it, and procedures for its destruction where required, must be available.

## Prepare the team, the protocol and the consent form

The protocol is the study's master plan, and everything the site does during the study should be traceable to a specific protocol requirement. ### Check credentials and training

Teams sometimes assume that Quality Assurance's check covers everyone. During the study, the principal investigator must ensure that the people delegated tasks keep the study's level of institutional training.

Before you submit, the principal investigator must hold Human Research Privileges, Researcher Status or a written institutional authorization. [Privileges are required](/kb/submission/preparing#name-the-principal-investigator) to lead a study that Health Canada regulates with an investigational natural health product.

Everyone on the study should hold [Level III training or a higher level](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) before signing its Task Delegation Log. Level III training is the SOP Reader and Assessment for Level III and [good clinical practice (GCP) training](/training/external-certifications#ich-e6-r3-gcp) on guideline E6 of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH). Renew any credential or training that is [close to expiry](/training/compliance-requirements#validity-and-the-90-day-rule) before you submit.

A Sub-Investigator [should be named as the principal investigator's backup](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#name-a-backup-for-the-principal-investigator) when the study is being prepared.

## Submit the study in Nagano

Every submission goes through Nagano. The F11 starts the institutional feasibility review at the same time as the REB's review, and your answers on pharmacy, nursing and laboratory services start those services' reviews. Incomplete submissions are a common cause of delay.

Create the project in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> as a clinical trial, which uses the F11-H form. ## Activate the site

Once the study is authorized, it is not yet ready to run. Site activation covers the work between authorization and the first participant: the site initiation visit, the Task Delegation Log, the investigator site file and logistics such as the pharmacy.

Set up the <Tooltip tip="The qualified or principal investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines, so that the conduct and integrity of the study and the safety of participants at the site can be verified." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file (ISF)</Tooltip> before the first participant is approached, and [file the printed REB approval letter](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#check-and-file-the-approval-letter) in it. Teams sometimes keep only the electronic copy. The RI-MUHC's SOPs require the printed copy until a document management system that complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations is in place.

Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.

Registering a trial publicly before it recruits helps prevent selective publication of results. [The sponsor or sponsor-investigator registers the trial](/kb/close-out/publications#trial-registration) in a public registry before the first participant is recruited.

[Do not begin the study](/kb/site-activation/site-setup#wait-for-the-go-letter-before-you-enrol) until you have the REB's written approval, MUHC Authorization and the sponsor's go letter, if it issues one. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email.

Do not ask anyone to consent before you have the REB's written approval and MUHC Authorization, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator has held the site initiation visit, and the go letter has arrived, if the sponsor issues one. Then [recruit, consent and enrol participants](#recruit-consent-and-enrol-participants).

### Report safety events and deviations

In a natural health product trial, a serious adverse event can need reports to the sponsor, to the REB and, through the sponsor, to Health Canada. The sponsor's and the REB's deadlines run from the date the principal investigator learns of the event, so record that date.

In an interventional study, assess and record every [adverse event](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#assess-and-report-adverse-events) on the AE Assessment Log and in the source documents.

<Warning>
Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.
</Warning>

At a site under the MUHC REB's approval, a serious adverse event also needs an expedited report to the REB, by [the REB's deadline](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines), if the principal investigator judges that it meets the REB's three criteria. A physician investigator reviews each report of a suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction from another site that the sponsor sends, and the principal investigator [documents their medical oversight](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#review-and-report-external-susars) by initialling and dating it.

Report a reaction from another site to the REB by its deadline if the principal investigator judges that it meets the REB's three criteria and also requires a change to the research or requires that participants be told immediately for their safety.

Every [protocol deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations) must be assessed, addressed and documented. Report every deviation to the sponsor, whatever its tier. Major and critical deviations also go to the REB by its deadline. Whatever their tier, report these deviations to the REB too:
- A sponsor-approved waiver of an eligibility criterion.
- A change to the approved consent process, such as an improper translation or not using the current consent form.
- A deviation that leads to a serious adverse event.
- Any other deviation that the principal investigator judges jeopardizes participants' safety, the efficacy of the research or data integrity, or could otherwise affect participants' rights, safety or well-being.

Other minor deviations may be collected and submitted to the REB at its annual continuing review.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps that only some natural health product trials take</div>

In a study of a marketed product, [report adverse reactions to the sponsor or the product's market authorization holder](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#assess-and-report-adverse-events), so that the sponsor or the holder can report them to Health Canada.

If a participant in an interventional study becomes pregnant, [collect and follow the exposure in utero reports](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#track-a-pregnancy-during-a-trial) to learn the outcome, even without an adverse event.

### Keep approvals and the team current

Amendments are routine, and happen in most studies, sometimes several times. While an amendment is under review, the study continues on the documents the REB has already approved.

Before you implement an [amendment](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#wait-for-every-approval-before-you-implement-the-amendment), wait for every approval it needs. A change needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants may be made before the REB's approval, and must be reported to the REB immediately.

The principal investigator initials and dates each [new version of the Investigator's Brochure](/kb/site-activation/protocol-documents#review-each-new-version-of-the-ib) before it is filed. Inform the REB of each new version before it is implemented. [Re-consent each affected participant](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#re-consent-participants-on-an-amended-consent-form) on an amended consent form.

The REB sets how long its approval lasts. [Request the continuing review in Nagano](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid) before the approval expires. If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk.

The Task Delegation Log [should be kept up to date](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#keep-the-task-delegation-log-up-to-date) throughout the study. A [change of principal investigator](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#change-the-principal-investigator) is an amendment that the REB must review and approve before it takes effect.

[Read revised SOPs on time](/training/compliance-requirements#read-revised-sops). A Health Canada inspector may compare your training records with SOP effective dates and record a lapse in SOP knowledge as a finding in their inspection report.

### Prepare for monitoring, audits and inspections

Three kinds of oversight check a study while it runs and after it ends. The sponsor monitors it, the sponsor or the RI-MUHC's [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) department may audit it, and Health Canada may inspect a study it regulates. All three rely on the study's records.

The sponsor or sponsor-investigator monitors the study through a monitor dedicated to it. [Prepare for each monitoring visit](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#prepare-for-a-monitoring-visit), and respond in writing to each observation in the monitoring letter. [Notify the REB of any planned audit or inspection](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#tell-the-reb-about-audits-and-inspections) of the study.

## Close the trial

A study doesn't end with the last participant's visit. Close-out is a sequence of steps, not a single event.

Consider starting to prepare for the [close-out visit](/kb/close-out/close-out#preparing-for-the-close-out-visit) well before the last participant's last visit. Every unit of the investigational product must be fully accounted for in the accountability log, and the supplies are [returned or destroyed](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving) as arranged with the sponsor or sponsor-investigator.

The sponsor-investigator or the principal investigator should submit the Closure of Research Project form (F10) in Nagano once [the conditions for closure](/kb/close-out/close-out#notifying-the-reb-and-health-canada) are met. The Task Delegation Log must be complete before the form can be submitted. Registering a trial and making its results public are separate obligations. A trial funded by CIHR, and a trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, each have [rules or guidance for making the results public](/kb/close-out/publications#make-the-results-public).

The study is closed when the REB acknowledges its closure form in Nagano. Then [archive the records](#archive-the-records).

<div className="hub-steps">Steps that only some natural health product trials take</div>

If the sponsor stops the trial, at the site or in its entirety, the qualified investigator must [inform the participants and the REB immediately](/kb/close-out/close-out#if-a-drug-or-natural-health-product-trial-stops-early), give them the reasons, and advise them in writing of any potential risks to the health of participants or others.

## Archive the records

Archiving is more than storing files. Documents must be organized, indexed and available to authorized people for an inspection or an audit, which may come years after a study closes.

The principal investigator or a delegate should inform the archiving service of [the required retention period](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving). The essential study documents must remain preserved, organized and accessible for inspection for the full retention period. The obligation to report safety events [continues after close-out](/kb/close-out/close-out#obligations-that-continue-after-close-out), through the follow-up period that the protocol and the consent form define.
