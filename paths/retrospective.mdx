<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Retrospective and biobank studies (Level V)</span></div>

Some studies use only data or samples that already exist. They review patients' charts, analyze data already held in health records, the [MUHC Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca), registries or other databases, or use samples already held in a biobank. This is retrospective research.

A retrospective study still needs the approval of the Research Ethics Board and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> before any research activity begins. Two questions shape most of its steps: whether the study needs participants' consent, and how the team will get access to the data.

If your study will collect new data or samples from participants, or test a drug, a medical device or a natural health product, other steps apply. The [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help you place a study that doesn't fit.

## Confirm that the project is research

Some chart reviews are quality improvement projects, which don't need review by the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> when [they are used only for assessment, management or improvement](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html). Many are health records research, which the REB reviews. Answer the [screening questions](/is-it-research#screening-questions) before you plan the study.

A quality improvement project that needs patients' health information without their consent still requires a privacy impact assessment (EFVP). Start it by writing to [efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca). A quality improvement project that needs hospital resources must also obtain authorization from the authorities responsible for them. The [MUHC Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) provides data for research projects, not quality improvement projects.

## Plan the data and the analysis

Before you write the protocol, decide which data sources the study needs and how identifiable the data will be. These two choices decide whether the study needs consent, and whether it needs an EFVP. [Data count as anonymized only if no key exists](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#know-how-identifiable-your-data-are) that links them back to people.

To check a study's feasibility before you request real patient data, active RI-MUHC staff and members with valid MUHC credentials can explore synthetic MUHC patient data in [MDClone](/kb/design-support/patient-data#explore-synthetic-data-in-mdclone), which needs no REB approval. Projects that need real patient-level data for validation, publication or detailed statistical analyses should request them from the [Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca).

The [Data Warehouse team](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) recommends a consultation before you finalize the protocol and submit it in Nagano, to define the patient selection criteria, the sample size and the variables. Request one through the [RI-MUHC Data Warehouse Request Center](https://rimuhc.atlassian.net/servicedesk/customer/user/login?destination=portals).

For the statistical plan, the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, can consult the [Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)](/kb/design-support/bcu). For a REDCap database to hold the extracted data, consult [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/design-support/cord). The [Centre for Applied Ethics](https://muhc.ca/cae) publishes a [protocol template for health records research](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms).

## Check credentials and training

Before you submit, the principal investigator must hold Human Research Privileges, Researcher Status or a written institutional authorization. [Which credential applies](/training/compliance-requirements#know-which-credential-you-need) depends on whether the study requires medical oversight.

Everyone on the team should hold [Level V training or a higher level](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) before signing the study's <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>. Renew any credential or training that is [close to expiry](/training/compliance-requirements#validity-and-the-90-day-rule) before you submit. Level V training is the course on the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs), the SOP Reader and Assessment. A sponsor or a funder may require more training.

A Sub-Investigator [should be named as the principal investigator's backup](/sops/cr-002#5-1-2). The backup must hold the same Privileges or Researcher Status and institutional training as the principal investigator, or better. A fellow or a resident cannot be the backup, because neither is eligible for Privileges or Researcher Status.

Before the study starts, its study-specific training needs must be assessed. All training related to the qualification of the study's team members [should be documented](/training/compliance-requirements). Training that gives no certificate or dated printout, such as reading the protocol on your own, must be recorded on a Training Log.

Keep the training records in the investigator site file or in each person's training records. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-003#5-4) require the documentation of study-specific training to include the title and content of the training, such as the learning objectives, the training method, who trained, who attended and when the training took place.

## Decide whether the study needs consent

[If it is feasible, participants must give informed consent](/sops/cr-008#5-4), even in a retrospective study. The REB reviews and approves the study's consent requirement.

The REB may approve the use of identifiable information without consent only if the researcher satisfies all of the conditions in the federal research agencies' [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html), including that seeking consent is impossible or impracticable. TCPS 2 sets [the same kind of conditions for identifiable samples](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter12-chapitre12.html) already held in a biobank.

Research that relies only on the secondary use of non-identifiable information or samples needs REB review, but not participants' consent. It is up to the researcher to show the REB that the information or samples can be considered non-identifiable for all practical purposes. Coded data can identify people when the researcher has access to the key that links the codes to names.

Anonymous information and samples never had identifiers associated with them, such as the answers to an anonymous survey. [TCPS 2 requires no REB review](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html) for research that relies only on their secondary use, as long as linking the data, recording them or disseminating the results generates no identifiable information.

The MUHC REB counts biological materials collected as part of health care as never anonymous. At the MUHC, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs still require REB approval for a retrospective study](/sops/cr-008#5-4-1). When in doubt, researchers should consult the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca).

Quebec's [Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:22) allows a part of the body removed from a person as part of their care to be used for research with the person's consent, or that of the person qualified to consent for them. If the person has died, consent comes from the person who could have consented to their care. Before you plan to use such samples without consent, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) how this applies to your study.

A study that uses identifiable health information without consent also needs an approved privacy impact assessment, the <Tooltip tip="The privacy impact assessment that Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information requires before a study accesses identifiable health information without consent, including for pre-screening." headline="EFVP (privacy impact assessment)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#efvp">évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP)</Tooltip>, before it can receive MUHC Authorization, which includes access to the records. [Whether a study needs an EFVP](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed) depends on how it uses the information and where the information goes. If the REB decides that the study needs consent, follow the [consent steps](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-before-any-study-procedure).

A biobank may have its own policies on access to its samples, and [researchers should be aware of them](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter12-chapitre12.html). If the REB lets you use identifiable samples without consent, comply with any known preferences the participants expressed about the use of their samples.

## Submit the study in Nagano

Submit the study in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> with the F11 form. [Choose the project type](/kb/submission/preparing#choose-the-project-type) when you create the project, because the type can't be changed once the project has its Nagano number. Retrospective health records research uses the F11-HRR. A project that only uses an existing bank or registry is non-interventional research, and uses the F11-NIR.

[Submit the study protocol](/sops/cr-007#5-2-5) with the F11, even for retrospective health records research.

A research agreement is needed when the study receives or sends human data or samples, or works with collaborators outside the RI-MUHC, McGill University researchers included, and in [the other cases the RI-MUHC's SOPs list](/sops/cr-007#5-4-6).

Upload the agreement in Nagano and in My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal, which starts the review by the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca). The agreement must be reviewed and fully signed before MUHC Authorization. For a study at more than one public institution in Quebec, follow [Multi-Centric Studies in Quebec](/kb/design/multi-centric).

If the study will use identifiable health information without consent, the principal investigator completes the EFVP in French and signs it. Submit it as form F18, with the F11. Answer the F11's [questions on data access](/kb/submission/preparing#answer-the-f11-questions-on-services-and-data-access) accurately. A wrong answer re-opens the F11, and the correction triggers an EFVP review.

## Wait for REB approval and MUHC Authorization

The REB reviews the study and decides on any waiver of consent. The MUHC's [Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) reviews and approves the EFVP. The <Tooltip tip="The person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research: the Director of Innovation, Performance and Quality, who issues MUHC Authorization for each study." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">personne mandatée</Tooltip> gives MUHC Authorization only after the EFVP is approved. Questions about MUHC Authorization go to the [Personne mandatée's office](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca).

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

While the study is under review, answer the REB's requests within [the REB's deadline for replies](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#answer-the-reb-within-three-months). When access without consent is authorized, the principal investigator [signs a written agreement with the MUHC](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent) that sets how the information may be used.

File every REB submission, approval and piece of correspondence in the study's [investigator site file](/kb/site-activation/isf).

## Obtain and protect the data

The [Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) sets [different requirements](/kb/design-support/patient-data#requirements-by-data-type) for aggregate, anonymized, de-identified and identifiable data before it releases them. Submit the data request through the Request Center once the study has REB approval and MUHC Authorization. Before Medical Records Services gives you access to patients' medical records, sign the [MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement](/kb/governance/privacy#keep-participant-information-confidential) and send it to them. Being able to see a record as a caregiver does not allow you to use it for research.

Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.

Where you keep the data, who may receive them and how you send them depend on [how identifiable they are](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#apply-the-controls-for-each-kind-of-data). Keep the key that links coded data back to participants apart from the data. [Linking datasets needs the REB's approval](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html) before you do it.

Before data leave the MUHC for another site, or arrive from one, [a data sharing or transfer agreement](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#put-an-agreement-in-place-before-data-leave-or-arrive) must be in place.

If personal information is collected, used or disclosed without authorization, [report it to the REB in Nagano](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person) as soon as you become aware of it. Information that is lost or stolen, sent to the wrong person, copied, changed or disposed of without authorization, or used in a way the REB did not approve counts too.

The REB counts any re-identification of a participant as a serious adverse event. Report it to the REB, whether it happened at the MUHC or elsewhere. Remove all participant identifiers from every report to the REB.

## Keep REB approval current

Before you change the extraction plan, such as new variables, a wider population or a new data source, submit an [amendment](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments) and wait for its approval, including the approval of any MUHC review the change affects. If your access was authorized without consent, your authorization and your agreement with the MUHC cover only the information and the linkages described in your request. Ask the [Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you add variables or data sources.

Document every [deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations) and report it as its classification requires. Access to data outside the approved scope is also a privacy breach. [Report it to the REB](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person) as soon as you become aware of it.

The REB sets how long its approval lasts. [Request the continuing review in Nagano](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid) before the approval expires. If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk.

## Close the study

When the study ends, notify the REB and the personne mandatée with the closure form (F10) in Nagano. If access was authorized without consent, destroy the information at the end of the period your agreement with the MUHC sets, and [tell the MUHC that you have](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent). Archive the study's documents, and the data on individuals, in [a secure, institutionally approved environment](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#archive-the-data-when-the-study-ends) for [the retention period set by the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).
