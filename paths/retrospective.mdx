<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Retrospective and biobank studies (Level V)</span></div>

You want to answer a research question from information or samples that already exist. You may plan to review patients' charts, to analyze data already held in health records, the [MUHC Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca), registries or other databases, or to use samples already held in a biobank. A study that uses only data that already exist, or only samples already held in a biobank, is at the RI-MUHC's [training Level V](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs), the level for the lowest-risk studies.

A study of records, data or samples already held in a biobank does no procedures on participants for the study. It has no study visits and no screening procedures. The work happens in the records, the databases or the biobank.

The people whose information or samples the study uses are still its participants. Two questions shape most of the steps that follow: whether the study needs their consent, and how the team will get access to the data or the samples.

A retrospective study still needs the approval of the Research Ethics Board and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> before any research activity begins, as every study does. The <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip>, who leads the study at the MUHC, is responsible for conducting it. In [a project that a trainee runs](/roles/trainee#run-your-own-project-under-a-supervisor), the supervisor is the principal investigator.

Retrospective studies have [fewer stages](/kb/foundations/study-lifecycle) than most clinical studies: feasibility, submission and ethics review, conduct, closing the study in Nagano, and archiving.

If the study will interview or survey participants about past events, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) which level applies. If the study will collect new data or samples from participants in another way, or test a drug, a medical device or a natural health product, other steps apply.

Some studies [develop or validate software on existing data or samples](/kb/design/software-medical-devices#develop-or-validate-software-on-existing-data-or-samples), such as an algorithm. For such a study, ask Health Canada's [Investigational Testing division](mailto:it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca) whether it needs an investigational testing authorization (ITA), and ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) which training level applies.

A prospective study that Health Canada does not regulate and that involves no surgery, such as a study that follows participants for outcomes or events after they are identified, follows [Prospective Studies Not Regulated by Health Canada](/paths/level-iv). [Types of Study](/paths) explains each kind of study, and the Research Facilitator can help you place yours.

## Confirm that the project is research

Some chart reviews are quality improvement projects, which don't need review by the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> when [they are used only for assessment, management or improvement](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html). Many are health records research, which the REB reviews. Answer the [screening questions](/is-it-research#screening-questions) before you plan the study.

A quality improvement project that needs patients' health information without their consent still requires a privacy impact assessment (EFVP). Start it by writing to [efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca). A quality improvement project that needs hospital resources must also obtain authorization from the authorities responsible for them. The [MUHC Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) provides data for research projects, not quality improvement projects.

## Plan the data and the analysis

Before you write the protocol, decide which data sources the study needs and how identifiable the data will be. These two choices shape whether the study needs consent, and whether it needs an EFVP. [Data count as anonymized only if no key exists](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#know-how-identifiable-your-data-are) that links them back to people.

For the statistical plan, the principal investigator can consult the [Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)](/kb/design-support/bcu). For a REDCap database to hold the extracted data, consult [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/design-support/cord). The [Centre for Applied Ethics](https://muhc.ca/cae) publishes a [protocol template for health records research](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms).

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level V studies take</div>

To check a study's feasibility before you request real patient data, active RI-MUHC staff and members with valid MUHC credentials can explore synthetic MUHC patient data in [MDClone](/kb/design-support/patient-data#explore-synthetic-data-in-mdclone), which needs no REB approval. Projects that need real patient-level data for validation, publication or detailed statistical analyses should request them from the [Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca).

If the study will use data from the Data Warehouse, the [Data Warehouse team](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) recommends a consultation before you finalize the protocol and submit it in Nagano, to define the patient selection criteria, the sample size and the variables. Request one through the [RI-MUHC Data Warehouse Request Center](https://rimuhc.atlassian.net/servicedesk/customer/user/login?destination=portals).

If participants' information will leave Quebec, or the study acquires, develops or alters technology to handle it, the study [may need an EFVP](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed). Information leaving Quebec is the trigger teams most often miss. Consider asking [efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) before the protocol is final.

To use Quebec's provincial administrative databases, such as those of the Régie de l'assurance maladie du Québec (RAMQ) and MED-ÉCHO, follow [Real-World Evidence and Pragmatic Trials](/kb/design/real-world-evidence#choose-a-quebec-data-source).

Research that uses data or biological materials identifiable as coming from a First Nations, Inuit or Métis community or People follows [Indigenous Research Ethics](/kb/recruitment/indigenous-research-ethics). The [joint review with the Cree Board of Health and Social Services of James Bay (CBHSSJB)](/kb/recruitment/indigenous-research-ethics#check-whether-the-procedure-applies) also covers a study that uses, as a variable at any stage of the research, a catchment area that includes Eeyou Istchee, Quebec's health and social services region 18, identified for example by postal code.

## Check credentials and training

Leading a study takes a credential as well as training. Before you submit, the principal investigator must hold Human Research Privileges, Researcher Status or a written institutional authorization. [Which credential applies](/training/compliance-requirements#know-which-credential-you-need) depends on whether the study requires medical oversight.

Everyone on the team should hold [Level V training or a higher level](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) before signing the study's <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>. Renew any credential or training that is [close to expiry](/training/compliance-requirements#validity-and-the-90-day-rule) before you submit. Level V training is the course on the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs), the SOP Reader and Assessment. Download the certificate issued when you pass the assessment, and file it with your study training records. A sponsor or a funder may require more training.

Teams sometimes assume that the check by [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) covers everyone. During the institutional feasibility review, QA checks that each investigator who submits the study holds Privileges or Researcher Status and the level of institutional training the study requires.

A Sub-Investigator [should be named as the principal investigator's backup](/sops/cr-002#5-1-2). The backup must hold the same Privileges or Researcher Status and institutional training as the principal investigator, or better. A fellow, a resident or a medical student cannot be the backup, because none of them is eligible for Privileges or Researcher Status.

Before the study starts, its study-specific training needs must be assessed. All training related to the qualification of the study's team members should be documented. Training that gives no certificate or dated printout, such as reading the protocol on your own, must be recorded on a Training Log.

Keep the training records in the investigator site file or in each person's training records. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-003#5-4) require the documentation of study-specific training to include the title and content of the training, such as the learning objectives, the training method, who trained, who attended and when the training took place.

## Decide whether the study needs consent

[If it is feasible, participants must give informed consent](/sops/cr-008#5-4), even in a retrospective study. The REB reviews and approves the study's consent requirement. What the REB can approve depends on whether the information or samples can identify anyone, and on whether seeking consent is possible.

Research that relies only on the secondary use of non-identifiable information or samples needs REB review, but not participants' consent. It is up to the researcher to show the REB that the information or samples can be considered non-identifiable for all practical purposes. Coded data can identify people when the researcher has access to the key that links the codes to names.

Anonymous information and samples never had identifiers associated with them, and the risk of identifying anyone from them is low or very low. The answers to an anonymous survey are anonymous information. [The federal research agencies' *Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2) requires no REB review](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html) for research that relies only on their secondary use, as long as linking the data, recording them or disseminating the results generates no identifiable information.

The MUHC REB counts biological materials collected as part of health care as never anonymous. At the MUHC, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs still require REB approval for a retrospective study](/sops/cr-008#5-4-1). If you are in doubt about whether TCPS 2 applies to your project, seek the opinion of the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca). The REB makes the final decision on exemption from research ethics review.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level V studies take</div>

If the REB decides that the study needs consent, follow the [consent steps](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-before-any-study-procedure).

The REB may approve the use of identifiable information without consent only if the researcher satisfies all of the conditions in [TCPS 2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html), including that seeking consent is impossible or impracticable. TCPS 2 sets [the same kind of conditions for the secondary use of identifiable samples](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter12-chapitre12.html), such as samples already held in a biobank.

Quebec's [Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:22) allows a part of the body removed from a person as part of their care to be used for research with the person's consent, or that of the person qualified to consent for them. If the person has died, consent comes from the person who could have consented to their care.

If you are in doubt about how this applies to your study, you can seek the opinion of the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you plan to use such samples without consent.

A study that uses identifiable health information without consent also needs an approved privacy impact assessment, the <Tooltip tip="The privacy impact assessment, or évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée, that Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information requires with a researcher's request for access to health and social services information without consent, including access before consent for pre-screening." headline="EFVP (privacy impact assessment)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#efvp">évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP)</Tooltip>, before it can receive MUHC Authorization, which includes access to the records. [Whether a study needs an EFVP](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed) depends on how it uses the information and where the information goes.

After the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) approves the use of identifiable information or samples without consent, a study may need to contact some of the people concerned, for more information or materials or for reasons related to their welfare. [Seek the REB's approval of the plan for making contact](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent) before you contact anyone. Any new collection of data or materials from the people you contact needs their consent.

A biobank may have its own policies on access to its samples, and [researchers should be aware of them](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter12-chapitre12.html). If the REB lets you use identifiable samples without consent, comply with any known preferences the participants expressed about the use of their samples.

## Submit the study in Nagano

Every study at the MUHC is submitted and managed in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>, from the first submission to the study completion report. Incomplete submissions are a common cause of delay.

Submit the study with the F11 form. [Choose the project type](/kb/submission/preparing#choose-the-project-type) when you create the project, because the type can't be changed after the project is created. Retrospective health records research uses the F11-HRR. [Submit the study protocol](/sops/cr-007#5-2-5) with the F11, even for retrospective health records research.

Answer the F11's [questions on data access](/kb/submission/preparing#answer-the-f11-questions-on-services-and-data-access) accurately. A wrong answer re-opens the F11, and the correction triggers an EFVP review.

A research agreement is needed when the study receives or sends human data or samples, or works with collaborators outside the RI-MUHC, McGill University researchers included, and in [the other cases the RI-MUHC's SOPs list](/sops/cr-007#5-4-6). ## Obtain and protect the data

Once the study is authorized, the team gets access to the records, the data or the samples, and protects them from then until they are archived.

Before Medical Records Services gives you access to patients' medical records, sign the [MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement](/kb/governance/privacy#keep-participant-information-confidential) and send it to them. Being able to see a record as a caregiver does not allow you to use it for research.

Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.

Where you keep the data, who may receive them and how you send them depend on [how identifiable they are](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#apply-the-controls-for-each-kind-of-data). Keep the key that links coded data back to participants apart from the data. [Linking datasets needs the REB's approval](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html) before you do it.

If personal information is collected, used or disclosed without authorization, [report it to the REB in Nagano](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person) as soon as you become aware of it. Information that is lost or stolen, sent to the wrong person, copied, changed or disposed of without authorization, or used in a way the REB did not approve counts too. As applicable, also report the breach to the appropriate organizational officials.

The REB counts any re-identification of a participant as a serious adverse event. [Report it to the REB](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#assess-and-report-adverse-events), whether it happened at the MUHC or elsewhere. Remove all participant identifiers from every report to the REB.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level V studies take</div>

The [Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) sets [different requirements](/kb/design-support/patient-data#requirements-by-data-type) for aggregate, anonymized, de-identified and identifiable data before it releases them. Aggregate data can be requested at any time, under the Data Warehouse's [*Guidelines on Aggregate Data Requests*](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:360). For other data, submit the data request through the Request Center once the study has REB approval and MUHC Authorization.

Before data leave the MUHC for another site, or arrive from one, [a data sharing or transfer agreement](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#put-an-agreement-in-place-before-data-leave-or-arrive) must be in place.

If your access was authorized without consent, make sure that anyone you retain to help with the project, such as an analyst or a firm, meets [the obligations of your agreement with the MUHC](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent) too.

When access was authorized without consent, your agreement with the MUHC also requires you to inform the MUHC and Quebec's access-to-information commission, the [Commission d'accès à l'information](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/), without delay of:
- Any breach of the agreement's conditions.
- Any failure to follow its security measures.
- Any event that could breach the information's confidentiality.

## Keep REB approval current

Studies change as they run, and amendments are routine. Before you change the extraction plan, such as new variables, a wider population or a new data source, submit an [amendment](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments) and wait for its approval, including the approval of any MUHC review the change affects.

Document every [deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations) and report it as its classification requires. Access to data outside the approved scope is also a privacy breach. [Report it to the REB](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person) as soon as you become aware of it.

The REB sets how long its approval lasts. Request the continuing review in Nagano by [the deadline the RI-MUHC's SOPs set before the approval expires](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid). If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level V studies take</div>

If your access was authorized without consent, your authorization and your agreement with the MUHC cover only the information and the linkages described in your request. Consider asking the [Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you add variables or data sources.

## Close the study

When the study ends, notify the REB and the personne mandatée with the closure form (F10) in Nagano. [The study is closed](/kb/close-out/close-out) when the REB acknowledges the form. Archive the study's documents, and the data on individuals, in [a secure, institutionally approved environment](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#archive-the-data-when-the-study-ends) for [the retention period set by the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).

Researchers shall [make their results public](/kb/close-out/publications), including results that don't support the research hypotheses, in a timely manner and without undue restriction.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level V studies take</div>

If access was authorized without consent, destroy the information at the end of the period your agreement with the MUHC sets, and [tell the MUHC that you have](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent).
