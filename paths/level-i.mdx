<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span></div>

You want to test a drug in people. A company may have asked the RI-MUHC to take part in a trial of a product it is developing. Or the question may be your own, such as a drug approved for one indication that you want to test in another population, and you would run the trial yourself. The drug can also be a biologic, a gene or cell therapy, a blood product, a vaccine or a radiopharmaceutical.

Drug studies are at the RI-MUHC's [training Level I](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs), the highest risk level. Health Canada regulates drug trials, and many need its authorization before they start. The investigational drug is sent to the MUHC Pharmacy, which stores it and dispenses it to participants, so the pharmacy takes part from submission to close-out. Every serious adverse event in a participant is reported to the sponsor, which reports some of them to Health Canada.

A drug study goes through the stages every clinical study does: feasibility, design and planning, submission and review, site activation, recruitment, conduct, close-out and archiving. Before any research activity begins, it needs the approval of the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, as every study does.

Several people share the work. The sponsor [takes responsibility for initiating, managing or financing the trial](/paths#who-sponsors-the-study). In an industry-sponsored trial, a company is the sponsor. The company develops the protocol, applies to Health Canada where the study needs it, runs the monitoring and is responsible for the safety reporting and the integrity of the data.

An investigator who both initiates and conducts a clinical trial is its <Tooltip tip="An individual who both initiates and conducts, alone or with others, a clinical trial, and under whose immediate direction the investigational product is administered to, dispensed to or used by a participant." headline="Sponsor-Investigator" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sponsor-investigator">sponsor-investigator</Tooltip>, with the obligations of both a sponsor and an investigator. When you sponsor the trial yourself, no company holds the protocol above you, and you do all of that work.

Health Canada's rules call the person responsible to the sponsor for the trial at the site the <Tooltip tip="The person responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a drug or natural health product trial at a clinical trial site, who is entitled to provide health care under the laws of the province where the site is located." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">qualified investigator</Tooltip>. Health Canada requires the qualified investigator to be a physician, or a physician or a dentist for a drug used for dental purposes only. The principal investigator leads the study at the MUHC, and the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) often join the two as "QI/PI".

The research team, such as coordinators and nurses, does the tasks the principal investigator delegates on the <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>, and the principal investigator keeps the overall responsibility for them.

Researchers sometimes use one abbreviation for two different things. On the Hub, the <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip> is only the application to Health Canada. The contract with the sponsor is the clinical trial agreement, always written out.

If you can't tell whether your study is a drug study, [Types of Study](/paths) explains each type of study, and the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help you place it.

## Place the study

Health Canada authorizes the sale or import of a drug for a clinical trial. If the trial needs that authorization, the sponsor applies for it, and the <Tooltip tip="The person whom an establishment of Quebec's health network formally mandates to authorize the research conducted under its auspices." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> waits for it before issuing MUHC Authorization.

A drug trial needs Health Canada's authorization through a CTA, unless the trial uses a marketed drug within the conditions of its notice of compliance or its drug identification number. A basic clinical research study of a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical needs the Minister's authorization under Division 3 of the Food and Drug Regulations instead of a CTA. [Check which applies to your study](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study).

[Division 5 does not apply to the radiopharmaceutical in a basic clinical research study under Division 3](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.03.302.html). Division 3's sections on these studies set their own rules, including on the research ethics board, good clinical practices, adverse reaction reporting and records.

If a drug trial uses a Class II, III or IV medical device that is not licensed in Canada, an application for Health Canada's <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">investigational testing authorization (ITA)</Tooltip> must be filed for the device, separately from the CTA. Both should be authorized before the trial starts in Canada. The device's manufacturer must sign the ITA application.

A trial of a marketed drug within its approved conditions is still conducted in accordance with Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations. The sections of the Hub tagged <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span> apply to it. The sections tagged <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span> apply to a trial that needs Health Canada's authorization. [Studies Regulated by Health Canada](/paths/hc-regulated) lists every section with either tag, by stage of the study.

## Plan an industry-sponsored trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Industry-sponsored studies</span></div>

In a company's trial, the site's research team conducts the study here. The team follows the sponsor's protocol, and its members keep their own professional and institutional obligations.

Most industry-sponsored trials reach the RI-MUHC through a company, or a contract research organization working for it, that has already picked the RI-MUHC as a possible site. [Answer the feasibility questionnaire](/kb/planning/sponsored-trials#answer-the-feasibility-questionnaire) the sponsor sends, and prepare for its [site feasibility visit](/kb/planning/feasibility#the-site-feasibility-visit). If you are considering having the [Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) run your study, contact it before you answer the questionnaire.

Before the team receives confidential study documents, such as the protocol, a [confidentiality agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#know-when-the-study-needs-an-agreement) between the sponsor, the institution and the investigator must be signed and dated. The [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) reviews and approves it before it is signed. The investigator must then submit the signed and dated agreement in Nagano.

The sponsor writes and owns the protocol, provides the <Tooltip tip="A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip> and applies to Health Canada for the trial's authorization where the trial needs it. The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, [negotiates the budget with the sponsor](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#negotiate-the-budget-with-an-industry-sponsor) while the Research Agreements Office negotiates the [clinical trial agreement's clauses](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#read-the-clauses-of-a-clinical-trial-agreement).

The RI-MUHC is a partner institution of CATALIS Québec's [FAST TRACK Evaluation Service](/kb/design/multi-centric#catalis-fast-track) for industry-sponsored clinical trials. Ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) whether your study is eligible.

## Plan a trial you sponsor yourself

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

When the trial is yours, no contract research organization monitors it and no company's regulatory affairs department prepares Health Canada's applications. Investigators sometimes discover the full scope of the sponsor's obligations only once they are committed to a study, often after a grant has been awarded.

Before you commit to a trial you initiate yourself, weigh [the sponsor's obligations](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#what-the-two-roles-mean) that you will hold as well as the investigator's. None of the sponsor's obligations is reduced or waived because the sponsor is also the investigator. Consider writing to [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) when you start planning the trial.

[Develop the protocol and the Investigator's Brochure](/kb/planning/iit-startup#1-develop-the-protocol-and-the-investigator-brochure). For the RI-MUHC's insurance to cover the trial, the RI-MUHC should be [named as its sponsor](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#cover-a-study-you-sponsor-yourself) on the agreement, on regulatory documents and on ClinicalTrials.gov. The principal investigator [must ensure that adequate liability insurance coverage is held](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#meet-the-insurance-duties-of-the-principal-investigator), including the institutional coverage that applies to research activities. The institutional feasibility review analyzes the liability insurance coverage that applies to the project.

Develop a risk-based monitoring plan and arrange an [independent monitor](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#monitoring-independence). You cannot monitor your own study.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs have the sponsor-investigator ensure that all electronic software used in the study has been validated](/kb/planning/iit-startup#7-set-up-the-data-management) and complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations. Keep the [trial master file](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#tmf-and-isf) as well as the investigator site file.

Give the REB an acceptable [plan for monitoring safety, efficacy or effectiveness (where feasible) and validity](/kb/planning/iit-startup#plan-the-safety-monitoring). For each site, keep the qualified investigator's undertaking, signed and dated before they start their responsibilities in the trial. [Ensure that the drug bears a label](/kb/planning/iit-startup#keep-each-undertaking-and-label-the-drug) with the information the Food and Drug Regulations list, in both official languages.

## Check that the site can carry out the trial

Feasibility is the earliest point at which serious problems can be caught, before money and time are committed. A protocol submitted without a realistic feasibility assessment often raises questions during review that delay authorization.

Once you have the protocol, assess its feasibility to find out what the site will need. Secure, adequate and temperature-controlled space for storing the investigational drug [should be identified](/kb/planning/feasibility#before-the-site-feasibility-visit). A qualified person responsible for managing it, and procedures for its destruction where required, must be available.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps that only some drug trials take</div>

For a radiopharmaceutical study, consider contacting the MUHC Research Pharmacy [early in planning](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products#radiopharmaceuticals).

## Prepare the team, the protocol and the consent form

The protocol is the study's master plan, and everything the site does during the study should be traceable to a specific protocol requirement. The consent form is drafted at this stage, not as a last task before submission. During the institutional feasibility review, [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) checks that each investigator who submits the study holds Privileges or Researcher Status and the level of institutional training the study requires.

### Check credentials and training

Teams sometimes assume that Quality Assurance's check covers everyone. During the study, the principal investigator must ensure that the people delegated tasks keep the study's level of institutional training.

Before you submit, the principal investigator must hold Human Research Privileges, Researcher Status or a written institutional authorization. [Privileges are required](/kb/submission/preparing#name-the-principal-investigator) to lead a study that Health Canada regulates with an investigational drug. At the MUHC, [the credential requirements](/training/compliance-requirements#know-which-credential-you-need) apply to drug trials too, including where Health Canada's interim policy lets a person who is not a physician or dentist act as the qualified investigator.

Everyone on the study should hold [Level I training](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) before signing its Task Delegation Log. Level I training is the SOP Reader and Assessment for Level I, good clinical practice (GCP) training on guideline E6 of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), and [Health Canada Division 5 training](/training/external-certifications#health-canada-division-5). Renew any credential or training that is [close to expiry](/training/compliance-requirements#validity-and-the-90-day-rule) before you submit.

## Submit the study in Nagano

Every submission goes through Nagano. The F11 starts the institutional feasibility review at the same time as the REB's review, and your answers on pharmacy, nursing and laboratory services start those services' reviews. Incomplete submissions are a common cause of delay.

Create the project in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> as a clinical trial, which uses the F11-H form. [Choose the project type with care](/kb/submission/preparing#choose-the-project-type), because the type can't be changed once the project has its Nagano number. Upload [the documents the RI-MUHC's SOPs list](/kb/submission/preparing#prepare-the-documents) with the F11, including the current Investigator's Brochure, or the product monograph for a phase IV trial, and Health Canada's letter for a trial that needs one.

All drugs must be managed by the pharmacy. In the Nagano submission, the investigator must answer the questions that start [the pharmacy's feasibility review](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products#know-what-the-pharmacy-does-and-what-the-principal-investigator-keeps). A pharmacy agreement then comes to the investigator for signature.

Get the approval of [the head of each department the study affects](/kb/submission/preparing#get-approvals-start-the-agreements-and-declare-conflicts) before you submit. [Upload every research agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#submit-the-agreement-in-nagano-and-my-contracts) both to Nagano and to My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal. Teams sometimes declare an agreement in Nagano and never make the request in My Contracts. The upload to My Contracts starts the review by the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).

<div className="hub-steps">Steps that only some drug trials take</div>

If a nurse is to dispense the drug directly to participants, [request the approval](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products#ask-for-a-nurse-to-dispense-the-product) in Nagano at submission.

A study funded wholly or partly by private enterprise is billed the [review and authorization fees](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#budget-the-reb-and-authorization-fees) when the private funding exceeds the exemption amount. For a trial at more than one public institution in Quebec, follow [Multi-Centric Studies in Quebec](/kb/design/multi-centric). For a trial with sites outside Quebec, follow [International and Cross-Border Studies](/kb/design/cross-border-studies).

## Wait for every approval

Study teams sometimes take the REB's approval for permission to start, and recruit before the study is authorized.

[Answer the REB's requests within its deadline](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#answer-the-reb-within-three-months). Researchers should never sign a binding contract or agreement before the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) has reviewed it. [Every agreement must be fully executed before MUHC Authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview#institutional-feasibility-review).

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

Once the Personne mandatée issues MUHC Authorization, [activate the site](#activate-the-site).

## Activate the site

Once the study is authorized, it is not yet ready to run. Site activation covers the work between authorization and the first participant: the site initiation visit, the Task Delegation Log, the investigator site file and logistics such as the pharmacy.

Inform the MUHC Pharmacy and invite it to the [site initiation visit](/kb/site-activation/site-setup#prepare-for-the-site-initiation-visit), for training on the protocol and the drug.

The principal investigator or a delegate [should create](/kb/site-activation/isf#set-up-the-isf) the <Tooltip tip="The qualified or principal investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines, so that the conduct and integrity of the study and the safety of participants at the site can be verified." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file (ISF)</Tooltip> after the sponsor or sponsor-investigator first makes contact, or as soon as a protocol outline is available, and keep it current. [File the printed REB approval letter](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#check-and-file-the-approval-letter) in the ISF. Teams sometimes keep only the electronic copy. The RI-MUHC's SOPs require the printed copy until a document management system that complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations is in place.

Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.

Registering a trial publicly before it recruits helps prevent selective publication of results. [The sponsor or sponsor-investigator registers the trial](/kb/close-out/publications#trial-registration) in a public registry before the first participant is recruited.

[Do not begin the study](/kb/site-activation/site-setup#wait-for-the-go-letter-before-you-enrol) until you have the REB's written approval, MUHC Authorization and the sponsor's go letter, if it issues one. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email.

Do not ask anyone to consent before you have the REB's written approval and MUHC Authorization, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator has held the site initiation visit, and the go letter has arrived, if the sponsor issues one. Then [recruit, consent and enrol participants](#recruit-consent-and-enrol-participants).

A sponsor-investigator who registers on ClinicalTrials.gov uses the MUHC's organization account. To get access, write to [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca). A trial funded by the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR) also meets [CIHR's conditions](/kb/close-out/publications#cihr-funded-trials).

## Recruit, consent and enrol participants

Recruitment follows set rules, and consent is a process, not only a signature. Recruitment waits for every [start condition](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid). [Obtain consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-before-any-study-procedure) on the current REB-approved form, following the steps the RI-MUHC's SOPs require.

Study teams sometimes start a screening procedure before the form is fully signed. Most often, they draw blood for screening lab tests or enter the participant's medical history in the electronic case report form.

<Warning>
All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes before all the required signatures are on the form is a deviation.
</Warning>

When a participant starts a study of an investigational product, send MUHC Medical Records Services the [Confirmation of Participation in Research form (FMU-9997)](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#notify-medical-records-and-give-a-wallet-card) and a copy of the signed consent form. When a participant's participation ends, send Medical Records the End of Participation in Research form (FMU-9907).

In an interventional study of an investigational product, the investigator should give each participant a wallet-size card when their participation begins. The card should be approved by the REB before it is used.

Quebec limits [what participants may be paid](/kb/recruitment/consent#compensate-participants-within-the-limits).

### Manage the investigational drug

The RI-MUHC's SOPs require that any medication given to a patient, whether in care or in a study, be dispensed by a pharmacist. The SOPs base the rule on Quebec's regulations and the rules of the Ordre des pharmaciens du Québec. Most other Canadian provinces have no equivalent requirement.

The MUHC Pharmacy stores the drug and dispenses it to participants. The principal investigator delegates these tasks to the pharmacy but [remains responsible for the investigational product](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products#know-what-the-pharmacy-does-and-what-the-principal-investigator-keeps). Only the principal investigator and sub-investigators who are physicians can prescribe medication in the study.

Every step of [the drug's path at the site](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products#follow-the-product-from-receipt-to-return-or-destruction), from receipt to return or destruction, is documented. ### Report safety events and deviations

In a drug trial, a serious adverse event can need reports to the sponsor, to the REB and, through the sponsor, to Health Canada. The sponsor's and the REB's deadlines run from the date the principal investigator learns of the event, so record that date.

In an interventional study, assess and record every [adverse event](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#assess-and-report-adverse-events) on the AE Assessment Log and in the source documents.

<Warning>
Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.
</Warning>

At a site under the MUHC REB's approval, a serious adverse event also needs an expedited report to the REB, by [the REB's deadline](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines), if the principal investigator judges that it meets the REB's three criteria.

A physician investigator reviews each report of a suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction from another site that the sponsor sends, and the principal investigator [documents their medical oversight](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#review-and-report-external-susars) by initialling and dating it. Every investigator who assesses adverse events, serious adverse events or suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions must hold [Human Research Privileges](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status).

Report a reaction from another site to the REB by its deadline if the principal investigator judges that it meets the REB's three criteria and also requires a change to the research or requires that participants be told immediately for their safety.

The hospital has [its own duty to report serious adverse drug reactions to Health Canada](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#hospital-reporting-of-serious-drug-reactions-and-device-incidents), separate from the trial's reporting. The hospital must report a reaction that involves a suspected drug the regulations' exemptions do not cover, such as a marketed drug outside the trial's authorization.

Every [protocol deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations) must be assessed, addressed and documented. Report every deviation to the sponsor, whatever its tier. Major and critical deviations also go to the REB by its deadline. Whatever their tier, report these deviations to the REB too:
- A sponsor-approved waiver of an eligibility criterion.
- A change to the approved consent process, such as an improper translation or not using the current consent form.
- A deviation that leads to a serious adverse event.
- Any other deviation that the principal investigator judges jeopardizes participants' safety, the efficacy of the research or data integrity, or could otherwise affect participants' rights, safety or well-being.

Report other minor deviations to the REB with the annual continuing review. The minor deviations that occurred since the previous REB review may be collected and submitted together.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps that only some drug trials take</div>

In a post-marketing (phase IV) study or another study of a marketed product, [report adverse reactions to the sponsor or the product's market authorization holder](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#assess-and-report-adverse-events), so that the sponsor or the holder can report them to Health Canada.

If a participant in an interventional study becomes pregnant, [collect and follow the exposure in utero reports](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#track-a-pregnancy-during-a-trial) to learn the outcome, even without an adverse event.

### Keep approvals and the team current

Amendments are routine, and happen in most studies, sometimes several times. While an amendment is under review, the study continues on the documents the REB has already approved.

Before you implement an [amendment](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#wait-for-every-approval-before-you-implement-the-amendment), wait for every approval it needs. A change needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants may be made before the REB's approval, and must be reported to the REB immediately.

The principal investigator initials and dates each [new version of the Investigator's Brochure](/kb/site-activation/protocol-documents#review-each-new-version-of-the-ib) before it is filed. Inform the REB of each new version before it is implemented. [Re-consent each affected participant](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#re-consent-participants-on-an-amended-consent-form) on an amended consent form.

The REB sets how long its approval lasts. Request the continuing review in Nagano by [the deadline the RI-MUHC's SOPs set before the approval expires](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid). If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk.

The Task Delegation Log [should be kept up to date](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#keep-the-task-delegation-log-up-to-date) throughout the study. For a drug trial, a sub-investigator who meets the criteria of a qualified investigator may assume the qualified investigator's full responsibilities [for short absences only](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#document-who-acts-as-qualified-investigator-during-a-short-absence). Who is acting as qualified investigator at any point in time should be well documented. A [change of principal investigator](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#change-the-principal-investigator) is an amendment that the REB must review and approve before it takes effect.

[Read revised SOPs on time](/training/compliance-requirements#read-revised-sops). A Health Canada inspector may compare your training records with SOP effective dates and record a lapse in SOP knowledge as a finding in their inspection report.

## Close the trial

A study doesn't end with the last participant's visit. Close-out is a sequence of steps, not a single event.

Consider starting to prepare for the [close-out visit](/kb/close-out/close-out#preparing-for-the-close-out-visit) well before the last participant's last visit. Every unit of the drug must be fully accounted for in the accountability log, and the supplies are [returned or destroyed](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving) as arranged with the sponsor or sponsor-investigator.

The sponsor-investigator or the principal investigator should submit the Closure of Research Project form (F10) in Nagano once [the conditions for closure](/kb/close-out/close-out#notifying-the-reb-and-health-canada) are met. The Task Delegation Log must be complete before the form can be submitted. Registering a trial and making its results public are separate obligations. A trial funded by CIHR, and a trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, each have [rules or guidance for making the results public](/kb/close-out/publications#make-the-results-public).

The study is closed when the REB acknowledges its closure form in Nagano. Then [archive the records](#archive-the-records).

<div className="hub-steps">Steps that only some drug trials take</div>

If the sponsor stops a drug trial, at the site or in its entirety, the qualified investigator must [inform the participants and the REB immediately](/kb/close-out/close-out#if-a-drug-or-natural-health-product-trial-stops-early), give them the reasons, and advise them in writing of any potential risks to the health of participants or others.
