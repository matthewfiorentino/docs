Health Canada regulates the studies conducted under its drug, natural health product and medical device regulations. The studies Health Canada regulates include drug trials, natural health product trials, the investigational testing of medical devices and basic clinical research studies of positron-emitting radiopharmaceuticals. [Check whether Health Canada regulates your study, and whether the study needs its authorization](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study).

Some of these studies need Health Canada's authorization before they start, and some don't. A study that needs no authorization is still regulated. The sponsor of a trial of a marketed drug within its approved conditions must still conduct it in accordance with Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, including good clinical practices, labelling and the approval of a <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">research ethics board (REB)</Tooltip>. Health Canada [inspects drug trials, and has begun inspecting medical device studies](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#health-canada-inspections).

What Health Canada authorizes, and who applies for it, depends on the product:

| | Drug trial | Natural health product trial | Medical device study |
|---|---|---|---|
| What Health Canada authorizes | The sale or import of the drug for the trial. | The sale or import of the product for the trial. | The sale or import of a Class II, III or IV device for investigational testing. |
| Who applies | The sponsor, through a <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip>. | The sponsor. | The manufacturer or importer, even when an investigator sponsors the study. |
| Health Canada's letter | A No Objection Letter (NOL). | A Notice of Authorization (NOA). | An <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">investigational testing authorization (ITA)</Tooltip>. |
| When no authorization is needed | The trial uses a marketed drug within the conditions of its notice of compliance or its drug identification number. | The trial is for a recommended use or purpose for which the product holds a product licence. | The device is Class I. For a Class II, III or IV device, [it depends on the device's licence and on the study](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization). |
| What still applies without an authorization | Division 5, including good clinical practices, labelling and REB approval. | Good clinical practices, REB approval and the Natural Health Products Regulations' requirements on labelling and records. | For a Class I device, the manufacturer or importer must hold the records that Health Canada requires. REB approval and the MUHC's requirements still apply. |

A basic clinical research study of a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical is authorized [under Division 3 of the Food and Drug Regulations](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study), instead of through a CTA.

If you can't tell whether Health Canada regulates your study, the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help.

## Weigh the sponsor obligations while you plan the study

Investigators sometimes discover the full scope of the sponsor's obligations only once they are committed to a study, often after a grant has been awarded.

A researcher who both initiates and conducts a clinical trial is its <Tooltip tip="An individual who both initiates and conducts, alone or with others, a clinical trial, and under whose immediate direction the investigational product is administered to, dispensed to or used by a participant." headline="Sponsor-Investigator" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sponsor-investigator">sponsor-investigator</Tooltip>, and holds the obligations of a sponsor and those of an investigator at the same time. [Health Canada's requirements for a drug trial are the same for a sponsor-investigator as for a commercial sponsor](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#what-the-two-roles-mean).

In a drug or natural health product trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, the sponsor [must ensure that the product is manufactured and stored in accordance with good manufacturing practices](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#know-who-ensures-the-product-meets-good-manufacturing-practices), and that a drug is also handled in accordance with them.

An investigator who sees an unlicensed Class II, III or IV device at a conference may want to build a study around it. [Only the manufacturer or importer can apply for the ITA](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#only-the-manufacturer-or-importer-can-apply) such a study needs, so the investigator needs the manufacturer's cooperation. [The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) require Human Research Privileges](/sops/cr-500#4-0-definitions) of the qualified investigator of every Health Canada-regulated study with an investigational product. [Plan the Health Canada application during study design](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization).

## Get authorization from Health Canada before the study starts

Study teams use “CTA” for two different documents: Health Canada's Clinical Trial Application and the clinical trial agreement signed with a sponsor. Only the application goes to Health Canada.

For a trial of a new drug or natural health product, or of a new Class II to IV medical device, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs have the sponsor obtain Health Canada's approval first](/sops/cr-007#5-1-1). The sponsor should forward Health Canada's letter to the investigators, to be filed in their investigator site file and submitted to the REB.

The order of Health Canada's letter and the REB's approval can differ by product. Whatever the order, the [Personne mandatée](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca) [waits for Health Canada's letter](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization) before issuing <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> to a trial that needs one. The trial cannot begin until it has both Health Canada's authorization and MUHC Authorization.

## Activate the site

The site initiation visit comes after Health Canada's authorization, where the study needs one, and after MUHC Authorization, in [the order the steps to activation follow](/kb/site-activation/site-setup#follow-the-steps-to-activation-in-order). For a drug trial under Division 5, the <Tooltip tip="The person responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a drug or natural health product trial at a clinical trial site, who is entitled to provide health care under the laws of the province where the site is located." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">qualified investigator</Tooltip> must complete and sign Health Canada's [Qualified Investigator's Undertaking (QIU)](/kb/foundations/glossary#qualified-investigators-undertaking-qiu) form, agreeing to conduct the study in accordance with Canadian regulations and guidelines.

[The investigator site file holds the QIU](/kb/site-activation/isf#add-the-documents-for-a-study-regulated-by-health-canada) for a drug or natural health product trial. For a device study, the manufacturer or importer keeps the qualified investigator's written undertaking in its records.

MUHC and RI-MUHC investigators are not to sign the US Food and Drug Administration's Form FDA 1572, because it requires investigators conducting studies in Canada to follow US laws. If a US sponsor provides no Canadian version of its form, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs recommend the RI-MUHC's attestation form on the good clinical practice (GCP) guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) instead](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#the-qiu-and-the-fda-1572).

Before a sponsor-supplied device is used at the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM), [confirm with the sponsor that Health Canada has issued an ITA](/cim/services#confirm-health-canada-authorization-for-a-sponsor-supplied-device) where the device needs one.

## Obtain consent and enrol participants

In a study regulated by Health Canada, the process for obtaining consent with an electronic consent form [should be well detailed in a written document or work instruction](/kb/recruitment/consent#meet-the-added-conditions-for-a-study-regulated-by-health-canada). [The RI-MUHC's REDCap can't be used for electronic consent in these studies](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-remotely-or-electronically).

In a drug trial, Health Canada accepts consent documented in new ways, such as electronic signatures, when the process meets its conditions. At the MUHC, [Quebec's Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:24) still requires written consent for research that could interfere with a person's integrity, unless the REB finds that the circumstances justify another way. The REB then decides how the consent is documented.

For a study of an investigational product, [notify MUHC Medical Records Services](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#notify-medical-records-and-give-a-wallet-card) when each participant starts and when their participation ends, so that any caregiver knows about the study and can reach the principal investigator.

## Run the study

### Keep the product, the team and the changes in line with the regulations

The regulations set what [the label of an investigational drug, natural health product or device](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products#check-the-label-against-the-regulations) must show, in English and French.

For a drug or natural health product trial, the QIU must be reissued under the incoming principal investigator's name when the principal investigator changes, and [Health Canada's other forms may need updating too](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#update-the-undertakings-and-forms-to-health-canada).

Some amendments need Health Canada's authorization before they take effect, as well as the REB's approval. For a Class II to IV device study, [any protocol amendment that requires REB approval also requires Health Canada's authorization](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#get-a-revised-health-canada-authorization-for-a-device-trial). [A drug trial](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#get-health-canada-authorization-for-a-drug-trial-amendment) and [a natural health product trial](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#get-health-canada-authorization-for-a-natural-health-product-trial-amendment) each have their own list of amendments that need it.

### Report safety events to Health Canada

In a drug or natural health product trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, [the sponsor reports to Health Canada every serious adverse event (SAE) that meets Health Canada's criteria](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#know-who-reports-an-sae-to-health-canada). If you are the sponsor-investigator of such a trial, report an SAE that meets Health Canada's criteria to Health Canada yourself, whether or not it meets the REB's criteria.

Health Canada's deadlines are in [the reporting timelines](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines), beside the sponsor's and the REB's, with the qualified investigator's report of a medical device incident.

### Be ready for an inspection

A sponsor-investigator's team sometimes assembles its trial master file only once an inspection is announced. Notice can be short, and an inspection can be unannounced, so [there is no time to catch up on documentation that was never done](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#being-inspection-ready).

Health Canada inspects a drug trial against Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations and the ICH's good clinical practice guideline, and a device trial against the Medical Devices Regulations and the International Organization for Standardization's standard ISO 14155.

If an inspector announces an inspection or arrives with little or no notice, [immediately inform the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, the research team and Quality Assurance (QA), as required](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#when-an-inspection-is-announced-or-arrives-without-notice). [Hosting a Health Canada Inspection](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/hc-inspection-guide) gives the steps, from the notice to the response to the inspector's report.

## Close the study and keep the records

Where a CTA or an ITA was submitted, [the sponsor or sponsor-investigator should also inform Health Canada that the study has ended](/kb/close-out/close-out#tell-health-canada-that-the-study-has-ended). A sponsor that stops a drug or natural health product trial early must inform Health Canada if the trial needs Health Canada's authorization. Whenever the sponsor stops a drug or natural health product trial early, the qualified investigator [must inform the participants and the REB immediately](/kb/close-out/close-out#if-a-drug-or-natural-health-product-trial-stops-early).

If at least one participant signed a consent form, the study's documents are kept for [the retention period set for Health Canada-regulated studies](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving). In a drug trial regulated by Health Canada, every electronic system used to capture, process, manage or archive clinical trial information [should be adequately validated](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#meet-the-added-expectations-for-a-drug-trial-regulated-by-health-canada).

Health Canada also expects the sponsors of trials within the scope of [its guidance on registration and results](/kb/close-out/publications#health-canada-direction) to register the trial and submit its summary results.

## Train the team

Teams conducting Health Canada-regulated studies must complete other mandatory training in addition to the SOP Reader and Assessment.

[The training each level needs](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs) is Health Canada Division 5 training and ICH E6 GCP training for a drug study, ICH E6 GCP training for a natural health product study, and ISO 14155 GCP training for a medical device study.
