The RI-MUHC sorts studies into five levels, from Level I (highest risk) to Level V (lowest risk). A study's level sets the training its team needs and which of the two series of the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs), SOP-CR or SOP-LR, applies to it. Whether Health Canada must authorize the study, how it is designed and who sponsors it change some of its steps too.

## Place your study

To place a study, ask these questions in order and stop at the first yes. A study that tests a drug is a Level I study. A study that tests a medical device or a surgical procedure is at Level II, and one that tests a natural health product is at Level III.

A study that tests none of these is at Level V if it is a retrospective study or a study of samples already held in a biobank, and it never contacts participants or collects new data or samples from them. ## Training levels

Each level has its own tag and its own SOP Reader and Assessment course. Teams conducting drug, medical device or natural health product studies must also complete [other mandatory training](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level). The RI-MUHC's SOPs come in two series: the high-risk SOP-CR series for Levels I to III, and the low-risk SOP-LR series for Levels IV and V.

| Tag | The studies it covers | SOP series |
|---|---|---|
| <span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span> | Drug studies, including biologics, gene and cell therapies, blood products, vaccines and radiopharmaceuticals. | SOP-CR |
| <span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span> | Medical device and surgery studies. | SOP-CR |
| <span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span> | Natural health product studies. | SOP-CR |
| <span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span> | Prospective studies that Health Canada does not regulate and that involve no surgery. | SOP-LR |
| <span className="hub-scope-tag">Retrospective and biobank studies (Level V)</span> | Retrospective studies, including studies of samples already held in a biobank. | SOP-LR |

## Studies that need Health Canada authorization

Health Canada authorizes the sale or import of a drug or a natural health product for a clinical trial, and of a medical device for investigational testing. The sections of the Hub about Health Canada's application, authorization, reports and notices carry the tag <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span>. The following studies need Health Canada's authorization:

- A drug trial, through a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip>, unless the trial uses a marketed drug within the conditions of its notice of compliance or its drug identification number.
- A natural health product trial, unless the trial is for a recommended use or purpose for which the product holds a product licence.
- A study of a Class II, III or IV medical device that needs an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">investigational testing authorization (ITA)</Tooltip>. [Whether a device study needs one](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization) depends on the device's licence and on the study.

No Health Canada authorization is needed for a Class I device, for a surgery study that tests no drug, natural health product or device, or for a study at Level IV or Level V.

The sponsor of a trial of a marketed drug within its approved conditions must still conduct it in accordance with Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, including good clinical practices, labelling and the approval of a <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">research ethics board (REB)</Tooltip>. The sponsor of a natural health product trial within the product's licence must still ensure that the trial follows good clinical practices, and must still meet the Natural Health Products Regulations' requirements on labelling and records.

For a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor applies to Health Canada. For a device, the manufacturer or importer holds the authorization. Where a study needs Health Canada's authorization, [the Personne mandatée waits for it](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization) before issuing <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>.

## Study design

A study's design says how it gets its data. Design and level overlap, but an interventional study can be at any of Levels I to IV, depending on what it tests.

| Design | What it is | Level |
|---|---|---|
| Interventional (a clinical trial) | The study assigns participants to an intervention, or to a comparison or control group, to study the effect of a medical intervention on a health outcome. The intervention can be a drug, a surgical procedure, a device, a behavioural treatment or a change in the process of care. | I, II, III or IV, by what it tests |
| Prospective observational | The study follows participants from the time they are identified, for outcomes or events that happen afterwards. Participants may receive treatments in their care, but the investigator doesn't assign them to one. | IV |
| Retrospective | The study reviews records from the past, such as medical records, or obtains information about past events through interviews or surveys. The study can also use samples already held in a biobank. | V, if it never contacts participants or collects new data or samples from them |

The project type you choose in Nagano, which sets [the version of the F11 form](/kb/submission/preparing#choose-the-project-type) you complete, also follows the kind of research.

{/* Sources: the RI-MUHC's SOP Glossary of Terms (Apr. 27, 2023): "Clinical trial (or interventional study MUHC definition): Any research project that prospectively assigns human participants to intervention or concurrent comparison or control groups to study the cause-and-effect relationship between a medical intervention and a health outcome. Medical interventions include drugs, surgical procedures, devices, behavioral treatments, process-of-care changes, and the like."; "Prospective studies (MUHC): Studies designed to observe outcomes or events that occur subsequent to the identification of the group of participants to be studied. Prospective studies need not to involve manipulation or intervention but may be purely observational or involve only the collection of data."; "Observational study: … Participants may receive diagnostic, therapeutic, or other types of interventions, but the investigator does not assign participants to a specific intervention/treatment."; "Retrospective studies (MUHC): Research conducted by reviewing records from the past (e.g. birth and death certificates, medical records, school records, or employment records) or by obtaining information about past events through interviews or surveys. ## Sponsorship

The sponsor is the individual, company, institution or organization that takes responsibility for initiating, managing or financing a clinical trial, or more than one of these. ## Cases that add steps

Some studies take further steps, whatever their level:

- Creating a biobank or a registry is [its own project type in Nagano](/kb/submission/preparing#choose-the-project-type), with its own F11 form.
- A study of samples already held in a biobank is at Level V, and [the rules of the *Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2) on consent for samples](/paths/retrospective#decide-whether-the-study-needs-consent) apply to it.
- Consent for minors and for adults who can't consent [follows Quebec's Civil Code](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent). Research that could interfere with their integrity must be approved and monitored by a research ethics board that Quebec's health minister has formed or designated.
- A study at more than one public institution in Quebec's health and social services network is reviewed by one evaluating REB for all the sites, and each institution [authorizes the study at its own site](/kb/design/multi-centric).
