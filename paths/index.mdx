Studies at the MUHC range from drug trials to reviews of medical charts. Each of these studies is [clinical research](/kb/foundations/clinical-research-101#tell-research-apart-from-clinical-care): research involving human participants, their data or their biological material. A research study that develops or tests software, such as an app or an algorithm, with participants or their data is clinical research too.

What a study tests decides whether Health Canada must authorize it. What it tests and how it gets its data also decide which of the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) apply to it and what training its team needs. Who sponsors the study changes some of its steps, and some studies have extra steps whatever their kind.

## How a study gets its data

Clinical studies are of two types. An interventional study, also called a clinical trial, assigns participants to an intervention. An observational study assesses participants without assigning them to one. A study can also look forward, following participants after they are identified, or look back at the past.

| Design | What it is | Training level |
|---|---|---|
| Interventional (a clinical trial) | The study assigns participants to an intervention, or to a comparison or control group, to study the effect of a medical intervention on a health outcome. The intervention can be a drug, a surgical procedure, a device, a behavioural treatment or a change in the process of care. | I, II, III or IV, by what it tests |
| Prospective observational | The study follows participants from the time they are identified, for outcomes or events that happen afterwards. Participants may receive treatments in their care, but the investigator doesn't assign them to one. | IV |
| Retrospective | The study reviews records from the past, such as medical records, or obtains information about past events through interviews or surveys. The study can also use samples already held in a biobank. | V for a study of records or of samples already held in a biobank. For a study that interviews or surveys participants, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca). |

The design alone doesn't decide the training level. An interventional study can be at any of Levels I to IV, depending on what it tests.

A [retrospective study](/paths/retrospective) is clinical research, even when it only reviews records. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs require Research Ethics Board (REB) approval for a retrospective study](/sops/cr-008#5-4-1). A retrospective study also needs MUHC Authorization before any research activity begins, as every study does. A study that uses identifiable health information without the participants' consent also needs an approved [privacy impact assessment](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent), the <Tooltip tip="The privacy impact assessment, or évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée, that Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information requires with a researcher's request for access to health and social services information without consent, including access before consent for pre-screening." headline="EFVP (privacy impact assessment)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#efvp">évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP)</Tooltip>, before MUHC Authorization.

The project type you choose in Nagano depends on the kind of research, and the type sets [the version of the F11 form](/kb/submission/preparing#choose-the-project-type) you complete.

## Check whether Health Canada must authorize the study

A study that tests a drug, a natural health product or a medical device may need Health Canada's authorization. Health Canada authorizes the sale or import of a drug or a natural health product for a clinical trial, and of a medical device for investigational testing. The sections of the Hub about Health Canada's application, authorization, reports and notices carry the tag <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span>. The following studies need Health Canada's authorization:

- A drug trial, through a <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip>, unless the trial uses a marketed drug within the conditions of its notice of compliance or its drug identification number.
- A basic clinical research study of a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical, through the sponsor's application for the Minister's authorization under Division 3 of the Food and Drug Regulations, instead of a CTA.{/* b161, second reader (for MF: an addition): from the Food and Drug Regulations, C.03.301, C.03.302, C.03.303(d), C.03.304 and C.03.309 (quoted in the note on the drug-trial item and in this section's Sources comment); the study's purpose, its requirements (C.03.305, added in b171, for MF with this item) and the exception are in the paragraphs after the list. C.03.302(3) takes a study drug "manufactured from a bulk process intermediate that is of biological origin" out of sections C.03.303 to C.03.319; the route for such a study is asked of QA in the master doc. */}
- A natural health product trial, unless the trial is for a recommended use or purpose for which the product holds a product licence.
- A study of a Class II, III or IV medical device that needs an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">investigational testing authorization (ITA)</Tooltip>. [Whether a device study needs one](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization) depends on the device's licence and on the study.

Under Division 3, a basic clinical research study obtains data on the radiopharmaceutical's pharmacokinetics or metabolism, on normal human biochemistry or physiology, or on changes caused to human biochemistry or physiology by aging, disease or medical interventions. The study is not primarily intended to discover, identify or verify the radiopharmaceutical's pharmacodynamic effects, identify adverse reactions, fulfil an immediate therapeutic or diagnostic purpose, or ascertain its safety or efficacy.

A basic clinical research study under Division 3 must also meet all of the following requirements:

- Before the radiopharmaceutical is used in the study, there are sufficient data from testing it in animals and humans to demonstrate its safety in humans.
- The amount of active ingredients, or combination of active ingredients, in the radiopharmaceutical has been shown not to cause any clinically detectable pharmacodynamic effect in humans.
- The total radiation dose each participant incurs in a year will be not more than 50 millisieverts (mSv). That total includes the radiation from multiple administrations of the radiopharmaceutical, from significant contaminants or impurities, and from other procedures used for the study.
- Any concomitant drug used in the study has a drug identification number or, if it is a new drug, a notice of compliance.
- Participants are at least 18 years old and have legal capacity at the time of the study.
- Female participants are confirmed at the outset of the study as not pregnant, on the basis of a pregnancy test, or state in writing that they are not pregnant.
- Female participants are advised that if they are lactating, they are to suspend lactation for 24 hours after the radiopharmaceutical is given.
- The study involves no more than 30 participants.

A study may involve more than 30 participants if the sponsor provides the Minister with a scientific rationale for the increase and the Minister approves it.

[Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations does not apply to the radiopharmaceutical in a basic clinical research study under Division 3](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.03.302.html). Division 3's sections on these studies set their own rules, including on the research ethics board, good clinical practices, adverse reaction reporting and records.

Division 3's rules for these studies don't apply to a radiopharmaceutical manufactured from a bulk process intermediate of biological origin.

No Health Canada authorization is needed for a Class I device, for a surgery study that tests no drug, natural health product or device, or for a study at Level IV or Level V.

The sponsor of a trial of a marketed drug within its approved conditions must still conduct it in accordance with Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, including good clinical practices, labelling and the approval of a <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">research ethics board (REB)</Tooltip>. The sponsor of a natural health product trial within the product's licence must still ensure that the trial follows good clinical practices, and must still meet the Natural Health Products Regulations' requirements on labelling and records.

Sections whose rules apply to every study conducted under Health Canada's drug, natural health product or medical device regulations, whether or not the study needs authorization, carry the tag <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span>. These studies include:

- Drug trials under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, including a trial of a marketed drug within the conditions of its notice of compliance or its drug identification number.
- Basic clinical research studies of a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical under Division 3 of the Food and Drug Regulations, for which the Minister authorizes the sponsor to sell or import the radiopharmaceutical.
- Natural health product trials under Part 4 of the Natural Health Products Regulations, including a trial within the product's licence.
- Investigational testing of a medical device under Part 3 of the Medical Devices Regulations, including the testing of a Class I device, which needs no authorization.

For a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor applies to Health Canada. For a device, the manufacturer or importer holds the authorization. Where a study needs Health Canada's authorization, [the Personne mandatée waits for it](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization) before issuing <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>.

## Who sponsors the study

The sponsor is the individual, company, institution or organization that takes responsibility for initiating, managing or financing a clinical trial, or more than one of these. Sponsorship is a separate question from the type of study. A company can sponsor an observational study, for example.

| Sponsorship | What it means | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Industry-sponsored | [A company sponsors the study](/kb/planning/sponsored-trials). For a drug or natural health product trial, the company applies to Health Canada. | <span className="hub-scope-tag">Industry-sponsored studies</span> |
| Sponsor-investigator | An investigator both initiates and conducts a clinical trial, and [takes on the sponsor's duties](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator) as well as the investigator's. Health Canada's requirements for a drug trial are the same for a sponsor-investigator as for a commercial sponsor. | <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span> |
| Grant-funded or academic | No company sponsors the study. If an investigator both initiates and conducts it as a clinical trial, that investigator is its sponsor-investigator. For a study that Health Canada does not regulate, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-007#4-0-definitions) call the principal investigator of an investigator-initiated study its sponsor-investigator. | <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span>, if the study has a sponsor-investigator. Otherwise, none. |

A sponsorship tag goes only on a section whose steps change with the sponsor.

## Check whether the study needs extra steps

Some studies need extra steps, whatever their level:

- Creating a biobank or a registry is [its own project type in Nagano](/kb/submission/preparing#choose-the-project-type), with its own F11 form.
- A study that only uses samples already held in a biobank is at Level V, and [the rules of the *Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2) on consent for samples](/paths/retrospective#decide-whether-the-study-needs-consent) apply to it.
- Consent to research that could interfere with the integrity of a minor or of an adult who can't consent [follows Quebec's Civil Code](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent). The research project must be approved and monitored by a research ethics board that Quebec's health minister has formed or designated.
- A study at more than one public institution in Quebec's health and social services network is reviewed by one evaluating REB for all the sites, and each institution [authorizes the study at its own site](/kb/design/multi-centric).

## Find the training level your study needs

The RI-MUHC sets five training levels, from Level I for the highest-risk studies to Level V for the lowest-risk ones. A study's level decides [the training its team needs](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level). The level also decides which of the RI-MUHC's two series of SOPs, the high-risk SOP-CR series or the low-risk SOP-LR series, applies to the study.

To find the training level a study needs, read this table from the top and stop at the first row that fits the study:

| Kind of study | Training level |
|---|---|
| A study that tests a drug. | Level I |
| A study that tests a medical device, including [software as a medical device](/kb/design/software-medical-devices), or a surgical procedure. | Level II |
| A study that tests a natural health product. | Level III |
| A study that is only retrospective, or that only uses samples already held in a biobank. | Level V. If the study will interview or survey participants about past events, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) which level applies. |
| Any other study, which is a prospective study not regulated by Health Canada. | Level IV |

Asking participants for their consent to use their records or samples doesn't change the study's level. [If it is feasible, participants must give informed consent](/paths/retrospective#decide-whether-the-study-needs-consent), even in a retrospective study.

The principal investigator or sponsor-investigator who leads a study at the MUHC is responsible for obtaining the level of institutional training the study requires before submitting the study to the Research Ethics Board in Nagano.{/* b151: this requirement's home is training/compliance-requirements, which does not carry it yet (14-open-questions.md, "Leads from b126"; QA's draft SOP-CR-003_11, section 3.0). If you can't tell which training level your study needs, the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help.
