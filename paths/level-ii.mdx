<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span></div>

You want to test a medical device or a surgical procedure in people. A company may have asked the RI-MUHC to take part in a study of a device it makes. Or the question may be your own, such as a modified surgical technique that you want to formalize as a randomized controlled trial.

Medical device and surgery studies are at the RI-MUHC's [training Level II](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs), a high-risk level. The two kinds of study share the level, its training and most of their steps. Before any research activity begins, each needs the approval of the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, as every study does.

Health Canada regulates the investigational testing of medical devices, and its rules add steps to a device study. The Medical Devices Regulations class devices from Class I, the lowest risk, to Class IV, the highest. Whether a device study needs Health Canada's authorization depends on the device's class and licence and on the study. Only the manufacturer or the importer of a device can apply for that authorization, even when an investigator sponsors the study.

A device being tested is an <Tooltip tip="A pharmaceutical form of an active ingredient or placebo, a medical device or a natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip>, so a device study also takes the steps for studies of an investigational product, such as notifying Medical Records when each participant starts.

Several people share the work. The sponsor [takes responsibility for initiating, managing or financing the trial](/paths#who-sponsors-the-study). In an industry-sponsored study, a company is the sponsor. An investigator who both initiates and conducts a clinical trial is its <Tooltip tip="An individual who both initiates and conducts, alone or with others, a clinical trial, and under whose immediate direction the investigational product is administered to, dispensed to or used by a participant." headline="Sponsor-Investigator" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sponsor-investigator">sponsor-investigator</Tooltip>, with the obligations of both a sponsor and an investigator.

The principal investigator leads the study at the MUHC. For a device under investigational testing, Health Canada's rules call the health professional that the REB designates to conduct the testing the qualified investigator. The research team, such as coordinators and nurses, does the tasks the principal investigator delegates on the <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>.

A study that tests a drug as well as a device or a procedure is at Level I, and follows [Drug Studies](/paths/level-i). If a drug trial uses a Class II, III or IV medical device that is not licensed in Canada, an application for Health Canada's [investigational testing authorization](#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-ita) must be filed for the device, separately from the drug's Clinical Trial Application. Both should be authorized before the trial starts in Canada. The device's manufacturer must sign the device's application.

If you can't tell whether your study is a medical device or surgery study, [Types of Study](/paths) explains each type of study, and the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help you place it.

## Place the study

A device study has steps that come from Health Canada's rules and from the device itself. If a surgery study also tests a medical device, Health Canada's [Medical Devices Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/) apply to the device.

The Regulations' part on investigational testing applies to a device that is imported or sold for investigational testing involving human subjects. Research with medical devices should also follow good clinical practice under the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) standard ISO 14155. The study's training level stays Level II, which covers surgical and medical device studies alike.

### Check whether a device study needs an ITA

A device study gets Health Canada's authorization through an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">investigational testing authorization (ITA)</Tooltip>, the device counterpart of a drug trial's Clinical Trial Application. A Class II, III or IV device needs an ITA if it is not licensed in Canada, or if a licensed device is used outside its licensed indications in a study that the manufacturer sponsors or that is meant to support a licence application.

A licensed device used within its licensed indications needs no ITA. A device whose legal manufacturer is the physician using it needs no ITA when it is used at one site, on that physician's own patients, and no one else conducts the testing.

[Check which applies to your study](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization), including the device's class.

Class I devices need no ITA. The manufacturer or importer must still hold the records that Health Canada requires, and the REB's approval and the MUHC's requirements still apply.

An investigator who sees an unlicensed Class II, III or IV device at a conference may want to build a study around it. [Only the manufacturer or importer can apply for the ITA](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#only-the-manufacturer-or-importer-can-apply) such a study needs, so the investigator needs the manufacturer's cooperation. If you are unsure whether your study needs an ITA, ask Health Canada's Investigational Testing division at [it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca](mailto:it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca).

The <Tooltip tip="The person whom an establishment of Quebec's health network formally mandates to authorize the research conducted under its auspices." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> waits for Health Canada's authorization before issuing MUHC Authorization to a study that needs one.

The sections tagged <span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span> apply to a study that needs an ITA. ## Plan an industry-sponsored study

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Industry-sponsored studies</span></div>

In a company's study, the site's research team conducts the study here. The team follows the sponsor's protocol, and its members keep their own professional and institutional obligations.

Most industry-sponsored trials reach the RI-MUHC through a company, or a contract research organization working for it, that has already picked the RI-MUHC as a possible site. [Answer the feasibility questionnaire](/kb/planning/sponsored-trials#answer-the-feasibility-questionnaire) the sponsor sends, and prepare for its [site feasibility visit](/kb/planning/feasibility#the-site-feasibility-visit). If you are considering having the [Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) run your study, contact it before you answer the questionnaire.

Before the team receives confidential study documents, such as the protocol, a [confidentiality agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#know-when-the-study-needs-an-agreement) between the sponsor, the institution and the investigator must be signed and dated. The [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) reviews and approves it before it is signed. The investigator must then submit the signed and dated agreement in Nagano.

The sponsor writes and owns the protocol. For a device study, the sponsor provides an <Tooltip tip="A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip>, or [an equivalent document or a report of prior investigations](/kb/site-activation/protocol-documents#review-each-new-version-of-the-ib), and the manufacturer or importer applies for the ITA where the study needs one. The principal investigator [negotiates the budget with the sponsor](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#negotiate-the-budget-with-an-industry-sponsor) while the Research Agreements Office negotiates the [clinical trial agreement's clauses](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#read-the-clauses-of-a-clinical-trial-agreement).

The RI-MUHC is a partner institution of CATALIS Québec's [FAST TRACK Evaluation Service](/kb/design/multi-centric#catalis-fast-track) for industry-sponsored clinical trials. Ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) whether your study is eligible.

## Plan a study you sponsor yourself

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

When the study is yours, you do the sponsor's work yourself. No company holds the protocol above you, no contract research organization monitors the study and no company's regulatory affairs department prepares Health Canada's applications. Investigators sometimes discover the full scope of the sponsor's obligations only once they are committed to a study, often after a grant has been awarded.

Before you commit to a study you initiate yourself, weigh [the sponsor's obligations](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#what-the-two-roles-mean) that you will hold as well as the investigator's, the protocol among them. [Set up the trial step by step](/kb/planning/iit-startup#set-up-the-trial-step-by-step). For a device trial, then [add the device steps](/kb/planning/iit-startup#add-the-steps-for-a-device-trial). [The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) make the sponsor-investigator responsible for both the sponsor's and the principal investigator's duties](/sops/cr-007#3-0-responsibilities). Consider writing to [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) when you start planning the study.

For the RI-MUHC's insurance to cover the study, the RI-MUHC should be [named as its sponsor](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#cover-a-study-you-sponsor-yourself) on the agreement, on regulatory documents and on ClinicalTrials.gov. You keep all the sponsor-investigator's responsibilities for conducting the research under the RI-MUHC's SOPs. The principal investigator [must ensure that adequate liability insurance coverage is held](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#meet-the-insurance-duties-of-the-principal-investigator), including the institutional coverage that applies to research activities. The institutional feasibility review analyzes the liability insurance coverage that applies to the project.

Develop a risk-based monitoring plan and arrange an [independent monitor](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#monitoring-independence). You cannot monitor your own study.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs have the sponsor-investigator ensure that all electronic software used in a study has been validated](/sops/cr-004#5-1-3) and complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations. Keep the [trial master file](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#tmf-and-isf) as well as the investigator site file.

Give the REB an acceptable [plan for monitoring safety, efficacy or effectiveness (where feasible) and validity](/kb/submission/preparing#plan-the-safety-monitoring-of-a-clinical-trial).

For a device trial that you initiate, other than one with an in vitro diagnostic device, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs require its investigational testing](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#follow-good-clinical-practice-in-a-study-regulated-by-health-canada) to be conducted in compliance with the *Declaration of Helsinki*, ISO 14155 and ISO 14971 on the application of risk management to medical devices.

### Work with the manufacturer on the ITA

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

A sponsor-investigator of a drug trial applies to Health Canada on their own. A sponsor-investigator of a device study cannot obtain an ITA without the manufacturer, even if the manufacturer has no other role in the study. [Only the manufacturer or the importer of a device can apply for an ITA](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#only-the-manufacturer-or-importer-can-apply), and a senior official of the manufacturer must sign the application. The manufacturer can delegate the regulatory correspondence and the clinical oversight to the investigator.

[Whoever modifies a device can become its manufacturer](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#consequences-for-academic-device-research), and an academic institution can be a manufacturer if it designed or owns the device. For a novel or complex device, the manufacturer or importer can ask Health Canada for a [pre-ITA application meeting](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#pre-ita-meeting-with-health-canada), and may invite the qualified investigators who will take part in the study.

Consider writing to [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) and the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) at the earliest stage of planning a device study, to assess the ITA and the manufacturer's involvement it needs. In a device trial that you initiate and that needs an ITA, other than one with an in vitro diagnostic device, do not begin the study before [Health Canada's written authorization](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization) is received.

### Check credentials and training

Teams sometimes assume that Quality Assurance's check covers everyone. During the study, the principal investigator must ensure that the people delegated tasks keep the study's level of institutional training.

Before you submit, the principal investigator must hold Human Research Privileges, Researcher Status or a written institutional authorization. [Who can be named principal investigator](/kb/submission/preparing#name-the-principal-investigator) depends on whether the study needs medical oversight. Privileges are required to lead a study that Health Canada regulates with an investigational medical device.

Everyone on the study should hold [Level II training](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) or a higher level before signing its Task Delegation Log. Level II training is the SOP Reader and Assessment for Level II. Renew any credential or training that is [close to expiry](/training/compliance-requirements#validity-and-the-90-day-rule) before you submit.

A Sub-Investigator [should be named as the principal investigator's backup](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#name-a-backup-for-the-principal-investigator) when the study is being prepared.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps that only some device and surgery studies take</div>

The RI-MUHC has no ISO 14155 course, and the RI-MUHC's SOPs highly recommend that a medical device study's team obtain the ISO 14155 document and self-train on it.

Self-training on the ISO 14155 document gives no certificate or dated printout, so it [must be documented on a Training Log](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#add-a-new-team-member).

#### Give a written undertaking as qualified investigator

For a device under investigational testing, the Medical Devices Regulations require a [written undertaking from each qualified investigator](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#the-qiu-and-the-fda-1572). The undertaking is among the records the manufacturer or importer must possess.

Each qualified investigator undertakes in writing to:
- Conduct the investigational testing in accordance with the protocol the manufacturer provides.
- Inform each patient who is to be diagnosed or treated with the device of the risks and benefits of its use, and obtain the patient's written consent to its use.

- Report a [medical device incident](#report-a-medical-device-incident) to Health Canada and to the manufacturer or importer.

## Activate the site

Once the study is authorized, it is not yet ready to run. Site activation covers the work between authorization and the first participant: the site initiation visit, the Task Delegation Log, the investigator site file and logistics.

The principal investigator or a delegate [should create](/kb/site-activation/isf#set-up-the-isf) the <Tooltip tip="The qualified or principal investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines, so that the conduct and integrity of the study and the safety of participants at the site can be verified." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file (ISF)</Tooltip> after the sponsor or sponsor-investigator first makes contact, or as soon as a protocol outline is available, and keep it current. [File the printed REB approval letter](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#check-and-file-the-approval-letter) in the ISF. Teams sometimes keep only the electronic copy. The RI-MUHC's SOPs require the printed copy until a document management system that complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations is in place.

Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.

Registering a trial publicly before it recruits helps prevent selective publication of results. [The sponsor or sponsor-investigator registers the trial](/kb/close-out/publications#trial-registration) in a public registry before the first participant is recruited.

[Do not begin the study](/kb/site-activation/site-setup#wait-for-the-go-letter-before-you-enrol) until you have the REB's written approval, MUHC Authorization and the sponsor's go letter, if it issues one. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email.

Do not ask anyone to consent before you have the REB's written approval and MUHC Authorization, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator has held the site initiation visit, and the go letter has arrived, if the sponsor issues one. Then [recruit, consent and enrol participants](#recruit-consent-and-enrol-participants).

A sponsor-investigator who registers on ClinicalTrials.gov uses the MUHC's organization account. To get access, write to [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca). ### Report safety events and deviations

In an interventional study, assess and record every [adverse event](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#assess-and-report-adverse-events) on the AE Assessment Log and in the source documents. The sponsor's and the REB's deadlines run from the date the principal investigator learns of a serious adverse event, so record that date.

<Warning>
Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.
</Warning>

At a site under the MUHC REB's approval, a serious adverse event also needs an expedited report to the REB, by [the REB's deadline](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines), if the principal investigator judges that it meets the REB's three criteria. Remove all participant identifiers from every report to the REB.

Every [protocol deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations) must be assessed, addressed and documented. Report every deviation to the sponsor, whatever its tier. Major and critical deviations also go to the REB by its deadline. Whatever their tier, report these deviations to the REB too:
- A sponsor-approved waiver of an eligibility criterion.
- A change to the approved consent process, such as an improper translation or not using the current consent form.
- A deviation that leads to a serious adverse event.
- Any other deviation that the principal investigator judges jeopardizes participants' safety, the efficacy of the research or data integrity, or could otherwise affect participants' rights, safety or well-being.

Report other minor deviations to the REB with the annual continuing review. The minor deviations that occurred since the previous REB review may be collected and submitted together.

### Keep approvals and the team current

## Close the study

A study doesn't end with the last participant's visit. Close-out is a sequence of steps, not a single event.

Consider starting to prepare for the [close-out visit](/kb/close-out/close-out#preparing-for-the-close-out-visit) well before the last participant's last visit. All study-related supplies are [returned or destroyed](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving) as arranged with the sponsor or sponsor-investigator.

The sponsor-investigator or the principal investigator should submit the Closure of Research Project form (F10) in Nagano once [the conditions for closure](/kb/close-out/close-out#notifying-the-reb-and-health-canada) are met. The Task Delegation Log must be complete before the form can be submitted. Registering a trial and making its results public are separate obligations, and [the rules for making the results public](/kb/close-out/publications#make-the-results-public) depend on who funds the trial and whether Health Canada regulates it.

The study is closed when the REB acknowledges its closure form in Nagano. Then [archive the records](#archive-the-records).

## Archive the records

Archiving is more than storing files. Documents must be organized, indexed and available to authorized people for an inspection or an audit, which may come years after a study closes.

The principal investigator or a delegate should inform the archiving service of [the required retention period](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving), which is longer for a study regulated by Health Canada in which at least one participant signed a consent form. The essential study documents must remain preserved, organized and accessible for inspection for the full retention period. The obligation to report safety events [continues after close-out](/kb/close-out/close-out#obligations-that-continue-after-close-out), through the follow-up period that the protocol and the consent form define.
