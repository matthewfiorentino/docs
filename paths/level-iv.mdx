<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

Your study will follow people over time, or give them an intervention to see whether it helps. In a prospective observational study, the team follows participants from the time they are identified, for outcomes or events that happen afterwards. Participants may receive treatments as part of their care, but the investigator doesn't assign them to one.

In an interventional study at this level, the team assigns participants to an intervention that Health Canada does not regulate and that involves no surgery, such as an educational or non-invasive intervention (psychotherapy, physiotherapy, diet, exercise or massage). Both kinds of study are at the RI-MUHC's [training Level IV](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs).

A Level IV study works with its participants directly. The team recruits them, asks for their consent and follows them as the protocol sets. Health Canada's authorization is not needed for a study at Level IV. Before any research activity begins, the study needs the approval of the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, as every study does.

A company may sponsor a Level IV study, or the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, may initiate it. For a study that Health Canada does not regulate, the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) call the principal investigator of an investigator-initiated study its sponsor-investigator. In [a project that a trainee runs](/roles/trainee#run-your-own-project-under-a-supervisor), the supervisor is the principal investigator.

The principal investigator delegates study tasks to trained team members, such as research coordinators and research nurses, on the <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>.

The study goes through the stages most clinical studies take, in the same order: feasibility, design and planning, submission and review, site activation, recruitment, conduct, close-out and archiving.

If your study tests a drug, a medical device, a surgical procedure or a natural health product, other steps apply. A study that only uses data or samples that already exist follows [Retrospective and Biobank Studies](/paths/retrospective). [Types of Study](/paths) explains each kind of study, and the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help you place yours.

Software, such as an app or an algorithm, [can be a medical device](/kb/design/software-medical-devices#know-when-software-is-a-medical-device). A study that tests software as a medical device in participants follows [Medical Device and Surgery Studies](/paths/level-ii).

## Confirm that the project is research

Some projects that study a program or a service at the MUHC are quality improvement, and others are research. A quality assurance or quality improvement project used only for assessment, management or improvement [doesn't need REB review](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html).

If the goal of the project is to generate new generalizable knowledge, by answering a question or testing a hypothesis with methods the scientific community recognizes, the project should be submitted to the REB. Answer the [screening questions](/is-it-research#screening-questions) before you plan the study.

## Check that the study can be done here

Feasibility is the earliest point at which serious problems can be caught, before money and time are committed. A study that passes ethics review but cannot enrol participants, or that needs equipment the site doesn't have, stalls at activation.

Teams sometimes submit a protocol before they have thought through whether the site can carry it. A protocol submitted without a realistic feasibility assessment often raises questions during review that delay authorization. [Feasibility](/kb/planning/feasibility) costs least to check at the design stage, before the protocol is final and before contracts or budgets are negotiated.

The site should complete the [Study Feasibility Checklist](/kb/planning/feasibility#feasibility-checklist) before it agrees to take part. To set a recruitment target you can meet, [work from your own numbers](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#set-a-realistic-recruitment-target), starting from the potentially eligible people you can actually screen.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level IV studies take</div>

If your study may use the staff, facilities or platforms of the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM), consider involving the [CIM](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) at the feasibility stage, before the protocol is final and the budget set. Any researcher at The Institute can use [CIM services](/cim/services). If your study uses them, a CIM agreement must be in place.

## Design the protocol and plan the data

The protocol sets the research question, the design, the eligibility criteria, the procedures, the data management plan and the statistical analysis. The investigator writes it for an investigator-initiated study, and the sponsor provides it for a sponsored one.

For a statistical plan or a sample size, consider involving the [Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)](/kb/design-support/bcu) at the study design or grant application stage, before data collection starts, because statistical decisions are harder to change once the data are collected.

To collect the study's data in REDCap, consider involving [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/design-support/cord) at the protocol or grant stage, because the design of a database is costly to change once data collection has started. A research study needs REB approval [before its REDCap project moves to production](/kb/design-support/cord#before-your-redcap-project-collects-data) and starts collecting data.

Where you may keep the study's data, who may receive them and how you may send them depend on whether the data are published and on [how identifiable they are](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#know-how-identifiable-your-data-are). Data are anonymized only if no key exists to link them back to people.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level IV studies take</div>

For the full protocol of an investigator-initiated interventional study, ask the RI-MUHC's clinical trial unit, [Accelerating Clinical Trials–Clinical Trial Unit (ACT-CTU)](/kb/design-support/act-ctu). The ACT-CTU also runs Protocol Review Sessions, in which trialists receive real-time feedback from experts on a proposed trial. For help with a trial's methods, the RI-MUHC's [Novel Trial Methods Hub](/kb/design-support/act-ctu#get-help-with-the-methods-of-a-trial) has its own intake form.

If participants' information will leave Quebec, or the study acquires, develops or alters technology to handle it, such as an app or a wearable device, the study [may need a privacy impact assessment (EFVP)](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed). Information leaving Quebec is the trigger teams most often miss. Consider asking [efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) before the protocol is final.

## Plan the budget and the agreements

The principal investigator prepares the [study budget](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts), negotiates it and approves it. No institutional office reviews the budget. For a study the CIM runs, the CIM's budget specialists review it before the principal investigator approves it. Once agreed, the budget is written into the study's agreement.

A research agreement is needed when the study works with collaborators outside the RI-MUHC, McGill University researchers included, or receives or sends human samples or data, and in [the other cases that require one](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#know-when-the-study-needs-an-agreement). Before data go to a site outside the MUHC or come from one, all parties must sign a [data sharing or transfer agreement](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#put-an-agreement-in-place-before-data-leave-or-arrive).

Teams sometimes declare an agreement in Nagano and never make the request in My Contracts. [Upload every research agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#submit-the-agreement-in-nagano-and-my-contracts) both to Nagano and to My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal, because the upload starts the review by the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca). Researchers should never sign a binding contract or agreement before the Research Agreements Office has reviewed it. [Every agreement must be fully executed before MUHC Authorization](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#sign-the-agreement-at-the-right-time).

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level IV studies take</div>

If the study is funded wholly or partly by private enterprise, it pays the [review and authorization fees](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#budget-the-reb-and-authorization-fees) when the private funding exceeds the exemption amount. The MUHC REB counts any body governed by private law as a private enterprise, not-for-profit organizations included, so funding from a private foundation can make a study billable.

## Check credentials and training

Leading a study takes a credential as well as training. Before you submit, the principal investigator must hold Human Research Privileges, Researcher Status or a written institutional authorization. [Which credential applies](/training/compliance-requirements#know-which-credential-you-need) depends on the principal investigator's profession and on whether the study requires medical oversight.

Researcher Status is for researchers who are not members of the MUHC's Council of Physicians, Dentists, Pharmacists and Midwives, such as holders of a doctorate, nurses, physiotherapists, psychologists and radiation therapy technologists. Although pharmacists are members of the council, the MUHC requires pharmacists acting as principal investigator to obtain Researcher Status instead of Privileges. Researcher Status lets its holders lead only research that does not require medical oversight.

Everyone on the team should hold [Level IV training or a higher level](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) before signing the study's Task Delegation Log. At Level IV, the training is the SOP Reader and Assessment for Level IV, with no other mandatory training. Renew any credential or training that is [close to expiry](/training/compliance-requirements#validity-and-the-90-day-rule) before you submit.

Teams sometimes assume that the check by [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) covers everyone. During the institutional feasibility review, QA checks that each investigator who submits the study holds Privileges or Researcher Status and the level of institutional training the study requires. [Everyone listed on the Task Delegation Log](/training/compliance-requirements#who-needs-training) must hold current training for their role and for the study's risk level.

A Sub-Investigator [should be named as the principal investigator's backup](/kb/submission/preparing#check-credentials-and-training). The backup must hold the same Privileges or Researcher Status and institutional training as the principal investigator, or better.

Volunteers and other people who are not affiliated with the RI-MUHC or the MUHC [may not contact or recruit participants or perform procedures on them](/training/compliance-requirements#who-cannot-work-with-participants), because they are not covered by professional or academic liability insurance.

## Prepare the consent form and the recruitment materials

The consent form gives participants the information essential to an informed decision about taking part. Quebec's [Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:24) requires written consent for research that could interfere with a person's integrity. The REB may accept consent given another way if the circumstances justify it, and then decides how the consent is documented.

Start the consent form from the [Centre for Applied Ethics template that fits your study](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates#choose-the-consent-form-template), and follow the [rules for preparing the consent form](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form), including its English and French versions. The form needs information about any payments, and [Quebec limits what participants may be paid](/kb/recruitment/consent#compensate-participants-within-the-limits).

Every recruitment material, such as a poster, a social media post or the text of an email, [needs approval before you use it](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#have-recruitment-materials-approved). The sponsor or sponsor-investigator approves it first, then the REB, then [MUHC Communications](mailto:communications@muhc.mcgill.ca) for any material the public or patients may see.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level IV studies take</div>

If the study is interventional, it is a clinical trial, and [it must be registered in a public registry](/kb/close-out/publications#trial-registration) before the first participant is recruited. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator registers it. The consent form must give the registry's name, its web address and the trial's registration number.

If the study may include minors or adults who can't consent, [the Civil Code decides who consents for them](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent). In research that could interfere with their integrity, an REB formed or designated by Quebec's health minister must approve and monitor the research, and the MUHC REB is a designated one. [Pediatric Research Considerations](/kb/recruitment/pediatric-research) and [Research with Participants in Vulnerable Circumstances](/kb/recruitment/vulnerable-populations) cover what the study needs for them, such as assent and the representative's line on the form.

If the study will obtain consent at a distance or with an electronic consent form, [the conditions for remote and electronic consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-remotely-or-electronically) apply. Consult the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you set up the workflow.

## Wait for REB approval and MUHC Authorization

The REB reviews the study, and the MUHC's and the RI-MUHC's services carry out the [institutional feasibility review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#institutional-feasibility-review) at the same time. Full-board review is the default. Studies that involve no more than minimal risk are [eligible for delegated review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#how-a-study-is-reviewed), which runs continuously. A new project that could interfere with the integrity of a minor or of an adult who can't consent cannot go through delegated review at its first review.

While the study is under review, the reviewers write to you in Nagano. Answer the REB's requests within [the REB's deadline for replies](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#answer-the-reb-within-three-months). The <Tooltip tip="The person whom an establishment of Quebec's health network formally mandates to authorize the research conducted under its auspices." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> issues MUHC Authorization only once the study has been approved in its scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews. Where the study needs an EFVP, MUHC Authorization is given only once the EFVP is approved. Questions about MUHC Authorization go to the [Personne mandatée's office](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca).

Study teams sometimes start recruiting as soon as Nagano shows the study's approval by the REB. The REB's approval alone does not let the study begin.

<Warning>

</Warning>

## Get the site ready

MUHC Authorization doesn't yet open the study to participants. Between authorization and the first participant, tasks are delegated, the site file is kept current and the remaining start conditions are met.

The principal investigator or a delegate should [create the investigator site file](/kb/site-activation/isf#set-up-the-isf) after the sponsor or sponsor-investigator first makes contact, or as soon as a protocol outline is available, and keep it current. [Every task is delegated in writing](/kb/team/building-your-team#delegate-every-task-on-the-task-delegation-log) on the Task Delegation Log.

Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.

Recruitment begins only after [every start condition](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid) is met, including the sponsor's go letter where the sponsor issues one. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email. Once every condition is met, [recruit, consent and enrol participants](#recruit-consent-and-enrol-participants).

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level IV studies take</div>

If the study takes measurements on participants with equipment, such as blood pressure cuffs, scales or an electrocardiogram (ECG) machine, [document the equipment the study will use](/kb/site-activation/site-setup#calibrate-and-maintain-the-study-equipment). The principal investigator should make the equipment's maintenance, safety and calibration certificates available on request.

If the study collects specimens from participants, [obtain the instructions for managing them](/kb/conduct/study-operations/biological-specimens#prepare-before-specimen-collection-starts) before the study is activated, and [collect them as the protocol requires](/kb/conduct/study-operations/biological-specimens#collect-and-process-specimens).

## Recruit, consent and enrol participants

Recruitment follows set rules, and consent is a process, not only a signature. If the study needs a [recruitment plan](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#plan-your-recruitment), it is developed before the study starts. You should be [inclusive in choosing participants](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#include-participants-fairly), taking into account the scope and objectives of the research.

Before anyone is asked to consent, follow [the conditions and steps for obtaining consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-before-any-study-procedure). [Who consents](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent) depends on the participant's age and whether they can consent.

Study teams sometimes start a screening procedure before the form is fully signed.

<Warning>
All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes before all the required signatures are on the form is a deviation.
</Warning>

[Screen candidates](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#screen-candidates) in the order and on the timeline the protocol sets, and record each person on the Screening Log. The final medical decision on eligibility is ultimately the principal investigator's responsibility. [Enrol each eligible participant](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#enrol-participants-and-keep-the-logs) using the protocol's enrolment procedure, and keep the study's logs.

## Run the study

Visits take place and data are collected and entered. Record the study's data [following the ALCOA+ principles](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#follow-alcoa+), which make each record attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring and available. Record them in [source documents](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#know-what-counts-as-a-source-document). [Correct an entry](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#record-and-correct-data-properly) so that the original stays readable.

[Keep each participant's consent valid](/kb/recruitment/consent#keep-consent-current) throughout the study, and tell participants in a timely way about any important new information that may be relevant to their consent. Participants can withdraw their consent at any time, even verbally.

If a participant misses a scheduled visit and can't be reached, [the site should make every effort to contact them](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#contact-a-participant-who-misses-a-visit). Keep [participants' information confidential](/kb/governance/privacy#keep-participant-information-confidential), and keep and send the data [with the controls for their kind](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#apply-the-controls-for-each-kind-of-data).

Record every change to the team on the Task Delegation Log, as [Team Management During a Study](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management) describes.

## Report events and deviations

While participants take part, some events must be reported to the REB in Nagano, each by its own deadline. [The events to report to the REB](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#report-participant-safety-and-ethical-concerns) include a change to the research's risks or potential benefits, an unanticipated problem that could significantly affect the conduct of the research at the site, and a concern a participant raises about their rights or about ethical issues in the research.

Each report has [its deadline](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines). Remove all participant identifiers from every report to the REB.

If participants' information is lost or seen by the wrong person, [report the incident to the REB in Nagano](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person) as soon as you become aware of it. As applicable, also report it to the appropriate organizational officials as soon as you become aware of it.

Every protocol deviation [must be assessed, addressed and documented](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations), whether it is minor, major or critical, and reported as its tier requires. Every major or critical deviation [should have a corrective and preventive action (CAPA)](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management#working-through-a-capa), captured on the RI-MUHC's CAPA form.

[Notify the REB of any planned audit or inspection](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#tell-the-reb-about-audits-and-inspections) of the study. [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) conducts [process audits](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#ri-muhc-quality-assurance-audits), and may conduct for-cause audits when non-compliance is reported. A principal investigator can ask [QA](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) to audit their study.

<div className="hub-steps">Steps only some Level IV studies take</div>

In an interventional study, every adverse event must be [assessed and recorded](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#assess-and-report-adverse-events) on the AE Assessment Log and in the source documents. Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.

At a site under the MUHC REB's approval, a serious adverse event also needs an expedited report to the REB, by [the REB's deadline](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines), if the principal investigator judges that it meets [the REB's three criteria](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#assess-and-report-adverse-events).

If another Quebec REB reviews the study, [report each serious adverse event at the MUHC that is unexpected and is related or possibly related to the study](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines) to that REB by its own procedure. Without waiting for the reviewing REB's decision, also report it in Nagano to the MUHC REB and the MUHC's Personne mandatée.

In any study the MUHC REB reviews, a serious adverse event at a site outside its approval also needs a report to the REB if the principal investigator judges that it meets [the REB's four criteria](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#report-an-sae-at-another-site-to-the-reb).

If a participant in an interventional study becomes pregnant, [collect and follow the exposure in utero reports](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#track-a-pregnancy-during-a-trial) to learn the outcome, even without an adverse event.

## Keep REB approval current

Studies change as they run, and amendments are routine. While an amendment is under review, the study continues on the documents the REB has already approved.

Before you change the protocol, the consent form or another approved document, submit an [amendment](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments) in Nagano and [wait for every approval](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#wait-for-every-approval-before-you-implement-the-amendment) before you implement it. A change needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants may be made before the REB's approval, and must be reported to the REB immediately.

Not every amendment requires participants to re-sign the consent form. When one does, [re-consent each affected participant](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#re-consent-participants-on-an-amended-consent-form) on the amended form once the REB has approved it in writing and the sponsor's go letter has arrived, if there is one.

The REB sets how long its approval lasts. Request the continuing review in Nagano by [the deadline the RI-MUHC's SOPs set before the approval expires](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid). If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk.

## Close the study and archive the records

A study doesn't end with the last participant's visit. When the [conditions for closure](/kb/close-out/close-out#notifying-the-reb-and-health-canada) are met, such as no participants remaining involved at the site and all data collection complete, notify the REB and the personne mandatée with the Closure of Research Project form (F10) in Nagano. The study is closed when the REB acknowledges the form.

If the study stops before its planned end, [notify the REB and the organization promptly](/kb/close-out/close-out#if-the-study-stops-early), with a detailed explanation.

Archive the study's documents for [the retention period that applies to your study](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving), and the data on individuals in [a secure, institutionally approved environment](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#archive-the-data-when-the-study-ends).

Researchers shall [make their results public](/kb/close-out/publications), including results that don't support the research hypotheses, in a timely manner and without undue restriction. [Decide who is an author](/kb/close-out/publications#decide-who-is-an-author) of each publication.
