## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Where do I start if I want to conduct a study at the MUHC?">
    Start with a [feasibility assessment](/kb/planning/feasibility) — before any submission, confirm that your protocol is scientifically and ethically sound, that your team has the required qualifications, and that the facilities and patient population are available. Once ready, submissions go through **Nagano** using the F11 form, which simultaneously triggers REB review and institutional feasibility review. Confirm your Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status before any activities begin.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What's the difference between the REB and the Personne mandatée?">
    The **Research Ethics Board (REB)** conducts the scientific and ethics review — participant safety, informed consent, risk–benefit ratio, and methodological soundness. REB approval is valid for one year and must be renewed annually.

    The **Personne mandatée** is the MUHC institutional authority who issues the final authorization to conduct research. This authorization is granted only after all reviews are complete — REB approval, feasibility reviews, and any required Health Canada approvals. Conditional REB approval is not sufficient. The two roles are independent — REB approval does not automatically confer MUHC authorization.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does my study need Health Canada approval?">
    It depends on whether your study involves an investigational product. **Investigational drug or biologic** → Clinical Trial Application (CTA), No Objection Letter (NOL). **Medical device** (Class II, III, or IV) → Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA). **Natural health product** → NHP CTA, Notice of Authorization (NOA).

    Observational studies and studies using biological specimens only — with no investigational product — do not require a Health Canada submission. When in doubt, contact the Research Facilitator early in your planning process.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What training do I need to join a study team?">
    Training requirements depend on study type. All research staff must complete the **RI-MUHC SOP Reader** (tracked acknowledgement of each applicable SOP via TalentLMS) and the **Competency Assessment** (renewed every 2 years for interventional studies, every 5 years for observational or retrospective work).

    For drug studies (Level I), **ICH GCP** and **Health Canada Division 5** training are also required. Device studies (Level II) require GCP and **ISO 14155**. No team member may perform any study task before their training is documented and they appear on the Task Delegation Log. See [Training Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What is a Task Delegation Log and why does it matter?">
    The **Task Delegation Log (TDL)** is the central compliance document for every study at the RI-MUHC. It records who is authorized to perform which tasks and confirms that each person has the required training. No person may perform any study task before they appear on the TDL, their training is documented, and the QI/PI has countersigned their entry.

    The TDL is an inspection-facing document — during an audit or sponsor monitoring visit, it will be one of the first documents reviewed. Gaps (tasks performed before delegation, undocumented training, missing signatures) are among the most common inspection findings.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How does a new researcher get Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status?">
    **Human Research Privileges** are required for CPDP members (physicians and dentists) acting as QI or PI on studies involving medical oversight — drug trials, device studies, or protocols involving acts reserved to physicians. **Researcher Status** applies to non-CPDP researchers (PhDs, nurses, physiotherapists) conducting studies that do not require medical oversight.

    Both are applied for through TalentLMS and managed by the Performance Office. Applications are typically processed within 1–5 business days. Contact [riprograms@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:riprograms@muhc.mcgill.ca) or speak with the Research Facilitator.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a fellow or rotating resident be on my study team?">
    Yes, but with important restrictions. Fellows and rotating residents **cannot conduct research independently** or serve as QI/PI backup. Any medical acts they perform as part of a study must be supervised by a QI/PI or qualified Sub-Investigator who holds Human Research Privileges. They must still appear on the Task Delegation Log with their training documented, and their delegation must be clearly scoped to tasks appropriate to their role.

    Because residents and fellows rotate, their departure must be tracked and the TDL updated promptly.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What do I do if I identify a protocol deviation?">
    First, **document it and notify the Sponsor as soon as possible**. Then classify the deviation: minor deviations are logged and reported to the REB at annual renewal; major deviations must be reported to the REB within 15 days with a CAPA; critical deviations within 7 days with a CAPA.

    A CAPA (Corrective and Preventive Action) form is required for major and critical deviations — submit to QA and include with the REB deviation report. Remove all participant nominative information and use study number only. See [Reportable Events and Protocol Deviations](/kb/conduct/reportable-events).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Starting a new study?

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm your credentials">
    Your Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status and institutional training must be current and not within 90 days of expiry before submission. [Check requirements →](/training/compliance-requirements)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assess feasibility">
    Confirm your protocol is scientifically sound, your team is in place, and the site can support the protocol's demands. [Feasibility guide →](/kb/planning/feasibility)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare your submission">
    Assemble your protocol, ICF, recruitment materials, and Health Canada authorization documents if applicable. [Submission checklist →](/kb/submission/preparing)
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit via Nagano">
    Use the F11 form to open your study file and simultaneously trigger REB review and institutional feasibility review. [Nagano guide →](/apps/nagano/overview)
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  A study cannot begin until the **Personne mandatée** grants MUHC Authorization. REB approval alone does not authorize research activities — both approvals must be in your ISF before any study procedures begin.
</Note>
