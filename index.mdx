## Find the steps for your type of study

Studies at the MUHC range from reviews of patients' charts to drug trials. Each of these studies is [clinical research](/kb/foundations/clinical-research-101#tell-research-apart-from-clinical-care): research involving human participants, their data or their biological material. A research study that develops or tests software, such as an app or an algorithm, with participants or their data is clinical research too. Find the type that matches yours to see the steps it takes, stage by stage.

## Where is your study now?

Most clinical studies at the RI-MUHC go through the same stages in the same order: feasibility, design and planning, submission and review, site activation, recruitment, conduct, close-out and archiving. Retrospective studies, such as chart reviews and analyses of databases, have fewer stages: feasibility, submission and ethics review, conduct, closing the study in Nagano, and archiving.

## Submit your study and follow its review in Nagano

Every study at the MUHC is submitted and managed in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>, from the first submission to the study completion report. After you submit, the reviewers write to you there. Check your project regularly until the study receives <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>.

Study teams sometimes start recruiting as soon as Nagano shows the study's approval by the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip>. [MUHC Authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview) is issued only once the study has been approved in its scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews, and by Health Canada where it needs that authorization.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

## Get help designing and running your study

Units at the RI-MUHC and the MUHC help with a study's design, its data and its conduct. Consider involving them while you plan the study, before the protocol is final:

<ul className="hub-home-services">
  <li>The <a href="/kb/design-support/bcu">Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)</a> gives statistical advice across a whole study, from the grant application and the design to the analysis and the publications. Its services include sample size calculations, randomization plans and statistical analysis plans. Statistical decisions are harder to change once the data are collected.</li>
  <li><a href="/kb/design-support/cord">Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)</a> builds and hosts REDCap databases for RI-MUHC studies. CORD also reviews data plans, case report forms and questionnaires, and helps teams manage their data to the end of the project.</li>
  <li>While they plan a study, researchers at the RI-MUHC can explore synthetic <a href="/kb/design-support/patient-data">MUHC patient data</a> in MDClone to estimate cohort sizes and test eligibility criteria, with no approval from the REB. The MUHC <a href="mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca">Data Warehouse team</a> can help define a protocol's patient selection criteria, sample size and variables. Once the study has REB approval and MUHC Authorization, researchers can request real patient data from the Data Warehouse. Aggregate data can be requested at any time, under the Data Warehouse's <em>Guidelines on Aggregate Data Requests</em>.</li>
  <li>The <a href="/cim/services">Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)</a> provides staff, facilities and technical platforms for pediatric and adult clinical research at the Glen site and the Montreal General Hospital. The CIM can run an industry-sponsored study from start to finish, or provide individual services for a study initiated by its <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip>. Any researcher at The Institute can use CIM services.</li>
  <li>The RI-MUHC's <a href="/kb/design-support/act-ctu#get-help-with-the-methods-of-a-trial">Novel Trial Methods Hub</a> helps investigators with the methods of a trial at the level of the protocol: framing the research question, optimizing the design, checking the protocol's feasibility and using new or non-standard trial designs.</li>
  <li>The <a href="/kb/design-support/act-ctu">Accelerating Clinical Trials–Clinical Trial Unit (ACT-CTU)</a> is the RI-MUHC's clinical trial unit. In its Protocol Review Sessions, trialists receive real-time feedback from experts on a proposed trial. The unit also helps RI-MUHC researchers start trials that have sites in other provinces.</li>
</ul>
