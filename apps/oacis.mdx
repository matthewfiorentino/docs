OACIS is the MUHC's <Tooltip tip="A digital record of patients' health information kept within one institution." headline="Electronic Medical Record (EMR)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#electronic-medical-record-emr">electronic medical record</Tooltip>. Research teams use it in two ways. Research staff and investigators may need patients' records for a study, for example to pre-screen patients or for research on health records. The sponsor's monitors use it to verify study data during monitoring visits.

[MUHC Medical Records Services](mailto:archiviste.mgh@muhc.mcgill.ca) is responsible for access to the information in the health records of the MUHC's patients who take part in research.

## Sign the MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement before access

<Warning>
Every person who accesses OACIS for research, whether a monitor, a coordinator or an investigator, must sign the MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement before access is granted.
</Warning>

[The agreement](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=105487170812722201) is an institutional requirement for access to the MUHC network and to patients' medical records. The MUHC agreement is separate from any confidentiality agreement with the sponsor. The agreement covers obligations on the use of data, confidentiality incidents and the security of systems. [Send the signed agreement to Medical Records Services](/kb/governance/privacy#keep-participant-information-confidential).

## Get OACIS access and training

Research staff who need OACIS access for a study request it from the Health Records (Archives) department at their site: [archiviste.rvh@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:archiviste.rvh@muhc.mcgill.ca) for the Glen site, or [archiviste.mgh@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:archiviste.mgh@muhc.mcgill.ca) for the Montreal General Hospital. Health Records asks why you need access, for documents showing an approved study that requires it, and for your ID. Before your access goes live, it has you meet an OACIS trainer or asks whether you need training.

MUHC clinicians already have OACIS access, with the same view as clinician-researchers.

<Warning>
Having access to OACIS does not give you the right to use a patient's chart for your research. Before you use a chart for research, the study needs the approval of the Research Ethics Board (REB) and MUHC Authorization, which includes access to the records.
</Warning>

## Researcher access for pre-screening and records research

Before you look at patients' records to find people for your study, or use their records without their consent, the study needs the approval of the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, which includes access to the records. Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information (Law 5) governs this access.

The request for access is part of the study's Nagano submission. [Follow the steps for using health records without consent](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent). The steps include the questions on access to participant information in Nagano and the privacy impact assessment (<Tooltip tip="The privacy impact assessment, or évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée, that Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information requires with a researcher's request for access to health and social services information without consent, including access before consent for pre-screening." headline="EFVP (privacy impact assessment)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#efvp">EFVP</Tooltip>). [An EFVP is required for access to identifiable health information before consent (pre-screening) or without consent](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed). An EFVP may also be required in other cases, such as when participants' information leaves Quebec. Questions about the EFVP go to [efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca).

[Monitors' access](#requesting-access-for-external-monitors) goes through [Medical Records](mailto:archiviste.mgh@muhc.mcgill.ca) instead.

## Requesting access for external monitors

The sponsor's monitors check the data recorded in the case report forms against participants' medical records, and confirm that participants exist, across all MUHC sites. [Medical Records](mailto:archiviste.mgh@muhc.mcgill.ca) can give an external monitor temporary, restricted access to OACIS, limited to the study's patient list.

Before an ongoing monitoring visit, [make sure that the monitor has documented OACIS training](/sops/cr-013#5-8-7), if not already trained, and appropriate access to OACIS, if required. Organize and document the monitor's OACIS training, or obtain documentation of it.

Medical Records requires [five working days' notice](/sops/cr-013#5-9-7) for every monitor's request for a new OACIS ID, access to a new patient list or more patients on a current list. Medical Records needs that time to build the lists and to train a monitor before the first on-site monitoring visit. Consider confirming that the monitor's access is in place before the visit date is set.

### Documents required for the initial request

Submit the initial request to MUHC Medical Records at the Glen site. [Medical Records' generic address for the Montreal Children's Hospital, the Montreal General Hospital and the Royal Victoria Hospital](/sops/cr-013#5-9-7) is [archiviste.mgh@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:archiviste.mgh@muhc.mcgill.ca). The request must include:

- The MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement, signed by the monitor.
- The DPS Chart Request form ([SOP-CR-013, Appendix 2b](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=51243988255236718)), completed and signed by the monitor, the <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip> and the supervisor. Send it for approval and signature to the Director of Medical and Professional Services (DMPS), formerly the Director of Professional Services (DPS). The DMPS's office is at [cathy.martell@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:cathy.martell@muhc.mcgill.ca).
- A letter from the principal investigator requesting the monitor's access to OACIS, on the [Monitor OACIS Access Request](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=51243631491223164) (SOP-CR-013, Appendix 2). The letter gives the reason the monitor needs access, the monitor's role, the study number and a list of the participants' medical record numbers (MRNs).
- A copy of the study's MUHC Authorization letter.
- A copy of the study's initial REB approval letter.

The list of MRNs must be updated regularly.

Medical Records then tells the OACIS team that the request is approved, and gives the monitor's name and how long the access will last. The OACIS Training and Support team creates the monitor's OACIS ID, with a termination date, and tells Medical Records.

From a list the <Tooltip tip="A nurse, health professional or other qualified member of the research team who handles most of the administrative responsibilities and day-to-day activities of a clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">clinical research coordinator</Tooltip> provides, Medical Records creates a restricted OACIS patient list of the participants recruited into the study. The OACIS team then arranges OACIS training for a monitor who has not been trained. Keep the monitor's documented training in the monitoring section of the <Tooltip tip="The qualified or principal investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines, so that the conduct and integrity of the study and the safety of participants at the site can be verified." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">Investigator Site File (ISF)</Tooltip>.

### Keep monitor access current

- [The coordinator or a delegate must renew the monitor's OACIS ID each year](/sops/cr-013#5-9-6) by sending the study's REB annual renewal approval to [Medical Records](mailto:archiviste.mgh@muhc.mcgill.ca).
- When new participants are enrolled, send Medical Records the updated list of MRNs.
- When a monitor no longer works on the study, notify Medical Records and ask them to remove the monitor's access to OACIS. Arrange access for the incoming monitor before their first visit.

## Reach OACIS remotely for a visit or audit at the CIM

For a monitoring visit or a sponsor audit hosted by the <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's Centre for Innovative Medicine, which provides clinical research services, facilities and technical platforms at the Glen site and the Montreal General Hospital." headline="Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#centre-for-innovative-medicine-cim">Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)</Tooltip>, monitors and auditors reach OACIS through the MUHC's Citrix environment, IRIAS3. Monitors and auditors use it where source data are reviewed remotely or from within the CIM's facilities. To connect:

1. Install the Citrix Workspace app from [Citrix's download page](https://www.citrix.com/downloads/workspace-app/). Citrix recommends installing and using the app with Google Chrome. If the installation asks for a server name or an email address at the end, cancel: there is nothing to enter.
2. Go to [irias3.muhc.mcgill.ca](https://irias3.muhc.mcgill.ca) and log in with the MUHC network credentials that the CIM's audit lead provides.
3. On the Additional Information Required screen, enter the 4-digit personal identification number (PIN) sent to your mobile phone.
4. Open the Clinical Applications folder, then the OACIS-EMR icon. Log in with your OACIS login and password.

While you use the remote access:

- External users cannot be connected to the institute's server.
- Do not download, save or print records from OACIS or the MUHC shared drive, and do not take screen captures of them. Request any copies from the CIM's audit host.
- After three failed login attempts, the system locks you out, with no message. Write to the help desk at [c2system@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:c2system@muhc.mcgill.ca) to regain access.
- Log out at the end of the day.

## Access rules and restrictions

OACIS access for research follows these rules, whatever the kind of access:

- Access is restricted to the records of participants enrolled in, or being considered for, the study for which access was granted. Browsing records beyond what the study requires is prohibited.
- All research access is authorized in advance, through [Medical Records](mailto:archiviste.mgh@muhc.mcgill.ca) for monitors or through the study's Nagano submission for researchers.
- Access may be revoked if confidentiality obligations are not met.

[All MUHC medical records in OACIS are kept for at least 15 years](/sops/cr-008#5-3-1). [Medical Records Services keeps research participants' health records](/kb/governance/privacy#know-who-keeps-the-records-after-the-study) for its own period, which the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) set. Signed informed consent forms filed in OACIS are part of the participant's permanent medical record.
