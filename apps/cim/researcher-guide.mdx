## About CIM

The McConnell Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM) is a dedicated clinical research facility operating within the MUHC hospital environment at the Glen site and the Montreal General Hospital. CIM provides specialized staffing, clinical infrastructure, and technical platforms to support the conduct of clinical trials — from Phase I drug studies and oncology trials to functional testing and specimen processing.

CIM staff work alongside study teams whose QIs and CRCs are bound by the same RI-MUHC SOPs that govern all clinical research at the Institute. What distinguishes the CIM environment is the additional layer of CIM-specific Work Instructions that govern clinical procedures, safety and emergency preparedness, and monitoring activities carried out within CIM's facilities.

<Note>
**Two sites.** CIM operates at the Glen (Block B4 and C4) and the Montreal General Hospital. Some Work Instructions and training courses are site-specific. Confirm with your manager which site-specific requirements apply to your role.
</Note>

## Role tracks

CIM staff work across several distinct role tracks. Most staff are active in one primary track, though some roles span more than one. Your manager will confirm which Work Instructions and training courses apply to your position.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Research — CRC and CRC-N">
    Clinical Research Coordinators and Clinical Research Coordinator-Nurses are the operational core of CIM study teams. CRCs manage study documentation, participant coordination, and regulatory compliance. CRC-Ns additionally hold nursing credentials that allow them to perform clinical procedures — IV infusion, blood draws, ECGs, vital signs — under delegation from the QI. The scope of delegated tasks must be documented on the study Task Delegation Log and must reflect each CRC-N's actual qualifications and professional licence.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Oncology and Phase I — Oncology nursing">
    CRC-Ns and infusion nurses working in the oncology or Phase I programs must complete a competency-based educational program specific to chemotherapy and biotherapy administration before performing these tasks. This is a distinct requirement beyond GCP and standard nursing qualifications. Safe handling of hazardous drugs is a separate but related requirement covered by WI-CIM-ON002. Staff in this track must follow both WIs and must not administer study chemotherapy or biotherapy before completing the required competency program.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Monitoring — CRA and project management">
    For Sponsor-Investigator studies conducted through CIM, designated CIM staff act as Clinical Research Associates (CRAs) and project managers responsible for monitoring. This includes site feasibility visits, site initiation visits, ongoing monitoring visits, close-out visits, and IP accountability monitoring. The MO series Work Instructions govern these activities. Staff in this track work to the same ICH GCP monitoring standards as external CRAs.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Laboratory — Specimen processing and technical platforms">
    Staff working in the CIM Containment Level II Processing Laboratory, imaging facilities, or functional testing platforms operate under a separate set of SOPs and Work Instructions specific to their platform. Laboratory access requires completion of external training certificates (PHAC General Safety, CITI Transport of Dangerous Goods where applicable) as well as CIM-specific lab safety training and SOP/WI reading on TalentLMS. See the [CIM Training](/apps/cim/training) page for the full lab access pathway.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Administrative — Emergency preparedness and safety">
    All CIM staff — regardless of role track — are required to complete emergency preparedness training covering Code Blue (cardiac and respiratory emergencies), Code Red and Code Green (fire and evacuation) for their site, and UBEC (Unit Based Emergency Cart) verification where assigned. These are not optional. CRC-Ns must hold current CPR certification before working in clinical areas. Emergency training is covered by dedicated courses on TalentLMS.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Your responsibilities

CIM staff operate under distinct but overlapping layers of obligation. Which layers apply depends on your role track, but the principle is consistent: the CIM operational layer does not replace the SOP layer, and professional credentials impose obligations that exist independently of both.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Layer 1 — RI-MUHC SOPs">
    The same standard operating procedures that apply to all RI-MUHC clinical research staff. These cover roles and responsibilities, training requirements, ICF processes, AE reporting, ISF management, IP handling, monitoring, and close-out. CIM staff follow these SOPs in the same way as any other study team member. GCP training and RI-MUHC institutional training (SOP Reader and Competency Assessment on TalentLMS) are required at the level appropriate to your study type. See [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements) for the full pathway.

    **Applies to:** CRCs, CRC-Ns, monitors, and all staff active on clinical research studies.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Layer 2a — CIM Work Instructions">
    CIM-specific procedures that govern how work is conducted within CIM's clinical and research environment. Work Instructions cover clinical procedures (IV infusion, TIVAD access, chemotherapy administration), safety and emergency management (Code Blue, Code Red/Green, UBEC verification), study team coordination (study briefings, nurse delegation), monitoring visit conduct, and IP accountability. WIs are version-controlled documents accessed via the [RI-MUHC Portal](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:420) and tracked through the CIM Work Instructions Reader on TalentLMS.

    **Applies to:** CRCs, CRC-Ns, monitors, oncology nursing staff, and all staff working in CIM clinical areas.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Layer 2b — Technical Platform SOPs and WIs">
    A separate set of SOPs and Work Instructions governing the CIM technical platforms: the Containment Level II Processing Laboratory (SOP_CL and WI_TEC series), imaging facilities, and functional testing platforms. These documents cover lab safety, equipment use and maintenance, specimen handling, and platform-specific procedures. They are accessed via TalentLMS (lab SOPs) and the [RI-MUHC Portal](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:389) (functional testing WIs). Imaging documents are available on the shared drive.

    **Applies to:** Lab technicians, specimen processing staff, imaging staff, and functional testing platform staff.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Layer 3 — Professional credentials">
    For CRC-Ns, infusion nurses, and other clinically credentialled staff, your professional licence and scope of practice impose obligations that exist independently of the SOP and WI layers. You are responsible for knowing the limits of your practice and for refusing tasks that fall outside your scope, regardless of what a study protocol or QI delegation may specify. The QI retains ultimate responsibility for the study, but that does not shield you from personal accountability for clinical acts you perform.

    **Applies to:** CRC-Ns, infusion nurses, research nurses, and any clinically licenced staff.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Study briefings and nurse delegation

When nurses are assigned general nursing tasks on a study (blood draws, vital signs, ECG), the lead CRC or CRC-N prepares a Study Briefing — a version-controlled document summarizing key protocol information, IP safety details, and relevant safety data for the assigned nurses. Nurses review the Study Briefing and sign the General Nursing Task Delegation Log before performing delegated tasks.

Study Briefings are required whenever external nursing resources are used. The Study Briefing process is governed by WI-CIM-GN005 and is separate from the main study Task Delegation Log used for protocol-specific clinical trial tasks.

<Warning>
**Pre-visit safety check.** Before any study visit involving an investigational product, the QI is responsible for confirming IP side effects, ensuring emergency equipment is ready, and verifying that qualified staff are available. The Safety Checklist Prior to Study Visit (WI-CIM-AD001 Appendix 1) is completed before each such visit. CIM staff should be familiar with this requirement and flag any gaps to the QI before the visit begins.
</Warning>

## Training requirements

CIM training requirements vary by role track. Clinical research staff follow the RI-MUHC training pathway plus CIM-specific courses for their clinical environment. Lab and platform staff follow a different set of requirements centred on the Technical Platform SOPs and external safety certificates. Confirm with your manager which courses apply to your position. All completion is tracked in TalentLMS.

### RI-MUHC clinical research training

CIM staff active on clinical research studies must complete the standard RI-MUHC training pathway at the level appropriate to their study type: SOP Reader, Competency Assessment, and any required external training (GCP via CITI, Health Canada Division 5 for Level I drug trials). Lab and platform staff who are not on study teams are not required to complete this pathway. See [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements) for the full pathway and renewal cycle.

### Laboratory access

Access to the CIM specimen processing laboratory requires completion of external training certificates in addition to the TalentLMS courses above. Requirements vary by the type of access needed. See the [CIM Training](/apps/cim/training) page for the full lab access pathway, certificate requirements, and the process for requesting access from the CIM Laboratory and Test Center Supervisor.
