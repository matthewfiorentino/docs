CIM is built for interventional studies requiring dedicated clinical infrastructure, specialized nursing, or technical platforms that a standard department setting cannot provide. The question is not just whether your study is interventional — it is whether the specific procedures, staffing needs, or platforms in your protocol require what CIM offers.

## Study types and CIM fit

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Phase I drug studies — Core mandate">
    Phase I trials are CIM's primary clinical mandate. These studies typically require intensive participant monitoring, frequent blood sampling (PK), IV drug administration, and close medical oversight during dosing visits. CIM's dedicated Phase I capacity at the Glen site is specifically designed for this.

    **Capacity is limited and pre-booking is common — engage CIM as early as possible.**

    Verify: confirm whether MGH also accepts Phase I, or whether Phase I is Glen-only.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Oncology trials — Core mandate">
    Oncology studies — including studies involving IV chemotherapy, biotherapy, and immunotherapy as study drugs — are a core part of CIM's portfolio. CIM has dedicated oncology nursing staff with competency-based certification for hazardous drug administration. Both the Glen and MGH sites support oncology studies, with site assignment depending on the patient population and department affiliation.

    Verify: confirm whether there are disease-site or department restrictions at each site.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Industry-sponsored trials requiring IV administration or infusion — Strong fit">
    Any industry-sponsored trial where the protocol requires IV drug administration, IV infusions, or access to a dedicated infusion centre is a strong candidate for CIM involvement. This includes biologics, monoclonal antibodies, and other infused study drugs outside oncology. CIM provides the infusion infrastructure, nursing staff, and monitoring environment that most departments cannot replicate independently.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sponsor-Investigator studies — Strong fit">
    Investigator-initiated studies where the PI acts as Sponsor-Investigator are well-supported at CIM. CIM can provide both clinical execution and monitoring infrastructure for these studies, including CRA/monitoring services for SI studies that lack a commercial sponsor. Budget planning is especially important for SI studies — CIM costs must be built into the initial budget before the CTA is filed.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Studies requiring functional testing — Likely fit">
    Studies that include cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET), pulmonary function testing (PFT), ECG, body composition assessment, or other functional testing procedures may be a fit for CIM's Functional Testing Platform at the Glen site. This platform provides specialized equipment and trained personnel for these assessments as part of study visit procedures.

    Verify: confirm which functional testing procedures CIM accepts on an individual study basis, and whether there is a minimum study complexity requirement.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Studies requiring specimen processing or biobanking — Likely fit">
    Studies requiring biological specimen processing, storage, or shipping may be able to use CIM's Containment Level II Processing Laboratory. This is relevant for studies collecting blood, tissue, or other biological samples that require processing beyond standard clinical lab capabilities.

    Verify: confirm whether the lab accepts external study specimens on a fee-for-service basis or only for studies with full CIM involvement, and what the access criteria are.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Observational and prospective cohort studies — Partial fit">
    Observational studies without interventional procedures generally do not require CIM involvement. However, if your observational study includes procedures that CIM specializes in — such as functional testing, specimen collection, or imaging — CIM may support those specific components. Discuss with CIM whether a service-only arrangement makes sense.

    Verify: confirm CIM's policy on partial or service-only engagement for observational studies.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Medical device studies — Verify with CIM">
    Whether CIM supports device studies depends on the nature of the device and procedures involved. Studies involving implantable or surgical devices are unlikely to be within CIM's scope. Studies using wearable devices, monitoring devices, or diagnostic equipment as part of a protocol that otherwise fits CIM's profile may be considered.

    Verify: confirm whether CIM accepts device studies and under what conditions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Natural health product studies — Verify with CIM">
    CIM's primary focus is on drug and biologic clinical trials. Whether natural health product studies fall within scope depends on the procedures involved. If your NHP study requires the same infrastructure as a drug trial — IV administration, intensive monitoring, dedicated nursing — CIM may be appropriate.

    Verify: confirm whether CIM accepts NHP studies and whether there are study-type restrictions.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Glen vs MGH — what each site supports

CIM operates at two sites. Many studies can be conducted at either site, but some capabilities are specific to one location. Your CIM feasibility discussion will confirm which site is appropriate for your protocol.

| Capability | Glen (Blocks B4 and C4) | MGH |
|---|---|---|
| Phase I unit | Yes — dedicated Phase I capacity | Verify |
| Oncology infusion | Yes — adult oncology (Block C) | Yes — confirm with CIM |
| Paediatric studies | Yes — paediatric side (Block B) | Verify |
| Functional testing platform | Yes — full platform (CPET, PFT, ECG, body composition, imaging) | Verify — limited or not available |
| Specimen processing lab | Yes — Containment Level II | Yes — Containment Level II |
| General IV infusion | Yes | Yes — confirm capacity |
| Research monitoring (SI studies) | Yes | Yes |

Site capabilities are confirmed during the CIM feasibility assessment. This table is a general guide only — verify with CIM before making site selection decisions.

## Capacity and timing

CIM has finite capacity, particularly for Phase I studies. Understanding timing constraints before committing to a protocol timeline is important.

- **Phase I capacity is limited and pre-booking is common.** If your Phase I study has a tight activation timeline, contact CIM during protocol development — not after REB submission.
- **Oncology and infusion capacity varies by period.** Availability depends on concurrent study volume. CIM will confirm capacity during the feasibility review.
- **CIM costs must be in your initial budget.** Adding CIM after the Site Agreement is signed requires a budget amendment, which delays activation. The feasibility conversation and budget estimate must happen in parallel with protocol review — before REB submission and before sponsor confirmation.
- **The Site Initiation Visit gates participant recruitment.** CIM staff are formally onboarded at the SIV. Recruitment cannot begin until the SIV is complete and Nagano approval is confirmed.

<Warning>
**The most common source of delay is late engagement.** If you are reading this page while your study is already in REB review or your Site Agreement is being negotiated, contact CIM immediately. A budget amendment is always possible, but it costs time.
</Warning>

## Not sure? Start here

If you have reviewed this page and are still unsure whether CIM is right for your study, the right move is to contact CIM directly. The feasibility conversation is not a commitment — it is a preliminary discussion to determine whether CIM's services are appropriate and available for your protocol.
