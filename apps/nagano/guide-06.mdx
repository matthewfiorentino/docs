# F11 Part 4 — Recruitment, Enrolment and Consent

This section covers Part 4 of the F11 form: Recruitment, Enrolment and Consent.

## Part 4: Recruitment, Enrolment and Consent

This part covers how participants will be identified, approached, and enrolled, and how consent will be obtained. It also asks about study visits, compensation, and the materials you will use to recruit. Reviewers use these responses to assess whether your recruitment approach is ethical, operationally feasible, and consistent with the rest of your submission.

## Section 1 — Participant study visits

Describe what participants will be asked to do across the course of the study. Be as specific and realistic as possible — reviewers and feasibility assessors will use this information to evaluate burden and resource needs.

- **Number of visits** — If the number of visits is known, enter the planned total. If it varies by participant (e.g., early withdrawal is expected), describe the typical case.
- **Visit duration** — If duration is consistent, provide an average. If it varies by visit type (screening, treatment, follow-up), break it down and name each visit type.
- **If you cannot estimate** — explain why in a way that makes sense to a feasibility reviewer. Vague or blank entries may generate follow-up questions.

<Note>
If participants are patients receiving care, align visit descriptions with clinical realities — feasibility reviewers will often be the departments providing that care.
</Note>

## Section 2 — Participant compensation

Indicate whether and how participants will be compensated. Nagano asks for the compensation type, amount, rationale, and method of payment. Ensure everything here is consistent with the consent form and any recruitment documents.

### Compensation type

- **Monetary compensation** — for losses and constraints participants may experience (time, inconvenience, discomfort). Specify the amount and explain how it was established — what elements were considered (e.g., visit duration, number of procedures, burden relative to standard care). Indicate whether the amount is proportional to study length.
- **Reimbursement of expenses** — for out-of-pocket costs incurred by participating. Specify which expenses will be covered (parking, travel, meals, accommodation, babysitting, or other) and provide reimbursement details including the method of payment.
- **Other compensation** — describe clearly what will be provided and how it relates to participation.
- **No compensation** — a justification is required. Explain why compensation is not being offered (e.g., minimal burden, procedures aligned with standard care visits participants would attend regardless).

<Warning>
**Consistency check:** The compensation type, amount, and method described here must match exactly what appears in the consent form and any recruitment materials. Inconsistencies between documents are a common review flag.
</Warning>

## Section 3 — Methods of recruitment

This section covers two steps: how potential participants will be identified before contact (pre-selection), and how the first approach will be made (initial contact). Check all methods that apply for each step.

### Part A — Pre-selection (identifying potential participants)

Options include a research bank, departmental or government database (e.g., RAMQ, MED-ÉCHO), medical records, or other. If you select a bank, ensure your answer is consistent with the "Special characteristics" field elsewhere in the form. For database options, Nagano will ask you to specify which one.

<Warning>
**Consistency check:** If you indicate use of a bank here, make sure your answer matches what you have entered under "Special characteristics." Reviewers will cross-reference these fields.
</Warning>

### Part B — Initial contact (approaching prospective participants)

Options include letter, phone, direct contact during a consultation, advertising material (posters, leaflets, electronic ads), or other (including social media). Any materials used to approach participants must be uploaded in the Project Files section.

<Note>
**MUHC context — circle of care:** When feasible, patients should first be informed by someone in their circle of care — such as a bedside nurse or attending physician — that a research team may approach them. If your study will not follow this approach, be prepared to explain why.
</Note>

<Warning>
**Multi-department recruitment:** If participants will be identified or approached through departments outside the PI's home department, or at another centre, obtain department head authorization from each department or centre involved before recruitment begins.
</Warning>

## Section 4 — Terms of consent

Select how consent will be obtained. Choose the option that matches what you will actually do in practice.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Signed informed consent form — standard written consent with signature">
    The default for most clinical research. The participant reads, signs, and receives a copy of the consent form before any study procedures begin. Use a consent template from the **Centre for Applied Ethics (CAE)**.

    The consent form uploaded to Nagano must match the version described in this section.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Electronic informed consent form — digital consent process">
    Use when consent will be obtained through a validated electronic platform. The electronic form must be equivalent in content to a written consent form. Describe the platform and process clearly in the submission.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Verbal consent — no written signature, requires justification">
    Acceptable when there are valid reasons for not requiring consent in writing (e.g., telephone-only studies, minimal-risk procedures). **A justification is required.** The methods used to obtain verbal consent must be recorded in the participant's file (TCPS 2, art. 3.12 and 10.2).

    Verbal consent does not mean informal — the information provided must meet the same standards as written consent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Implied consent / anonymous participation">
    Used when completing or returning a survey or questionnaire constitutes implied consent. Typically limited to anonymous, minimal-risk studies where participation itself is the consent signal. **Not appropriate for interventional studies.**
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Consent already obtained (secondary use of data)">
    Used when the study involves data for which consent was already obtained in a prior context. Nagano will ask you to provide details and context explaining how and when that consent was obtained.

    If re-consent is not feasible rather than already obtained, review the consent waiver criteria in TCPS 2, Chapter 3 and consider whether an exemption request is more appropriate.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Deferred consent (emergency context)">
    Applies in emergency research where the participant cannot consent at the time of enrolment. Research activities in a medical emergency can only proceed if they meet the immediate needs of the person concerned and satisfy criteria pre-established by the REB (TCPS 2, art. 3.8).

    Nagano will ask you to confirm that your study meets all applicable prerequisites (e.g., serious risk requiring immediate intervention, no effective standard treatment, participant incapacity, inability to obtain third-party consent in time) and to provide a justification. **The REB will scrutinize this option carefully.**
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Exemption requested — no consent">
    Used when you are requesting that the REB waive the consent requirement entirely. **A specific justification is required.** This option is distinct from secondary use of already-consented data — it applies when individual consent is neither obtainable nor appropriate and a formal exemption is being sought.

    Review the consent waiver criteria in TCPS 2, Chapter 3 before selecting this option.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
If your consent approach is non-standard or you are unsure which option applies, flag it early — contact the Research Facilitator or the REB before submitting.
</Note>

## Section 5 — Recruitment and consent process

Describe both the recruitment process and the consent process in concrete, role-specific terms. Nagano asks for the same core details in each: who will be involved, and when and where each step will take place.

### Recruitment process

Indicate the name and function of the people who will inform potential participants about the study, and when and where initial contact will take place — from screening through to first approach.

### Consent process

Indicate the name and function of the people who will approach potential participants for the consent process, and when and where the consent process will take place.

<Warning>
**Avoid vague descriptions:** "The research team will recruit participants" is not sufficient. Name the specific role or function for each step, and specify the setting and timing. Vague process descriptions are among the most common reasons this section is flagged for clarification.
</Warning>

## Recruitment materials — where they go and what to prepare

Recruitment materials are not uploaded in this section of the F11 — they belong in **Project Files**. This section is a reminder of what to prepare and what rules apply.

### What counts as a recruitment material

Think about everything a potential participant will see or hear before enrolling:

- Posters and advertisements
- Email or letter invitations to participate
- Telephone recruitment scripts
- Social media text or digital ads
- Explanatory material given to potential participants before consent

### Language and scope rules

- **Multi-site projects** — Upload only materials used to recruit within the Réseau de la santé et des services sociaux (RSSS). Materials used at other sites are managed by those sites.
- **For initial REB review** — Materials may be submitted in either English or French.
- **For final institutional authorization** — Both English and French versions will typically be required before the Personne Mandatée's Office issues authorization. Plan ahead.

<Note>
**MUHC Communications review:** Recruitment materials may require review by the MUHC Public Relations and Communications Office after REB approval — see SOP-CR-009_07 RI-MUHC ADDENDUM and MUHC Policy ADM 235. If you are unsure whether your materials fall under this requirement, contact the Communications office before finalizing them.
</Note>

## Before you move on

Confirm the following before continuing to the next part of the F11.

- ✓ Study visit numbers and durations described as specifically as possible
- ✓ Compensation details (or rationale for none) consistent with consent form and recruitment materials
- ✓ Pre-selection method consistent with data access and bank/registry fields elsewhere in the form
- ✓ Initial contact approach described, including circle-of-care approach or rationale for not using it
- ✓ Department head authorization planned if recruitment spans multiple departments
- ✓ Consent type selected and matches practice; non-standard consent choices include a rationale
- ✓ Recruitment and consent process describes specific roles, timing, and setting — not just "the research team"
- ✓ Recruitment materials identified and ready to upload in Project Files; language versions planned
