Once you submit your project in Nagano, review and decision-making happens on the platform. This chapter covers how to track your submission through the three review streams, respond when action is required, and complete post-approval obligations throughout the life of your study.

## Tracking your project

### Returning to your project after submission

After submitting, access your project through **My Projects**. As reviews progress and decisions are recorded, the project workspace will populate with more information.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to My Projects">
    Select **My Projects** from the Nagano navigation bar.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open your project">
    Click the **project title** or **project number** to open the project workspace.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### The Status tab — your primary progress tracker

The **Status tab** gives the fastest, most reliable overview of where your submission stands. Check it each time you sign in — especially after submitting something or expecting a review update.

The Status tab shows:

- Whether your study is still in progress or moving toward authorization
- Which review elements are completed and which are pending
- Whether something is waiting on a response or update from your team

Make checking Status a habit when you sign in — it is where you see what is currently pending and what is required next.

### Reading the Status screen

Status typically displays a table with a review or evaluation element on the left and its current status on the right. Depending on your study, this may include:

- **Project-level authorization status** — whether the study is authorized for research
- **Reviewing REB status** — the current state of the ethics review
- **Feasibility elements** — services, resources, and readiness checks, each with its own status
- **Timestamps** — when authorization or decisions were recorded

### Two kinds of status labels

Not all status labels mean the same thing. Nagano uses two distinct types — REB review decisions and form workflow statuses. Knowing which type you are looking at helps you determine whether action is required.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="REB review decisions">
    These labels reflect the formal decision of the reviewing ethics board. They appear once a review cycle is complete.

    - **Approved** — the study is approved as submitted, typically valid for up to one year.
    - **Conditional approval** — the REB requests clarifications or modifications before granting final approval.
    - **Deferred** — significant changes or additional information are required; the submission will be reviewed again.
    - **Disapproved** — the study cannot proceed as submitted.

    Researchers have the right to request reconsideration or appeal. See REB-SOP 401 for the process.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Form workflow statuses">
    These labels reflect the current state of a form in the Nagano workflow. Use them to answer: "Can we do something right now, or are we waiting?"

    - **Created** — the form exists but has not been submitted yet. Action is required.
    - **Submitted** — the form has been sent for review. No action required at this time.
    - **Re-opened** — the reviewing body has opened the form so your team can respond or edit. Action is required.
    - **Approved / Closed** — a decision has been recorded. No action required.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Responding when follow-up is needed

When a reviewing body needs something from your team, they re-open the relevant form in Nagano.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="If the form shows Re-opened">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Review the comments">
        Read the comments or conditions in the re-opened form carefully.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Update the form and upload revised documents">
        Make the required changes and upload revised files as needed.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Re-submit the form">
        Re-submit the form once all responses are complete.
      </Step>
    </Steps>
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="If the form is not yet re-opened">
    If you see a decision but the form has not been re-opened, you cannot enter your response yet — the reviewing body must re-open the form first.

    Contact the REB to request that the form be re-opened.

    <Warning>
      Do not wait for something to happen on its own. If you are expecting to respond and the form is not re-opened, reaching out to the REB is the appropriate unblocking step — the process will not advance without it.
    </Warning>
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### The Messages tab — feasibility follow-ups

While Status shows overall progress, the **Messages tab** is where follow-up requests and clarifications from reviewing bodies appear. Check it:

- When Status shows something pending and you are not sure why
- When you are expecting a request for clarification or missing documentation
- When your project has feasibility items that depend on confirmations such as training or privileges

A routine that works:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open your project from My Projects" />
  <Step title="Check Status — what is complete vs. pending" />
  <Step title="Check Messages — any follow-ups on pending items" />
  <Step title="Respond in the appropriate place">
    Respond via Messages and/or the relevant form once it has been re-opened.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Activities tab and email notifications

Two additional tools help you stay aware of project activity between sign-ins.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Activities tab">
    A chronological timeline of project updates: new messages, evaluation changes, and status changes. Useful when you manage multiple projects or want to see what has changed since you last checked.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Email notifications">
    Nagano can send email updates about project activity, but these may not be enabled by default. To enable them, go to your **Profile** (top-right menu) and look for notification settings. Opt into the updates that are relevant to your work.

    <Note>
      Even with email notifications enabled, always confirm progress directly in Nagano. Email is a convenience — Status is where you see what is currently pending and what is required next.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Post-approval actions

Once your study has received MUHC Authorization, your work in Nagano continues. The table below summarizes the actions that may arise during the life of your study and the form used for each.

| Action | When it applies | Form |
|--------|----------------|------|
| Conditions of approval | REB has approved the study subject to specific changes or clarifications being addressed | F11 |
| Amendment | Significant changes to the protocol, consent form, study team, or study documents | F1H |
| Deviation report | An unapproved departure from the protocol has occurred | Form |
| Annual renewal | Continuing the study past the current approval period requires renewal before expiry | Form |
| Study close-out | Official closure of the study once all activities are complete | Form |

### Responding to conditions of approval

When the REB approves your study with conditions, the F11 form is re-opened in Nagano so you can respond directly. You will receive a notification in Nagano and by email when conditions are ready for you to address.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the re-activated F11 form">
    Open the re-activated F11 form from your project dashboard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review each condition">
    Read each condition listed by the REB in the reviewer comments panel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make the required changes and upload revised documents">
    Update the relevant sections of the F11 and upload any revised documents to Project Files.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resubmit the F11">
    Resubmit once all conditions have been addressed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Address each condition explicitly. If a condition requires a document revision, upload the revised file and reference it in your written response. Reviewers check for direct correspondence between each condition and your reply.
</Tip>

### Submitting an amendment

Use the **F1H form** in Nagano to submit an amendment when you need to make significant changes to an approved study. Do not implement changes until the amendment has been reviewed and approved by the REB and MUHC Authorization has been updated.

Common triggers for an amendment include:

- Changes to the protocol (eligibility criteria, procedures, endpoints, dosing)
- Revisions to the consent form or participant-facing materials
- Addition or removal of study team members in key roles
- Changes to the study site or study population
- New safety information that affects the risk-benefit assessment

<Note>
  Not all changes require a full amendment. Administrative changes — such as updating a contact phone number or correcting a typographical error — may be submitted as a minor modification. When in doubt, contact the Research Facilitator or REB office before submitting.
</Note>

### Reporting protocol deviations

A protocol deviation is any departure from the approved protocol that was not authorized in advance. Deviations must be documented and reported according to their severity. Refer to SOP-CR-026 — Ongoing REB Review and Reportable Events for the complete reporting requirements.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Minor deviation — low risk, aggregate reporting at renewal">
    Minor deviations are protocol departures that do not affect participant safety, rights, or the integrity of study data, and were not reasonably foreseeable. They do not require an immediate individual report to the REB.

    **Reporting:** Document the deviation in your site files. Minor deviations are reported in aggregate at the time of annual renewal — include a summary of all minor deviations in the renewal submission.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Major deviation — significant impact, report within 15 days">
    Major deviations are those that affect participant safety, rights, or study data integrity, or that were foreseeable and preventable. They require prompt individual reporting.

    **Timeline:** Report to the REB within **15 calendar days** of becoming aware of the deviation. If the deviation is life-threatening, the reporting window is **7 calendar days**.

    **Who to notify:** Report to both the REB (via Nagano) and the Quality Assurance office at [QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca). Include a Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) plan in your report.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Critical deviation — immediate risk, report immediately">
    Critical deviations involve an immediate risk to participant safety or a serious breach of GCP principles. They require urgent action and notification.

    **Timeline:** Report immediately upon discovery. If the situation is life-threatening, a written report must be submitted within **7 calendar days**.

    **Who to notify:** Notify the REB (via Nagano), the Quality Assurance office at [QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca), and the Sponsor. Coordinate with QA on any immediate corrective actions required.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  Delays in reporting deviations — particularly major and critical ones — can affect your site's standing with the REB and with sponsors. When in doubt about classification or timing, contact the Research Facilitator or QA office before the deadline passes.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Serious adverse events (SAEs) have their own reporting pathway and timelines — see SOP-CR-012 — Adverse Events, SAEs and SUSAR Reporting. For medical device studies, unanticipated serious adverse device effects (USADEs) are governed by SOP-CR-024 — Investigational Testing Authorization (Medical Devices).
</Note>

### Annual renewals

REB approval is time-limited. You must submit a renewal before your current approval period expires to continue the study without interruption. The renewal form is submitted in Nagano and asks you to report on:

- The current status of the study (enrolment numbers, study phase)
- Any new safety information or relevant literature that has emerged since the last review
- A summary of minor deviations accumulated since the last approval period
- Any changes anticipated in the coming year

<Warning>
  Conducting research after REB approval has expired is a serious protocol deviation. Submit your renewal well in advance of the expiry date — allow at least 6–8 weeks. If your approval has lapsed, stop study activities and contact the REB office immediately.
</Warning>

### Study close-out

When all study activities are complete, the study must be formally closed in Nagano. Refer to SOP-CR-016 — Study Close-Out for the full close-out checklist and requirements.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Submit the close-out report in Nagano">
    Confirm that enrolment is complete, all participants have exited the study, and all outstanding obligations to the REB have been met.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Secure all study documents, data, and specimens">
    Ensure all study documents, data, and biological specimens have been transferred, secured, or disposed of in accordance with the protocol and applicable regulations.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Archive the Investigator Site File">
    Archive the ISF and all essential documents in accordance with retention requirements — see SOP-CR-015 — Investigator Site Files and Essential Documents.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  For Health Canada–regulated studies (drugs, devices, natural health products), essential documents must be retained for a minimum of **15 years** after the study ends, or longer if required by the sponsor. For studies not subject to Health Canada oversight, the minimum retention period is **7 years**. Confirm the applicable requirement with your sponsor and QA office at study close-out.
</Note>

## Key reminders

- Return to your project via **My Projects** — click the title or project number to enter the workspace.
- Check the **Status tab** each time you sign in — it is your primary tracker for what is pending and what is required.
- Distinguish REB decisions (what the REB decided) from form workflow statuses (what is happening in Nagano right now).
- When a form is re-opened, review the comments, update and/or upload as needed, then re-submit.
- If you see a decision but the form is not yet re-opened, contact the REB to request access — do not wait.
- Check **Messages** for follow-ups when something is pending and the reason is unclear.
- Enable email notifications from your **Profile** if you haven't already — but always confirm status in Nagano directly.
- Address each REB condition explicitly and resubmit the F11 once all are resolved.
- Do not implement protocol changes before the amendment is approved and MUHC Authorization is updated.
- Classify deviations carefully — major and critical deviations have strict reporting timelines.
- Submit annual renewals well before the approval expiry date — allow at least 6–8 weeks.
- Complete all close-out obligations before submitting the close-out report in Nagano.
- Confirm document retention requirements with your sponsor and QA at study close-out.
