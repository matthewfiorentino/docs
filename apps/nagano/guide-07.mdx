# F11 Part 5 — Privacy and Confidentiality

Part 5 asks how you will protect participants' personal information, how study data will be stored and retained, and who will be responsible for archiving once the study closes.

## Part 5: Privacy and Confidentiality

This part covers three screens: protection of personal information, data retention, and archiving location and responsibility. Reviewers are looking for internal consistency — your data classification, access list, retention period, and archiving plan should align with each other and with what you have described in the consent and recruitment sections of the form.

## Protection of personal information

This screen asks how you will manage personal information collected during the study — what protection strategy you will use, who can access identifiable data, and whether you can reach participants quickly if needed.

### Principal protection strategy

Select the option that best describes how personal information will be protected.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Anonymous information — collected without an identifier">
    Data is collected in a way that makes it **impossible to identify the participant** — no names, no codes, no linkage. Anonymous data cannot be re-identified at any point.

    Select this only if identification is truly not possible given how the data is collected.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Anonymized information — identifiers have been permanently redacted">
    Data was once identifiable but **all identifiers have been permanently redacted** — the link between the data and the individual cannot be restored.

    If a linkage key still exists somewhere, select coded information instead.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Coded information — identifier replaced by a code">
    Participants are assigned a **code that replaces direct identifiers** in the research data. A linkage key connecting codes to identities is held separately, under controlled access.

    This is the most common approach in clinical research. Be prepared to describe where the linkage key is stored, who can access it, and how long it is retained.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Identifying information stored securely">
    The study retains **directly identifiable information** alongside study data. This requires robust security controls — describe the specific technical and procedural safeguards in place (encrypted systems, role-based access, locked storage, etc.).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Another mechanism">
    If none of the standard options accurately reflects your approach, select this option and provide a plain-language explanation of how personal information is handled and protected. Be specific — reviewers need to understand exactly what data is held and how it is secured.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
**Consistency check:** The strategy you select here must be consistent with what you have described in other parts of the F11 — particularly consent procedures and data access. A mismatch between sections is a common reason for reviewer questions.
</Note>

### Medical file inclusion

Nagano asks whether research data or the informed consent form will be included in participants' medical files. Select **Yes** or **No**. If you select Yes, two sub-fields appear:

- **What data will be included** — for example, the consent form, test results, treatment name.
- **Justification** — what warrants inclusion in the medical file, for example participant safety or impact on follow-up care.

<Note>
**Parent field note:** Nagano will warn you that sub-field answers won't be saved until the parent question (Yes/No) is answered first. Set the parent field before filling in the sub-fields.
</Note>

### Access to personal information

The form asks you to identify any people or organizations — beyond sponsor representatives, regulatory authorities (such as Health Canada), the REB, and quality assurance — who may access participants' personal information during the study. Use the **Add** button to enter each additional party. Typical examples include core research team members and sub-investigators at participating sites.

### Quick contact

Indicate whether there is a way to quickly contact participants if needed. Select **Yes** or **Not applicable**. If Yes, describe the contact method — for example, a phone number held separately from the coded study data.

## Data retention

This screen asks about how data will be stored and protected during the conduct of the study — who holds it, in what formats, with what controls, and for how long.

- **Authority / organization responsible during the project** — Indicate which establishment or organization has authority over the data during the study.
- **Storage contact** — If the person responsible for storage after study completion differs from the primary study contact, name them here.

### Format

Select all formats that apply to your study data:

- Paper
- Electronic files
- Audio/video files
- Other

- **Data protection policies** — Describe how access to research data is restricted (for example, files locked with a username and password, role-based permissions, encrypted drives).
- **Backup strategies** — Describe how data loss is mitigated (for example, backup to a secure server, scheduled institutional backups).

### Retention period

The dropdown offers two options: **25 years** or **Other**. The 25-year default does not reflect current regulatory expectations. Use **Other** and enter the appropriate period:

- **Health Canada–regulated studies** — 15 years after project closure
- **Non–Health Canada–regulated human clinical studies** — 7 years after project closure

<Warning>
**Important — outdated interface default:** Do not select **25 years**. Choose **Other** and type the appropriate retention period based on your regulatory context. The interface has not been updated to reflect current expectations.
</Warning>

## Archiving location and responsibility

This screen covers where study documents will be stored after the project closes, and who is responsible for that archive.

- **Archiving location** — Name the planned post-completion storage location: an institutional archiving service, an approved secure facility, a controlled server, or another approved site.
- **Responsibility** — In most cases, each participating institution is responsible for archiving its own study documents. For single-site MUHC studies, this is typically the RI-MUHC. Refer to [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015), section 5.3 for archiving requirements.
