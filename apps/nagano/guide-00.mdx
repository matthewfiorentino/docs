# Introduction and Orientation

Everything you need to get started in Nagano — from what the platform does, to your first login, to how this user guide is organized.

## What is Nagano?

Nagano is a centralized, web-based platform used across many institutions in the Québec health and social services network (RSSS) to manage the submission, review, and oversight of research studies involving human participants. At the MUHC, Nagano coordinates scientific, ethics, and institutional feasibility reviews — a process known as the **triple review**.

Researchers use Nagano to:

- Submit new projects for review
- Track review statuses
- Respond to reviewer comments
- Manage study documentation throughout the lifecycle — from initial submission through amendments, renewals, and closure

<Warning>
A project submitted to Nagano cannot commence until it has received final MUHC Authorization from the Personne Mandatée's Office, which follows REB approval. See the [Study review workflow](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:224) on the RI-MUHC portal for more detail.
</Warning>

## Creating an account and logging in

To use Nagano, you will need a user account.

### Requesting access

If this is your first time using Nagano, complete the Nagano Access Request Form. After your request is approved, you will receive a username and a temporary password.

<Note>
If you encounter any issues creating or accessing your account, email the Nagano support team.
</Note>

### Logging in

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to the login page">
    Navigate to the [Nagano login page](https://nagano.muhc.mcgill.ca/login).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your credentials">
    Enter your **username** (given to you at account creation) and your **password**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read pop-up notifications">
    After logging in, **pop-up messages** may appear with important notifications. Read them carefully before proceeding.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Exploring the user interface

Before submitting or managing your studies, take a few minutes to get familiar with the Nagano interface. The four main areas of the platform are described below.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Navigation bar — top bar with site-wide access points">
    The navigation bar at the top of every page contains five links: **Home**, **My Projects**, **Documentation**, **Search**, and **Profile**. Keep your Profile contact information up to date — notifications and reviewer feedback are sent to the email address listed there.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Menu bar — Dashboard, Messages, Calendar, Activities">
    **Dashboard** (default) — lists all your projects. **Messages** — full history of department conversations. **Calendar** — upcoming REB meeting schedule; shows when meetings are scheduled, not when decisions will be received. **Activities** — a searchable log of key actions, filterable by project and date, colour-tagged by source.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Activities panel — recent activity on your projects">
    The activities panel on the homepage displays recent updates across your projects — new messages, status updates, evaluation changes — giving you a quick view of what has changed since you last logged in.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Context panels — discussions, outstanding forms, reviews">
    The right side of the homepage shows context-dependent panels: active discussions, forms that are outstanding, and pending reviews. The panels visible depend on your profile and assigned role in Nagano.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
The panels visible on your homepage depend on your profile and assigned role in Nagano.

## What a strong submission looks like

A strong submission moves through review with minimal preventable back-and-forth. That means a file that is:

- **Complete** — key documents are uploaded where expected.
- **Consistent** — your answers match your protocol and other materials. Copy directly from the protocol where possible to avoid typos and discrepancies.
- **Easy to review** — reviewers can find what they need without follow-up.

<Note>
**Tip:** Nagano allows you to generate a PDF version of your form at any time — useful for proofreading before submission and for sharing with your team.
</Note>

<Warning>
Once a project is submitted in Nagano, it cannot be deleted by the user. If a project needs to be removed, a request must be made to the REB.
</Warning>

## Six phases from start to submit

Every submission in Nagano follows six phases. This user guide follows these phases in order.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Prepare your study package">
    Protocol, consent approach, data plan, and feasibility information ready before opening Nagano.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the study in Nagano">
    Select project type — the appropriate F11 form is generated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete F11 ethics and privacy sections">
    General information, Québec registry, benefits and risks, recruitment, privacy, and data access.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete feasibility inputs">
    MUHC resources and services: departments, nursing, labs, pharmacy, MNI, budget, contracts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload supporting documents">
    Protocol, consent forms, recruitment materials, and all required supporting files in Project Files.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Agreements, PI commitment, and submit">
    PI confirms privileges, training, and conflict of interest, then signs and submits.
  </Step>
</Steps>
