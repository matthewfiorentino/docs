# Post-Approval — Amendments, Deviations, Renewals and Close-Out

Once your study has received MUHC Authorization, your work in Nagano continues. This chapter covers how to respond to conditions of approval, submit amendments, report protocol deviations, renew your study, and close it out at the end.

## Post-approval actions at a glance

After receiving MUHC Authorization, several types of actions may arise during the life of your study. Each is handled through a specific form or process in Nagano.

| Action | When it applies | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Conditions of approval | REB has approved the study subject to specific changes or clarifications being addressed | F11 |
| Amendment | Significant changes to the protocol, consent form, study team, or study documents | F1H |
| Deviation report | An unapproved departure from the protocol has occurred | Form |
| Annual renewal | Continuing the study past the current approval period requires renewal before expiry | Form |
| Study close-out | Official closure of the study once all activities are complete | Form |

---

## Responding to conditions of approval

When the REB approves your study with conditions, the F11 form is re-opened in Nagano so you can respond directly. You will receive a notification in Nagano and by email when conditions are ready for you to address.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the re-activated F11 form">
    Open the re-activated F11 form from your project dashboard.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review each condition">
    Review each condition listed by the REB in the reviewer comments panel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Make the required changes">
    Update the relevant sections of the F11 and upload any revised documents to Project Files.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resubmit the F11">
    Resubmit the F11 once all conditions have been addressed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
Address each condition explicitly. If a condition requires a document revision, upload the revised file and reference it in your written response. Reviewers check for direct correspondence between each condition and your reply.
</Tip>

## Submitting an amendment

Use the F1H form in Nagano to submit an amendment when you need to make significant changes to an approved study. Do not implement changes until the amendment has been reviewed and approved by the REB and MUHC Authorization has been updated.

Common triggers for an amendment include:

- Changes to the protocol (eligibility criteria, procedures, endpoints, dosing)
- Revisions to the consent form or participant-facing materials
- Addition or removal of study team members in key roles
- Changes to the study site or study population
- New safety information that affects the risk-benefit assessment

<Tip>
**Minor changes:** Not all changes require a full amendment. Administrative changes — such as updating a contact phone number or correcting a typographical error — may be submitted as a minor modification. When in doubt, contact the Research Facilitator or REB office before submitting.
</Tip>

## Reporting protocol deviations

A protocol deviation is any departure from the approved protocol that was not authorized in advance. Deviations must be documented and reported according to their severity. Refer to SOP-CR-026 — Ongoing REB Review and Reportable Events for the complete reporting requirements.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Minor deviation — low risk, aggregate reporting at renewal">
    Minor deviations are protocol departures that do not affect participant safety, rights, or the integrity of study data, and were not reasonably foreseeable. They do not require an immediate individual report to the REB.

    **Reporting:** Document the deviation in your site files. Minor deviations are reported in aggregate at the time of annual renewal — include a summary of all minor deviations in the renewal submission.

    See SOP-CR-026 for classification guidance.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Major deviation — significant impact, report within 15 days (7 days if life-threatening)">
    Major deviations are those that affect participant safety, rights, or study data integrity, or that were foreseeable and preventable. They require prompt individual reporting.

    **Timeline:** Report to the REB within **15 calendar days** of becoming aware of the deviation. If the deviation is life-threatening, the reporting window is **7 calendar days**.

    **Who to notify:** Report to both the REB (via Nagano) and the Quality Assurance office at QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca. Include a Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) plan in your report.

    See SOP-CR-026 for the required report content and submission process.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Critical deviation — immediate risk, report immediately (7 days if life-threatening)">
    Critical deviations involve an immediate risk to participant safety or a serious breach of GCP principles. They require urgent action and notification.

    **Timeline:** Report immediately upon discovery. If the situation is life-threatening, a written report must be submitted within **7 calendar days**.

    **Who to notify:** Notify the REB (via Nagano), the Quality Assurance office at QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca, and the Sponsor. Coordinate with QA on any immediate corrective actions required.

    See SOP-CR-026 for the full reporting process and required documentation.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
**Report promptly:** Delays in reporting deviations — particularly major and critical ones — can affect your site's standing with the REB and with sponsors. When in doubt about classification or timing, contact the Research Facilitator or QA office before the deadline passes.
</Warning>

<Note>
**Note on serious adverse events and device deficiencies:** Serious adverse events (SAEs) have their own reporting pathway and timelines — see SOP-CR-012 — Adverse Events, SAEs and SUSAR Reporting. For medical device studies, unanticipated serious adverse device effects (USADEs) are governed by SOP-CR-024 — Investigational Testing Authorization (Medical Devices).
</Note>

## Annual renewals

REB approval is time-limited. You must submit a renewal before your current approval period expires to continue the study without interruption. The renewal form is submitted in Nagano and asks you to report on:

- The current status of the study (enrolment numbers, study phase)
- Any new safety information or relevant literature that has emerged since the last review
- A summary of minor deviations accumulated since the last approval period
- Any changes anticipated in the coming year

<Warning>
**Do not let your approval lapse:** Conducting research after REB approval has expired is a serious protocol deviation. Submit your renewal well in advance of the expiry date — allow at least 6–8 weeks. If your approval has lapsed, stop study activities and contact the REB office immediately.
</Warning>

## Study close-out

When all study activities are complete, the study must be formally closed in Nagano. Refer to SOP-CR-016 — Study Close-Out for the full close-out checklist and requirements.

- Submit the close-out report in Nagano, confirming that enrolment is complete, all participants have exited the study, and all outstanding obligations to the REB have been met.
- Ensure all study documents, data, and biological specimens have been transferred, secured, or disposed of in accordance with the protocol and applicable regulations.
- Archive the Investigator Site File (ISF) and all essential documents in accordance with retention requirements — see SOP-CR-015 — Investigator Site Files and Essential Documents.

<Note>
**Retention periods:** For Health Canada–regulated studies (drugs, devices, natural health products), essential documents must be retained for a minimum of **15 years** after the study ends, or longer if required by the sponsor. For studies not subject to Health Canada oversight, the minimum retention period is **7 years**. Confirm the applicable requirement with your sponsor and QA office at study close-out.
</Note>

## Key reminders

- ✓ Address each REB condition explicitly and resubmit the F11 once all are resolved.
- ✓ Do not implement protocol changes before the amendment is approved and MUHC Authorization is updated.
- ✓ Classify deviations carefully — major and critical deviations have strict reporting timelines.
- ✓ Report major deviations to both the REB and QA; critical deviations to the REB, QA, and Sponsor.
- ✓ Submit annual renewals well before the approval expiry date — allow at least 6–8 weeks.
- ✓ Complete all close-out obligations before submitting the close-out report in Nagano.
- ✓ Confirm document retention requirements with your sponsor and QA at study close-out.
