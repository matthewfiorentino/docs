This section of the Nagano User Guide covers the two things you need to do before creating a project: understand what the platform does and how it is organized, and confirm that your study package is ready. Most avoidable review delays happen before a submission is even created — the preparation steps in this guide are designed to prevent them.

<Note>
  This guide currently covers the **F11-H pathway** (Clinical Research / Clinical Trial) for single-site MUHC submissions. Multi-site and other pathways are in development. Contact the Research Facilitator for guidance on those pathways in the meantime.
</Note>

## Introduction and orientation

### What Nagano is

Nagano is a centralized, web-based platform used across many institutions in the Québec health and social services network (RSSS) to manage the submission, review, and oversight of research studies involving human participants. At the MUHC, Nagano coordinates scientific, ethics, and institutional feasibility reviews — a process known as the **triple review**.

You use Nagano to:

- Submit new projects for review
- Track review statuses across all three review streams
- Respond to reviewer comments and conditions
- Manage study documentation throughout the lifecycle — from initial submission through amendments, renewals, and closure

<Warning>
  A project submitted to Nagano cannot commence until it has received final MUHC Authorization from the Personne Mandatée's Office, which follows REB approval. See the [Study review workflow](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:224) on the RI-MUHC Portal for more detail.
</Warning>

### Creating an account and logging in

To use Nagano, you need a user account. If this is your first time using Nagano, complete the [Nagano Access Request Form](https://forms.office.com/r/Uau6LgNbNL). After your request is approved, you will receive a username and a temporary password.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to the Nagano login page">
    Navigate to [nagano.muhc.mcgill.ca](https://nagano.muhc.mcgill.ca/login).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enter your credentials">
    Enter your **username** (given to you at account creation) and your **password**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read any pop-up messages">
    After logging in, pop-up messages may appear with important notifications. Read them carefully before proceeding.
  </Step>
</Steps>

If you encounter any issues creating or accessing your account, email the Nagano support team at [info.nagano@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:info.nagano@muhc.mcgill.ca).

### Exploring the user interface

Before submitting or managing your studies, take a few minutes to get familiar with the Nagano interface. The platform has four main areas.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Navigation bar — top bar with site-wide access points">
    The navigation bar at the top of every page contains five links: **Home**, **My Projects**, **Documentation**, **Search**, and **Profile**. Keep your Profile contact information up to date — notifications and reviewer feedback are sent to the email address listed there.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Menu bar — Dashboard, Messages, Calendar, Activities">
    - **Dashboard** (default) — lists all your projects.
    - **Messages** — full history of department conversations.
    - **Calendar** — upcoming REB meeting schedule; shows when meetings are scheduled, not when decisions will be received.
    - **Activities** — a searchable log of key actions, filterable by project and date, colour-tagged by source.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Activities panel — recent activity on your projects">
    The activities panel on the homepage displays recent updates across your projects — new messages, status updates, evaluation changes — giving you a quick view of what has changed since you last logged in.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Context panels — discussions, outstanding forms, reviews">
    The right side of the homepage shows context-dependent panels: active discussions, forms that are outstanding, and pending reviews. The panels visible depend on your profile and assigned role in Nagano.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### What a strong submission looks like

A strong submission moves through review with minimal preventable back-and-forth. That means a file that is:

- **Complete** — key documents are uploaded where expected.
- **Consistent** — your answers match your protocol and other materials. Copy directly from the protocol where possible to avoid discrepancies.
- **Easy to review** — reviewers can find what they need without follow-up.

<Tip>
  Nagano allows you to generate a PDF version of your form at any time — useful for proofreading before submission and for sharing with your team.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Once a project is submitted in Nagano, it cannot be deleted by the user. If a project needs to be removed, a request must be made to the REB.
</Warning>

### Six phases from start to submit

Every submission in Nagano follows six phases. This user guide follows these phases in order.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Prepare your study package">
    Protocol, consent approach, data plan, and feasibility information ready before opening Nagano.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the study in Nagano">
    Select project type — the appropriate F11 form is generated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete F11 ethics and privacy sections">
    General information, Québec registry, benefits and risks, recruitment, privacy, and data access.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete feasibility inputs">
    MUHC resources and services: departments, nursing, labs, pharmacy, MNI, budget, and contracts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload supporting documents">
    Protocol, consent forms, recruitment materials, and all required supporting files in Project Files.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Agreements, PI commitment, and submit">
    PI confirms privileges, training, and conflict of interest, then signs and submits.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Before you start a new submission

### Why preparation matters

Common causes of preventable delays are not technical — they are preparation gaps. The most frequent ones are:

- Key details not yet confirmed — roles, study type, oversight requirements
- Documents not ready when the form asks for them
- Answers and uploads that do not match each other

Taking time to align your study package before opening Nagano reduces back-and-forth with reviewers and shortens the path to authorization.

### What to have ready before you begin

Before creating your project in Nagano, you should have at minimum:

- A finalized protocol — or the version you are ready to submit
- A clear recruitment and consent approach
- A clear data and privacy plan — what is collected, how it is protected, who can access it
- Any known participant-facing materials you plan to use
- An early view of feasibility needs — services, resources, and required approvals

For a full list of required documentation by study type, consult the [Study Submission page](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:220) on the RI-MUHC Portal.

### Who builds the submission

A submission can be completed by the PI, a coordinator, or another study team member. Regardless of who builds the file, the submission is not complete until the PI completes the final commitment and signature step in Nagano.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="PI-led workflow">
    The PI creates the project, completes the form, uploads supporting documents, and completes the final commitment and signature step.

    Ensure the PI has **administrator-level access** to the project from the start.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Team-supported workflow">
    A coordinator or team member creates the project, completes the form, and uploads documents.

    The PI must be added to the project with **administrator-level access** and completes the final commitment and signature step using their own Nagano credentials. If the PI is submitting the signature form independently (rather than through Nagano directly), attach the signed PI Commitment and Signature form as instructed in Nagano.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

### Confirm the PI early

The PI field in Nagano is not simply about who wrote the protocol or who will publish the results. It is tied to whether the study requires medical oversight — and this has compliance implications.

<Note>
  **PI vs. Qualified Investigator (QI):** "PI" is the term Nagano uses throughout, but for interventional clinical studies the PI is effectively acting in the role of Qualified Investigator (QI) — the legally defined term in Canada for the person responsible for the conduct of a clinical trial at a site, who is entitled to provide health care under provincial law.
</Note>

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Interventional clinical study">
    The PI must be a physician (Qualified Investigator):

    - Must be a CPDP member (physician or dentist) in good standing at the MUHC
    - Must hold **Human Research Privileges** and have up-to-date institutional training
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Non-interventional study">
    The PI can be a non-CPDP RI-MUHC researcher:

    - Must hold **Human Researcher Status** — for researchers who are not physicians or dentists (e.g., PhDs, nurses, physiotherapists, psychologists)
    - Human Researcher Status does not permit research involving medical oversight
    - If the protocol author is not the PI, they may be listed as a co-investigator or collaborator
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Warning>
  Choosing the wrong PI early can require revising key fields or reworking parts of the submission later. Confirm PI eligibility before creating the project. See RI-MUHC SOP-CR-500_05, section 4.0.
</Warning>

### Identify feasibility requirements early

Feasibility questions in Nagano confirm whether the study can be conducted at the MUHC with the right resources and approvals. Knowing what applies to your study before you begin saves time later in the form. Review the following areas and identify what your study will require:

- Departments involved — and whether Department Head Authorizations will be required
- MUHC nursing resources, or CIM services / dedicated research nurse
- Laboratory services
- Pharmacy services
- MNI imaging resources — PET, MRI, MEG
- Budget and funding documentation
- Contracts or agreements — with external collaborators, service providers, data-sharing partners, or the sponsor

### Pre-submission checklist

Use this checklist to confirm you are ready to create your project.

- Protocol is finalized (or ready for submission)
- Recruitment and consent approach is confirmed
- Data and privacy plan is in place
- Participant-facing materials are identified
- Feasibility needs are identified (departments, resources, approvals)
- Correct PI is confirmed — eligibility verified for this study type
- Workflow is agreed — who builds the submission and who signs
