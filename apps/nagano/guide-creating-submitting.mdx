---
title: "Nagano User Guide — F11 Form Walkthrough, Parts 1–9"
description: "How to create a new project in Nagano and complete all nine parts of the F11 form, from general information and registry to PI signature."
---

Once you have completed the preparation steps in [Getting Started](/apps/nagano/guide-getting-started), you are ready to create your project and work through the F11 form. This guide covers project creation and each of the nine F11 parts in order, from general information through the PI's final signature.

<Note>
  This guide uses the **F11-H (Clinical Research / Clinical Trial)** form as the primary example. If you selected a different project type, your sections and questions may differ, but the structure and logic are the same.
</Note>

<Steps>
  <Step title="Creating a new project">
    Creating a project is your first action in Nagano. The selections you make here determine which F11 form is generated and how your submission is routed for review.

    **Start from the My Projects dashboard**

    1. Go to **My Projects** in the navigation bar.
    2. Click **New project**.

    **Nagano identifier and project language**

    - **Nagano identifier (acronym)** — Enter a short label or acronym for your own tracking. This helps you find the right project quickly in My Projects, especially if you manage multiple studies.
    - **Project language** — Select the language that best matches your submission materials: protocol, consent forms, and participant-facing documents.

    **Ethics evaluation location**

    This field determines who is responsible for scientific and ethical evaluation.

    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="MUHC is the lead site (or only site)">
        Select the **MUHC REB**. This applies when:
        - The study is single-site at the MUHC, or
        - The MUHC is the lead site for other Québec RSSS participating sites.

        If the MUHC is the only Québec RSSS site — even if sites outside Québec are also participating — the study is treated as single-site for MUHC REB purposes.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="MUHC is a participating site only">
        Do **not** select the MUHC REB. The reviewing REB (RREB) of the lead institution handles scientific and ethical evaluation.

        Guidance on participating-site (MEO) submissions will be covered in a separate section of this user guide. Contact the Research Facilitator in the meantime.
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>

    **Project type**

    This is the most important selection on this screen — it determines which F11 form Nagano generates.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="F11-H — Clinical Trial / Clinical Research">
        Use this for **interventional trials and most forms of clinical research** — drug trials, device studies, surgical interventions, process-of-care changes, and more. This is the form used as the example throughout this guide.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="F11-BR — Bank / Registry">
        Use this when **the bank or registry itself is what you're submitting** — an organized system for collecting and storing data or biospecimens for future research use. If you're using an existing bank rather than creating one, do not select this type.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="F11-NIR — Fundamental research">
        Use this for **experimental or theoretical research** aimed at generating new knowledge, without a primary focus on immediate clinical application.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="F11-NIR — Psychosocial / Qualitative / Nursing sciences">
        Use this when your main approach is **psychosocial, qualitative, or nursing-science oriented** — studies exploring lived experience, behaviour, communication, or care environments.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="F11-HRR — Retrospective chart review">
        Use this when the study **uses information already documented in health records**, with no direct participant contact and no new data collection.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="F11-NIR — Epidemiology / Health services / Global health">
        Use this when the study's main lens is **populations, health systems, or services** — including how care is organized and delivered.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Imaging">
        Use this when **imaging is the primary focus** of the research question or when the study depends heavily on imaging resources such as PET, MRI, or MEG. Confirm with the Research Facilitator if unsure.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    **Research field, sub-study, and team access**

    - **Main study / sub-study** — Leave blank for most submissions. If your study should be formally linked to a parent project, contact the Research Facilitator before selecting a parent.
    - **Research field** — Select the closest match to your study's primary discipline.
    - **Users with access** — Add the PI, coordinator(s), and any collaborators involved in preparing the submission.

    <Warning>
      Ensure the PI has **administrator-level access** to the project. If the original creator later leaves the team, the PI must be able to manage access independently.
    </Warning>

    **Create the project**

    Click **Create**. You will be returned to My Projects, where your new project appears under **My projects to follow**. Open the generated **F11 form** to begin.

    Project ID formats: single-site MUHC projects are assigned IDs in the format `YYYY-#####`; MUHC-led multi-site projects use `MP-37-YYYY-####`; participating-site projects use `MEO-XX-YYYY-####`. Always include the project ID when contacting support.

    **Before you continue — checklist**
    - Nagano identifier entered
    - Project language selected
    - Ethics evaluation location confirmed (MUHC REB or other)
    - Correct project type selected
    - PI has administrator-level access to the project
  </Step>

  <Step title="F11 Part 1 — General information">
    Part 1 captures the foundational details of your study: titles, the study team, the MUHC site(s) involved, study design, and any specific characteristics that trigger additional review steps. Getting these right early shapes how the rest of the submission is interpreted by both feasibility and REB reviewers.

    **Sections 1–2: Study titles**

    - **Section 1 — Study title** — Enter the full study title. Match the protocol title or the official working title your team uses consistently.
    - **Section 2 — French study title** — Complete this if the study has an official French title or if your team uses one consistently.

    **Section 3: Study author**

    This question asks who initiated or created the study protocol — not who is completing Nagano today. Choose the option that best reflects how the study was initiated.

    **Section 4: Local PI**

    Identify the MUHC local PI responsible for the study. Use the contact search — create a new contact only if the person is not in the list.

    <Warning>
      **PI eligibility — confirm before you proceed.** For interventional clinical studies requiring medical oversight, the PI must be an MD with CPDP membership in good standing and up-to-date research privileges and training. Choosing the wrong PI early can require revising key fields or reworking parts of the submission later. See RI-MUHC SOP-CR-500_05, section 4.0.
    </Warning>

    **Sections 5–7: Co-investigators, collaborators, and coordinators**

    - **Section 5 — Local co-investigators & collaborators** — Add MUHC/RI-MUHC collaborators formally involved in the study. Note: **co-investigators** are doctors who will actively participate in study procedures and see participants directly — they must be delegated on the Task Delegation Log. **Collaborators** have a different role and should not be listed in the co-investigator section.
    - **Section 6 — External co-investigators & collaborators** — Capture collaborators outside the MUHC, especially for multi-site studies.
    - **Section 7 — Coordinator(s)** — Add local study coordinator(s), clearly identifying the primary point of contact with reviewers.

    **Section 8: MUHC site(s)**

    Select the MUHC site(s) where study activities will take place — recruitment, visits, procedures, and data collection. Choose based on where work will actually happen.

    **Section 9: Specific characteristics**

    Select only the characteristics that truly apply to your study. Each selection may trigger additional required fields, forms, or review steps.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Research using an existing bank">
        Select this only if you are **accessing** an already-established bank. Nagano will ask you to indicate whether the bank is located within or outside the institution, and to provide the bank's name and the contact information of the person responsible. Attach a copy of the management framework, consent form, and consent for biobank in Project Files.

        Contact the Contracts Review Office to confirm whether a Material Transfer Agreement (MTA) is required.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Research involving creation of a bank">
        Select this if creating a new bank is part of this project. Nagano will ask you to clarify how biospecimens will be procured.

        **Separate F11-BR submission required:** if your study also involves the creation of a bio- or databank, you must submit the bank as its own project using form F11-BR.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Access to information">
        Select this for studies based solely on access to participant files or existing data, with no direct participant contact. This triggers the "Access to information" section of the F11 (covered in Part 6).
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Genetic or genomic component">
        Select this if your study involves collection, analysis, or use of genetic or genomic data. This may trigger additional ethics review considerations related to privacy, consent, and incidental findings.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Neuroimaging component">
        Select this if your study uses neuroimaging. MNI resources (PET, MRI, MEG) require separate approval letters from the relevant MNI review committees — uploaded in the Feasibility section.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Artificial intelligence methods">
        Select this for projects that involve the development of artificial intelligence technologies using previously collected health data. Nagano will prompt you to download and complete the corresponding AI protocol template.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Adaptive design trial">
        Select this if your clinical trial uses a pre-specified adaptive design — where interim data may be used to modify the trial while it is ongoing.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="University or graduate students">
        Select this if the research involves university or graduate students. Nagano will ask you to indicate the students' level of education.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    **Sections 10–15: Dates, design, funding, and reviews**

    - **Section 10 — Estimated start date** — Enter a realistic date after anticipated approvals.
    - **Section 11 — Experimental design** — Indicate randomization, blinding, placebo. Select "not applicable" where a feature does not apply.
    - **Section 12 — Duration** — Expected study duration in months, from authorization through closure.
    - **Section 13 — Funding** — Select all funding sources that apply.
    - **Section 14 — Scientific evaluation** — Answer based on whether scientific evaluation is required at the reviewing institution.
    - **Section 15 — Other ethics reviews** — Indicate whether another REB has reviewed the project. If yes, attach approval documentation.

    **Before you move on — checklist**
    - The correct MUHC local PI is identified and their eligibility confirmed
    - The right MUHC site(s) are selected
    - Only specific characteristics that truly apply are checked in Section 9
    - Study title matches the protocol title
    - All team members are added in the correct sections
  </Step>

  <Step title="F11 Part 2 — Québec Registry">
    This part of the F11-H populates the Québec Clinical Trial Registry. The information you enter here is public-facing — write for a potential participant, not for a scientific reviewer. This section applies to F11-H forms only.

    **Section 2 — MSSS research categories (2019)**

    Select the category that best describes your study. This also has implications for PI eligibility.

    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="Health Canada–regulated (PI must be a physician)">
        The PI must be a physician with CPDP membership and up-to-date Research Privileges. Categories include:

        - **Experimental drug** — select the trial phase (I, II, I/II, III, II/III, IV)
        - **Cell therapy** — indicate NOL status
        - **Medical device** — select device class (I, II, III, IV)
        - **Software** — select software class (I, II, III, IV)
        - **Radiopharmaceutical product** — indicate NOL status
        - **Natural health product** — indicate NOL status
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="Not Health Canada–regulated (PI must hold Privileges or Status)">
        The PI must hold the appropriate RI-MUHC Research Privileges or Researcher Status. Categories include:

        - **Quasi-experimental research** — cohort, case-control, cross-sectional, or population survey
        - **Descriptive research**
        - **Qualitative research**
        - **Mixed design** (quantitative/qualitative)
        - **Creation of a databank or biological material**
        - **Basic research**
        - **Observational research**
        - **Translational research**
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>

    **Sections 3–5 — Field, summary, and intervention**

    - **Section 3 — Field of research** — Choose the predominant field. Use "Other" if nothing fits.
    - **Section 4 — Study summary (public-facing)** — Write as if explaining to a potential participant. Use plain language — no protocol jargon. Structure around: the hypothesis or research question, the study objectives, the methodology, and any additional context.
    - **Section 5 — Intervention (if applicable)** — List the drug, molecule, product, or instrument being studied (trade name, generic name, common nomenclature).

    **Sections 6–8 — Cohort, keywords, and criteria**

    - **Section 6 — Cohort (if applicable)** — Only use this if recruitment is organized into distinct cohorts.
    - **Section 7 — Key words** — Choose terms a potential participant might search. Keep them specific and in everyday language.
    - **Section 8 — Inclusion / exclusion criteria** — Write at a high level, in recruitment-friendly language. Help someone quickly understand who might qualify — do not reproduce your full protocol eligibility criteria.

    **Sections 9–12 — Participants, duration, and contact**

    - **Section 9 — Total participants** — Enter totals for the entire study, for Québec, and for your institution. If numbers are not yet defined, enter **0** and add a brief explanation.
    - **Section 10 — Participant profile** — Select all options that apply. Note: declare "not covered by RAMQ" only when a specific participant has been identified as not covered — do not select it speculatively.
    - **Section 11 — Duration of participation** — Enter the time from consent signing through the end of a participant's involvement, not the total study duration.
    - **Section 12 — Contact for recruitment (public)** — This contact will be visible on the public registry. Use a monitored study email address or clinic phone line — not a personal number.

    **Before you move on — checklist**
    - Correct Health Canada regulation status selected in Section 2
    - Study summary written in plain language, suitable for a potential participant
    - Inclusion/exclusion criteria written at a high level — not reproduced from the protocol
    - Participant numbers entered, or a note explains why they are not yet available
    - Recruitment contact is a monitored address or line
  </Step>

  <Step title="F11 Part 3 — Benefits, risks and inconveniences">
    This part asks you to situate the study in relation to the standard of care, declare and justify any use of placebo, and disclose whether a safety committee is in place. The logic chain between treatment change, associated risks, and proposed benefit is examined closely by reviewers.

    **Section 1 — Standard treatment comparison**

    Confirm:
    - A clear description of the current standard treatment
    - How the experimental treatment differs from what participants would otherwise receive
    - A clear yes/no on whether participation requires stopping or changing any existing treatment

    If stopping or changing a current treatment is required, explain: why the change is necessary, the risks created by stopping or changing it, and the potential benefit of the proposed approach. Gaps in this logic chain — particularly around risks of stopping current treatment — are a common source of REB questions.

    **Section 2 — Use of placebo**

    Indicate whether your study uses a placebo. If yes:

    - **Ethical justification** — Select the option that best fits your study context and clarify anything that could be misunderstood (e.g., placebo added on top of standard therapy, rescue medication permitted).
    - **Placebo-related risks** — Describe risks including the risk of delayed effective treatment, symptom worsening, and any additional monitoring burden.

    <Warning>
      If placebo adds meaningful risk, the ethical justification must be proportionally strong. Vague or minimal justifications are a common source of REB follow-up on studies with a placebo arm.
    </Warning>

    **Section 3 — Advisory/safety committee (DMC/DSMB)**

    Indicate whether your project has a safety review committee such as a **Data Monitoring Committee (DMC)** or **Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)**.

    - If yes, indicate whether the committee is independent of the sponsor. If not independent, provide a justification.
    - If no committee exists, provide a justification and a detailed safety monitoring plan explaining who reviews safety data, at what intervals, and what criteria would trigger an early stop or protocol change.

    **Before you move on — checklist**
    - Standard treatment described and experimental approach clearly differentiated
    - If treatment change is required, rationale and associated risks explained
    - Placebo use declared and ethically justified; placebo-related risks described
    - Safety committee presence declared; independence confirmed or non-independence justified
  </Step>

  <Step title="F11 Part 4 — Recruitment, enrolment and consent">
    This part covers how participants will be identified, approached, and enrolled, and how consent will be obtained. Reviewers use these responses to assess whether your recruitment approach is ethical, operationally feasible, and consistent with the rest of your submission.

    **Section 1 — Participant study visits**

    Describe what participants will be asked to do across the course of the study. Be as specific as possible:
    - Number of visits (or typical case if it varies)
    - Visit duration (or breakdown by visit type if it varies)
    - If you cannot estimate, explain why in a way that makes sense to a feasibility reviewer

    **Section 2 — Participant compensation**

    Indicate whether and how participants will be compensated. The compensation type, amount, and method must match exactly what appears in the consent form and any recruitment materials.

    - **Monetary compensation** — specify the amount and explain how it was established
    - **Reimbursement of expenses** — specify which expenses are covered and the method of payment
    - **Other compensation** — describe clearly what will be provided
    - **No compensation** — a justification is required

    **Section 3 — Methods of recruitment**

    This section covers two steps: how potential participants will be identified before contact (pre-selection), and how the first approach will be made (initial contact).

    - **Part A — Pre-selection** — Options include a research bank, departmental or government database (e.g., RAMQ, MED-ÉCHO), medical records, or other. Ensure your answer is consistent with the "Special characteristics" field elsewhere in the form.
    - **Part B — Initial contact** — Options include letter, phone, direct contact during a consultation, advertising material, or other (including social media). Materials used to approach participants must be uploaded in Project Files.

    <Info>
      When feasible, patients should first be informed by someone in their circle of care — such as a bedside nurse or attending physician — that a research team may approach them. If your study will not follow this approach, be prepared to explain why.

      If participants will be identified or approached through departments outside the PI's home department, or at another centre, obtain Department Head Authorization from each department involved.
    </Info>

    **Section 4 — Terms of consent**

    Select how consent will be obtained.

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Signed informed consent form (default for most studies)">
        The participant reads, signs, and receives a copy of the consent form before any study procedures begin. Use a consent template from the **Centre for Applied Ethics (CAE)**. The consent form uploaded to Nagano must match the version described in this section.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Electronic informed consent form">
        Use when consent will be obtained through a validated electronic platform. The electronic form must be equivalent in content to a written consent form. Describe the platform and process clearly.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Verbal consent">
        Acceptable when there are valid reasons for not requiring consent in writing (e.g., telephone-only studies). A justification is required. The methods used to obtain verbal consent must be recorded in the participant's file (TCPS 2, art. 3.12 and 10.2). Verbal consent does not mean informal — the information provided must meet the same standards as written consent.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Implied consent / anonymous participation">
        Used when completing or returning a survey constitutes implied consent. Typically limited to anonymous, minimal-risk studies. Not appropriate for interventional studies.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Consent already obtained (secondary use of data)">
        Used when the study involves data for which consent was already obtained in a prior context. Nagano will ask for details on how and when that consent was obtained.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Deferred consent (emergency context)">
        Applies in emergency research where the participant cannot consent at the time of enrolment. Research activities can only proceed if they meet the immediate needs of the person and satisfy criteria pre-established by the REB (TCPS 2, art. 3.8). The REB will scrutinize this option carefully.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Exemption requested — no consent">
        Used when you are requesting that the REB waive the consent requirement entirely. A specific justification is required. Review TCPS 2, Chapter 3 before selecting this option.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    **Section 5 — Recruitment and consent process**

    Describe both the recruitment process and the consent process in concrete, role-specific terms: who will be involved, and when and where each step will take place.

    <Warning>
      "The research team will recruit participants" is not sufficient. Name the specific role or function for each step, and specify the setting and timing. Vague process descriptions are among the most common reasons this section is flagged for clarification.
    </Warning>

    **Recruitment materials**

    Recruitment materials are not uploaded in this section — they belong in **Project Files**. What counts as a recruitment material:
    - Posters and advertisements
    - Email or letter invitations to participate
    - Telephone recruitment scripts
    - Social media text or digital ads
    - Explanatory material given to potential participants before consent

    For initial REB review, materials may be submitted in English or French. For final institutional authorization, both English and French versions will typically be required.

    **Before you move on — checklist**
    - Visit numbers and durations described as specifically as possible
    - Compensation details (or rationale for none) consistent with consent form and recruitment materials
    - Pre-selection method consistent with data access and bank/registry fields elsewhere in the form
    - Consent type selected and matches practice; non-standard choices include a rationale
    - Recruitment and consent process describes specific roles, timing, and setting
    - Recruitment materials identified and ready to upload in Project Files
  </Step>

  <Step title="F11 Part 5 — Privacy and confidentiality">
    Part 5 covers three screens: protection of personal information, data retention, and archiving. Reviewers look for internal consistency — your data classification, access list, retention period, and archiving plan should align with each other and with what you have described in the consent and recruitment sections.

    **Protection of personal information**

    Select the principal protection strategy that best describes how personal information will be protected.

    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="Anonymous">
        Data is collected in a way that makes it impossible to identify the participant. Select this only if identification is truly not possible given how the data is collected.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="Anonymized">
        Data was once identifiable but all identifiers have been permanently redacted. If a linkage key still exists somewhere, select coded information instead.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="Coded">
        Participants are assigned a code that replaces direct identifiers. A linkage key is held separately under controlled access. This is the most common approach in clinical research. Be prepared to describe where the linkage key is stored, who can access it, and how long it is retained.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="Identifiable">
        The study retains directly identifiable information. Describe the specific technical and procedural safeguards in place (encrypted systems, role-based access, locked storage).
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>

    Nagano also asks whether research data or the informed consent form will be included in participants' medical files. If yes, specify what data will be included and provide a justification.

    For access to personal information: identify any people or organizations beyond sponsor representatives, regulatory authorities, the REB, and quality assurance who may access participants' personal information during the study.

    **Data retention**

    Select all data formats that apply (paper, electronic, audio/video, other). Describe data protection policies and backup strategies.

    For the retention period, use **Other** and enter the appropriate period:
    - **Health Canada–regulated studies** — 15 years after project closure
    - **Non–Health Canada–regulated human clinical studies** — 7 years after project closure

    <Warning>
      Do not select "25 years" from the dropdown — it is an outdated interface default that does not reflect current regulatory expectations. Always choose "Other" and type the appropriate period.
    </Warning>

    **Archiving location and responsibility**

    Name the planned post-completion storage location and confirm who is responsible for the archive. For single-site MUHC studies, responsibility is typically the RI-MUHC. See SOP-CR-015, section 5.3 for archiving requirements.

    **Before you move on — checklist**
    - Data classification selected and consistent with the rest of the form
    - All parties who could access personal information listed
    - Data formats, access controls, and backup strategy described
    - Retention period set to 15 years (Health Canada–regulated) or 7 years (other) using "Other" in the dropdown
    - Archiving location named and responsibility confirmed
  </Step>

  <Step title="F11 Part 6 — Data access (F17/F18/EFVP)">
    Part 6 asks whether your project involves accessing health and social services information, under what consent conditions that access occurs, and whether supplementary privacy forms (F17, F18, or EFVP) are required. Answering accurately here prevents delays in feasibility review.

    **Access to health and social services information**

    Answer **Yes** when any part of the project involves accessing health and social services information — especially if you will access medical records before or without consent.

    Answer **No** only when your project collects research data with consent, without accessing health and social services information (e.g., surveys, questionnaires, or focus groups) and you are not accessing identifiable information in records to determine eligibility.

    **If Yes — three scenarios**

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="Scenario A — Access before consent (pre-screening)">
        Any research team member accesses identifiable personal information in institutional records **before consent** to determine participant eligibility.

        **Action required:** Create, complete, and submit an **F17** in the project's Forms to begin the required privacy process. A guide for completing the F17 is available directly in Nagano.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Scenario B — Access without consent">
        Any research team member accesses and collects identifiable personal information **without first obtaining participant consent** — even if this applies to only a portion of the project.

        **Action required:** Create, complete, and submit an **F18** in the project's Forms to begin the EFVP (full privacy impact assessment) process.

        If all requested data can be obtained from the RI-MUHC Data Warehouse in anonymized or aggregated form, an EFVP may not be required. If DW services are needed, you must also submit a separate [IT Rescue request](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:360) through the RI-MUHC Portal — this is in addition to what you indicate in Nagano.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="Scenario C — Access only with consent">
        Access to health and social services information occurs **only after consent is obtained**, and the consent covers that access.

        No additional privacy form is triggered. Indicate this scenario in Nagano and ensure your consent documentation accurately reflects the scope of access.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    **RAMQ data access**

    If your project will access data held by the Régie de l'assurance maladie du Québec (RAMQ), a separate RAMQ authorization is required in addition to your standard ethics approval. You must obtain and upload an authorization letter. More information is available on the [RAMQ website](https://www.ramq.gouv.qc.ca/en/node/89631).

    **Sharing data outside your organization**

    If information will be shared with a researcher or collaborator outside your organization, Nagano will direct you to complete the Contractual aspects section. An agreement may be required before data sharing can proceed.

    **Before you move on — checklist**
    - Health and social services information question answered (Yes or No)
    - If Yes: correct scenario identified (A, B, or C) and corresponding action taken in Nagano
    - If Scenario A applies: F17 created in the project's Forms
    - If Scenario B applies: F18 created; Data Warehouse path considered; IT Rescue request submitted if DW services are needed
    - If RAMQ data will be accessed: separate RAMQ authorization process initiated
    - If data will be shared externally: Contractual aspects section addressed
  </Step>

  <Step title="F11 Part 7 — Feasibility">
    Part 7 asks you to document the MUHC, MNI, and Institute resources your study requires — departments, nursing, clinical staff, space and equipment, laboratory services, pharmacy, and budget and contract information. Where a resource is not applicable, indicate "No" or "None" as prompted.

    **Section 1 — Departments involved**

    Add all MUHC departments where the project will primarily take place, and any department where participants are likely to receive care as a result of their participation. Upload a signed **Department Head Authorization letter** for each additional department involved.

    **Section 2 — MUHC nursing resources**

    If MUHC nurses will be involved, indicate which aspects they will be involved in, estimated total hours, and which settings. You will also be asked whether the project involves **research product administration** — your answer determines which Nursing Feasibility Review form to download, sign, and upload.

    If you have CIM services or a dedicated research nurse and will not require MUHC nursing services, answer No.

    **Section 3 — Other MUHC human resources (clinical)**

    Document any MUHC-affiliated clinical staff involved other than nurses (e.g., technicians, psychologists, social workers, medical records staff). For each site, select the site, add each type of human resource, describe their role, and estimate total hours.

    **Section 4 — Material resources**

    Detail material resources per site. For each site, indicate which resource types apply: exam or interview room, patient room (overnight hospitalization), or specialized equipment.

    <Warning>
      If you plan to use CIM space, equipment, or services, confirm availability with CIM directly before completing this section. Do not assume CIM resources are available — availability must be verified before you indicate their use in Nagano.
    </Warning>

    **Section 5 — Laboratory services**

    Indicate whether the project requires MUHC clinical laboratory services (testing, sample processing, storage, LIS/CERNER data extraction). If tests are needed for study purposes only — beyond standard of care — Nagano will ask whether a request for lab services has already been transmitted.

    **Section 6 — Pharmacy services**

    If the study involves a drug or natural product, complete the pharmacy questions: study context, drug supply source, services required, dispensing details. Upload the **Pharmacy Manual** as prompted. Note: the Pharmacy Manual belongs here — not in Project Files.

    **Section 7 — MNI resources**

    If the project uses MNI resources (PET, MRI, MEG), upload the required approval letter(s) from the appropriate MNI review committees.

    **Section 8 — Budget aspects**

    Indicate whether the project requires financial resources and which institution will manage the funds. Use the pricelist in Nagano to estimate costs. The REB service fee schedule (Barème de facturation) is in the Documentation section of Nagano under "Billing" — account for these fees in your budget.

    For **industry-sponsored studies**, note that the 30% overhead calculation applies to industry-sponsored studies only, and budget negotiation with the sponsor is the responsibility of the study team.

    **Section 9 — Contractual aspects**

    If the project requires a contract or agreement, Nagano will ask you to select the relevant contracts office.

    <Warning>
      **Dual submission required for RI-MUHC contracts:** uploading a contract to Nagano does not start the RI-MUHC contract review process. You must submit in **both Nagano and [MyContracts](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:371)** to trigger review.
    </Warning>

    **Before you leave this section — checklist**
    - All departments listed, with Department Head Authorization letters uploaded where required
    - Nursing involvement answered; Nursing Feasibility Review form signed and uploaded if applicable
    - All other MUHC human resources documented with role descriptions and hour estimates
    - CIM availability confirmed directly before indicating CIM resource use
    - Laboratory services requirements described
    - If drugs involved: pharmacy details and Pharmacy Manual complete and uploaded
    - MNI approval letters uploaded for any PET, MRI, or MEG resources
    - Budget indicated with costs estimated using the pricelist; REB service fees accounted for
    - If a contract is required: submitted in both Nagano and MyContracts
  </Step>

  <Step title="F11 Part 8 — Project files">
    Part 8 contains a dedicated upload screen for each document type. Reviewers use these files to assess your study — version accuracy matters. Every document should show a version date or number within the file itself, and those details must match what you enter in Nagano's version fields.

    **Upload slots**

    <AccordionGroup>
      <Accordion title="1. Research protocol (all studies)">
        Upload the protocol with version date, page numbers, and any annexes or appendices.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="2. Informed consent form (if consent required)">
        Upload all participant informed consent forms. Use the Centre for Applied Ethics (CAE) consent templates as a starting point. Plan for French and English versions before final institutional authorization.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="3. Biobank framework (if applicable)">
        Upload the biobank framework document with version details if your project includes a biobank component.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="4. Investigator's Brochure (if applicable)">
        Upload the most recent Investigator's Brochure when your project includes an investigational product. For Health Canada–regulated drug trials, a current IB is typically expected. Note: the Pharmacy Manual belongs in the Pharmacy services screen — not here.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="5. Recruitment documents (if applicable)">
        Upload all materials used to recruit or approach participants: posters, advertisements, invitation letters, emails, phone scripts, social media text. For multi-site projects, upload only materials used within the RSSS.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="6. Questionnaires and research material (if applicable)">
        Upload questionnaires, participant diaries, reminder cards, SMS text, and team instructions. Include version dates on all documents.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="7. Information for participants (if applicable)">
        Upload information sheets, educational materials, and drug or device information sheets provided to participants. Plan for French and English versions before final institutional authorization.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="8. Health Canada documents (if applicable)">
        Upload the No Objection Letter (NOL) and any other relevant Health Canada authorization documentation.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="9. Letters of collaboration / support (if applicable)">
        Upload letters of support or collaboration from external partners or co-investigators. Record the letter date in the field Nagano provides.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="10. Service agreements (if applicable)">
        Upload service agreements with departments involved in the study. Record the agreement date.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="11. Other REBs involved (if applicable)">
        If another REB has reviewed your study, upload the approval letter and associated approved documents, including consent forms from the other site.
      </Accordion>
      <Accordion title="12. Supplementary documents (if applicable)">
        Upload any other review-relevant documents that do not belong in a dedicated slot above. Documents related to feasibility assessments go in the appropriate screen under Institution-specific evaluation — not here.
      </Accordion>
    </AccordionGroup>

    **Before you leave this section — checklist**
    - Every uploaded document is the current version
    - Version date and version number fields are completed and match the document
    - Each document is in the correct slot for its type
    - Documents that need French and English versions are noted for final institutional authorization
  </Step>

  <Step title="F11 Part 9 — Agreement and PI signature">
    This is the final part of the F11 before submission. It covers the PI's research privileges and training status, any conflict of interest, additional comments for reviewers, and the PI's formal commitment and signature.

    **Research privileges, status, and training**

    Indicate whether the PI holds medical practice privileges at the MUHC, and which designation applies:

    - **Human Research Privileges** — for physicians and dentists on the MUHC CPDP. Note: pharmacists are CPDP members but are not eligible for Privileges under MSSS and MUHC frameworks — they must obtain Human Researcher Status instead.
    - **Human Researcher Status** — for individuals who are not CPDP members (e.g., PhDs, nurses, physiotherapists, psychologists). Individuals with this status cannot conduct research involving human medical oversight.

    <Note>
      Fellows, residents, medical students, and others listed on the Task Delegation Log are not eligible for Human Research Privileges or Human Researcher Status — these apply to PIs, Sponsor-Investigators, and Sub-Is acting as PI backup only. All research personnel are still required to complete institutional training.
    </Note>

    The PI and all team members on the Task Delegation Log must complete the training required for the study's risk level and type:

    | Training | When required |
    |----------|---------------|
    | SOP Training | All studies — level varies by study risk |
    | Good Clinical Practice (GCP) | Clinical trials |
    | Health Canada Division 5 | Drug studies regulated under Health Canada Division 5 |
    | ISO 14155 GCP (Medical Devices) | Medical device studies |

    **Conflict of interest**

    Indicate whether you or any collaborators are in a real, potential, or perceived conflict of interest — including financial, professional, or institutional conflicts and any payments or relationships with sponsors. When in doubt, disclose.

    **Additional comments**

    Use this optional field to provide key context that will help reviewers interpret your submission — clarifications or study-specific considerations that do not fit elsewhere in the form. Do not repeat information already covered in the form.

    **Commitment and PI signature**

    <Tabs>
      <Tab title="PI is submitting directly">
        The PI logs in with their own Nagano credentials and completes the commitment and signature step directly within the form. No separate document is required.
      </Tab>
      <Tab title="Coordinator or collaborator is submitting">
        The coordinator or collaborator cannot sign on behalf of the PI. The PI must complete and sign the **Principal Investigator's Commitment and Signature form** and the signed form must be attached as instructed in Nagano before submitting.
      </Tab>
    </Tabs>

    <Warning>
      The PI's commitment and signature must be in place before the submission is complete. Missing or incomplete PI sign-off is one of the most common reasons for submission delays.
    </Warning>

    **Before you submit — checklist**
    - PI holds valid Human Research Privileges (MD/dentist) or Human Researcher Status (non-MD/dentist)
    - Required institutional training is complete for the PI and delegated staff
    - Conflict of interest declaration completed
    - Additional comments added if any study-specific context is needed
    - PI commitment and signature step completed — either directly in Nagano or via signed attachment
  </Step>
</Steps>
