# Before You Start a New Submission

Most avoidable delays in the review process happen before a submission is even created. This section covers what to confirm, what to prepare, and what to resolve before you open Nagano.

## Why preparation matters

Common causes of preventable delays are not technical — they are preparation gaps. The most frequent ones are:

- Key details not yet confirmed — roles, study type, oversight requirements
- Documents not ready when the form asks for them
- Answers and uploads that do not match each other

Taking time to align your study package before opening Nagano reduces back-and-forth with reviewers and shortens the path to authorization.

---

## What to have ready before you begin

Before creating your project in Nagano, you should have at minimum:

- A finalized protocol — or the version you are ready to submit
- A clear recruitment and consent approach
- A clear data and privacy plan — what is collected, how it is protected, who can access it
- Any known participant-facing materials you plan to use
- An early view of feasibility needs — services, resources, and required approvals

<Note>
For a full list of required documentation by study type, consult the [Study Submission page](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:220) on the RI-MUHC Portal.
</Note>

## Who builds the submission

A submission can be completed by the PI, a coordinator, or another study team member. Regardless of who builds the file, the submission is not complete until the PI completes the final commitment and signature step in Nagano.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="PI-led workflow — PI builds and signs">
    The PI creates the project, completes the form, uploads supporting documents, and completes the final commitment and signature step. Ensure the PI has **administrator-level access** to the project from the start.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Team-supported workflow — coordinator or team member builds; PI signs">
    A coordinator or team member creates the project, completes the form, and uploads documents. The PI must be added to the project with **administrator-level access** and completes the final commitment and signature step using their own Nagano credentials. If the PI is submitting the signature form independently (rather than through Nagano directly), attach the signed PI Commitment and Signature form as instructed in Nagano.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Confirm the PI early

The PI field in Nagano is not simply about who wrote the protocol or who will publish the results. It is tied to whether the study requires medical oversight — and this has compliance implications.

### A note on terminology

<Note>
**PI vs. Qualified Investigator (QI):** "PI" (Principal Investigator) is a commonly used term but is not a legally defined term in Canada. The legally defined term is **Qualified Investigator (QI)** — the person responsible for the conduct of a clinical trial at a site, who is entitled to provide health care under provincial law. Nagano uses "PI" throughout, but for interventional clinical studies, the PI in Nagano is effectively acting in the QI role.
</Note>

### PI eligibility by study type

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Interventional clinical study — PI must be a physician (Qualified Investigator)">
    The PI must be a CPDP member (physician or dentist) in good standing at the MUHC and must hold **Human Research Privileges** with up-to-date institutional training.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Non-interventional study — PI can be a non-CPDP RI-MUHC researcher">
    The PI must hold **Human Researcher Status** — for researchers who are not physicians or dentists (e.g., PhDs, nurses, physiotherapists, psychologists). Human Researcher Status does not permit research involving medical oversight. If the protocol author is not the PI, they may be listed as a co-investigator or collaborator.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
Choosing the wrong PI early can require revising key fields or reworking parts of the submission later. Confirm PI eligibility before creating the project.
</Warning>

<Note>
Reference: RI-MUHC [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500), section 4.0.
</Note>

## Identify feasibility requirements early

Feasibility questions in Nagano confirm whether the study can be conducted at the MUHC with the right resources and approvals. Knowing what applies to your study before you begin saves time later in the form.

Review the following areas and identify what your study will require:

- Departments involved — and whether department head authorizations will be required
- MUHC nursing resources, or CIM services / dedicated research nurse
- Laboratory services
- Pharmacy services
- MNI imaging resources — PET, MRI, MEG
- Budget and funding documentation
- Contracts or agreements — with external collaborators, service providers, data-sharing partners, or sponsor

## Before you start in Nagano

Use this checklist to confirm you are ready to create your project.

- ✓ Protocol is finalized (or ready for submission)
- ✓ Recruitment and consent approach is confirmed
- ✓ Data and privacy plan is in place
- ✓ Participant-facing materials are identified
- ✓ Feasibility needs are identified (departments, resources, approvals)
- ✓ Correct PI is confirmed — eligibility verified for this study type
- ✓ Workflow is agreed — who builds the submission and who signs
