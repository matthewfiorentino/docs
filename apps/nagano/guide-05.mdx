# F11 Part 3 — Benefits, Risks and Inconveniences

Covers the benefits, risks, and inconveniences fields in the F11-H — including standard treatment comparison, placebo use, and any safety committee overseeing the study.

## Part 3: Benefits, Risks and Inconveniences

This part of the F11-H asks you to situate the study in relation to the standard of care, declare and justify any use of placebo, and disclose whether a safety committee is in place. Reviewers are looking for a clear, honest account of what participants stand to gain or lose by enrolling — including any risks created by changing or stopping existing treatment. The logic chain between treatment change, associated risks, and proposed benefit is examined closely.

## Section 1 — Standard treatment comparison

Confirm the following in your response:

- A clear description of the current standard treatment.
- How the experimental treatment differs from what participants would otherwise receive.
- A clear yes/no on whether participation requires stopping or changing any existing treatment.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Stopping or changing treatment is required">
    If stopping or changing a current treatment is required, explain:
    - Why the change is necessary.
    - The risks created by stopping or changing the current treatment.
    - The potential benefit of the proposed approach in relation to that change.

    <Warning>
      **Reviewers will examine this logic closely:** The justification for requiring a treatment change must be clear and reviewable. Gaps in this chain — particularly around risks of stopping current treatment — are a common source of REB questions.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="No change to existing treatment">
    Indicate that participation does not require stopping or altering any existing treatment. No additional justification is required for this field, but ensure your description of the experimental approach is still complete and accurate in relation to the standard of care.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Section 2 — Use of placebo

Indicate whether your study uses a placebo. If yes, Nagano will prompt for an ethical justification and a description of placebo-related risks.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Placebo is used">
    - **Ethical justification** — Select the option that best fits your study context. Use the notes area to clarify anything that could be misunderstood — for example, if the placebo is added on top of standard therapy, if rescue medication is permitted, or if the placebo phase is short.
    - **Placebo-related risks** — Describe the risks associated with placebo use, including the risk of delayed effective treatment, symptom worsening, and any additional monitoring burden placed on participants.

    <Warning>
      **Reviewers will look closely at placebo risk:** If placebo adds meaningful risk, the ethical justification must be proportionally strong. Vague or minimal justifications are a common source of REB follow-up on studies with a placebo arm.
    </Warning>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="No placebo">
    Select **No** and continue to Section 3. No additional information is required in this field.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Section 3 — Advisory/safety committee (DMC/DSMB)

Nagano asks whether your project has a safety review committee — such as a **Data Monitoring Committee (DMC)** or **Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)**.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Yes — committee exists or is planned">
    Nagano will ask whether the committee is independent of the sponsor.
    - **If yes (independent)** — no further input is required for this field.
    - **If no (not independent)** — provide a justification in the text field that appears.

    <Note>
      **What reviewers may look for:** Be prepared to describe the committee's role (safety signal review, interim data review, stopping rules), how often it meets or reviews data, and whether a charter exists or is in development.
    </Note>

    <Note>
      **Reference:** TCPS 2 (2022), Chapter 11, Section C provides guidance on when an independent safety committee is appropriate for your study type and risk level.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="No committee">
    Provide a justification and a detailed plan for monitoring participant safety. If you have a supporting document, attach it in the **Additional documents** field in the file submission section — not here.

    <Note>
      **What to cover in your justification and monitoring plan:** Explain why a formal committee is not warranted — for example, that the study is low-risk, single-site, short in duration, or uses a well-established intervention. Then describe what monitoring looks like instead: who reviews safety data, at what intervals, and what criteria would trigger an early stop or protocol change. If your team uses a documented PI-led safety review process, reference it here.
    </Note>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Before you move on

Confirm each item before continuing to Part 4.

- ✓ Standard treatment is described and the experimental approach is clearly differentiated.
- ✓ If stopping or changing a current treatment is required, the rationale and associated risks are explained.
- ✓ Placebo use is declared and ethically justified; placebo-related risks are described.
- ✓ Safety committee presence is declared; independence confirmed or non-independence justified. If no committee, a safety monitoring plan is provided.
