Protecting participant safety requires prompt and accurate reporting whenever something unexpected happens during a study. These two SOPs define your obligations when adverse events occur, when a Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction (SUSAR) must be reported to Health Canada, and when deviations or other events must be reported to the REB. They apply to Levels I–IV, with specific obligations varying by whether you are acting as investigator only or as Sponsor-Investigator.

<Warning>
**Controlled documents — always use the online version.** Do not rely on downloaded or printed copies. Your first reading of each SOP must be completed through the SOP Reader on TalentLMS to count toward your training record.
</Warning>

## Critical reporting timelines

Understanding when each type of safety event must be reported — and to whom — is essential. Below is a summary of the key timelines before the full SOP details.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="24-hour reporting">
    **Fatal or life-threatening SUSARs (Sponsor-Investigator studies):** You must notify Health Canada within 24 hours of becoming aware of a fatal or life-threatening Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction. This is a hard regulatory deadline under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations.

    For industry-sponsored trials, the Sponsor holds this obligation — but you must still report the event to the Sponsor immediately upon awareness so they can meet their deadline.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="7-day reporting">
    **Fatal or life-threatening SUSARs — full written report:** After the 24-hour expedited notification, a complete written report must follow within 7 calendar days. For Sponsor-Investigator studies, use the RI-MUHC SAE Report (SOP-CR-012, Appendix 4) and Health Canada's CIOMS Form 1 (Appendix 5).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="15-day reporting">
    **Non-fatal, non-life-threatening SUSARs (Sponsor-Investigator studies):** A complete written report must be submitted to Health Canada within 15 calendar days of first awareness.

    **All SUSARs to the REB:** Report to the RI-MUHC REB within 15 days using the Nagano reportable event submission process (SOP-CR-026).
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## SUSAR classification

A Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction (SUSAR) must meet **all three** of the following conditions simultaneously:

1. **Serious** — the event results in death, is life-threatening, requires hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization, results in persistent or significant disability/incapacity, or is a congenital anomaly/birth defect.
2. **Unexpected** — the nature, severity, or frequency of the reaction is not consistent with the current Investigator's Brochure (IB) or the study protocol.
3. **Related** — there is a reasonable possibility that the reaction is causally related to the investigational product.

<Warning>
If you are uncertain whether an event meets the SUSAR threshold, treat it as a SUSAR and report it within the required timelines. Underreporting is a serious GCP violation. Consult the Research Hub Quality team if you need guidance.
</Warning>

  <Accordion title="SOP-CR-012 — Adverse Events, SAEs and SUSAR Reporting · v19-Jun-2023 · Levels I–III">
    This SOP defines how the research team must identify, classify, document, and report adverse events (AEs), serious adverse events (SAEs), and SUSARs throughout the conduct of a clinical research study. It covers the obligations of the site investigator for both industry-sponsored and Sponsor-Investigator studies.

    **Key points:**
    - Every adverse event must be recorded on the **Adverse Event Log** (Appendix 1), regardless of whether it is considered related to the study intervention. Log entries must include onset date, resolution date, severity grade, relationship assessment, and action taken.
    - All SAEs must be reported to the Sponsor within the timeframe specified in the study protocol (typically 24 hours of awareness). For industry-sponsored studies, use the Sponsor's own SAE report form in addition to completing the Local SAE Reporting Log.
    - For Sponsor-Investigator studies, SAEs with a reasonable possibility of being related to the IP must also be assessed for SUSAR criteria and reported to Health Canada using the **RI-MUHC SAE Report** (Appendix 4) and **Health Canada SAE CIOMS Form 1** (Appendix 5) within the applicable 7- or 15-day deadline.
    - External SUSARs (received from the Sponsor for industry-sponsored trials) must be logged on the **External SUSAR Reporting Log** (Appendix 3) and reported to the REB per SOP-CR-026.
    - Follow-up reports are required until an SAE is resolved or stabilized. Each follow-up must reference the original report.

  <Accordion title="SOP-CR-026 — Ongoing REB Review and Reportable Events · v01-May-2025 · Levels I–IV">
    This SOP defines the events and changes that must be reported to the RI-MUHC REB during the conduct of a study, the timelines for each type of report, and how to manage protocol deviations and corrective actions. It works in conjunction with SOP-CR-012 to ensure the REB has all the information needed to maintain ethical oversight.

    **Key points:**
    - Protocol amendments — changes to the protocol, ICF, recruitment materials, or other approved documents — must be submitted to the REB and approved **before** implementation, except in emergencies where immediate deviation is required to protect participants.
    - **Protocol deviations** (unplanned departures from the approved protocol) must be documented on the **Protocol/GCP/SOP Deviation Log** (Appendix 1) as soon as they are identified. Deviations that affect participant safety or data integrity must also be reported to the Sponsor and REB.
    - **Annual renewals** (Continuation Reports) must be submitted to the REB before the anniversary of initial approval. The Nagano system manages renewal reminders; you are responsible for ensuring submission occurs on time.
    - Reportable events include: SUSARs, unanticipated problems involving risks to participants, significant protocol deviations, participant complaints, and data breaches involving participant information.
    - When a deviation is identified, a **CAPA Form** (Appendix 2) must be completed documenting the root cause, corrective actions taken, and preventive measures implemented.
