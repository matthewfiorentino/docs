## 1 · Purpose {#purpose}

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the management of investigational
products, regardless of the source (e.g., includes commercially available products).
This SOP describes receipt, labelling, storage, distribution, accountability, and return or
authorized destruction. lnvestigational products include drugs, biological products,
natural health products, radiopharmaceuticals, and medical devices.

---

## 2 · Scope {#scope}

This SOP is applicable to all clinical studies undertaken at the site, and to those clinical
research personnel responsible for managing investigational products for a clinical
study.

The study protocol should outline specific procedures for handling the investigational
product/s. The term investigational product, as used in this SOP includes drugs,
biological products, natural health products, radiopharmaceuticals, and medical devices,
as necessary.

## 5 · Procedure {#procedure}
### 5.1 Role of the MUHC Pharmacy

**5.1.1** Based on the regulations of *An Act Respecting Health Services and Social Services* (RSQ s-4.2) and the Quebec Professional Order of Pharmacists (OPQ), any medication to be administered to a patient, whether clinical or on an experimental protocol, must be dispensed by a pharmacist. Although the pharmacist is delegated this task, the responsibility remains with the Qualified Investigator (QI).

**5.1.2** For all studies (industry as well as QI-initiated) where MUHC Pharmacy Services will be utilized, the MUHC Pharmacy must be informed and invited to the Study Initiation Visit for training on the research protocol and investigational product. Once the study is initiated, the IP will be sent directly to the MUHC Pharmacy where it will be stored and dispensed. Pharmacy should also be delegated accountability of IP.

**5.1.3** Only the QI and Sub-Investigators (MDs) can prescribe medication.

**5.1.4** At the time of study submission through <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> (refer to [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007)), in specific conditions or circumstances, the Investigator may request approval for the RI-MUHC Research Nurse or MUHC Nurse to dispense IP directly to research subjects. The MUHC Research Pharmacy will only approve this after verifying compliance with Quebec regulations (Pharmacy Act). If approved by the pharmacy, the pharmacy waiver must be kept with essential documents.

**5.1.5** It is the QI's responsibility to ensure that the Pharmacy is provided the IP Receipt Log and the IP Dispensing Log and to ensure the completion of these documents. The QI will also delegate tasks related to IP to pharmacy (reception, inventory/accountability, labelling, storage, dispensing, return/destruction and randomization if applicable).

**5.1.6** It is the QI's responsibility to ensure that, if the drug is dispensed by a trained and qualified staff member, the pharmacy will provide an identical label (to that of the IP) which must be applied to the appropriate source document form.

### 5.2 Labelling and coding of investigational product

**5.2.1** As documented in Health Canada's Food and Drug Regulations, C.05.011 — Labelling: the Sponsor shall ensure that the investigational drug bears a label in both official languages with the following information:

- "Investigational Product" / "Produit expérimental"
- Name, address, and telephone number of the Sponsor
- Protocol code number
- Route of administration, dosage form, and quantity of dosage units
- Lot or code number
- Expiry date
- Storage conditions

**5.2.2** Investigational products must not be dispensed to a subject before the Investigator or delegated pharmacist has verified that the IP label is correct and complete. Any labelling discrepancies must be reported to the Sponsor.

### 5.3 IP receipt and storage

**5.3.1** On receipt, confirm that the IP shipment is accompanied by appropriate shipping documentation and that the IP package integrity is intact. Document receipt on the IP Receipt Log, including receipt date, quantity, lot number, expiry date, and condition on arrival.

**5.3.2** Store IP immediately upon receipt under the conditions specified in the protocol, <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ib">Investigator Brochure</Tooltip>, or Sponsor instructions (temperature, light, humidity). Pharmacy records the storage conditions.

**5.3.3** Temperature excursions — any deviation from specified storage conditions — must be documented immediately, assessed for impact on product integrity, and reported to the Sponsor. Do not administer affected IP without Sponsor authorization.

**5.3.4** IP inventory must be reconcilable at any time. Maintain a running count of units received, dispensed, returned, and destroyed.

### 5.4 IP dispensing and accountability

**5.4.1** For each dispensing event, document: participant study ID, date, amount dispensed, lot number, expiry date, and staff initials. Dispensing records must match inventory accountability records exactly at all times.

**5.4.2** Compliance aids (pill counts, drug diary, returned packaging) must be collected, documented, and reconciled at each visit as per protocol.

**5.4.3** IP accountability records must be available for inspection at all times. File all IP documentation (Receipt Log, Dispensing Log, temperature logs, deviation forms) in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>.

### 5.5 IP return and destruction

**5.5.1** Unused, returned, or expired IP must be segregated from active study IP. Document quantity and condition.

**5.5.2** Return or destroy IP only per the Sponsor's instructions and applicable regulations. Obtain Sponsor authorization before any on-site destruction.

**5.5.3** Document all returns and destructions: date, quantity, lot number, method of destruction or return tracking number, and signatures of witnesses where required.

**5.5.4** Do not dispose of IP through general waste. Follow the MUHC environmental health and safety guidelines for pharmaceutical waste.
