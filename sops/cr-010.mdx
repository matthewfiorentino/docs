<a id="purpose"></a>

### 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the management of investigational products, regardless of the source (e.g., includes commercially available products). This SOP describes receipt, labelling, storage, distribution, accountability, and return or authorized destruction. Investigational products include drugs, biological products, natural health products, radiopharmaceuticals, and medical devices.

<a id="scope"></a>

### 2.0 Scope

This SOP is applicable to all clinical studies undertaken at the site, and to those clinical research personnel responsible for managing investigational products for a clinical study.

The study protocol should outline specific procedures for handling the investigational product/s. The term investigational product, as used in this SOP includes drugs, biological products, natural health products, radiopharmaceuticals, and medical devices, as necessary.

<a id="responsibilities"></a>

### 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor-Investigator or Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>)/Investigator is responsible for ensuring that investigational products are managed according to all of the applicable regulatory, International Conference on Harmonisation (ICH) Good Clinical Practice (GCP), sponsor, and local requirements.

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor-Investigator or Qualified Investigator (QI)/Investigator.

### 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

#### 5.1 Receipt and inventory of investigational products

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** Review the shipping documentation upon receipt of the investigational product /s. Inventory the products received in order to ensure that the information on the shipment invoice corresponds to the products sent and received, including the quantity and lot number, if applicable. Record any damages and/or discrepancies. Document the result of the inventory, and retain with the essential study documentation.

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** Communicate any inconsistency to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, as soon as possible. Document and retain with the essential study documentation

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** Retain all documentation related to transportation and receipt of the investigational products throughout the study, with the essential study documents.

<a id="5-2"></a>

#### 5.2 Labelling and coding of investigational products

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** The manufacturing, labelling, packaging and shipping of investigational product (including active comparator(s), placebos, and devices, as applicable) is the responsibility of the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** Ensure that the label on investigational products is not hidden/covered, withdrawn, or modified without the authorization of the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** Apply an additional label, if required by the protocol or institution (e.g., subject name, institution name, etc). Do not cover the original label of the investigational product.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** Ensure that there is no subject-identifying information (nominative data) on container or device labels, when/if medication or device is returned to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, in order to maintain subject confidentiality.

<a id="5-3"></a>

#### 5.3 Storage of investigational products

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** Store investigational products in a secure environment (e.g., locked room) with controlled access restricted to authorized personnel. Refer to special storage conditions for biologics, radio-pharmaceuticals, or devices, as needed. Site should have a procedure on how to correct environmental changes and have a backup plan (e.g. plan and who should be notified, with contact information).

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** Ensure that the storage location has appropriate and controlled temperature/humidity, as stated in the protocol or other written information provided by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** Monitor the temperature/humidity conditions, and record regularly, either manually or by an automatic device. Be prepared to move the product to an alternate storage area, if problems arise. Document any storage issues

<a id="5-3-4"></a>**5.3.4** Keep storage records within easy access of the investigational product, and have it available for monitors, auditors, etc., if requested.

<a id="5-3-5"></a>**5.3.5** File temperature records with essential study documentation at the end of the clinical trial.

<a id="5-4"></a>

#### 5.4 Distribution/dispensing the investigational product

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** Use the investigational product only in accordance with the approved protocol. Document any use of the investigational product not in accordance with the approved protocol. Report to Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, as required.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** Maintain a Dispensing Log (provided by Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, or equivalent) to document assignment of investigational product to specific study subjects.

<a id="5-4-3"></a>**5.4.3** Inform each study subject about the correct use of the investigational product/s. Inform the subject of his/her responsibility to return all unused devices (if applicable), medication and medication packaging (bottle, container, syringe, etc.), even if empty, as specified in the protocol. Document this interaction in the source documents.

<a id="5-4-4"></a>**5.4.4** Assess subject compliance with the instructions at intervals appropriate for the trial/protocol.

<a id="5-5"></a>

#### 5.5 Accountability for investigational products

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** Document the return of all devices (if applicable), investigational products and/or containers by subjects.

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** Perform a subject or pharmacy follow-up in the event of accounting inconsistency, for the safety of the subject. Document this inconsistency.

<a id="5-5-3"></a>**5.5.3** Maintain documentation of all investigational products and/or empty containers returned by subjects, with the essential study documents.

<a id="5-5-4"></a>**5.5.4** Never give investigational product assigned to a subject and not used, to another study subject, to a subject outside the study, or to another site.

<a id="5-6"></a>

#### 5.6 Return/destruction of investigational products

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Return investigational products left after study completion or for product recall reasons to the sponsor/distributor, or follow the instructions in the protocol or other study document, as required.

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** Obtain written authorization from the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator for destruction of product at the study site, if applicable.

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** Ensure that the institution/pharmacy has appropriate procedures for the destruction of investigational products, and that the destruction is performed in accordance with those procedures.

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** Return or destroy defective or outdated products in the same manner, unless otherwise requested by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-6-5"></a>**5.6.5** File return and/or destruction documentation with essential study documents.

<a id="5-7"></a>

#### 5.7 Randomization procedure

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** Follow the randomization procedures as described in the protocol.

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** Retain all documents relating to randomization by external sources, such as an Interactive Voice Response System (IVRS).

<a id="5-7-3"></a>**5.7.3** Ensure that the randomization code is broken only in accordance with the protocol.

<a id="5-7-4"></a>**5.7.4** File randomization documentation with essential study documents.

<a id="5-8"></a>

#### 5.8 Blinded trials only: unblinding procedure

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** Confirm that the coding system for the investigational product/s includes a mechanism that permits rapid identification of the product/s in case of a medical emergency, but does not permit undetectable breaks of the blinding.

<a id="5-8-2"></a>**5.8.2** Follow the protocol-specific requirements for unblinding the investigational product.

<a id="5-8-3"></a>**5.8.3** Promptly document and explain to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator any premature unblinding of the investigational product/s such as, accidental unblinding or unblinding due to a serious adverse event/drug reaction. Inform <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>/IEC as per their procedures.

<a id="5-8-4"></a>**5.8.4** File unblinding documentation with the essential study documents.

### 1.0 Specific RI-MUHC policies and procedures

<a id="1-1"></a>

<a id="5-1-role-of-the-muhc-pharmacy"></a>

#### 1.1 Role of the MUHC Pharmacy

<a id="1-1-1"></a>**1.1.1** Based on the regulations of An Act Respecting Health Services and Social Services (RSQ s-4.2) and the Quebec Professional Order of Pharmacists (OPQ), any medication to be administered to a patient, whether clinical or on an experimental protocol, must be dispensed by a pharmacist. Although the pharmacist is delegated this task, the responsibility remains with the Qualified Investigator (QI).

<a id="1-1-2"></a>**1.1.2** For all studies (Industry as well as QI-Initiated) where the MUHC Pharmacy Services will be utilized, the MUHC Pharmacy must be informed and invited to the study initiation visit for training on the research protocol and Investigational Product (<Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip>). Once the study is initiated, the IP will be sent directly to the MUHC Pharmacy where it will be stored and dispensed. Pharmacy should also be delegated accountability of IP.

<a id="1-1-3"></a>**1.1.3** Only the QI and Sub-Investigators (MDs) can prescribe medication.

<a id="1-1-4"></a>**1.1.4** At the time of study submission through the [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview) system (refer to [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007)), in specific conditions or circumstances, the Investigator may request approval for the RI-MUHC Research Nurse/MUHC Nurse to dispense IP directly to research subjects. The MUHC Research Pharmacy will only approve this procedure after verifying the compliance of planned drug dispensation with Quebec regulations (Pharmacy Act). If approved by the pharmacy, the pharmacy waiver should be kept with essential documents.

<a id="1-1-5"></a>**1.1.5** It is the QI’s responsibility to ensure that the Pharmacy is provided the IP Receipt Log and the IP Dispensing Log and ensure the completion of these documents. Also the <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> will delegate tasks related to IP to pharmacy (reception, inventory-accountability, labelling, storage, dispensing, return-destruction and randomization if applicable)

<a id="1-1-6"></a>**1.1.6** It is the QI’s responsibility to ensure that, if the drug is dispensed by a trained and qualified staff member, the pharmacy will provide an identical label (to that of the IP) which should be applied on the appropriate source document form.

<a id="1-2"></a>

#### 1.2 Labelling and coding of investigational product (IP)

<a id="1-2-1"></a>**1.2.1** As documented in [Health Canada’s Food and Drug Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/), C.05.011 - Labelling: The Sponsor shall ensure that the investigational drug bears a label on which appears the following information in both official languages:

- A statement indicating that the drug is an Investigational Product to be used only by a Qualified Investigator;
- Name, number or identifying mark of the drug;
- Expiration date of the drug;
- Recommended storage conditions for the drug;
- Lot number of the drug;
- Name and address of the Sponsor;
- The protocol code or identification; and
- Information required by subparagraph C.03.202(1)1(b)(vi), if the drug is a radiopharmaceutical as defined in section C.03.201.

<a id="1-2-2"></a>**1.2.2** Although, the Sponsor has the overall responsibility, the QI should ensure that the labelling of a clinical trial drug meets the requirements listed above.

<a id="1-3"></a>

#### 1.3 Additional comments on investigational storage

<a id="1-3-1"></a>**1.3.1** IP should be stored in a dedicated area, separate from regular medication (refer to Pharmacy SOPs).

<a id="1-3-2"></a>**1.3.2** Have back-up methods for fridges and freezers in case of power failures and ensure pharmacy staff is trained on these backup methods (refer to Pharmacy SOPs).

<a id="1-3-3"></a>**1.3.3** In the event of a temperature excursion, this must be documented and the Sponsor must be informed immediately. The drug must be quarantined until the Sponsor has confirmed that the drug is fit for use or not fit for use.

<a id="1-4"></a>

#### 1.4 Additional comments on IP accountability

<a id="1-4-1"></a>**1.4.1** It is the QI’s responsibility to ensure that an IP Inventory Log and a Dispensing Log are maintained. For these logs, refer to the Appendices of Pharmacy SOP, MON_PEC11_FR01 Titre: Registres d’inventaire et de distribution de médicaments d’essais cliniques, which may be found in the Nagano system.

<a id="1-4-2"></a>**1.4.2** The QI must document any modification to, or deviation from, the drug dosage required by the protocol (in source documents) and report this deviation to the REB.

<a id="1-4-3"></a>**1.4.3** Drug accountability records are NOT required for:

- Drugs that are the subject of a Phase IV clinical trial
- Comparator drug, if it has market authorization, is used as per Canadian monograph, and hasn't been altered in any manner
- Rescue or concomitant medications that may be used off-label but are not subject of the clinical trial (for example, they are used as supportive medications for known clinical applications).
