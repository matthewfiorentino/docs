# Clinical Trial Application (Natural Health Products)

<Note>
This SOP describes the process for submitting a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application</Tooltip> (CTA) to Health Canada's Natural and Non-prescription Health Products Directorate (NNHPD), requesting approval to carry out an investigational trial on a Natural Health Product (NHP). This SOP applies to Sponsor-Investigators at the RI-MUHC conducting investigator-initiated NHP trials. It does not apply to externally-sponsored trials or to CTAs for drugs (→ [SOP-CR-018](/sops/cr-018)) or ITAs for medical devices (→ [SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024)).

## Key points

- **Research privileges required:** Refer to [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500). Mandatory investigator privilege to conduct research at the RI-MUHC must be in place before submitting a CTA.
- A CTA must be filed for Phase I–III NHP trials and comparative bioavailability trials, including studies using a marketed NHP outside the conditions specified in its Product License/Monograph/Labelling Standard or NPN application (different indication, population, dosage regimen, route, or dosage form).
- **Contact NNHPD before preparing the CTA** to confirm whether your trial will be reviewed by NNHPD (self-care use) or TPD/BGTD (not appropriate for self-care use) and to confirm required templates and format.
- Main documents required: NHP Submission Application Form (CTA and Attestation Form), Study Protocol, Protocol Synopsis, <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip>, <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> Attestation/approval letter, signed <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">Qualified Investigator</Tooltip>'s Undertaking (QIU) form, and <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip> or NHP Monograph/Labelling Standard plus CMC information.
- The NNHPD review has **no default review period** (unlike the 30-day review for drugs); typical review is estimated at 90+ days. Wait for the **Notice of Authorization (NOA)** before commencing any trial activities.
- A Clinical Trial Site Information (CTSI) form, signed QIU form, and REB Attestation must be submitted to NNHPD at least **15 days before** the trial site commences the study.
- CTA amendments (CTA-A) require NNHPD approval before implementing changes, except for immediate safety-related protocol changes (implement immediately, notify within same day, submit full CTA-A within 15 days).
- NNHPD may **suspend or cancel** the NHP CTA authorization per Section 80 of the NHP Regulations if there are safety concerns. The authorization will be restored if corrective information is provided within 30 days; otherwise it is cancelled.
- Records must be retained by the Sponsor-Investigator for **25 years** and made available to Health Canada within 2 calendar days (safety concern) or 7 calendar days (any other case).
