## Purpose and scope

This SOP describes the set-up, maintenance, archiving, and destruction of Investigator Site Files (ISF) and Sponsor-Investigator Trial Master Files (TMF) for clinical research studies at the RI-MUHC. It covers essential document management from study initiation through the long-term archiving period, for both drug/NHP trials (per ICH-GCP E6 R2, section 8) and medical device trials (per ISO 14155, Annex E).

This SOP applies to the research community involved in the management of essential study documents at the MUHC/RI-MUHC.

---

## Responsibilities

The **Sponsor-Investigator** is responsible for the set-up, maintenance, and update of the Trial Master File (TMF), which consists of essential documents from each participating site. The **QI/PI** is responsible for the set-up, maintenance, and update of the Investigator Site File (ISF), which consists of all study-specific essential documents for the QI/PI's site.

Per ICH-GCP (for drug studies), all parts may be delegated to appropriately trained team members but remain the QI/PI's ultimate responsibility. Per ISO-GCP (for device studies), responsibility is delegated along with tasks.

---

## Procedure

### 5.1 TMF and ISF set-up

**5.1.1** Ensure the agreement between the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and the Investigator, and between the Investigator and the ARO (e.g. CIM), describes essential document management including archiving length, return or destruction of documents, and any delegation to a third party.

**5.1.2** The Sponsor-Investigator maintains the TMF; the QI/PI maintains the ISF. Best practice: if a Sponsor-Investigator is also QI/PI at a site in a multicentre study, maintain both a TMF and an ISF.

**5.1.3** For drug trials: the TMF/ISF must include all applicable ICH-GCP E6 R2, section 8 essential documents (→ Appendix 1).

**5.1.4** For device trials: follow ISO 14155, Annex E.

**5.1.5** The Sponsor-Investigator/QI/PI must review and verify the reliability of the TMF/ISF even if this task is delegated. The site's ISF should mirror the Sponsor's/Sponsor-Investigator's TMF, with the exception of a few documents. For single-site Sponsor-Investigator studies, only one structure is required (ISF/TMF are one and the same). → Appendix 4 for TMF/ISF filing and archiving structure.

**5.1.6** Before delegating TMF/ISF tasks, obtain RI-MUHC Privileges/Researcher Status (→ SOP-CR-500) and the appropriate level of training (→ SOP-CR-003). Create a Training Log and Task Delegation Log. Identify team member(s) responsible for collecting, filing, updating, and storing study documents. Ensure staff have documented training (GCP, Health Canada Division 5 for drug trials, IATA/TDG for biological materials, and study-specific training) and are delegated these tasks on the Task Delegation Log (→ SOP-CR-002).

**5.1.7** The QI/PI must ensure participant confidentiality at all times; restrict access to documentation to authorized team members as described on the Task Delegation Log.

**5.1.8** Create the ISF as soon as the first Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator contact occurs or a protocol outline is available. The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator should create the TMF at the same point.

**5.1.9** Until the MUHC/RI-MUHC has a validated 21 CFR Part 11-compliant document management system, the primary TMF/ISF must be paper-based in binders along with individual source document participant files. An electronic backup (without participant source data) may be kept on a share drive.

- All current study documents must be filed in paper form in binders.
- All documents with wet signatures (original penned signatures) must be kept in paper: Agreements, Protocol and IB signature pages, ICFs, paper CRFs, and all documents initialled and dated by the QI/PI, team member, or participant. Note: eSignatures are only compliant within a 21 CFR Part 11-validated system.
- Superseded PDF documents (older versions of signed protocols, IBs, blank ICF versions, study manuals, training slides) may be stored electronically; keep a NTF with the current paper version indicating the location of superseded documents.
- Superseded PDF versions of blank participant-specific source forms may be filed electronically by version number; keep a NTF with the current paper version.

**5.1.10** Create a logical, organized filing system allowing rapid retrieval of essential documents (binders with tabs and a Table of Contents). Industry Sponsors may provide an ISF TOC template and/or pre-labeled binders/boxes. If no ISF structure is provided, use Appendix 4. No blank tabs should remain in the ISF binders. If a document is stored elsewhere (e.g. electronically in PDF form), write the location in a NTF under the binder tab (→ SOP-CR-014 for the NTF template).

**5.1.11** Create a separate reference binder/file for regulations, guidelines, and other relevant clinical trial references.

**5.1.12** Electronic essential documents may be stored on a share drive as backup only. Primary source essential documents must remain in paper form until the RI-MUHC has a validated 21 CFR Part 11-compliant Document Management System.

**5.1.13** Protect the confidentiality of all participant records (e.g. Participant ID code list, ICFs). No participant information should be saved on electronic share drives (except for temporary data transfer).

**5.1.14** Ensure all corrections and updates to documents are attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, available, enduring, consistent, and complete (ALCOA+). Changes must be traceable, unobscured, and explained (→ SOP-CR-014).

**5.1.15** The Sponsor-Investigator/QI/PI must document medical oversight by initialling and dating all essential documents pertaining to patient safety and data integrity in a timely fashion, before filing in the ISF — including all safety reports (local and external SAE/SUSAR), applicable Sponsor newsletters, protocol procedure clarifications, and all study document amendments. Documents with QI/PI initials and dates must be maintained in paper form. If medical oversight is confirmed by email, the email must list the document names and versions read and be printed and placed in the ISF.

**5.1.16** When tasks are delegated to specialists (e.g. Ophthalmologist, Respirologist, Radiologist), the specialist must have signed the MD Specialist agreement (→ SOP-CR-002) and the agreement filed in the ISF with the Task Delegation Log. The specialist must initial and date documents related to their assessment (in paper form or as a printed email confirmation). The QI/PI then initials and dates to document QI/PI oversight.

**5.1.17** When sharing a copy of an original document, ensure participant confidentiality (nominative information removed/blacked out) and fulfill certified copy requirements. The person who makes the copy must mark it "True and Exact Copy," initial, and date. The same person who made the copy attests that it is an accurate and complete representation of the original.

### 5.2 TMF/ISF maintenance during the trial

**5.2.1** TMF/ISF documents should ideally be maintained in their original medium for the entire retention period. Per Health Canada GUI-0100, transfer from original medium to a secondary medium is acceptable — preferably at study completion and only if: corrections can be clearly captured in the secondary medium; the person performing the transfer attests (signs and dates) that the secondary documents are certified copies; the transfer process has been fully validated; the secondary medium allows successful retrieval for the entire retention period; and original medium records are kept as long as needed.

**5.2.2** Maintain and update essential documents as generated or received (ensuring QI/PI initialling and dating for appropriate documents first). Retain all original and revised versions of essential documents as "superseded" or "obsolete."

**5.2.3** Ensure appropriate study documents (Protocol amendments, IB updates, ICF revisions, new patient documents) are REB-approved/acknowledged before implementation. File all REB approvals/acknowledgments in the ISF.

**5.2.4** Superseded/obsolete documents kept in the same binder as current documents must be clearly marked: draw a line through the first page, indicate "superseded," initial, and date. Obsolete electronic files go into a folder marked "obsolete."

**5.2.5** The CRC maintains a Communication section in the ISF for each study, documenting relevant written or verbal communications (Telephone/Video Conference Log) chronologically. A separate communication log may be created for study participants. Pertinent emails should be printed and filed in the ISF.

### 5.3 TMF/ISF archive and storage after trial completion

**5.3.1** Store participant records per institutional policy, relevant privacy legislation, and regulatory requirements.

**5.3.2** Retention periods:
- **Health Canada-regulated studies**: 15 years from the point at which at least one participant signed an ICF (screen failures included) — as of 11-Feb-2022. Prior to this date, Health Canada required 25 years.
- **Non-Health Canada-regulated studies**: 7 years from publication date.
- **EMA requirement**: 25 years. Confirm Sponsor requirements and negotiate adequate long-term storage funding in the study budget before study start.

**5.3.3** Ensure TMF/ISF documents do not contain nominative participant information. Redact all nominative information (e.g. emails regarding SAEs) before archiving. Ensure essential documents are securely stored, protected from fire, water, theft, vandalism, tampering, and loss, while remaining accessible for review, audit, and inspection.

**5.3.4** Inventory the TMF/ISF contents (document type and box number) to facilitate retrieval in the event of an audit or inspection. The storage system must support document identification, version history, search, and retrieval. Maintain an inventory of all electronic backups or superseded documents (type of media, storage location) and monitor regularly. At archiving, all participant source documents and logs must be filed with the ISF.

**5.3.5** Update inventory documents if stored items are moved; notify the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

**5.3.6** The RI-MUHC highly recommends Iron Mountain for document storage. To open an account, obtain a department account number from the RI-MUHC Purchasing Agent (link on the RI Portal under Resources → Finance). Iron Mountain will supply standard boxes, plastic envelopes, transmittal sheets, and instructions. Key points:
- Archive all participant records (including screen failures and withdrawals) for 15 years (Health Canada-regulated) or 7 years from publication (non-regulated).
- Transmittal sheets must be detailed enough to facilitate retrieval of specific documents.
- Keep the original transmittal sheet in a labeled binder documenting all box contents.
- To retrieve boxes for an audit/inspection, call Iron Mountain with the required box numbers.

**5.3.7** For important email communications: archive electronically during the study and print and archive in paper form at study close-out (in the absence of a 21 CFR Part 11 document management system).

**5.3.8** Document when a document is not applicable (N/Ap) on the ISF Filing and Archiving Structure, as not all essential documents apply to all studies.

**5.3.9** The QI/PI or delegate must inform the archiving service of the required retention period.

### 5.4 Back-up and superseded electronic files

**5.4.1** Study folders should be identified with the MUHC REB Study Code.

**5.4.2** All documents must be classified in an existing folder; no orphan files outside the file management structure.

**5.4.3** Keep file names short, simple, and relevant:
- Name with the most important element first
- Avoid all capitals; do not use commas or dots — use dash (–) or underscore (\_)
- Use dd-Mon-yyyy format for dates
- Include a leading 0 for numbers 0–9 (e.g. 09)
- Use descriptive terms (draft, final, memo) at the end of filenames
- Indicate version as "v01, v02 … v10"
- Add initials and revision date at the very end of the filename when revising

**5.4.4** When a new version of a document is available, move the superseded/obsolete document to the appropriate "superseded" folder (only the current version remains in the active folder). Best practice: maintain superseded, current, and pending folders per document type.

**5.4.5** When a study is completed, move the entire electronic backup folder to an "Archive" folder.

**5.4.6** Validate the format and system where documents are retained for intended use.

**5.4.7** Per 21 CFR Part 11, an electronic document management system for essential documents must include:
- An audit trail of all revisions, corrections, and updates
- Backups at regular intervals
- Documented security measures protecting against data corruption
- Controlled access through password protection
- A plan for future accessibility (new staff, monitors, auditors, inspectors)
- Immediate accessibility for regulatory inspections
- If these features are not available, do not use that system except for backup PDFs of superseded documents — use a paper system instead.

### 5.5 TMF/ISF access for auditors and regulatory inspectors

**5.5.1** All essential study documents are subject to audit by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator's auditor, the RI-MUHC QA Department, and inspection by regulatory authorities (Health Canada, FDA).

**5.5.2** Records must be available to regulatory inspectors within an expedited timeframe: immediately for a For-Cause Inspection; 7–14 calendar days in all other cases. A record of archived study documents and their location must be readily available at all times.

**5.5.3** Auditors and inspectors must have access to the entire TMF/ISF (paper only, or paper/electronic hybrid). For hybrid systems, provide printed paper versions. For validated e-ISF/TMF, grant read-access only.

**5.5.4** If a copy of a document is required, make a certified copy and return the original to the archive. The QA Department may assist with certified copy control during an inspection. → SOP-CR-017.

**5.5.5** If originals are removed from storage for inspector review, ensure they are properly secured at all times and returned to the correct storage location.

**5.5.6** Update the ISF Filing and Archiving Structure (Appendix 4) by adding a "Date Document Accessed" and "Date Document Returned" column.

### 5.6 Study file destruction or return (after 15 years or 7 years)

**5.6.1** Consult the Agreement and the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator on file disposition after the required storage time elapses, or if site storage is no longer possible (e.g. facility closure). Do not destroy any files without QI/PI and Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator consent. Retain written permission in the Archiving Binder. → Appendix 5.

**5.6.2** Have the QI/PI sign the Essential Document Destruction Request form and send it to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator with a copy of the TMF/ISF Filing and Archiving Structure (Appendix 4/6).

**5.6.3** Consult the TMF/ISF Filing and Archiving Structure Checklist before contacting Iron Mountain for box numbers.

**5.6.4** If returning documents to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, do so as directed by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator.

**5.6.5** If destroying documents, complete Iron Mountain destruction forms and place an order for destruction as directed.

**5.6.6** Forward destruction documentation to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator; ensure it is completed, signed, dated, and retained on site with the completed ISF Filing and Archiving Structure.

**5.6.7** If destroying on-site, follow the MUHC Destruction Policy for Paper Documents:
- Retain and dispose of all nominative, confidential, and/or sensitive information as required by law or MUHC policy (IM 030POL)
- Discard in a properly identified locked bin for secure destruction per MUHC Document Management policy for recyclable paper
- Contact MUHC Document Management if the bin is full, compromised, or has disappeared

**5.6.8** For other media (CDs, DVDs, video cassettes): discard in a designated locked bin for shredding per the Document Management Services contract. For technological media containing information no longer required: delete the information and reformat or physically destroy the media.

**5.6.9** The QI/PI must ensure documentation related to the destruction of nominative, confidential, and/or sensitive information is completed, signed, dated, and retained.

### 5.7 Trial Master File — Sponsor-Investigator only

**5.7.1** Per ICH E6 R2 and ISO 14155, the TMF/ISF must be established at the beginning of the trial, both at the investigator/institution's site (ISF) and at the Sponsor's/Sponsor-Investigator's office (TMF). Final close-out can only be done after the Monitor has reviewed both files and confirmed all necessary documents are in order.

The TMF contains three levels: Trial Level, Country Level, and Site Level. For paper TMFs:
- 1 binder/box for Trial-level documents
- 1 binder/box per Country for country-level documents
- 1 binder/box per Site for site-level documents

**TMF Document List** → Appendix 7 (created to assist MUHC Sponsor-Investigators with collection and classification by level and study milestone: initiation/activation, updates/amendments, monitoring visits, close-out).

**5.7.2 TMF Plan** → Appendix 7 template for MUHC Sponsor-Investigator-initiated studies. Use the RACI model to outline team roles and responsibilities: **R** = Responsible; **A** = Accountable; **C** = Consulted; **I** = Informed.

**5.7.3** Specify the vendor for the software used for the e-TMF during the study and for TMF archiving at study end.

**5.7.4** Have a TMF archiving plan since the beginning of the study: vendor documentation (including software validation); timelines for short-term and long-term retention; identify and train the MUHC Sponsor-Investigator responsible for TMF archiving.

**5.7.5** The MUHC Sponsor-Investigator must always be inspection-ready and have a process to grant access to the TMF/e-TMF for auditors/inspectors during and after the study.
