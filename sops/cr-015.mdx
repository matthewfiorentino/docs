## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the creation and maintenance of the Investigator Site Files (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>)/Trial Master File (<Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip>), including both general operational documents and the essential documents, as defined by the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), E6, R2 for drug and as defined by ISO 14155 Annex E for medical device.

## 2.0 Scope

This procedure concerns the research community (employees, investigators, physicians, management, consultants, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in creating, maintaining, storing, archiving and destroying study files for research with human participants at the RI-MUHC/MUHC. This SOP is meant to be followed by all human research teams, however, in terms of the ISF/TMF structure, teams conducting device trials should consult ISO-14155 GCP Annex E.

## 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor-Investigator or Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>)/Principal Investigator (<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>) is responsible for ensuring that study file creation and maintenance meet all of the applicable regulatory, ICH-Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and/or ISO-GCP, Sponsor, and local requirements including regulations regarding electronic systems.

As per ICH-GCP, any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members (e.g. Clinical Research Coordinator), but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor-Investigator or QI/PI. Note that according to ISO-GCP (for device studies) responsibility is also delegated along with tasks.

## 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

<a id="5-1-tmf-and-isf-set-up"></a>

### 5.1 Trial master file and Investigator site file set-up

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** Ensure that the agreement between the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and the Investigator as well as the agreement between the Investigator and ARO (e.g. the CIM) describes essential document management, including length of time for archiving and return or destruction of essential documents as well as any delegation to a third party.

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** The Sponsor-Investigator is responsible for set-up, maintenance and update of a Trial Master File (TMF) that consists of essential documents from each of the participating sites. The QI/PI is responsible for the set-up, maintenance and update of an Investigator Site File (ISF) which consists of all study-specific essential documents for the QI/PI’s site. Note: If a Sponsor-Investigator of a multicentre study is also QI/PI at the site, best practice is to have both a TMF and an ISF.

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** For drug trials, the TMF/ISF should include all applicable ICH-GCP, section 8 essential documents (listed in [Appendix 1](#appendix-1)).

<a id="5-1-4"></a>**5.1.4** For device trials, ISO 14155 -GCP, Annex E should be followed.

<a id="5-1-5"></a>**5.1.5** The Sponsor-Investigator/QI/PI is responsible to review and verify the reliability of the TMF/ISF even if this task is delegated. See an example of a TMF/ISF filing and archiving structure in [Appendix 4](#appendix-4) based on ICH-GCP. Note that the site’s ISF should mirror the Sponsor’s/Sponsor-Investigator’s Trial Master File (TMF) with the exception of a few documents. In the case of Sponsor-Investigators with a single site study, only one structure is required at his/her site (ISF/TMF are one in the same).

<a id="5-1-6"></a>**5.1.6** If the Sponsor-Investigator/QI/PI is delegating TMF/ISF tasks, they must first obtain RI-MUHC [Privileges/Researcher Status](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status) as per [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500) and the appropriate level of training as per [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003) as well as create a Training Log and <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>. Identify the human research team member(s) who are responsible for collecting, filing, updating and storing study-related documents. Ensure staff obtains appropriate documented training, (Appropriate level of SOP training, GCP training, Health Canada Division 5 if working on a drug trial, IATA/TDG if shipping biological materials, and/or other general training as well as study-specific training), and are delegated these tasks on the Task Delegation Log and the QI/PI has signed off on this delegation as per [SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002).

<a id="5-1-7"></a>**5.1.7** The QI/PI must ensure participant confidentiality at all times and ensure access to documentation is restricted to authorized research team members as described on the Task Delegation Log.

5.1 8 The QI/PI or delegate should create an ISF after the first contact by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, or as soon as a protocol outline is available. Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator should create a TMF.

<a id="5-1-9"></a>**5.1.9** Note that until the MUHC/RI-MUHC has a validated (21 CFR, Part 11 compliant) document management system, the main TMF/ISF should ideally be paper based in binders along with the individual source document participant files. A back-up electronic version of the TMF/ISF (with no participant source data) can also be kept on a share drive.

- All current study documents should be filed in paper form in binders.
- All documents with wet signatures (original penned signatures) must be kept in original paper state e.g. Agreements, Protocol &amp; <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip> signature pages, ICFs, paper CRFs and all documents initialed and dated by the QI/PI, team member or participant. Note: eSignature is only compliant with regulations if exercised within a 21CFR, Part 11 validated system.
- Superseded PDF documents (e.g. older versions of signed protocols, IBs, blank <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> versions, study manuals, training slides) may be stored electronically and a NTF should be kept with current paper version indicating location of the superseded documents.

- Superseded PDF versions of blank participant-specific data collection source forms can also be filed electronically by version number. Again, a NTF should be kept with the current paper version to indicate location of the superseded documents.

<a id="5-1-10"></a>**5.1.10** Create a logical, organized filing system that allows for rapid retrieval of study essential documents (binders with tabs and a Table of Contents, TOC). Industry Sponsors may provide the site with an Investigator Site File TOC template and/or pre-labeled binders/boxes. If no ISF structure is provided by the Sponsor, refer to [Appendix 4](#appendix-4). No blank tabs should be in the ISF binders. If a document is stored elsewhere (e.g. electronically in PDF form), the location of the document should be written in a Note-to-File (NTF) under the binder tab. (Refer to [SOP-CR-014](/sops/cr-014) for a NTF template).

<a id="5-1-11"></a>**5.1.11** Create a separate reference binder/file to store regulations and guidelines and other relevant clinical trial references (e.g. published papers, articles).

<a id="5-1-12"></a>**5.1.12** Electronic essential documents may be stored on a share drive, however, only as back-up. Primary source essential documents must be in paper form until the RI-MUHC has a validated 21CRF, Part 11 compliant Document Management System (Containing password, audit trail, back-up).

<a id="5-1-13"></a>**5.1.13** Protect the confidentiality of all participant records (e.g. Participant Identification code list, Informed Consent Forms). No participant information should be saved on the electronic share drives (unless for temporary data transfer).

<a id="5-1-14"></a>**5.1.14** Ensure that any corrections/updates to documents made are attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, available, enduring, consistent and complete (following ALCOA+). Any changes made should be traceable, unobscured and should be explained (as per [SOP-CR-014](/sops/cr-014)).

<a id="5-1-15"></a>**5.1.15** Throughout the study, the Sponsor-Investigator/QI/PI is responsible to document medical oversight by initialing and dating all essential documents which pertain to patient safety and data integrity in a timely fashion prior to filing in the ISF. (e.g. all safety reports (drug/NHP/device local and external <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip>/Safety reports), all applicable Sponsor newsletters, protocol procedure clarifications, all study document amendments.) All documents with QI/PI initials and date should be maintained in their paper state. If the QI/PI is confirming medical oversight of documents (e.g. external safety reports, IB amendments) via email, the email should list the documents read with their corresponding file names to document the versions read and the email should be printed and placed in the ISF.

<a id="5-1-16"></a>**5.1.16** If tasks are delegated to other specialist(s) the delegated specialist should have signed the MD Specialist agreement ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002)) e.g. Ophthalmologist, Respirologist, Radiologist) and the agreement should be filed in the ISF with the Task Delegation Log. Any document regarding patient assessment in that specialty should be initialed and dated by that specialist(s). These initialed and dated documents should also be kept in their paper states or printed email confirmation. The QI/PI should then initial and date to document QI/PI medical oversight and file these assessments in the study participant files.

<a id="5-1-17"></a>**5.1.17** When a copy of an original document is shared (e.g. source document containing participant data), the copy should ensure participant confidentiality (all nominative information should be removed/blacked out) and should fulfill the requirements for certified copies. The person who certifies the copy as an accurate and complete representation of the original (having all of the same attributes and information) should be the same person who actually made the copy from the original. This person should mark the copy “True and Exact Copy” and initial and date the copy.

### 5.2 Trial master file/Investigator site file maintenance (during trial)

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** TMF/ISF documents should preferably be kept for the entire retention period in their original medium (Refer to [Appendix 2](#appendix-2) for Retention Timelines). As per Health Canada, [GUI-0100](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100.html) Guidance Document Part C, Division 5 of the [Food and Drug Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/) “Drugs for Clinical Trials Involving Human Subjects” transfer of source documents from their original medium to a secondary medium can be acceptable, preferably at the completion of a study, and only if:

- The corrections to the original data can be clearly captured in the secondary medium;

- The person that performs the task of transferring from the original to the secondary medium attests (signs and dates an attestation), that the secondary documents are certified copies of their respective primary documents;

- The transfer process has been fully validated. Evidence of validation should be available for inspection.

- Maintain control of all essential documents and records generated by the QI/PI site and institution before, during, and after the trial.
- The secondary medium allows the successful retrieval and use of the records for the entire record retention period.

- Records from their original medium (e.g. hardcopies) should be kept for as long as they are needed.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** Maintain and update essential documents as necessary, adding appropriate documents as they are generated or received (first ensuring documented QI/PI oversight by QI/PI initials and date on appropriate documents). Retain all original and revised versions of essential documents as “superseded” or “obsolete” documents.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** Ensure that appropriate study documents (e.g. Amendments of the Protocol, IB, ICF or any new documents for patients) are <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approved/acknowledged prior to their implementation and that REB approvals/acknowledgments are kept in the ISF.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** If superseded/obsolete documents are kept in the same binder as current, the superseded/obsolete documents should be clearly marked as superseded/obsolete. Draw a line through the first page of the document; indicate superseded, initial and date. Obsolete electronic files should be stored in a folder marked as obsolete.

<a id="5-2-5"></a>**5.2.5** The Clinical Research Coordinator (<Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip>) should maintain a Communication section in the ISF for each study, and document relevant written or verbal communications (Telephone/Video Conference Log) chronologically. A separate communication log can be created for study participants. Communications by e-mail may be filed electronically during the course of the study. All pertinent e-mails related to the study should be printed and filed in the ISF.

<a id="5-3"></a>

### 5.3 Trial master file/Investigator site file archive/storage (after trial completion)

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** Store participant records in compliance with institutional policy, relevant privacy legislation, and regulatory requirements.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** As of 11-Feb-2022, Health Canada requires the ISF to be stored for 15 years for studies regulated by Health Canada where at least one participant has signed an ICF (screen failures included). Note that prior to this date Health Canada requirements were to store essential documents for 25 years. For all other studies, store documents for 7 years from publication date. Note that the E.M.A. requirement for archiving is 25 years, hence some sponsors may request archiving for 25 years. Ensure that adequate funding for long-term storage was negotiated in the study budget prior to study start.

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** Ensure that the TMF/ISF documents do not contain any participant nominative information. All documents with nominative information (e.g. email regarding an SAE) should be redacted prior to archiving. Ensure the essential documents are securely stored, protected from fire, water, theft, vandalism, tampering, and loss, but are easily accessible for review, audit and inspection.

<a id="5-3-4"></a>**5.3.4** Inventory the TMF/ISF contents, detailing which type of documents are stored in which box (in order to facilitate document retrieval in the event of an audit or inspection, if required) and note the intended storage location. The storage system used during the trial and for archiving (irrespective of the type of media used) should provide for document identification, version history, search, and retrieval. Maintain an inventory of all electronic back-up or superseded documents, type of media, and storage location. Monitor and document study file and electronic document storage regularly to ensure quality of the storage. Note: At the time of archiving, all study participant source documents, including all logs, should be filed with the ISF.

<a id="5-3-5"></a>**5.3.5** Update inventory documents, if stored items are moved. Notify Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-3-6"></a>**5.3.6** The RI-MUHC highly recommends storing your study documents at Iron Mountain. To do so, you will need to obtain a department account number from the RI-MUHC Purchasing Agent (the link to submit a request is available in the RI portal under Resources, then Finance) before beginning the archiving of your essential documents. Once you contact Iron Mountain, they will send you all the required supplies (standard boxes, plastic envelops, transmittal sheets, instructions) needed to archive a study.

- Please remember essential documents should be archived for 15 years (current regulation for Health Canada regulated studies), or 7 years after publication (for non-regulated studies); This includes all records of all participants including those who screen failed or who withdrew from the study.

- The transmittal sheet should be detailed enough to facilitate retrieval of specific documents. i.e. document the content of the boxes.

- Keep the original transmittal sheet in a binder appropriately labelled which contains all the information in each box.

In the event of an audit or an inspection, you will be required to retrieve the boxes. In order to do so, call Iron Mountain and request the box numbers needed for retrieval.

<a id="5-3-7"></a>**5.3.7** When archiving important communications by e-mail, in addition to filing and archiving electronically (e.g. in Outlook) during the course of the study, in the absence of a [21CFR Part 11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11) document management system, all emails that provide information about the trial should have been printed and subsequently archived in paper form.

<a id="5-3-8"></a>**5.3.8** The content of the essential documents can vary depending of the type of study. Not all documents are applicable to all studies. For this reason, it is important to indicate when a document is not applicable (N/Ap) on the ISF Filing and Archiving Structure.

<a id="5-3-9"></a>**5.3.9** The QI/PI or delegate should inform the archiving service of the required period for document retention.

<a id="5-4"></a>

### 5.4 Back-up/superseded electronic files

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** Study Folders for each QI/PI should be identified with the MUHC REB Study Code related to the applicable study.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** All documents should be classified in an existing folder; no orphan files should remain outside the file management structure.

<a id="5-4-3"></a>**5.4.3** Keep file names short, simple and relevant.

- Name the file with the most important elements first. The element upon which a file is retrieved should appear first.

- Avoid naming a document all in capital letters. Do not use commas or dots, use dash (-) or underscore (_).

- Use a dd-Mon-yyyy format for dates. It helps to maintain chronological order and avoids ambiguity.

- Include a leading 0 for numbers 0 to 9 (e.g. 09). It helps maintain the numeric order in the directory.

- Use descriptive terms (e.g. draft, final, memo, etc.) at the end of the filenames.

- Indicate the version of a document as “v01, v02…v10”.

- When revising a document, the person revising can add their initials and date of revision at the very end of the file name to distinguish sub-versions.

<a id="5-4-4"></a>**5.4.4** When a new version of a document is available, place the “superseded” or “obsolete” document (in lieu of “archived” as archiving is done at Iron Mountain) in the appropriate “superseded” folder so that only the current version is available. For best practice, have a superseded folder, current folder and pending folder per document.

<a id="5-4-5"></a>**5.4.5** When a study is completed, the entire electronic back-up folder should be moved to an “Archive” folder.

<a id="5-4-6"></a>**5.4.6** The format and system where documents are retained should also be validated for its intended use.

<a id="5-4-7"></a>**5.4.7** According to 21CFR, part 11, features of an electronic document management system for essential documents should include the following:

- An audit trail of all revisions, corrections and updates
- Back-ups at regular intervals
- Security measures in place and documented to protect against data corruption
- Controlled access to appropriate individuals through password protection

- Have a plan in place for future accessibility (new staff, monitors, auditors, inspectors)
- Permits immediate accessibility for regulatory inspections
- If these features are not available in your document management system, then do not use that system except for backup or PDFs of superseded documents. Use a paper system instead.

### 5.5 Trial master file/Investigator site file access for auditors and regulatory inspectors

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** All essential study documents are subject to, and must be available for, audit by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator’s auditor, the RI-MUHC Quality Assurance Department and inspection by the regulatory authorities (e.g. Health Canada, FDA).

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** Records must be made available to regulatory inspectors within an expedited timeframe (Inspectors may give no prior notice) if there is justification for a For-Cause-Inspection and 7 to 14 calendar days in all other cases. Hence, a record of archived study documents and their location must be readily available at all times.

<a id="5-5-3"></a>**5.5.3** Auditors and Inspectors should have access to the entire TMF/ISF (whether paper only or a paper/electronic hybrid). If using a hybrid, provide the auditor or inspector with printed paper versions. If using a validated e-ISF/TMF the auditor or inspector should be granted read-access only.

<a id="5-5-4"></a>**5.5.4** If a copy of a document is required by the auditor or inspector, the study team should make a certified copy of the original document/s and return original to the archive. Note: in the case of an inspection, the <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> department may assist with certified copy control of documents the inspector requests. (Refer to [SOP-CR-017](/sops/cr-017)).

<a id="5-5-5"></a>**5.5.5** Inspectors/auditors may require that part or all of the ‘original’ archived file be available for their review (rather than copies). Ensure that any files removed from storage are properly secured at all times and returned to the correct storage location.

<a id="5-5-6"></a>**5.5.6** Update the [Appendix 4](#appendix-4): ISF Filing and Archiving Structure by adding a column for Date Document Accessed and Date Document Returned.

### 5.6 Study file destruction or return (after 15 years or 7 years)

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Consult Agreement and Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator on file disposition after the required storage time has elapsed, or if site storage is no longer possible at the time of, or following study completion (e.g., facility closure). Do not destroy any files without consulting the QI/PI and Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator (refer to [Appendix 5](#appendix-5)). Retain the written permission from the QI/PI and Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator in the Archiving Binder. (Refer to [Appendix 6](#appendix-6) for Request form).

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** Have the QI/PI sign the Essential Document Destruction Request and send the Request form to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator for signature along with a copy of the TMF/ISF Filing and Archiving Structure ([Appendix 6](#appendix-6)/4).

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** Consult the TMF/ISF Filing and Archiving Structure Checklist prior to contacting IRON Mountain to obtain box numbers for destruction request or return to Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** If returning essential documents to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, return as directed by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-6-5"></a>**5.6.5** If requested to destroy essential documents, complete Iron Mountain destruction forms and place an order for destruction, as directed by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and Investigator.

<a id="5-6-6"></a>**5.6.6** Forward destruction documentation to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and ensure that documentation related to destruction is completed, signed and dated and also retained on site along with the completed ISF Filing and Archiving Structure.

<a id="5-6-7"></a>**5.6.7** If destroying essential documents on site, refer to the MUHC Destruction Policy for Paper Documents.

- All nominative, confidential and/or sensitive information should be retained as required by law or MUHC policy (IM 030POL) and be disposed of in a fashion that ensures the protection of the information contained therein.
- The Sponsor-Investigator/QI/PI has the responsibility to ensure the secure destruction of nominative, confidential and/or sensitive information and to follow the defined procedures.

- All nominative, confidential and/or sensitive information must be discarded into a properly identified locked bin. The content of these containers will be destroyed in accordance with the MUHC Document Management policy for the disposal of recyclable paper.

- If the bin is full, you accidentally dropped something in the bin or the bin disappeared; please contact the MUHC Document management department.

<a id="5-6-8"></a>**5.6.8 MUHC Destruction Policy for other Medium**

- CD’s, DVD’s, video cassettes will be discarded into a designated locked bin. These items will be shredded as per the contract agreement between Document Management Services and the external provider.

- For information contained on technological media that is no longer required, the information must be deleted and medium must be reformatted or physically destroyed.

- If the bin is full, you accidentally dropped something in the bin or the bin disappeared; please contact the MUHC Document management department.

<a id="5-6-9"></a>**5.6.9 Documentation of Destruction**

- The QI/PI must ensure that documentation related to the process of destruction of nominative, confidential and/or sensitive information is completed, signed/dated and retained.

### 5.7 Trial master file (TMF): only applicable for Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator

As per ICH E6 (R2) Good clinical practice (GCP) and ISO-14155-GCP, TMF/ISF should be established at the beginning of the trial, both at the investigator/institution’s site (ISF) and at the Sponsor's/Sponsor-Investigator’s office (TMF). A final close-out of a trial can only be done when the monitor has reviewed both investigator/institution and sponsor files and confirmed that all necessary documents are in the appropriate files.

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1 TMF index/Expected Document List:**

Sponsors/Sponsor-Investigators should collect and file essential documents from all sites from all countries involved in the study. It contains three levels: Trial Level, country level and site level as shown in diagram:

For paper documents, the TMF should have:

- 1 Binder/Box for Trial level documents
- 1 Binder/Box per Country for country level documents
- 1 Binder/Box per Site for site level documents [Appendix 7](#appendix-7) (TMF index/Expected Document List) has been created in order to help MUHC Sponsor-Investigators with the collection and classifications of essential documents in the TMF according to level and study milestone;
- Site initiation/activation
- Any updates/Amendments
- Monitoring visits
- Close-out It is the MUHC Sponsor-Investigator’s responsibility to ensure that IT has validated the Software used for e-TMF, ( i.e. is [21CFR Part 11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11) compliant). It is recommended that the e-TMF respect the same levels of classifications mentioned above.

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2 TMF Plan:**

A TMF Plan template ([Appendix 7](#appendix-7)) has been created for MUHC Sponsor-Investigator initiated studies.

The RACI model should be used in the TMF Plan to outline MUHC Sponsor-Investigator team’s roles and responsibilities for each TMF activity.

RACI model:

- R = Responsible = The person who performs the work. There must be one “R” on every row, no more and no less. “R” is the only letter that must appear in each row.

- A = Accountable = The person ultimately accountable for the work or decision being made. Use this letter where appropriate, but not to excess – only when a key decision or task is at hand. There can be from zero to one “A’s” in each row, but no more than one.

- C = Consulted = Anyone who must be consulted with prior to a decision being made and/or the task being completed. There can be as many “C’s” as are appropriate in each row.

- I = Informed = Anyone who must be informed when a decision is made or work is completed. There can be as many “I’s” as are appropriate in each row.

<a id="5-7-3"></a>**5.7.3 TMF Vendor:**

MUHC Sponsor-Investigator should specify the vendor for the Software used for e-TMF during the study as well as the vendor for TMF archiving at the end of the study.

<a id="5-7-4"></a>**5.7.4 TMF archiving:**

Depending on hard copies and/or e-copies, MUHC Sponsor-Investigator should have a plan for TMF archiving since the beginning of the study:

- Vendor documentation (e.g. validation of software)
- Timelines for short term and long term retention
- Identify the MUHC Sponsor-Investigator who will be responsible for TMF archiving and train him/her on this task

<a id="5-7-5"></a>**5.7.5 TMF Inspection readiness:**

MUHC Sponsor-Investigator should always be inspection ready and should plan a process to grant access to TMF/e-TMF for audit/inspector, if needed during the study or after archiving.
