## Key points

- **Complete the Study Feasibility Checklist (Appendix 1) before agreeing to participate in any study.** The checklist covers scientific/ethical aspects, participant population, personnel, resources, facilities, and financial considerations across 7 parts. A completed checklist must be filed in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">Investigator Site File</Tooltip>.
- A **confidentiality agreement** between the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI should be signed and dated before receiving any confidential study information such as the protocol. Legal review is often required before signing.
- Assess **IT infrastructure** needs early — contact IT via "IT rescue" on the RI-MUHC Portal for evaluation of any study-specific software and assessment of e-connectivity requirements (21 CFR Part 11 compliance for electronic systems).
- Identify **secure, temperature-controlled storage** for investigational products and ensure a qualified <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> manager and destruction procedures are in place before committing.
- For industry-sponsored studies, the Sponsor's own feasibility questionnaire may supplement but does **not replace** the RI-MUHC checklist.
- Once the SFV is conducted and the study is deemed feasible, **a signed and dated copy of the site agreement** must be kept with the essential study documentation.
- Do not commit to **enrolment targets** the site cannot realistically meet. Document any feasibility concerns in writing before signing the site agreement.

## 1 · Purpose {#purpose}

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the processes for determining human research feasibility, and describes the processes to be followed prior to, during, and following the site selection visit by a Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

---

## 2 · Scope {#scope}

This procedure concerns the research community (employees, sponsor-investigators, investigators, physicians, management, consultants, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in hosting a feasibility visit for a human research study.

---

## 3 · Responsibilities {#responsibilities}

The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">Qualified Investigator (QI)</Tooltip>/<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">Principal Investigator (PI)</Tooltip> are responsible for ensuring that the study feasibility meets all of the applicable regulatory, International Council on Harmonisation (ICH) <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">Good Clinical Practice (GCP)</Tooltip>/ISO GCP, Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, and local requirements.

## 5 · Procedure {#procedure}
### 5.1 Preparation for the Sponsor's Site Feasibility Visit (SFV)

**5.1.1** A confidentiality agreement between the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI should be signed and dated before receiving any confidential study information such as the study protocol. The review of the confidentiality agreement by a legal advisor is often required before signing.

**5.1.2** Assess the feasibility of the protocol to determine the organizational needs of the site. The main issues to consider may include, but are not limited to: the technical and ethical feasibility of the protocol, compatibility with local healthcare practice, access to the target population for screening and accrual, time and resource requirements, and the availability and commitment of the research team and support services.

**5.1.3** The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator must ensure that all electronic software used in the study has been validated and is compliant with 21 CFR Part 11. The study team should contact IT via "IT rescue" on the RI-MUHC Portal for evaluation of any study-specific software and assessment of e-connectivity issues.

**5.1.3** The site should conduct a preliminary feasibility assessment using the Study Feasibility Checklist (Appendix 1) in preparation for the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/Monitor's SFV.

**5.1.4** Secure, adequate, and temperature-controlled space for storage of investigational products (drugs, biological products, natural health products, medical devices) under appropriate conditions should be identified. A qualified person responsible for <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> management and procedures for destruction (if required) must be available.

**5.1.5** The facilities must be adequate for all protocol procedures, and must include secure space for local data management activities.

### 5.2 Budget and financial contract

**5.2.1** The site must be aware of the financial implications of any research. It is the responsibility of the Institution/QI/PI to specify RI-MUHC policies regarding all agreements and contracts.

- Contracts for new studies are submitted through **<Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>**.
- Once submitted in Nagano, the investigator emails the contracts group at contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca notifying them. → [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007)

**5.2.2** Regulatory agreements may include:
- Confirmation that the Institution/QI/PI will conduct the study per the approved protocol, REB approval, and regulatory authorities
- Confirmation that the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and QI/PI will comply with the Declaration of Helsinki/TCPS2 and ICH-<Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>/ISO-GCP as well as applicable Health Canada regulations
- Dated signatures of the QI/PI/Institution and the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator

**5.2.3** Financial agreements between the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, QI/PI, institution, and/or CRO may include:

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Fees | For Sponsor-Investigator, QI/PI, Sub-Is, or other team members |
| Per-participant payment | Payment per participant or per visit; payment for study completers |
| Payment schedule | Defined in the agreement |
| Participant expenses | Reimbursement of travel and other participant costs |
| Satellite sites | Reimbursement for remote trial conduct by Sub-Is |
| Supporting services | Reimbursement for hospital departments involved in study conduct |
| Signatures | Dated signatures on all financial documents prior to each study |

### 5.3 Site Feasibility Visit (SFV) and site selection

**5.3.1** The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator (or CRO) conducts a Site Feasibility Visit to evaluate the site — ensuring the QI/PI has access to the appropriate study population, facilities, equipment, and suitably qualified staff to enroll the specified number of participants within the defined timeframe, per the protocol, Health Canada regulations, and GCP standards.

**5.3.2** Sponsor-Investigators and Monitors should refer to [SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-013) for an SFV Report template. Once completed, a copy must be placed in the <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">Trial Master File</Tooltip> (TMF).

**5.3.3** The <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip> or designate:
- Compiles requested information upon receiving the Sponsor's/Sponsor-Investigator's request for an SFV
- Assists the QI/PI with completion of the Study Feasibility Checklist (Appendix 1) to ensure all aspects of feasibility have been assessed before agreeing to participate
- Facilitates the SFV meeting format and ensures follow-up requirements are met

**5.3.4** Items assessed by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/Monitor may include, but are not limited to:

- Qualifications and experience of the QI/PI and Sub-Is
- Capabilities of the research team and supporting departments to conduct the study
- Site team's knowledge of the therapeutic area, GCP, Health Canada regulations, TCPS2, and FDA regulations (if applicable)
- Availability and qualifications of research team members
- Requirement for third-party/vendor involvement (e.g. external nursing agency)
- Investigator workload and competing studies
- Potential eligible participant population and projected screening-to-enrollment ratio
- Site team communication and responsiveness
- Clinical research management procedures (SOPs, WIs)
- Documents confirming training
- Ability to meet required study timelines
- Storage of confidential records

**5.3.5** Ensure that documentation supporting the above is available to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/Monitor at the SFV.

**5.3.6** Once the SFV has been conducted and the study is deemed feasible, a signed and dated copy of the agreement must be kept with the study-related essential documentation.

**5.3.7** As per [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007), the contract/agreements and required study documents (protocol, <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip>, <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip>, participant-facing documents) must then pass through an RI-MUHC/MUHC feasibility and ethical review.

## Feasibility checklist summary {#feasibility-checklist-summary}

The Study Feasibility Checklist (Appendix 1) covers 7 parts:

| Part | Area assessed |
|---|---|
| 1 | Scientific, technical, safety, and ethical aspects of the protocol |
| 2 | Participant population and eligibility criteria |
| 3 | Personnel availability — QI/PI, Sub-Is, CRCs, delegates |
| 4 | Resources — delegation needs, budget, third parties |
| 5 | Facilities and equipment — space, equipment, lab, pharmacy, IT |
| 6 | Financial resources — training, services, overhead, archiving |
| 7 | Other considerations |

Each item is assessed as Yes / No / N/A with a comments column.
