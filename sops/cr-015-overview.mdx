# Investigator Study Files and Essential Documents

<Note>
This SOP describes the set-up, maintenance, archiving, and destruction of Investigator Site Files (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>) and Trial Master Files (<Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip>) for clinical research studies at the RI-MUHC. It covers essential document management from study initiation through long-term archiving at Iron Mountain.

## Key points

- The **<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI** maintains the Investigator Site File (ISF); the **Sponsor-Investigator** maintains the Trial Master File (TMF). For single-site Sponsor-Investigator studies, the ISF and TMF are one and the same. For multi-site studies, the site ISF mirrors the Sponsor's TMF except for a few documents.
- The ISF must be created as soon as the first Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator contact occurs or a protocol outline is available. Create a Training Log and <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> first; then build out the ISF per Appendix 4 structure.
- All documents with **wet signatures** (original penned signatures) must be kept in paper: Agreements, Protocol and <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip> signature pages, ICFs, paper CRFs, and all documents initialled and dated by the QI/PI, team member, or participant.
- Until the RI-MUHC has a validated 21 CFR Part 11-compliant document management system, the primary ISF/TMF must be paper-based in binders. Electronic backups (without participant source data) may be kept on a share drive.
- The QI/PI must initial and date all essential documents pertaining to patient safety and data integrity in a timely fashion, including safety reports (local and external <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip>/<Tooltip tip="Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction — unexpected in nature, severity, or frequency; must be reported to Health Canada and the REB." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip>), Sponsor newsletters, protocol amendments, and procedure clarifications.
- **Retention periods**: 15 years for Health Canada-regulated studies (all participants, including screen failures); 7 years from publication date for non-regulated studies. The EMA requires 25 years — confirm Sponsor requirements in the study budget before study start. The RI-MUHC recommends Iron Mountain for long-term archiving.
- All essential documents must be audit/inspection-ready at all times. For a For-Cause Inspection, records must be available immediately. For routine inspections, 7–14 calendar days notice is standard.
- Do not destroy any study files without written authorization from the QI/PI and Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator. Retain destruction documentation on site.
