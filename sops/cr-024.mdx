<a id="purpose"></a>

### 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the process for submitting an Investigational Testing Authorization (<Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip>) to Health Canada, requesting an approval to carry out an investigational trial on a non-IVDD (in vitro diagnostic device) medical device. This procedure also includes information on manufacturer/sponsor obligations, including mandatory problem reporting.

<a id="scope"></a>

### 2.0 Scope

This procedure applies to all medical device (non-IVDD) clinical studies undertaken at the site, and to those clinical research personnel responsible for preparing, reviewing, approving, and submitting ITAs and subsequent amendments/notifications for investigator-initiated trials.

This SOP does not apply to externally-sponsored device trials, or trials involving in vitro diagnostic devices (IVDD).

<a id="responsibilities"></a>

### 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor-Investigator is considered to be the sponsor for investigator-initiated clinical trials, and therefore is responsible for submitting the Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA) to Health Canada.

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

### 4.0 Definitions

<a id="procedure"></a>

### 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

#### 5.1 Classification of medical devices

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** Medical devices are classified as Class I, II, III, or IV, based on the risk to the patient. Class I devices are the lowest risk, and Class IV bear the highest risk.

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** Refer to the Health Canada guidance document: [Keyword Index](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/guidance-industry-keyword-assist-manufacturers-class-medical-devices.html) … (or similar), or contact the Device Licensing Services Division, Medical Devices Bureau for assistance.

<a id="5-2"></a>

<a id="5-2-when-an-ita-is-required"></a>

#### 5.2 Class I medical devices – ITA requirements

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** Class I medical devices do not require ITAs to conduct investigational testing in Canada, provided that the seller possesses all the records and information detailed in Section 81 of the [Medical Device Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/) (MDR).

<a id="5-3"></a>

#### 5.3 Class II, III, and IV medical devices not licensed in Canada – ITA requirements

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** Device manufactured by/for one physician + used only for his/her patients = no ITA required. See note below regarding scope of practice.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** Device manufactured by/for one physician + used for another physician’s patients = ITA required. See note below regarding scope of practice.

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** Device manufactured by anyone (or any company) other than the physician using the device for his/her patients = ITA required.

<a id="5-3-4"></a>**5.3.4** Some devices manufactured and used by physicians may be considered as being covered by ‘scope of practice’. It is strongly recommended to contact the Head of Investigational Testing, Medical Devices Bureau, for advice before proceeding with testing.

<a id="5-4"></a>

#### 5.4 Class II, III, and IV medical devices licensed in Canada – ITA requirements

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** Device used under licence indication + study in Canada = no ITA required

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** Device used under licence indication + study outside of Canada to support/expand licence in foreign jurisdiction = no ITA required. Refer to regulatory/application requirements of foreign jurisdiction.

<a id="5-4-3"></a>**5.4.3** Device not used under licence indication + manufactured by/for one physician + used only for his/her patients in institution/site = no ITA required. See note below regarding scope of practice.

<a id="5-4-4"></a>**5.4.4** Device not used under licence indication + manufactured by/for one physician + used for another physician’s patients = ITA required. See note below regarding scope of practice.

<a id="5-4-5"></a>**5.4.5** Device not used under licence indication + manufactured by another physician or company + used to support/expand Canadian licence = ITA required.

<a id="5-4-6"></a>**5.4.6** Some devices manufactured and used by physicians may be considered as being covered by ‘scope of practice’. It is strongly recommended to contact the Head of Investigational Testing, Medical Devices Bureau for advice before proceeding with testing.

<a id="5-5"></a>

#### 5.5 Preparation of ITA for Class II device

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** Prepare a cover letter for the application (This will speed up the screening process, when the application is received by the Medical Devices Bureau). Include the following in the cover letter:

- Description and classification of the device,
- Protocol title,
- Number of devices required for testing (always add extra, e.g., 10%, to allow for packaging defects or breakage, and
- List of chapters included in the application (see descriptions below).

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** Refer to the guidance document, [Preparation of an Application for Investigational Testing - Medical Devices](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/investigational-testing-authorizations-guidance/guidance-document.html), (or equivalent) on the Health Canada website, for complete details of the application process, description of required information, and application form(s).

<a id="5-5-3"></a>**5.5.3** Include the following items presented in four distinct chapters titled: Introduction, Institutional Information, Protocol, and Labelling:

- Introduction: manufacturer identification, device identification
- Institutional Information: name of the institutions
- Protocol: objective, duration and follow-up period and subject selection, assessment methods, control group, <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> documentation.
- Labelling: device label, product monograph, advertising brochures, qualified investigator information, patient information.

<a id="5-5-4"></a>**5.5.4** Important notes re: labelling (see Section 86 of the [Medical Devices Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/)):

- The labelling requirements of Part 1 of the MDR do not apply to devices authorized for sale under Part 3 of the Regulations. However, there are specific labelling requirements for devices sold for investigational testing.
- Section 86 of the MDR sets out the requirements of a label on a device sold for investigational testing. In addition to the name of the device and the name of the manufacturer, two additional statements must be present.
- Subsections 86(c) and 86(d) require that the statements "Investigational Device" and "To be Used by Qualified Investigators Only" and "Instrument de recherche" and "Réservé uniquement à l'usage de chercheurs compétents" be present in English and French. It is possible to use alternate phrasing, provided the above meanings are conveyed.

<a id="5-6"></a>

#### 5.6 Preparation of ITA for Class III and IV devices

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Prepare a cover letter for the application (This will speed up the screening process, when the application is received by the Medical Devices Bureau). Include the following in the cover letter:

- Description and classification of the device,
- Protocol title,
- Number of devices required for testing (always add extra, e.g., 10%, to allow for packaging defects or breakage, and
- List of chapters included in the application (see descriptions below).

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** Refer to the guidance document, [Preparation of an Application for Investigational Testing - Medical Devices](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/investigational-testing-authorizations-guidance/guidance-document.html), (or equivalent) on the Health Canada website, for complete details of the application process, description of required information, and application form(s).

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** Include the following items presented in six distinct chapters titled: Introduction, Risk Analysis, Institutional Information, Protocol, Labelling, and Investigator Agreements:

- Introduction: manufacturer identification, device identification, device description, design philosophy, marketing history
- Risk Analysis: risk assessment and risk reduction measures, previous studies, alternate treatments, precautions
- Institutional Information: name of the investigators, names of the institutions, Research Ethics Board/Ethics Committee approval
- Protocol: objective, duration and follow-up period and subject selection, assessment methods, control group, ICF documentation.
- Labelling: device label, product monograph, advertising brochures, qualified investigator information, patient information.
- Investigator Agreements: responsibilities including: conduct the testing in accordance with the protocol; fully inform each enrolled patient; not to permit the device to be used outside the agreed protocol; supervise the use of the device; and report all incidents under Section 59 of the regulations to the Minister within 72 hours.

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** Important notes re: labelling (see Section 86 of the [Medical Devices Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/)):

- The labelling requirements of Part 1 of the MDR do not apply to devices authorized for sale under Part 3 of the Regulations. However, there are specific labelling requirements for devices sold for investigational testing.
- Section 86 of the MDR sets out the requirements of a label on a device sold for investigational testing. In addition to the name of the device and the name of the manufacturer, two additional statements must be present.
- Subsections 86(c) and 86(d) require that the statements "Investigational Device" and "To be Used by Qualified Investigators Only" and "Instrument de recherche" and "Réservé uniquement à l'usage de chercheurs compétents" be present in English and French. It is possible to use alternate phrasing, provided the above meanings are conveyed.

<a id="5-7"></a>

#### 5.7 Review and authorization of ITA

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** Submit the completed application for review. Ensure that the number of units of the device to be used is included in the application. Submit additional information, if/as requested by the Medical Devices Bureau/Therapeutic Products Directorate (TPD).

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** Do not begin the clinical study before written authorization is received from the Medical Device Bureau.

<a id="5-7-3"></a>**5.7.3** Note for Class III and IV applications: A conditional investigational testing authorization may be granted, pending the receipt of a Research Ethics Board approval. However, the investigational testing cannot begin until this approval has been obtained and submitted to the Medical Device Bureau.

<a id="5-8"></a>

#### 5.8 Changes made during ITA

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** Prepare a cover letter, stating that this is an amendment. Quote the ITA number that was assigned to the original application. Briefly describe the changes being requested.

<a id="5-8-2"></a>**5.8.2** Submit an application, using the same form, if any of the following information changes during the clinical study: investigational protocol, identity of the qualified investigators and the institution where the testing is being conducted, type of diagnosis and/or treatment for which the devices are being tested, and/or number of devices being tested.

<a id="5-8-3"></a>**5.8.3** Obtain the new authorization before implementing the change(s).

<a id="5-9"></a>

#### 5.9 Rejection and refusal of ITA

<a id="5-9-1"></a>**5.9.1** An investigational testing application may be rejected, if the manufacturer and/or device sponsor fails to provide the records described in Section 81 of the [Medical Device Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/). Ensure that the initial application is complete.

<a id="5-9-2"></a>**5.9.2** The authorization may be refused if it is determined that:

- the device cannot be used safely for investigational testing;
- the investigational testing is not in the best interests of patients; or
- the objective of the testing cannot be achieved.

<a id="5-9-3"></a>**5.9.3** In the case of a refusal to issue an investigational testing authorization, the manufacturer and/or device sponsor may appeal the decision.

<a id="5-10"></a>

#### 5.10 Cancellation of authorization

<a id="5-10-1"></a>**5.10.1** Section 85 of the [Medical Devices Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/) allows for the cancellation of an investigational testing authorization for Class II, III or IV devices and the stop sale of a Class I device for investigational purposes. The conditions for stopping an investigation test are outlined in subsection 85(1) paragraphs (a) to (e).

<a id="5-10-2"></a>**5.10.2** Prior to cancelling an authorization, Medical Device Bureau/TPD may request information from the manufacturer and/or device sponsor to substantiate that the conditions set out in subsection 83(1) are still applicable. If this information is not received, the authorization will be cancelled.

<a id="5-11"></a>

#### 5.11 Manufacturer/Sponsor responsibilities

<a id="5-11-1"></a>**5.11.1** Record Keeping

- Maintain the records described in Section 81 of the [Medical Devices Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/). For Class II, III and IV devices a portion of these records are submitted to TPD, in order to obtain the authorization detailed in Sections 82 and 83.
- Maintain distribution records described in Section 52 to 56.

<a id="5-11-2"></a>**5.11.2 Mandatory Problem Reporting**

- Refer to Sections 59 to 62 of the Medical Devices Regulations with respect to mandatory problem reporting apply to devices undergoing investigational testing. Ensure that the investigator notifies TPD and the manufacturer within 72 hours of any incident that meets the criteria, as defined in Subsection 59(1) of the Medical Devices Regulations. This may include, but not be limited to: (a) failure or a deterioration in the effectiveness of the device or any inadequacy in its labelling or in the directions for use accompanying it, or (b) an incident that has led to the death or a serious deterioration in the health of a patient, user or other person or, where it is reasonable to believe that such an incident were it to recur, could lead to the death or a serious deterioration of the state of health of a patient, user or other person.
- Consult the "Guidance Document for Mandatory Problem Reporting for Medical Devices" (or similar) for additional information.

<a id="5-11-3"></a>**5.11.3 Other Obligations**

- Refer to Sections 57 and 58 and 63 to 65 of the Medical Devices Regulations, which also apply to devices authorized for investigational testing. The manufacturer and/or device sponsor is required to have documented procedures in place to handle product complaints and recalls. In addition, the appropriate records of these activities must be maintained.
- Consult the "Guidance to Recall of Medical Devices" (or similar) for additional information.
- Refer to Sections 66 to 68 of the Medical Device Regulations for manufacturer’s responsibilities with regard to implant registration (as appropriate) to devices authorized for investigational testing.
- Refer to Section 87 of the Medical Device Regulations, which describes the limitations placed on the advertisement of devices undergoing investigational testing in Canada. Only devices which have been authorized under subsection 83(1) can be advertised. The advertisement must clearly state the device is the subject of investigational testing and the proposed purpose of the testing.

### 1.0 Specific RI-MUHC policies and procedures

<a id="1-1"></a>

<a id="5-1-ri-muhc-compliance-requirements"></a>

#### 1.1 Additional comments on compliance pertaining to ITA

<a id="1-1-1"></a>**1.1.1** See [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500) for mandatory privilege to conduct research.

<a id="1-1-2"></a>**1.1.2** Investigational testing must be conducted in compliance with the [Declaration of Helsinki](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/) (foundation of the ICH-GCP: Good Clinical Practice), the International Organization for Standardization, ISO 14155:2011 (E), Clinical investigation of medical devices for human subject – Good Clinical Practice, 2011-02-01 and ISO 14971:2007, Medical devices – Application of risk management to medical devices (indispensable for the application of this document). ISO 14155 and 14971 are a Health Canada recognized standard, and assuring conformity with this standard will help to meet Health Canada regulations/requirements governing quality.

<a id="1-2"></a>

#### 1.2 Cancellation of an Investigational Testing Authorization from Health Canada

<a id="1-2-1"></a>**1.2.1** Section 85 of the [Medical Devices Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/) allows for the cancellation of an investigational testing authorization for Class II, III or IV devices and the stop of sale of a Class I device for investigational purposes. The conditions for stopping an investigational test are outlined in subsection 83(1) paragraphs (a) to (e).

<a id="1-2-2"></a>**1.2.2** Prior to cancelling an authorization, Medical Device Bureau/TPP may request information from the manufacturer and/or device sponsor to substantiate that the conditions set out in subsection 83(1) are still applicable. If this information is not received, the authorization will be cancelled.

<a id="1-3"></a>

#### 1.3 Tri-Council Policy Statement (TCPS2) requirements

<a id="1-3-1"></a>**1.3.1 Responsible Conduct of Research Tri-Agencies Framework (CIHR, NSERC, SSHRC)**

- “To be able to receive and administer research funds from the Agencies, institutions must agree to comply with a number of policies set as schedules to a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the Agencies and Institutions. Institutions must therefore ensure that research conducted under their auspices adhere to this Policy.” (Adapted from [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html), 5).

<a id="1-3-2"></a>**1.3.2 Responsible Conduct of Research by Investigator**

- Qualified Investigators (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>) Investigators and Sub-Investigators responsible for the design and the conduct of Agency funded projects OR projects funded by a private company under the auspices of an institution eligible to administer Agency funds must be in compliance with all applicable Agency requirements and legislation for the conduct of research, including, but not limited to:
  - Current edition of Tri-Council Policy Statement: Ethical Conduct of Research Involving Humans (TCPS2)
  - Canadian Council on Animal Care Policies and Guidelines;
  - Agency policies related to the Canadian Environmental Assessment Act;
  - Licenses for research in the field;
  - Laboratory Biosafety Guidelines;
  - Controlled Goods Program;
  - Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission (CNSC) Regulations;
  - Canada’s [Medical Device Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/), SOR/98-282 (Health Canada)

<a id="1-3-3"></a>**1.3.3 Clinical Trials – Medical Device**

- Chapter 11 of the TCPS2 outlined the requirements for clinical trials, including medical device clinical trials. The guidance in this chapter as well as all relevant guidance in this document must be applied to your clinical trial. Chapter 11 is divided into the following sections:
  - Introduction
  - A. Key Concepts (Risk and Proportionate Approach, Clinical Equipoise, Duty of Care, Therapeutic Misconception and Dual Roles)
  - B. Clinical Trial Design and Registration
  - C. Assessing Safety and Minimizing Risk
  - D. Financial Conflicts of Interest
  - E. Analysis and Dissemination of Clinical Trial Outcomes
- These guidelines are meant for the researchers and the REBs members.
