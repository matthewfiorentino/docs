## 1 · Purpose {#purpose}
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the process for submitting an
lnvestigational Testing Authorization (ITA) to Health Canada, requesting an approval to
carry out an investigational trial on a non-lVDD (in vitro diagnostic device) medical
device. This procedure also includes information on manufacturer/sponsor obligations,
including mandatory problem reporting.

---

## 2 · Scope {#scope}
This procedure applies to all medical device (non-lVDD) clinical studies undertaken at
the site, and to those clinical research personnel responsible for preparing, reviewing,
approving, and submitting ITAs and subsequent amendments/notifications for
investigator-initiated trials.

This SOP does not apply to externally-sponsored device trials, or trials involving in vitro
diagnostic devices (IVDD).

## 3 · Responsibilities {#responsibilities}
The **Sponsor-Investigator** is responsible for submitting the ITA to Health Canada (Therapeutic Products Directorate [TPD] — Medical Devices Bureau).

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor-Investigator.

## 5 · Procedure {#procedure}
### 5.1 RI-MUHC compliance requirements

**5.1.1** Refer to [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500) for mandatory investigator privileges at the RI-MUHC. These must be in place before initiating an ITA submission.

**5.1.2** Investigational testing must be conducted in compliance with:
- Declaration of Helsinki (foundation of ICH-<Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>)
- ISO 14155:2011(E), Clinical investigation of medical devices for human subjects — Good Clinical Practice
- ISO 14971:2007, Medical devices — Application of risk management to medical devices

Both ISO 14155 and ISO 14971 are Health Canada recognized standards; conformity with these standards helps meet Health Canada regulations/requirements governing quality.

### 5.2 When an ITA is required

| Device class | Risk level | ITA required? |
|---|---|---|
| Class I | Minimal risk | **No** — ITA not required |
| Class II | Low to moderate risk | **Yes** |
| Class III | Moderate to high risk | **Yes** |
| Class IV | High risk, often life-sustaining | **Yes** |
| IVDD | In vitro diagnostic device | Use IVDD-specific process |

**Key risk factors for non-IVDD devices:**
- Degree of invasiveness (implantable vs. non-implantable)
- Active (powered) vs. non-active
- The body structure or anatomy modified or affected by the device

**Key risk factors for IVDDs:**
- Importance of the test to diagnosis (sole determinant vs. one of many)
- Patient-related factors (stress/anxiety)
- Public health issues

### 5.3 ITA preparation

**5.3.1** Prepare and submit the ITA to Health Canada's Medical Devices Bureau (Therapeutic Products Directorate). The ITA submission includes:
- Completed ITA application form
- Study Protocol
- <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#icf">Informed Consent Form</Tooltip>
- <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip> or equivalent device technical documentation
- Risk analysis (ISO 14971 conformance documentation)
- <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> Attestation/approval letter
- <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">Qualified Investigator</Tooltip>'s Undertaking (QIU) form

**5.3.2** If the medical device contains a drug component, submit to the Therapeutic Products Directorate and provide: brand name, active ingredient, drug manufacturer, and applicable Drug Identification Number (DIN).

**5.3.3** The label must conform with article 81(i) of the Medical Devices Regulations.

**5.3.4** Maintain all submission documents and correspondence in the Investigator Study File (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>) → [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015).

### 5.4 ITA review

**5.4.1** Following Health Canada's review of the application, an authorization to conduct the investigational testing will be issued.

**5.4.2** No trial activities may begin until the authorization has been received.

**5.4.3** Respond to any Health Canada queries within the specified timeframe.

### 5.5 Changes and notifications during an ITA

**5.5.1** Notify Health Canada of the following types of changes during an authorized ITA:
- Protocol changes
- Investigator/institution changes
- Indication/treatment changes
- Mandatory Problem Reporting events (see section 5.6)

### 5.6 Mandatory Problem Reporting

**5.6.1** Report incidents involving the investigational medical device as required under the Medical Devices Regulations. Mandatory problem reporting applies when a device incident (malfunction, deterioration, or inadequacy in labelling or directions) has led to or could lead to serious injury or death of a patient, user, or other person.

**5.6.2** Report all medical device incidents to the Sponsor, as required by the protocol → [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012) for <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip> and medical device incident (MDI) reporting timelines and procedures.

**5.6.3** The QI/PI must ensure that all device incidents are documented in source documents and recorded on the Medical Device Accountability Log (Appendix 1).

### 5.7 Device accountability

**5.7.1** Maintain the **Medical Device Accountability Log** (Appendix 1) for all devices received, used, returned, or disposed of. Record for each device:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Date received | Date the device arrived at site (dd/mmm/yyyy) |
| Lot # / Serial # | Device lot and serial number |
| Expiry date | Device expiry date |
| Patient ID | De-identified participant identifier |
| Device disposition | Code: I=Used, OS=Discard, R=Return, E=Expired, O=Opened/Not Used, SC=Study Completed, SR=Sponsor Recall |
| Returns/Goods Authorization (RGA) | Authorization number for device return |
| Monitor verification | Monitor initials confirming device accountability |
| Comments | Any notes on condition, damage, temperature excursions, etc. |

**5.7.2** Have the Investigator and Monitor sign the Device Accountability Log at close-out → [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016).

### 5.8 Cancellation of ITA authorization

**5.8.1** Section 85 of the Medical Devices Regulations allows for the cancellation of an ITA authorization for Class II, III, or IV devices (and stopping the sale of a Class I device for investigational purposes). The conditions for stopping an investigational test are outlined in subsection 83(1) paragraphs (a) to (e).

**5.8.2** Prior to cancelling an authorization, the Medical Device Bureau/TPD may request information from the manufacturer and/or device sponsor to substantiate that the conditions in subsection 83(1) are still applicable. If this information is not received, the authorization will be cancelled.

### 5.9 Record retention

**5.9.1** Records related to the clinical trial must be maintained by the Sponsor-Investigator for **25 years** and made available to Health Canada within:
- **2 calendar days** if there is a safety/risk concern for patients
- **7 calendar days** in any other case
