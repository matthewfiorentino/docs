## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}
This SOP describes the process for submitting a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application</Tooltip> (CTA) to Health Canada's Natural and Non-prescription Health Products Directorate (NNHPD), requesting approval to carry out an investigational trial on a Natural Health Product (NHP). This SOP does not include the CTA process for drugs.

This SOP is applicable to Sponsor-Investigators at the RI-MUHC conducting investigator-initiated NHP trials. It does not apply to externally-sponsored trials.

---

## 3 · Responsibilities {#responsibilities}
The **Sponsor-Investigator** is considered the sponsor for investigator-initiated clinical trials and is responsible for submitting the CTA to NNHPD (or TPD/BGTD where applicable).

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor-Investigator.

## 5 · Procedure {#procedure}
### 5.1 Background

**5.1.1** A CTA must be filed for clinical trials in Phases I through III of development (including comparative bioavailability trials). Approval must be received prior to study activation. This includes studies in which a marketed natural health product is used outside of the conditions specified in the Product License/Monograph/Labelling Standard or Natural Product Number (NPN) application — i.e., if the indication/s, clinical use, target population/s, dosage regimen/s, route/s of administration, or dosage form/s differ from those described in the Product Monograph.

**5.1.2** NNHPD will no longer review CTAs for NHPs where the use is not appropriate for self-care. Contact NNHPD before preparing the CTA submission to confirm:
- Which directorate the CTA should be submitted to (NNHPD vs. TPD or BGTD)
- Which templates are required for Chemistry, Manufacturing and Control (CMC) information
- The number of copies and format (paper and electronic) required

### 5.2 Preparation of Clinical Trial Application

**5.2.1** Request and attend a pre-CTA consultation meeting with NNHPD, if desired. Meetings are useful for new active substances or applications with complex issues.

**5.2.2** Main documents required for a CTA submission include:
- Natural Health Product Submission Application Form (CTA and Attestation Form)
- Study Protocol
- Protocol Synopsis (use appropriate NHPD template, where applicable)
- <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#icf">Informed Consent Form</Tooltip>
- <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board</Tooltip> (REB) Attestation, signed by the REB (or equivalent form), and a signed <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">Qualified Investigator</Tooltip>'s Undertaking (QIU) form (required for submission; must be retained in the essential study document file)
- <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip> (IB) **OR** Natural Health Product Monograph/Labelling Standard **AND** CMC information

**CMC information notes:**
- If the manufacturer has previously submitted information to NNHPD about the product, a letter authorizing cross-reference to that information may be submitted in lieu of the IB.
- If using a marketed product, the NHP Monograph or Labelling Standard and NPN will suffice **unless** the dosage form or strength differs from the Monograph/Labelling Standard — in the latter case, CMC information is required.

Most investigator-initiated CTAs requiring CMC information will use one of:
- A letter of cross-reference allowing NNHPD to access CMC information through a previously approved CTA on file
- A letter from the NHP manufacturer granting access to the Natural Health Product Master File at NNHPD
- Cross-referenced CMC information, if using a Canadian marketed product (cite NPN number)
- Full CMC information provided by the manufacturer for inclusion in the CTA

**5.2.3** Submit required information to NNHPD in both paper and electronic format. Ensure that no trial activities begin before the Notice of Authorization (NOA) is received.

**5.2.4** Detailed information and forms/templates for a CTA are available on NNHPD's website at www.hc-sc.gc.ca.

### 5.3 NNHPD CTA screening and review

**5.3.1** NNHPD reviews the application and, if any deficiencies are detected, will inform the Sponsor-Investigator. Submit further information as/if requested.

**5.3.2** Submission Screening: Minor issues with documentation or deficiencies with the package will be addressed by either:
- **Screening Rejection Letter** — itemizing deficiencies/significant information lacking from the CTA or CTA-A. All information requires re-submission and will be processed as "new" and assigned a new control number; or
- **Request for Clarification** — sent via fax, email, or phone. Response timeframe will be specified in the request.

**5.3.3** Receive the acknowledgement letter from NNHPD, indicating that screening is complete and the review has commenced (usually by fax, within 5–7 days of submission). Contact NNHPD if the acknowledgement letter is not received, to ensure the submission was received safely. There is no default/target review period from receipt of a completed application package. Typical review periods are estimated at 90 or more days.

**5.3.4** Receive queries (if any) for specific issues with the protocol, consent, or submission from the NNHPD reviewer via fax, email, or phone (Information Request).

**5.3.5** Respond to NNHPD within the specified timeframe on any request. Typically there is a 30-day response deadline.

**5.3.6** The reviewer may ask the Sponsor-Investigator to withdraw the CTA to avoid receiving a Not Satisfactory Notice (NSN) if deficiencies remain that NNHPD does not consider to have been satisfactorily addressed.

**5.3.7** An NSN will be issued if a timely response is not received, or if significant deficiencies are identified during the review and the applicant does not withdraw the CTA. A new review will be applied to any resubmission; it will be reviewed without prejudice if the CTA was withdrawn.

**5.3.8** Withdraw the CTA if amendment-worthy modifications are desired by the Sponsor-Investigator while the review is underway. Re-submit for approval as described above.

**5.3.9** Maintain all submission documents and correspondence in the essential study documents file → [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015).

### 5.4 Trial commencement

**5.4.1** Wait for the **Notice of Authorization (NOA)**, indicating a satisfactory review and approval, before commencing any trial activities. Submit a copy of the NOA to the REB if required.

**5.4.2** Complete and submit a completed Clinical Trial Site Information (CTSI) form, signed Qualified Investigator Undertaking form, and the REB Attestation form/approval letter (for each clinical trial site in Canada) to NNHPD. Ensure that:
- The NNHPD control number is present on the cover letter
- The name of the REB member experienced in natural and alternative methods is on the cover letter
- Trial start date information is included on the CTSI form
- NNHPD-specific forms are used

Regulation requires this notification package to be provided to NNHPD a **minimum of 15 days** before the clinical trial site commences the study.

### 5.5 Clinical Trial Application Amendment (CTA-A)

**5.5.1** Submit CTA Amendments (CTA-A) as/if required. Reasons for amendments include, but are not limited to, changes to:
- Clinical trial NHP supplies (e.g., manufacturing process, dose strength)
- Dosing regimen, inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Selection, selection criteria, follow-up, or withdrawal of a subject
- Evaluation of clinical efficacy
- Alteration of risk to the health of a subject
- NHP safety assessment (changed or additional)
- Extended clinical trial duration
- CMC information affecting safety or quality

**5.5.2** CTA amendments are subject to a review period and require approval from NNHPD before implementing the changes.

**5.5.3** Exception to review period: Implement protocol changes **immediately** only if there are serious safety concerns to study subjects (or others). Submit changes to NNHPD immediately as a Notification, with full explanation of the reasons for immediate change. Submit the full CTA-A within **15 days** of implementing the amended protocol.

**5.5.4** Submit a CTSI form, signed QIU form, and REB Attestation/approval letter for each Canadian site, using NNHPD forms. Regulation requires that this notification package is provided a minimum of 15 days before the clinical trial site commences the amendment.

### 5.6 Notifications to CTA or CTA-A

**5.6.1** Inform NNHPD when implementing any of the following types of protocol changes:
- Changes to the protocol that do not compromise participant safety and are not classed as amendments (see CTA-A section above)
- Information about a site closure or completion of a clinical trial
- Premature discontinuation of a trial, at one or all study sites, for any reason (safety, efficacy, administrative, recruiting issues, sponsor choice, etc.)

**5.6.2** Notification information for continuing trials includes changes in data quality (chemistry and manufacturing) that do not affect NHP quality or safety, such as:
- Increase in production without any change in process (pharmaceutical products)
- Narrowing of actual test specifications
- Changes related to research laboratories under contract
- Changes in packaging material
- Extension of shelf life (pharmaceutical products)
- All chemistry and manufacturing changes not affecting quality or safety

**5.6.3** Notification information for discontinued trials includes:
- A description of the effect of discontinuation on projected or ongoing trials of the NHP in Canada
- A statement confirming that each qualified investigator has been notified of the discontinuation and its reasons, and that they have been sent a written notice regarding potential health risks to participating subjects or others
- Confirmation that sale or importation of the NHP at each involved trial site has been discontinued
- Confirmation that reasonable measures will be taken to ensure return of all unused NHP

**5.6.4** Other types of Notification include:
- All information regarding refusals to approve the study by other regulatory authorities or REBs, at any time before or during the clinical study
- Discontinuation or partial/full clinical hold of a trial outside Canada, when equivalent trials are being conducted in Canada

**5.6.5** Section 80 of the NHP Regulations allows for the suspension and cancellation of the authorization to sell or import a NHP for investigational purposes. NNHPD may suspend the authorization if there are reasonable grounds to believe suspension is necessary to prevent injury to the health of a clinical trial subject or other person. A notice will be sent to the Sponsor detailing the reason for suspension, the effective date, and whether it applies in its entirety or at a particular site.

The authorization will be restored if, within **30 days** after the effective date of suspension, NNHPD is provided with information demonstrating that the situation causing the suspension either did not exist or has been corrected. If the information has not been provided within those 30 days, the authorization will be cancelled.

### 5.7 Investigator Brochure updates

**5.7.1** Submit updated Investigator Brochures (IB) as a Notification, unless included as part of a planned CTA-A. Include complete safety data and a general overview of the trial.

**5.7.2** Submit updated IB annually, or more often if needed. Highlight/summarize changes for ease of review and evaluation.

### 5.8 Submission of adverse reactions

**5.8.1** See [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012) for <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="A noxious, unintended response to a drug where a causal relationship is at least reasonably possible." headline="Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#adr">adverse drug reaction</Tooltip> reporting requirements.

### 5.9 Ongoing Sponsor-Investigator responsibilities

**5.9.1** Refer to sections 4 and 5 of the ICH-<Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> Consolidated Guideline (ICH Topic E6) for Sponsor-Investigator obligations.
