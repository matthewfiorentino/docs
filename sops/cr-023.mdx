<a id="purpose"></a>

### 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the process for submitting a Clinical Trial Application (CTA) to Health Canada, requesting approval to carry out an investigational trial on a Natural Health Product (NHP). This SOP does not include the CTA process for drugs.

<a id="scope"></a>

### 2.0 Scope

This SOP is applicable to all clinical studies undertaken at the site, and to those clinical research personnel responsible for preparing, reviewing, approving, and submitting CTAs and subsequent amendments/notifications for investigator-initiated trials.

This SOP does not apply to externally-sponsored trials.

<a id="responsibilities"></a>

### 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor-Investigator is considered to be the sponsor for investigator-initiated clinical trials, and therefore is responsible for submitting the Clinical Trial Application (CTA) to Natural and Non-prescription Health Products Directorate (NNHPD).

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

### 4.0 Definitions

<a id="procedure"></a>

### 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

#### 5.1 Background

A CTA must be filed for clinical trials in Phases I through III of development (including comparative bioavailability trials). Approval must be received prior to study activation. This includes studies in which a marketed natural health product is used outside of conditions specified in the Product License/Monograph/Labelling Standard or Natural Product Number (NPN) application, i.e., if the indication/s and clinical use, target population/s, dosage regimen/s, route/s of administration, or dosage form/s differ from those described in the Product Monograph.

NOTE: NNHPD will no longer review Clinical Trial Applications (CTAs) for natural health products (NHPs), where the use of the product is not appropriate for self-care. Contact the NNHPD prior to preparation of the CTA submission, to confirm which templates are required for Chemistry, Manufacturing and Control (CMC) Information and the number of copies and format (paper and electronic) are required, and to confirm which directorate the CTA is submitted.

<a id="5-2"></a>

#### 5.2 Preparation of Clinical Trial Application

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** Request and attend a pre-CTA consultation meeting with NNHPD, if desired. Note: Meetings are useful for new active substances, or applications with complex issues.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** Main documents required for preparation of a CTA submission include:

- Natural Health Product Submission Application Form (CTA and Attestation Form);
- Study Protocol;
- Protocol Synopsis (use appropriate NHPD template, where applicable);
- Informed Consent Form;
- Research Ethics Board (<Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>)Attestation, signed by the REB (or equivalent form) and a signed Qualified Investigator’s Undertaking (QIU) form; are required for submission, must be retained in the essential study document file; and
- Investigator’s Brochure (<Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip>) OR Natural Health Product Monograph/Labelling Standard AND Chemistry Manufacturing Control (CMC) information;\*\*
- CMC information (use the Quality Overall Summary (QOS) – Natural Health Product (phase I, II and III) template, where applicable).\*\*\*

\*\* If the manufacturer of the product in a dosage form or strength not specified in the Natural Health Product Monograph, has previously submitted information to NNHPD about the product in question, a letter authorizing cross-reference to their information may be submitted in lieu of the IB.

\*\*\* Chemistry Manufacturing Control (CMC) information: If using marketed product, the Natural Health Product Monograph or Labelling Standard and Natural Product Number (NPN) will suffice UNLESS the dosage form or strength is different from that specified in the Monograph/Labelling Standard. In the latter case, CMC information will be required.

Most investigator-initiated CTAs which require CMC information will use one of the following sources:

- A letter of cross-reference which allows NNHPD to access the CMC information for the product through a previously approved CTA on file at NNHPD.
- Letter from the natural health product manufacturer granting access to the Natural Health Product Master File at NNHPD for the product.
- Cross-referenced CMC information, if using a Canadian marketed product. Cite NPN number of product.
- Full CMC information provided by the manufacturer for inclusion in the CTA.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** Submit required information to NNHPD, in both paper and in electronic format. Ensure that no trial activities begin before the Notice of Authorization (NOA) is received.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** Note: Detailed information and forms/templates to be used for a CTA are available on NNHPD’s web site at the following address: www.hc-sc.gc.ca.

<a id="5-3"></a>

#### 5.3 NNHPD CTA screening and review

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** NNHPD reviews the application, and if any deficiencies are detected, should inform the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator. Submit further information, as/if requested.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** Submission Screening: Minor issues with documentation or deficiencies with the package will be addressed by either:

- Screening Rejection Letter, itemizing deficiencies/significant information lacking from the CTA or Clinical Trial Application Amendment (CTA-A). All information will require re-submission and will be processed as ‘new’ information, and hence will be assigned a new control number; or
- Request for Clarification, sent via fax, email, or phone. Response timeframe will be specified in the request.

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** Receive the acknowledgement letter from NNHPD, to indicate that screening is complete, and the review has commenced (usually by fax, within 5 - 7 days of submission). Contact NNHPD, if acknowledgment letter is NOT received, to ensure that submission has been received safely. There is no default/target review period from receipt of a completed application package. Typical review periods are estimated at 90 or more days.

<a id="5-3-4"></a>**5.3.4** Receive queries (if any) for specific issues with the protocol, consent, or the submission from NNHPD reviewer via fax, email, or phone (Information Request).

<a id="5-3-5"></a>**5.3.5** Respond to NNHPD (to any form of communication) within specified timeframe on the request. Typically there is a 30 day response deadline.

<a id="5-3-6"></a>**5.3.6** The reviewer may ask the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator to withdraw the CTA, to avoid receiving a Not Satisfactory Notice (NSN), if any deficiencies remain that NNHPD does not consider to have been satisfactorily addressed.

<a id="5-3-7"></a>**5.3.7** An NSN will be issued if a timely response is not received, or significant deficiencies are identified during the review, and the applicant does not withdraw the CTA. Note: A new review will be applied to this new CTA. The resubmission will be reviewed without prejudice, if the CTA is withdrawn.

<a id="5-3-8"></a>**5.3.8** Note: Withdraw the CTA, if amendment-worthy modifications are desired by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, while the review is underway. Re-submit for approval, as described above.

<a id="5-3-9"></a>**5.3.9** Maintain all submission documents and correspondence in the essential study documents file.

<a id="5-4"></a>

#### 5.4 Trial commencement

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** Wait for Notice of Authorization (NOA), indicating a satisfactory review and approval, before commencing trial activities. Submit copy of NOA to REB, if required.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** Complete and submit a completed Clinical Trial Site Information (CTSI) form, signed Qualified Investigator Undertaking form, the REB Attestation form/approval letter (for each clinical trial site in Canada) via fax to NNHPD. Ensure that the NNHPD control number, the name of the REB member who is experienced in natural and alternative methods is present on the cover letter, and trial start date information are included on the CTSI form. Ensure that you are using the NNHPD forms. Regulation requires this notification package is provided to NNHPD a minimum of 15 days from the submission of this information before the clinical trial site commences the study.

<a id="5-5"></a>

#### 5.5 Clinical Trial Application amendment (CTA-A)

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** Submit Clinical Trial Application Amendments (CTA-A), as/if required. Reasons for amendments include, but are not limited to, changes to the following:

- Clinical trial natural health product supplies (e.g., manufacturing process, dose strength);
- Dosing regimen, inclusion/exclusion criteria;
- Selection, selection criteria, follow-up, or withdrawal of a clinical trial subject;
- Evaluation of the clinical efficacy of the natural health product;
- Alteration of risk to the health of a clinical trial subject;
- Natural health product safety assessment (changed or additional);
- Extended clinical trial duration; and
- CMC information affecting safety or quality of the natural health product.

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** CTA amendments are subject to a review period, and require approval from NNHPD before implementing the changes.

<a id="5-5-3"></a>**5.5.3** Exception to 30-day review period: Implement protocol changes immediately only if there are serious safety concerns to study subjects (or others). Submit the changes to NNHPD immediately, as a Notification. Ensure that the reasons for immediate change are fully explained. Submit the full CTA-A, within 15 days of implementing the amended protocol.

<a id="5-5-4"></a>**5.5.4** Complete and submit a completed Clinical Trial Site Information (CTSI) form, signed Qualified Investigator Undertaking form, the Research Ethics Boards (REB) Attestation form/approval letter (for each clinical trial site in Canada) via fax to NNHPD. Ensure that the NNHPD control number for the CTA-A, the name of the Research Ethics Boards (REB) member who is experienced in natural and alternative methods is present on the cover letter and trial start date information are included on the CTSI form. Ensure that you are using the NNHPD forms. Regulation requires that this notification package is provided to NNHPD a minimum of 15-day notification from the submission of this information, before the clinical trial site commences the amendment.

<a id="5-6"></a>

#### 5.6 Notifications to CTA or CTA-A

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Inform NNHPD when implementing any of the following types of protocol changes:

- Changes to the protocol that do not compromise the safety of clinical trial participants, and are not classed as amendments (see CTA-A section);
- Information about a site closure or completion of a clinical trial;
- Premature discontinuation of a trial, at one or all of the study sites, for any reason, as specified in the protocol (e.g., safety, efficacy, administrative, recruiting issues, sponsor choice, etc.).

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** Notification information for continuing trials includes changes in data quality (chemistry and manufacturing) that do not affect natural health product quality or safety, such as:

- Pharmaceutical products: increase in production without any change in process;
- Narrowing of actual test specifications;
- Changes related to research laboratories under contract;
- Changes in packaging material;
- Pharmaceutical products: extension of shelf life; and
- Pharmaceutical products: all changes to the chemistry and manufacturing of the natural health product which do not affect its quality or safety (according to the criteria described elsewhere in this SOP).

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** Notification information for discontinued trials includes:

- A description of the effect of discontinuation on projected or ongoing trials of the natural health product in Canada;
- A statement confirming that each qualified investigator has been duly notified of the trial discontinuation and the reasons thereof, and that they have been sent a written notice regarding the potential health risks to participating subjects or others;
- Confirmation that the sale or importation of the natural health product at each involved trial site has been discontinued; and
- Confirmation that reasonable measures will be taken to ensure the return of all unused natural health product.

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** Other types of Notification include:

- All information regarding refusals to approve the study by other regulatory authority, or Research Ethics Boards (REBs), at any time before or during the clinical study.
- Discontinuation or partial/full Clinical Hold of a trial outside of Canada, when equivalent trials are being conducted in Canada.

<a id="5-6-5"></a>**5.6.5 Suspension and Cancellation**

- Section 80 of the NHPD Regulations allows for the Suspension and Cancellation of the authorization to sell or import a natural health product for investigational purposes. The conditions for suspending the authorization are outlined in subsection 80(1) paragraphs (a) to (d).

- NHPD may suspend the authorization to sell or import a natural health product for investigational purposes in its entirety or at a clinical trial site, before giving the sponsor an opportunity to be heard if, as a result of any circumstance, the NHPD has reasonable grounds to believe that it is necessary to do so to prevent injury to the health of a clinical trial subject or other person. A notice will be sent to the Sponsor detailing the reason for the suspension, the date the suspension begins, and if the authorization to suspend is in its entirety or at a particular clinical trial site.

The authorization for the clinical trial will be restored if, within 30 days after the day on which the suspension is effective, the NHPD is provided with information demonstrating that the situation causing the suspension either did not exist or has been corrected. If the information has not been provided to NPHD within those 30 days, the authorization for the clinical trial will be cancelled in its entirety or at a particular site.

<a id="5-7"></a>

#### 5.7 Investigator brochure updates

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** Submit updated Investigator Brochure (IB) as a Notification, unless included as part of a planned CTA-A. Include complete safety data and a general overview of the trial.

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** Submit updated IB annually, or more often if needed. Highlight/summarize changes for ease of review and evaluation.

<a id="5-8"></a>

#### 5.8 Submission of adverse reactions (ARs)

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** See specific SOP on Adverse Event/Drug Reaction Reporting.

<a id="5-9"></a>

#### 5.9 Ongoing Sponsor-Investigator responsibilities

<a id="5-9-1"></a>**5.9.1** Refer to sections 4 and 5 of the GCP Consolidated Guideline (ICH Topic E6).

### 1.0 Specific RI-MUHC policies and procedures

<a id="1-1"></a>

#### 1.1 Additional comments on compliance pertaining to CTA (NHP)

<a id="1-1-1"></a>**1.1.1** See [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500) for mandatory privilege to conduct research.

<a id="1-1-2"></a>**1.1.2** Natural Health Product Clinical Trials must be conducted in compliance with: Natural Health Product Regulations SOR/2003-196, Part 4 Clinical Trials Involving Human Subjects; ICH-E6, GCP: Good Clinical Practice; Provincial and local regulations.

<a id="1-2"></a>

#### 1.2 Cancellation of authorization from Health Canada

<a id="1-2-1"></a>**1.2.1** Section 80 and 81 of the Natural Health Product Regulations allows for the suspension or cancellation of a CTA authorization to sell or import a natural health product for the purposes of a clinical trial, in its entirety or at a clinical trial site.

<a id="1-2-2"></a>**1.2.2** If the authorization is suspended or cancelled, the Sponsor-Investigator should obtain a notice that sets out the reason(s), the day that it is effective and indicating whether it is in its entirety or at a clinical trial site.

<a id="1-3"></a>

#### 1.3 Tri-Council Policy Statement (TCPS2) requirements

<a id="1-3-1"></a>**1.3.1 Responsible Conduct of Research Tri-Agencies Framework (CIHR, NSERC, SSHRC)**

- “To be able to receive and administer research funds from the Agencies, institutions must agree to comply with a number of policies set as schedules to a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the Agencies and Institutions. Institutions must therefore ensure that research conducted under their auspices adhere to this Policy.” (Adapted from [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html), 5).

<a id="1-3-2"></a>**1.3.2 Responsible Conduct of Research by Investigator**

- Investigators, Sub-Investigators responsible for the design and the conduct of Agency funded projects OR projects funded by a private company under the auspices of an institution eligible to administer Agency funds must be in compliance with all applicable Agency requirements and legislation for the conduct of research, including, but not limited to:
  - Current edition of Tri-Council Policy Statement: Ethical Conduct of Research Involving Humans (TCPS2)
  - Canadian Council on Animal Care Policies and Guidelines;
  - Agency policies related to the Canadian Environmental Assessment Act;
  - Licenses for research in the field;
  - Laboratory Biosafety Guidelines;

  - Controlled Goods Program;
  - Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission (CNSC) Regulations;
  - Health Canada’s Natural Health Product Regulations, SOR/2003-196

<a id="1-3-3"></a>**1.3.3 Clinical Trials – Natural Health Product**

- Chapter 11 of the TCPS2, outlined the ethical issues for clinical trials, including natural health product clinical trials. The guidance in this chapter as well as all relevant guidance in this document must be applied to the clinical trial conducted by the Sponsor-Investigator. Chapter 11 is divided into the following sections:
  - Introduction
  - A. Key Concepts (Risk and Proportionate Approach, Clinical Equipoise, Duty of Care, Therapeutic Misconception and Dual Roles)
  - B. Clinical Trial Design and Registration
  - C. Assessing Safety and Minimizing Risk
  - D. Financial Conflicts of Interest
  - E. Analysis and Dissemination of Clinical Trial Outcomes
- These guidelines are meant for the researchers and the REB members.
