## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes mandatory and recommended training that should be obtained, documented and retained prior to any involvement in human research that is conducted at the RI-MUHC/MUHC.

Making sure any Sponsor-Investigator (Sp-I), Qualified or Principal Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>) and all other research team members are adequately trained, is of utmost importance for the conduct of human research in order to assure GCP and regulatory compliance, data integrity, and patient rights, safety and well-being.

The level of training required is aligned with the type of study being conducted. Depending on the level of training, compliance is assured via training on the applicable international guidelines, federal and provincial regulations and RI-MUHC procedures as well as task-specific and protocol-specific training.

<a id="scope"></a>

## 2.0 Scope

All Sponsor-Investigators, QI/PIs and all RI-MUHC/MUHC personnel, students (including but not limited to those performing an academic stage), research fellows and residents conducting MUHC research on human participants (including biological material, human embryos, fetuses, fetal tissue, reproductive materials and stem cells from living and deceased individuals) and/or staff involved in the study review submission process, are required to obtain documented training prior to study task delegation and conducting any task related to the study.

Note: Non-affiliated RI-MUHC/MUHC members (including volunteers) are not allowed to contact, recruit and/or perform procedures on a research participant at the RI-MUHC/MUHC as they are not covered by professional or academic liability insurance.

## 3.0 Responsibilities

Each research team member is responsible to assure they have the appropriate documented training that corresponds to their research (or to upgrade training to the required level) before study submission (or prior to their delegation of protocol tasks) and to maintain this compliance during the whole duration of the study. This applies to the whole research team: - QIs/PIs, Sponsor-Investigators, and Sub-Investigators (Sub-Is), as defined in the [Glossary of Terms](/kb/foundations/glossary) - RI-MUHC/MUHC members/employees working in their research teams.

Sponsor-Investigator or QI/PI: It is the responsibility of the RI-MUHC/MUHC Sponsor-Investigator or QI/PI to obtain the appropriate level of institutional training prior to submitting a study to the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> in the [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview) system. Note: The REB will not approve a study until the investigator has obtained this training.

It is also the responsibility of the Investigators who conduct research on humans, to make sure that every member of their team has appropriate documented training, prior to delegating protocol-specific tasks to them (i.e. prior to them signing the study <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>).

RI-MUHC Chief Operating and Development Officer and Chief Scientific Officer (COO/CSO):

The RI-MUHC Executive CODO/CSO is responsible for ensuring that the RI-MUHC ensures the requirements of the International Conference on Harmonization (ICH) Topic E6 (R2), the International Standards Organization ISO 14155 (Medical Devices - Good Clinical Practice), the RI-MUHC SOPs and federal and provincial regulatory authorities, are available to the RI-MUHC/MUHC research community, and that appropriate measures are taken to secure and maintain compliance with these regulations.

RI-MUHC Quality Assurance Department (<Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's Quality Assurance department, which checks the credentials and training of each investigator who submits a study, audits research and maintains the SOPs. In GCP, quality assurance is the planned, systematic actions that ensure a trial complies with GCP and the applicable regulatory requirements." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip>)

Is responsible for:

- Implementation of a process by which Institutional training on the conduct of human research is documented and maintained, via the [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) platform and CITI platforms.

- Manage the continuous improvement of existing training based on SOP revisions and the creation of new training to address human research training needs.

- For any questions regarding SOPs, contact QA (qaclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca).

RI-MUHC Training Advisor Is responsible for:

- Creating new and revised training based on SOPs provided by QA
- Management of the TalentLMS platform and import of CITI training certification-related data into TalentLMS
- Making sure that all versions of SOP training will be kept on TalentLMS for a 15 year period. For any questions regarding the TalentLMS platform or training reports from TalentLMS, contact the RI-MUHC Training Advisor at training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca

## 5.0 Procedures

<a id="5-1"></a>

### 5.1 Institutional training via the Talent Learning Management System

Institutional Training is now separate from Privileges (and the obsolete RI-MUHC Certification) and now requires all research team members to obtain and maintain individual certificates for each training completed.

TalentLMS is an RI-MUHC training tool that is made available via the Application section of the RI-MUHC portal. It manages the RI-MUHC’s human research course library and keeps track of course assignments, as well as their progress, evaluations, expirations, and communication of their renewal.

User training capsules have been designed specifically for the RI-MUHC users and are available within the TalentLMS course catalog.

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** Accessing mandatory training, course completion, reports and renewal The mandatory human research courses are available to all users of TalentLMS, who can access them as “Learners”.

The course ‘Introduction to TalentLMS for Learners’ is available on TalentLMS for learners who use TalentLMS for the first time. This course can be accessed either via the Welcome Page of TalentLMS under ‘Introduction to TalentLMS’ or in the Course Catalog.

Learners can access the RI-MUHC courses using the Course Catalog, by manually searching for and enrolling in the course of their choice on TalentLMS. The course will appear in the learner’s dashboard as the courses to be completed.

Please note that these courses can also be pre-assigned by a manager, an instructor or an administrator.

At the end of an RI-MUHC course, a series of questions about the material is asked. The training is marked as passed in the TalentLMS portal with a 100% completion percentage if 80% of the questions asked are answered correctly. Learners have 3 attempts to pass a set of quiz questions. If a learner fails the quiz 3 times, the learner cannot take the quiz again, and no certificate will be issued after completing the course. In this case, a learner should contact the Training Advisor who will notify the QA team and a meeting will be setup with the 3 parties to review failed course material. The purpose of this meeting is to review the quiz questions and answers and clarify the course content if needed. After this meeting, the learner may be granted another attempt to take the quiz. Any decisions will be documented.

A pdf certificate, which is valid for 2 years from the date of completion, is automatically generated once all units have been completed and quizzes successfully passed. This certificate is then available to download at any time from the Learner's training records.

To download a certificate, learners can access ‘My Dashboard’ from the Welcome Page of TalentLMS and then ‘Progress’ on the right-hand side. Then learners can click ‘Certifications’ to find all their valid certificates. Click on the download symbol to download.

Proof of training compliance will be obtained via printing an individual employee report from Talent LMS anytime it is needed, or by printing the certificate.

Newly revised and approved versions of SOPs are also communicated via TalentLMS and Learners can easily identify any training gap via their own dashboard on TalentLMS.

Various reports can also be obtained (e.g. by training, by group). Team Managers/Supervisors can request reports from the RI-MUHC Training Advisor for their team and define when they need them. For example, a report can be designed in order to identify overdue training in a team, and to be sent monthly to the requesting manager.

Mandatory human research courses are valid for two years after they are obtained. TalentLMS will send a reminder 30 days before the expiration date. If the Learner does not complete the required training prior to the expiration date, a notification that the course has formally expired will be sent on the day of the expiration date and that the Learner will need to renew their certificate(s).

<a id="study-level-specific-training-tiers"></a><a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2 Study Level-Specific Mandatory Human Research institutional training via TalentLMS**

- 7 Levels of Human Research SOP courses The RI-MUHC recognizes that different types of human research require different levels of knowledge and training. To address this need, the RI-MUHC’s training program offers 7 different levels of Human Research SOP training that are adapted to target research contexts based on study risk levels. The Level of SOP training required depends on the highest risk-level of the research that is conducted by the research team member (based on all studies they are currently - or plan to be - involved in). Note: Only one level of SOP training is required (not all 7).

Since the level of training is based on study level, all team members working on the same study should obtain the same institutional training level prior to signing the study Task Delegation Log.

Training Level I has the most complete GCP &amp; regulatory content on all RI-MUHC SOPs and allows for the conduct of the highest risk studies. Obtaining a Level I training certificate (required for drug studies) will provide the certificate holder with eligibility to conduct studies identified as Level II to Level VII.

[Appendix 1](#appendix-1) provides a matrix to determine the level of training that is required for a given study level. Note that GCP training is required for Levels I to IV and Health Canada Division 5 training is also required for Level I only. In addition to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1), an interactive placement test has also been developed within TalentLMS to help trainees identify the appropriate level of training required for a study level. If a trainee is involved in several types of studies at different risk levels, the auto-assessment of the training needed, will be based on the higher-risk studies they are involved in.

Learners have two options to renew the SOP course biennially:

1- In lieu of redoing the SOP training modules, learners are offered a renewal quiz to verify their knowledge of the RI-MUHC SOPs (if they are confident that they do not need to redo the course). If they pass the renewal quiz, learners will receive a certificate and do not need to complete the SOP training again or

2- learners can redo the SOP training and quizzes. Once they pass the training, a certificate will be automatically generated.

When a learner needs to upgrade their level of SOP course (lower numerical number of level e.g. Level VII to Level I if planning to conduct drug studies), the learner may not need to redo an entire SOP course. They can contact The RI-MUHC Training Advisor to be given access to the necessary modules in order to fill the training gap.

- Human Research [SOP Reader](/training/compliance-requirements#sop-reader-and-assessment) Course

Human Research SOP reading is available on TalentLMS. As with the RI-MUHC Human Research SOP courses, there are 7 levels of Human Research SOP readings. This is displayed in TalentLMS as a “course” and will facilitate SOP reading for Learners as they navigate through the required reading in the SOP Training modules.

If after completion of your reading, an SOP is updated, a notification will be sent to Learners, indicating that each individual should read the updated SOP in the Reader course and complete a short quiz.

Maintaining the Human Research SOP Reader course at 100% of completion at all times is required during the whole duration of the study in order to assure compliance. Each individual is responsible for maintaining their readings up-to-date.

- Health Canada Part C Division 5 Training and ICH-GCP training These courses are available via CITI. The procedure to access CITI training is presented in [Appendix 3](#appendix-3).

- ISO 14155 – GCP for Medical Devices Research with Medical Devices must follow ISO 14155 (or ISO-GCP). It is highly recommended for teams to obtain this training or self-train on the ISO-GCP document itself. This is a copy controlled document which is obtained via the ISO.org website at a cost per copy. Currently the RI-MUHC does not have access to an ISO-GCP course. Note: CITI is working with the Medical Devices Therapeutic Product Directorate to make a Canadian Medical Device course available soon. When available, this will be communicated to the research community.

Since ISO-GCP requirements in majority overlap ICH-GCP, if a Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator cannot provide sites with ISO-GCP training, in lieu of ISO-GCP, teams should be trained on ICH-GCP. Note: Health Canada inspectors expect teams who conduct medical device studies to be trained on ISO-GCP.

<a id="5-3"></a>

### 5.3 Study protocol and other study-specific training

The signature of the QI/PI on the protocol or any other document supplied by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, confirms his/her commitment to conduct the study in compliance with the REB-approved protocol, ICH-GCP/ISO-GCP, and applicable regulatory requirements.

The Investigator and the research team must undertake a complete review of the protocol and the Investigator Brochure (if applicable), prior to a Sponsor/CRO’s Site Feasibility Visit and Site Initiation Visit (<Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip>).

Protocol and study documentation training must be provided to the research teams by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, or delegate, who is thoroughly familiar with all aspects of the study (if applicable). This may occur at the SIV meeting and must occur prior to staff delegating tasks on the study Task Delegation Log. Subsequent initial training of new staff may be documented on a Training Log (including self-training).

Staff must also be trained on amendments of any protocol or study document (e.g. <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip>) in a timely fashion (i.e. directly after REB approval).During the course of the study, effective communication must be maintained by all parties involved in the study:

- The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator must provide the research team with all information or data relevant to the safety of participants and to the smooth conduct of the study and when appropriate, research team members must be trained on this new information.

- Documentation of communications, including the names of trainees (e.g. sign in sheet), meeting date, presentation slides, summary of discussions and resolutions is recommended.

<a id="documenting-training"></a>

### 5.4 Documentation of training

All training (general, task-specific, study-specific and ongoing) must be kept in the Investigator’s Site File (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>) with essential study documents or retained in the individual’s training records (as part of a Master Training File). Research personnel should be prepared to demonstrate all training obtained. As per [Health Canada Guide-0100](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100.html), documentation of training must include the title and content of the training, such as the learning objectives, who attended, and when the training occurred. This may include slide decks from presentations, course manuals, training certificates, meeting minutes and attendance logs, or updated staff CVs with supporting documentation.

An assessment of any additional protocol-specific skill requirements must be conducted prior to activation of each new protocol. All training related to qualification of research team members involved in the conduct of human studies must be documented and kept with the essential study documents. For training which does not provide a certificate or dated printout (e.g. ISO-14155 GCP document, protocol or IB self-training/reading), this training must be documented on a Training Log (Refer to [Appendix 2](#appendix-2)).

<a id="5-5"></a>

### 5.5 Leave of absence

In the event of a leave-of-absence exceeding 6 months, human research team members will be required to redo their SOP training at the appropriate training level based on studies conducted upon their return. If required to train at the same level upon their return, learners may opt to take the refresher quiz in lieu of redoing the SOP training to obtain the certificate (refer to Section [5.1.2](#5-1-2).). Other training may be required based on supervisor’s requirements and if the study level needs to be upgraded.
