## Key points

- **Training must precede delegation.** No team member may be listed on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> or perform any study task before their training is documented. Being on the log without a completed training record is a GCP violation.
- There are **7 levels of SOP training** on TalentLMS — one for each study risk tier. Only one level is required per person, but it must match the highest-risk study they are involved in. All team members on the same study train at the same level.
- The **SOP Reader** (TalentLMS) and **Competency Assessment** are the primary institutional training components. External certifications (CITI GCP, Health Canada Division 5) are required *in addition to*, not instead of, RI-MUHC institutional training.
- **Level I** (drug/biologic/gene therapy/radiopharmaceutical studies) requires CITI GCP + CITI Health Canada Division 5. Level II (medical device/surgery) requires CITI GCP. ISO 14155 training is required for anyone involved in medical device studies.
- Training certificates are valid for **2 years**. TalentLMS sends a reminder 30 days before expiry. At renewal, learners may take a refresher quiz in lieu of repeating the full course.
- If a team member is on leave for **more than 6 months**, SOP training must be renewed before they can return to study tasks.
- The **Training Log** (Appendix 2) must be maintained in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">Investigator Site File</Tooltip>. It must be updated for every new training completion and whenever a new team member joins.
- **Protocol-specific training** must also be documented — including sign-in sheets, slide decks, and meeting summaries from Site Initiation Visits or sponsor briefings.

## 1 · Purpose {#purpose}

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes mandatory and recommended training that should be obtained, documented and retained prior to any involvement in human research that is conducted at the RI-MUHC/MUHC. Making sure any Sponsor-Investigator (Sp-I), Qualified or Principal Investigator (QI/PI) and all other research team members are adequately trained, is of utmost importance for the conduct of human research in order to assure GCP and regulatory compliance, data integrity, and patient rights, safety and well-being.

The level of training required is aligned with the type of study being conducted. Depending on the level of training, compliance is assured via training on the applicable international guidelines, federal and provincial regulations and RI-MUHC procedures as well as task-specific and protocol-specific training.

---

## 2 · Scope {#scope}

All Sponsor-Investigators, QI/PIs and all RI-MUHC/MUHC personnel, students (including but not limited to those performing an academic stage), research fellows and residents conducting MUHC research on human participants (including biological material, human embryos, fetuses, fetal tissue, reproductive materials and stem cells from living and deceased individuals) and/or staff involved in the study review submission process, are required to obtain documented training prior to study task delegation and conducting any task related to the study.

Note: Non-affiliated RI-MUHC/MUHC members (including volunteers) are not allowed to contact, recruit and/or perform procedures on a research participant at the RI-MUHC/MUHC as they are not covered by professional or academic liability insurance.

## Institutional training — TalentLMS {#institutional-training-talentlms}

TalentLMS is the RI-MUHC training platform available through the Application section of the RI-MUHC portal. It manages the RI-MUHC human research course library and tracks course assignments, progress, evaluations, expirations, and renewal communications.

### Accessing and completing courses

Mandatory human research courses are available to all TalentLMS users as "Learners." Learners can access courses through the Course Catalog by manually searching for and enrolling in the course of their choice; courses may also be pre-assigned by a manager, instructor, or administrator.

- Courses are marked as passed at 100% completion when at least 80% of quiz questions are answered correctly.
- Learners have **3 attempts** to pass a set of quiz questions. After 3 failures, the learner cannot take the quiz again without contacting the Training Advisor, who will notify QA. A meeting will be arranged to review the material and quiz questions; after the meeting, another attempt may be granted and the decision documented.
- A PDF certificate, valid for **2 years** from the date of completion, is automatically generated once all units are completed and quizzes passed. Certificates can be downloaded from "My Dashboard" → "Progress" → "Certifications."
- Proof of training compliance can be obtained by printing an individual TalentLMS report or the certificate.
- Mandatory human research courses expire 2 years after completion. TalentLMS sends a reminder 30 days before expiry, and a formal expiry notification on the expiry date.

### Study level-specific training tiers

The RI-MUHC recognizes that different types of human research require different levels of training. Seven levels are available, adapted to study risk levels. Only **one level** is required per person — the level matching the highest-risk study they are currently or will be involved in.

All team members working on the same study must obtain the same institutional training level before signing the study <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>.

**Level I** has the most complete GCP and regulatory content and allows conduct of the highest-risk studies. A Level I certificate provides eligibility to conduct studies at Levels II through VII.

### Course renewal

Learners have two options for biennial SOP course renewal:

1. **Renewal quiz** — if confident in their knowledge, learners may complete the renewal quiz without repeating the full course. Passing the quiz generates a new certificate and resets the expiry clock.
2. **Redo the full course** — learners complete the SOP training and quizzes again. A certificate is automatically generated upon passing.

When a learner needs to **upgrade** their training level (e.g. from Level VII to Level I for a new drug study), they may not need to redo the entire course. Contact the RI-MUHC Training Advisor for access to the necessary modules to fill the training gap.

## External certification requirements {#external-certification-requirements}
### CITI courses

Health Canada Part C Division 5 training and ICH-GCP training are available via CITI (Collaborative Institutional Training Initiative). See Appendix 3 for course selection instructions.

### ISO 14155 — GCP for Medical Devices

Research with medical devices must follow ISO 14155 (ISO-GCP). It is highly recommended to obtain this training or self-train on the ISO-GCP document itself. This is a copy-controlled document available via ISO.org at cost. Currently the RI-MUHC does not have access to an ISO-GCP course through CITI; this will be communicated to the research community when available.

Since ISO-GCP requirements largely overlap with ICH-GCP, if a Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator cannot provide ISO-GCP training, teams should be trained on ICH-GCP in lieu of ISO-GCP. Note that Health Canada inspectors expect teams conducting medical device studies to be trained on ISO-GCP.

## Protocol and study-specific training {#protocol-and-study-specific-training}

The QI/PI's signature on the protocol or any Sponsor-supplied document confirms their commitment to conduct the study in compliance with the REB-approved protocol, ICH-GCP/ISO-GCP, and applicable regulatory requirements.

The Investigator and research team must undertake a complete review of the protocol and the <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator Brochure</Tooltip> (if applicable) before the Sponsor/CRO's Site Feasibility Visit and <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">Site Initiation Visit</Tooltip> (SIV).

Protocol and study documentation training must be provided by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator before staff delegating tasks on the Task Delegation Log. Subsequent initial training of new staff may be documented on the Training Log (including self-training).

Staff must also be trained on amendments to any protocol or study document (e.g. IB) in a timely fashion, directly after REB approval. Documentation of training communications should include names of trainees, meeting date, presentation slides, and a summary of discussions.

## Documenting training {#documenting-training}

All training (general, task-specific, study-specific, and ongoing) must be kept in the Investigator's Site File (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>) with essential study documents, or retained in the individual's training records as part of a Master Training File. Research personnel must be prepared to demonstrate all training obtained.

As per Health Canada GUI-0100, documentation of training must include:
- The title and content of the training, such as the learning objectives
- Who attended
- When the training occurred

Acceptable documentation includes slide decks, course manuals, training certificates, meeting minutes, attendance logs, and updated CVs with supporting documentation.

For training that does not provide a certificate or dated printout (e.g. ISO 14155 GCP document self-study, protocol or IB self-reading), the training must be recorded on the **Training Log** (Appendix 2).

---

## Leave of absence {#leave-of-absence}

In the event of a leave of absence exceeding 6 months, human research team members must redo their SOP training at the appropriate level based on their studies upon return. If returning to the same training level, learners may take the refresher quiz in lieu of redoing the full course. Other training may be required based on supervisor requirements or if the study level needs to be upgraded.
