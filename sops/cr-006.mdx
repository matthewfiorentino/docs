## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the activities involved in preparing the Informed Consent Form (<Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip>) in compliance with the requirements applicable to a given research project (e.g., [Declaration of Helsinki](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/), Tri-Council Policy Statement 2 ([TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html)), Good Clinical Practice (GCP), Health Canada (HC) regulations, MSSS requirements and Research Ethics Board (<Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>) requirements).

Note: This procedure also applies to other written participant-facing material supplied to the study participant, which may contain some of the same information, as the ICF and must also be reviewed and approved by the REB.

## 2.0 Scope

This procedure concerns the research community (employees, investigators, physicians, management, consultants, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in preparing (drafting, adapting, revising, reviewing or translating) study ICFs (hard copy or electronic) for research with human participants at the MUHC/RI-MUHC. This SOP is applicable to all human research studies (unless in cases where informed consent has been waived by the REB).

## 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator is responsible for providing the sites with a master Informed Consent Form compliant with the applicable requirements, which might include the Sponsor’s SOPs, study protocol, HC regulatory requirements, GCP, [Declaration of Helsinki](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/) and [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html).

The Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>)/ Principal Investigator (<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>) is responsible for ensuring that after revision of the Sponsor’s master ICF (if applicable), the site-specific ICF continues to meet all of the applicable international guidelines, federal regulations as well as provincial MSSS requirements and local REB requirements.

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately qualified and trained study team members, however they remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and QI/PI.

## 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

<a id="5-1-general-overview"></a>

### 5.1 General overview

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator provides the site with a study-specific ICF Master that requires customization to meet the site and/or local REB requirements.

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** The ICF is developed, adapted, or revised by the designated research team at the site based on the MUHC REB ICF template.

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** The ICF is reviewed by the QI/PI and Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-1-4"></a>**5.1.4** The Investigator then submits the site-specific ICF to the REB via the study submission platform (e.g., [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview)) for REB review/comments, subsequent site revisions and subsequent REB approval. The approved ICF is filed in the Investigator Site File (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>) along with the documented approval. Note that it is important to always submit the ICF (as well as participant-facing material) to the REB in editable Word format (not PDFs).

<a id="5-1-5"></a>**5.1.5.** The Investigator then sends the approved ICF to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator for their Trial Master File (<Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip>) with the documented approval.

<a id="5-1-6"></a>**5.1.6.** Once REB approval for the ICF is obtained, the ICF should be sent for translation and the translated ICF should also be submitted to the REB.

<a id="5-2"></a>

<a id="5-2-icf-guidelines"></a>

### 5.2 ICF guidelines

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** For studies involving investigational product (drug, medical device or natural health product), the ICF must be printed on MUHC hospital letterhead with the MUHC Medical Records bar code FMU-9997 (already on the REB ICF templates). Note that the FMU-9997 bar code ensures that the research ICF is stored in the Alert section of the electronic medical record (e.g., [OACIS](/apps/oacis)) of the patient. Other studies need not meet the bar code requirement. Users of the official letterhead or logo must adhere to the brand and logo guidelines on webpage, https://muhc.ca/muhc-logo-guidelines.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** Use language that is as non-technical as possible, and understandable to the participant, without compromising the content. An eighth-grade reading level is recommended.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** Define all medical and technical terms (such as random assignment, probability, blinding, and placebo) and acronyms, common initials, and other abbreviations when first used.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** Use headings, bulleted lists, tables (e.g., visit-specific schedule of procedures, and table of risks) and avoid dense paragraphs in order to improve readability and assist comprehension.

<a id="5-2-5"></a>**5.2.5** For printed paper copies, select a font size appropriate to the target population (e.g., font 12) considering such factors as age and underlying conditions, when possible, unless otherwise specified by the REB.

<a id="5-2-6"></a>**5.2.6** Clearly identify each ICF version with the version number and/or date on each page and ensure the file name also reflects the version number and/or date for electronic versions. Note that Health Canada inspection findings have included inaccurate file names/versions that were not updated.

<a id="5-2-7"></a>**5.2.7** It is recommended to have someone unfamiliar with the study, preferably a layperson, read the ICF and other written participant-facing materials to ensure clarity prior to finalization. This allows an assessment of comprehension of the material. Adjust the content, as necessary.

<a id="5-2-8"></a>**5.2.8** The [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html) requires that the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator register all clinical trials before recruitment of the first study participant in a recognized and easily web-accessible registry. The registry’s name, URL and the unique code assigned by the registry should appear as standard information in the REB-approved ICF. Contact the RI-MUHC Quality Assurance (<Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's Quality Assurance department, which checks the credentials and training of each investigator who submits a study, audits research and maintains the SOPs. In GCP, quality assurance is the planned, systematic actions that ensure a trial complies with GCP and the applicable regulatory requirements." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip>) department at QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca if you are a Sponsor-Investigator and do not already have an account in clinicaltrials.gov for registration of all your studies.

<a id="5-2-9"></a>**5.2.9** If using an electronic ICF with eSignature, ensure that a documented remote ICF procedure is submitted to the REB with the ICF and that it identifies the software used and confirms that its installation has been validated and complies with electronic record guidance and regulations. Refer to [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015) regarding electronic records.

<a id="5-3"></a>

<a id="5-3-icf-content"></a>

### 5.3 Development of informed consent form: content

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** If an ICF master is not provided by the Sponsor, refer to the Consent/Assent document templates on the REB webpage https://muhc.ca/cae/page/templates-consent-forms. Note that these templates have mandatory wording (indicated in red) that is required by the MSSS for clinical trials. For additional information refer to the MSSS guidance, Clauses légales types des formulaires d’information et de consentement dans le cadre d’essais cliniques.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** If a master ICF is provided by the Sponsor, when revising the master ICF prior to REB approval, ensure that the MSSS required paragraphs indicated in red in the REB ICF templates have been inserted into the site-specific ICF. The paragraphs to be textually inserted into the site-specific ICF refer to:

- Voluntary participation and the right to withdraw;
- Confidentiality;
- Should you suffer any harm;
- Contact information;
- Signatures.

Verify the content by completing the Verification of ICF Document ([Appendix 1](#appendix-1)). Without these MSSS required paragraphs, the ICF will be rejected by the REB. Also ensure that all information in the ICF (whether from a Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator) accurately reflects what is in the protocol which will be reviewed by the REB. If the site finds discrepancies in the ICF (e.g., omission of some protocol required procedures), the site should inform the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator prior to REB submission.

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** Ensure to have the revised ICF quality controlled (i.e., reviewed by someone else at the site) prior to submission to the REB to ensure it’s quality.

<a id="5-3-4"></a>**5.3.4** For clinical trials, if the participant agrees, the Investigator should obtain consent to contact the participant’s primary caregiver regarding the participant’s involvement in the study and any clinically useful information in the context of continued care. Refer to [SOP-CR-501](/sops/cr-501) for more information.

<a id="5-3-5"></a>**5.3.5** Also ensure that the ICF includes/complies with the following, when developing or adapting a master ICF:

- Consistency with the protocol (e.g., objectives, number of visits, study procedures, expected risks, alternative treatments);
- Sponsor’s and Investigator’s name;
- Page numbers on all pages (e.g., page 1 of 15);
- All signature (and initial) and date fields that are required (i.e., study participant, person obtaining consent and if applicable, QI/PI) or may be required (i.e., legally appointed representative, witness, translator).

<a id="5-3-6"></a>**5.3.6** All research advertisement(s) including posters, flyers, press releases, electronic/social media publicity, and videos must comply with the MUHC

Communication policy and should be submitted to the REB as part of the initial study application in the Nagano system. (Refer to [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007) and [SOP-CR-009](/sops/cr-009)).

<a id="5-3-7"></a>**5.3.7** It is generally mandatory to have all participant-facing materials (e.g., questionnaires, diary, phone script) available in both official languages. ICFs as well as any participant-facing information or advertising must be available in English and French and submitted to the REB. Translation of the ICF should be done by a qualified/certified translator when required and translation certificates should be obtained. Refer to [Appendix 2](#appendix-2) for a translation certificate template if a certificate is not provided by your translator. A Sponsor-Investigator, who does not have the funds for professional translation may translate (or delegate translation of) the ICF and best practice is to have this translation documented on the translation certificate. Forward translations to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and QI/PI for approval, prior to submission to the REB. The REB will generally not approve a study without the ICF and participant materials written in both official languages.

<a id="5-3-8"></a>**5.3.8** Do not consent participants before receiving REB written approval, MUHC Authorization, holding an <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip> and obtaining a go letter from the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, if applicable.

<a id="5-4"></a>

<a id="5-4-ongoing-amendments"></a>

### 5.4 Ongoing amendments to the informed consent form

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** Throughout the course of the study, revise the ICF, as needed, based on safety reports/issues, protocol or Investigator Brochure (<Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip>) amendments, REB recommendations, Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) reports, Health Canada/regulatory requests, or study Sponsor requests.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** Revise the ICF amendment version number and/or date on each page and ensure the electronic file name also reflects the version number and/or date.

<a id="5-4-3"></a>**5.4.3** Submit ICF revisions to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator for review, prior to submission to the REB (when applicable).

<a id="5-4-4"></a>**5.4.4** Submit a revised ICF (with track changes) to the REB for approval.

<a id="5-4-5"></a>**5.4.5** Do not begin using the amended ICF until receipt of written REB approval, as well as an implementation/Go letter/email from the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, if applicable. Refer to section [5.7.1](#5-7-1) and [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) for exceptions.

<a id="5-4-6"></a>**5.4.6** Print out the REB submission &amp; approval as well as all relevant emails and maintain a record/audit trail of communications related to the ICF amendment (communications with Sponsor, Sponsor-Investigator, regulatory authorities, REB, etc.). File these documents in the ISF.

<a id="5-4-7"></a>**5.4.7** Best practice is to create a Document Approval Tracking Log to track submissions to the REB including ICF submissions, approvals and implementation. Refer to [Appendix 3](#appendix-3) ICF Approval Tracker.

<a id="5-5"></a>

### 5.5 Waiver of participant’s legal rights

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** Do not use any language in the ICF that causes the participant or their legally appointed representative (LAR), to waive, or appear to waive, any legal rights.

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** Do not use any language in the ICF that releases, or appears to release, the Investigator, the institution, the site, the Sponsor, or their agents from liability for negligence. Statements such as, “we are not responsible for …” must be avoided.

<a id="5-6"></a>

<a id="5-6-cultural-considerations"></a>

### 5.6 Cultural considerations

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Ensure that the language and research activities respect the culture, priorities, and knowledge base of the cultural group being invited to participate in the clinical study. Ensure inclusive language throughout the document (e.g., use “they”, “their” in lieu of his/her, and naming participant groups according to their self denomination) and ensure that a translator is available, if applicable. For an inclusive writing guide and other guidance documents, refer to the Equity, Diversity and Inclusion page of the RI portal.

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** Anyone considering conducting research with a First Nation should have a basic understanding of First Nations Information Governance Centre’s (FNIGC’s) OCAP (Ownership-Control-Access-Possession).

- Ownership – First Nations communities or groups own their data collectively;
- Control – First Nations communities can control all aspects of data collection and data governance that impact them;
- Access – First Nations must have access to the data, regardless of where it is filed/stored;
- Possession – First nations must have physical control of the data.

For a course on OCAP fundamentals and applications in research and information governance click the link here.

For additional guidance, contact our RI-MUHC Equity, Diversity and Inclusion Specialists at edi.ri@muhc.mcgill.ca.

<a id="5-7"></a>

<a id="5-7-approval"></a>

### 5.7 Approval considerations

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** Obtain approval from the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, QI/PI, (as applicable) and from the REB before implementing any change from the protocol, ICF(s) or participant materials. The only exception is in emergency situations (immediate safety hazard to participants), or as covered under exceptions to informed consent process. In such an emergency case, a documented conversation with the participant or LAR is required. (Refer to [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) Informed Consent Process).

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** Depending on the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, you may also require documented approval (e.g., in the form of a Go Letter) from the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator prior to implementing the revised ICF at the site.
