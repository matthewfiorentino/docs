## Key points

- All specimen collection, processing, and shipping must follow the Sponsor's laboratory manual and the protocol exactly. Deviations (wrong tube, wrong processing time, incorrect storage temperature) must be documented and reported to the Sponsor.
- All laboratory abnormalities must be reported to the QI/PI for assessment; results must be printed, initialled, and dated by the Investigator. Clinically significant abnormalities must be documented as described in the protocol.
- Specimens must be labelled with the participant's study ID only — never with name, MRN, or date of birth — along with visit code, collection date/time, and any other Sponsor-required identifiers.
- Chain of custody must be maintained from collection through shipment. The **Human Sample Collection, Processing and Shipping Log** (Appendix 1) must be completed for every specimen batch.
- Ensure staff are qualified and trained on study-specific specimen handling tasks. Safety information about biological specimens must be accessible to all persons handling them; biosafety training is available through RI-MUHC Environmental Health and Safety (ri.ehs@muhc.mcgill.ca).
- The person responsible for shipping biological specimens must hold a valid TDG certification (3 years, ground) or IATA certification (2 years, air). Certification must be kept with training documents in the essential study file.
- Laboratory records to retain include: normal ranges for study tests; laboratory certification/accreditation; quality control documentation; laboratory results/reports; X-ray films, digital images, microfilms, and compact disks.
