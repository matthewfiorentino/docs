## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) outlines the processes for assessing and reporting adverse events (AEs), including adverse drug reactions (ADRs), serious adverse events (SAEs) and Suspect Unexpected Serious Adverse Reactions (SUSARs) to the study Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator (if applicable), the Research Ethics Board (<Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>), the Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) if applicable, and regulatory authorities, including Health Canada, US Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and the European Medicines Agency (EMA).

## 2.0 Scope

This SOP is applicable to all interventional clinical studies undertaken at the MUHC/RI-MUHC, and to those clinical research site personnel responsible for collecting/receiving, assessing, reviewing, processing, documenting and reporting all AEs, SAEs and SUSARs.

Serious adverse drug reaction reporting is in accordance with the International Council on Harmonisation (ICH) Guidelines E2A, Clinical Safety Data Management: Definitions and Standards for Expedited Reporting, applicable national regulations and guidance, and REB of record requirements.

Note that ICH-GCP E6 and this SOP do not cover mandatory problem reporting for Investigational Device. The Sponsor-Investigator/<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip> is required to report Device SAEs to Health Canada as per ISO 14155 GCP and to the manufacturer and importer within 72 hours of awareness. Refer to the Health Canada website for Medical Device Incident reporting forms.

## 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator is responsible for the collection of all AEs and SAEs, expediting reporting of SAEs to the regulatory authorities (Health Canada, FDA, EMA) and informing all involved Investigators of all SUSARs. The Qualified Investigator (QI)/Principal Investigator (<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>) is responsible for assessment of all AEs and ensuring that the local <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">AE</Tooltip>, <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip>, and external <Tooltip tip="A serious adverse reaction to a drug or natural health product whose nature, severity or frequency the Investigator's Brochure or product label does not anticipate; the sponsor reports it to Health Canada, and the site reports it to the REB when it meets the REB's criteria." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip> reporting meet all of the applicable regulatory, International Conference on Harmonisation (ICH) Good Clinical Practice (GCP), Sponsor, REB and local requirements.

Unless otherwise indicated, any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained clinical research site personnel, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor-Investigator and/or QI/PI.

## 4.0 Definitions

SAE Site Awareness date: The date that the QI/PI or any of his delegated research team members become aware of the serious adverse event. See [Glossary of Terms](/kb/foundations/glossary) for other definitions

## 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

### 5.1 General

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** Ensure that all study investigators who will be assessing AEs, SAEs and SUSARs obtain RI-MUHC Privileges to conduct research. Refer to [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500).

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** Ensure all investigators and research team members obtain the appropriate level of institutional training on GCP, Health Canada Division 5 (where applicable) and SOPs. Refer to [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003) regarding training. Ensure that all research team members are trained on and familiar with the AE/SAE/SUSAR process. Refer to [Appendix 6](#appendix-6) for a process flowchart.

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** Ensure that the QI/PI and Sub-investigators (Sub-Is) and trained staff members are delegated appropriate safety reporting tasks (AE/SAE/SUSAR). MD QI/PI/Su-Is are delegated AE/SAE Assessment and SUSAR review. <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip>/nurses are delegated AE/SAE/SUSAR collection and reporting.

<a id="5-1-4"></a>**5.1.4** It is essential to continue to educate participants about adverse events (AEs)/adverse drug reactions (ADRs i.e. AEs related to <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip>), serious adverse events (SAEs), and the importance of reporting any AE (whether believed to be related to IP/study or not) to the human research site (e.g. coordinator or Investigator) ASAP via email or at study visits and/or during telephone or virtual contact. Refer to [SOP-CR-009](/sops/cr-009) Appendix 6 for a contact log. All AE discussions must be documented and all AEs must be recorded on the AE Assessment log. The documentation of this exchange as well as the updated AE Assessment log should be kept in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>.

<a id="5-1-5"></a>**5.1.5** In the case of a participant who is not competent, or a minor (less than 18 years old), the QI/PI or delegated study team member should collect all data related to the AE/SAE from the legal representative.

<a id="5-1-6"></a>**5.1.6** Delegated team members should discuss all AEs/SAEs reported by the participant or legal representative with the QI/PI and obtain assessment on the AE Assessment log on the same day or at the latest 5 days after and document discussion in the ISF.

<a id="5-1-7"></a>**5.1.7** AEs include all events related and not related to the investigational drug/natural health product. Non-related AEs are NOT considered as ADRs, and are NOT reportable to the REB or regulatory authorities; however, all AEs must be assessed and captured in source documents and Case Report Forms (CRFs), as described in the protocol.

<a id="5-1-8"></a>**5.1.8** Abnormal laboratory or test results should be reported to the QI/PI on the same day or at the latest 5 days afterwards for assessment. The QI/PI should review the test results and indicate any abnormalities as clinically significant (c.s.) or not clinically significant (n.c.s.) and initial and date the results ASAP. Laboratory abnormalities deemed clinically significant should be documented as described in the protocol. Note: If test results are reviewed by the QI/PI online and subsequently initialed and dated at a later date, this process should be documented (e.g. a Working Instruction) and a copy placed in the ISF.

<a id="5-1-9"></a>**5.1.9** Ensure that the protocol outlines recording and reporting requirements for AEs, SAEs, reportable SAEs and reportable medically important events as well as SUSARs.

<a id="5-1-10"></a>**5.1.10** Post-marketing (Phase IV) studies or studies that involve a marketed product: Report to the Sponsor/Market Authorization Holder (MAH) in order for them to report adverse reactions to marketed products, as per the Health Canada guidance document for industry, Reporting Adverse Reactions to Marketed Health Products (effective 23-May-2018).

<a id="5-2"></a>

### 5.2 Documenting AEs in source documents for QI/PI assessment

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** Throughout the course of a clinical trial, all AE details must be collected at each study visit/telephone/virtual contact and reported in clinical notes and on an AE Assessment log from the time of the participant’s <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> signature date to the end of study as per REB approved protocol.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** It is important for delegated research team members to report all AEs, as per REB approved protocol, to the QI/PI for documented assessment on an AE Assessment log.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** Any change from the Medical History Events Log ([SOP-CR-014](/sops/cr-014), Appendix 2) should be captured on the AE Assessment log.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** The QI/PI should assess the event and provide the participant with appropriate medical care. Assessment must include: Severity (e.g. as per Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) An adverse event (AE) assessment log is to be completed for each study subject ([Appendix 1](#appendix-1) AE assessment log). Departments, study teams may adapt the AE assessment log or may implement a controlled version of their own log on condition that all elements described in this section and appendix 1 are included: 1- Mild; asymptomatic or mild symptoms; clinical or diagnostic observations only. Intervention not indicated.

2- Moderate: minimal, local or non-invasive intervention indicated; limiting age-appropriate Activities of Daily Living (ADL’s).

3- Severe or medically significant but not immediately life-threatening; hospitalization indicated. Disabling, limit of self-care/ADL’s. 4- Life-threatening consequences; urgent intervention indicated. 5- Death related to AE (based on appendix 1).

Note that the frequency of events should also be assessed by the QI/PI.

<a id="5-2-5"></a>**5.2.5** The QI/PI should assess the Relationship to A- IP(s)/study procedure i.e. all investigative drugs or NHPs individually Or B- the study procedures (1- Not Related, 2- Unlikely Related, 3- Possibly Related and 4. Definitely Related). Note that part of HC reporting criteria is that the AE be at least possibly “Related to IP”, however REB reporting criteria include relatedness to the study as a whole.

<a id="5-2-6"></a>**5.2.6** The QI/PI or delegate should assess Seriousness as per Health Canada reporting criteria:

1- Hospitalization or prolonged hospitalization; 2- Caused congenital malformation; 3- Persistent or significant disability or incapacity; 4- Life-threatening or Death; 5- Medically Important Event as per REB-approved protocol.

If any one of these criteria are met, then the AE is upgraded to an SAE. Note: This change in severity should be captured on the AE Assessment log and subsequently tracked on the SAE Reporting log.

<a id="5-2-7"></a>**5.2.7** In addition, the AE Description, Date Started, Date of Change (i.e. resolved, improved or worsened in intensity or resulted in death), Action Taken with Study Drug/Procedure, as well as Outcome of AE should be recorded on the AE Assessment log. Note that Action Taken and Outcome should correlate with any Concomitant Medications and dates on the Concomitant Medication log (refer to Appendix in [SOP-CR-014](/sops/cr-014), Appendix 4 for the Concomitant Medication log).

<a id="5-2-8"></a>**5.2.8** The delegated CRC who completed the AE Assessment log and the QI/PI providing medical oversight must initial and date the log.

<a id="5-2-9"></a>**5.2.9** As previously stated, the CRC must report the change in AE grade to SAE (e.g. patient is hospitalized) on the AE Assessment log, report the SAE to the QI/PI and document the QI Awareness date (e.g. printout the email exchange) in source and on the SAE Reporting log.

<a id="5-2-10"></a>**5.2.10** If a source document AE Assessment log template is not provided by a Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1) for the RI-MUHC AE Assessment log template.

<a id="5-3"></a>

### 5.3 Documenting SAEs in source documents for QI assessment and report tracking

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** Any AE which meets SAE criteria on the Local AE Assessment log ([Appendix 1](#appendix-1)) should be reported to the Sponsor within 24 hours of the QI/PI Awareness date.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** The SAE should also be documented on the SAE Reporting Log. Refer to [Appendix 2](#appendix-2) for the SAE Reporting Log. All documentation to support this date must also be kept on file.

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** The SAE Reporting Log documents the QI/PI Awareness date, the SAE Report &amp; Reporting date (Initial and Follow-up), the REB Criteria met, REB submission date, as well as a column for Sponsor-Investigators to capture Health Canada reporting (if applicable).

<a id="5-3-4"></a>**5.3.4** The Expectedness of the event (as per protocol, <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip> or PM), is an important assessment required to determine if the SAE is reportable to both the REB and to Health Canada.

<a id="5-3-5"></a>**5.3.5** A column may be added to the SAE Reporting log template to track SAE reporting to the DSMB as per DSMB charter (if applicable).

<a id="5-4"></a>

<a id="5-4-reporting-all-saes-to-the-sponsor-within-24-hours"></a>

### 5.4 Reporting all SAE’s to the Sponsor within 24 hours

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** All SAEs (i.e. All AEs that meet Health Canada criteria for seriousness) must be reported to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator within 24 hours of the QI/PI Awareness or as otherwise specified in the REB-approved protocol using the appropriate forms (even if only partially completed). Follow up with additional information, as soon as it becomes available. A complete SAE Follow-Up report should be sent within 8 calendar days from the Initial SAE reporting in order for the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator to meet their Health Canada reporting deadline.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** Ensure the SAE is reported on the AE Assessment log for assessment by the QI/PI. Ensure the QI/PI Awareness date, QI/PI’s assessment of Expectedness and Initial reporting date of the SAE are documented in source documents/on the SAE Reporting log.

<a id="5-4-3"></a>**5.4.3** Most Sponsors or Sponsor-Investigators provide an SAE reporting form which must be filled out, signed by the QI/PI and then emailed or faxed to the Sponsor’s Pharmacovigilance team. More recently, Sponsors are providing electronic methods for SAE reporting. If this is the case, it is recommended to use the RI-MUHC SAE report template to keep in the ISF prior to transcribing into the Sponsor’s eCRF SAE report. Ensure that the eCRF report and proof of SAE reporting with date of Sponsor’s receipt is printed and filed in the ISF and documented on the SAE Reporting Log.

<a id="5-4-4"></a>**5.4.4** QI/PIs may assess it to be necessary to break the blinding code to ensure optimal participant care. Refer to study protocol (or equivalent document) for detailed un-blinding procedure. Document the need to break the randomization code and communicate this to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-4-5"></a>**5.4.5** If a participant becomes pregnant, collect and follow prospective and retrospective Exposure in Utero (EIU) reports, in order to obtain outcome information. The absence of an AE does not preclude collection of this data.

<a id="5-4-6"></a>**5.4.6** Document and retain all correspondence (including phone conversations using SOP-CR-009_07_APPENDIX 6_Telephone Contact Log with the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator’s Medical Monitor) in the ISF.

<a id="5-4-7"></a>**5.4.7** If the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator did not provide an SAE Report form refer to the SAE Report Form template, [Appendix 4](#appendix-4).

<a id="5-5"></a>

### 5.5 Reporting unexpected related-to-IP/study SAE’s to the Research Ethics Board (REB)

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** For Local SAEs: The QI/PI must report to the REB any SAE which is:

- Unexpected (in terms of nature, severity, or frequency) given (a) the research procedures that are described in the REB approved documents, such as the research protocol and informed consent document, the Investigator Brochure or other relevant sources of information such as product labelling and package inserts; and (b) the event is not associated with the expected natural progression of any underlying disease, disorder, predisposing risk factor, or condition of the participant(s) experiencing the adverse event; AND
- Related or possibly related to participation in the research. Possibly related means there is a reasonable possibility that the incident, experience, or outcome may have been caused by the investigational product(s) or procedures involved in the research); AND
- Suggests that the research places research participants or others at a greater risk of harm (including physical, psychological, economic, or social harm) than was previously known or recognized.

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** If the reportable SAE is life-threatening or fatal, the SAE must be reported to the REB within 7 days of the QI/PI Awareness date.

<a id="5-5-3"></a>**5.5.3** If the reportable SAE is non-life-threatening or not fatal, the SAE must be reported to the REB within 15 days of the QI/PI Awareness date.

<a id="5-5-4"></a>**5.5.4** Reportable SAEs are reported to the REB through the [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview) system. The former SAE reporting form was Nagano F3a. The new 2021 Nagano form for SAE reporting is now the Nagano 3H form. Any SAE Report form must be uploaded and appended to the Nagano form.

<a id="5-5-5"></a>**5.5.5** The Nagano 3H form must include the following information:

- The patient study number and a description of the Unexpected, At least Possibly Related Serious Adverse Event
- Previous safety reports concerning similar events, if available
- An analysis of the significance of the current Unanticipated event
- The proposed research changes (if any), protocol, IB and/or ICF changes or other corrective actions to be taken by the Sponsor in response to the event(s).
- A copy of the current SAE report that was sent to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator.

- DSMB meeting information (if applicable).

<a id="5-5-6"></a>**5.5.6** Update the REB as soon as additional information is available, appending any Follow-Up SAE reports.

<a id="5-5-7"></a>**5.5.7** All other reports (those not meeting above criteria for expedited reporting) should be submitted with REB annual Continuing Review.

<a id="5-5-8"></a>**5.5.8** For studies reviewed by an external Quebec Multi-centrique REB, an SAE notification must be submitted to the MUHC person mandatee via the F3H as well as the external reviewing REB via their designated form (or as per REB SOPs).

<a id="5-6"></a>

### 5.6 Sponsor-Investigators reporting individual suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs) to Health Canada and the DSMB

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** The Sponsor or Sponsor/Investigator must comply with the regulatory requirements of Health Canada regarding expedited reporting of single cases of unexpected serious adverse reactions, (for which a causal relationship with the investigational product cannot be ruled out, unless otherwise stated in the study contract, or other document) or where an increase in the rate of occurrence or severity, was judged to be clinically important. Note that “Seriousness” (and not “Severity”) serves as a guide for regulatory reporting obligations (e.g. A severe headache is not serious). Reminder: Expectedness is determined by the information in the Investigator’s Brochure (IB) or other REB approved documents such as protocol and informed consent form. If the AE is not listed or is more severe than listed in the IB, the AE is considered Unexpected.

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** Medical and scientific judgement should be used to determine if expedited reporting is justified for other important medical events that may not result in hospitalization, death or be immediately life-threatening, however, alter the risk-benefit assessment (e.g. 1 – Lack of efficacy with IP used to treat a life-threatening disease; 2 - Treatment required in ER for anaphylaxis, blood dyscrasias or convulsions or development of drug dependency or drug abuse or 3- An expected serious ADR with an increase in frequency which is judged to be clinically important; 4 – Major safety data from newly completed animal studies e.g. carcinogenicity).

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** The minimum information for the submission of a report to regulatory health authorities includes at least one identifiable patient/participant, one identifiable reporter, at least one suspect medicinal product, one event or outcome that is assessed as serious and unexpected and there is reasonable suspected relationship to a medicinal product.

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** Prepare a Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences (CIOMS)- I form (or MedWatch for US reports) for submission to national health authorities (Health Canada, FDA, EMA, etc.), as appropriate. Unblind the Unexpected and Related SAE Reports for the purposes of expedited reporting, especially for reports submitted in Europe. Refer to [Appendix 5](#appendix-5) for the CIOMS report template.

<a id="5-6-5"></a>**5.6.5** Submit preliminary information on fatal and life-threatening reactions from a clinical trial within 7 calendar days following Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/CRO awareness, verbally if appropriate, and complete written follow-up submitted within another 8 calendar days from the date of the Initial Report.

<a id="5-6-6"></a>**5.6.6** Submit all other Unexpected and Related SAE reports (which are non-life-threatening and not fatal) within 15 calendar days.

<a id="5-6-7"></a>**5.6.7** For most blinded studies, it is recommended for the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator to break the blind for the one participant who is experiencing the expedited SADR. As per ICH E2A, it is also recommended that the blind be maintained for biometrics personnel responsible for the analysis and interpretation of study results. However, for studies where “Serious” or “Fatal” outcomes are primary study endpoints, unblinding could compromise the integrity of the study. In this case, it may be advantageous to reach an agreement with the health authorities prior to proceeding.

<a id="5-6-8"></a>**5.6.8** The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator is required to amend the IB to update the safety information as needed in accordance with regulatory requirements.

<a id="5-6-9"></a>**5.6.9** Submit periodic, quarterly or annual reports, as per national regulations.

<a id="5-6-10"></a>**5.6.10** For post-marketing studies, submit reports as per applicable national regulations.

<a id="5-6-11"></a>**5.6.11** The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator should report all reportable SAEs to the DSMB as per DSMB charter (if applicable).

<a id="5-7"></a>

### 5.7 Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator distribution of blinded external SUSARs to QI/PIs and QI/PI documented medical oversight

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator is responsible for distributing expedited external SAEs, usually referred to as Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reactions (SUSAR) reports to each investigator (of a multi-centre trial). Each investigator receives blinded serious adverse reaction reports.

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** It is the QI/PI’s responsibility to document their medical oversight of SUSARs.

<a id="5-7-3"></a>**5.7.3** The QI/PI should establish a written procedure for receiving and processing safety reports received from the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator. E.g. create a Working Instruction (WI) regarding how SUSAR reports will be read by the QI/PI.

<a id="5-8"></a>

### 5.8 Qi/pi reporting of blinded external SUSARs to the REB

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** For External Reportable SUSARs: The same criteria for reporting to the REB must apply with one additional criterion. Refer to [REB SOP 405A.001](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops). The QI/PI must report to the REB via Nagano any External SAE which is:

- Unexpected (in terms of nature, severity, or frequency) given (a) the research procedures that are described in the REB approved documents, such as the research protocol and informed consent document, the Investigator Brochure or other relevant sources of information such as product labelling and package inserts; and (b) the event is not associated with the expected natural progression of any underlying disease, disorder, predisposing risk factor, or condition of the participant(s) experiencing the adverse event; and
- Related or possibly related to participation in the research. Possibly related means there is a reasonable possibility that the incident, experience, or outcome may have been caused by the investigational product(s) or procedures involved in the research); and
- Suggests that the research places research participants or others at a greater risk of harm (including physical, psychological, economic, or social harm) than was previously known or recognized.

- AND requires a change to the research (amendment of protocol, IB and/or ICF) and/or requires immediate notification to participants for safety reasons. (You may want to confirm with the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator that an amendment is forthcoming).

<a id="5-8-2"></a>**5.8.2** The QI/PI must submit all SUSARs/summaries and follow-up information which comply with the above criteria to the REB via the Nagano system within 15 days from receipt/awareness. Retain all documentation in the ISF.

<a id="5-8-3"></a>**5.8.3** Maintain all SUSAR reports and associated REB correspondence in the study files. Refer to [Appendix 3](#appendix-3), External SUSAR Reporting Log.

<a id="5-9"></a>

### 5.9 Management of AEs and SAEs in studies with no investigational products

<a id="5-9-1"></a>**5.9.1** In the case of clinical studies without an investigational product, it is recommended that the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and QI/PI follow the same procedures for collecting clinical data related to AEs and SAEs, assessing and reporting to their REB/IEC. Note that part of the REB reporting criteria is that an SAE must be at least possibly related to study (not necessarily IP).

<a id="5-9-2"></a>**5.9.2** For Investigational Device trial, use the Mandatory Medical Device Problem Reporting form on the Health Canada website.

<a id="5-9-3"></a>**5.9.3 SAE Submission in the Multi-centrique Context**

For SAE submission in the Quebec MSSS Multi-centrique process (I.e. more than one Quebec site for a given study) please refer to the most recent MUHC REB information on the MUHC CAE website.
