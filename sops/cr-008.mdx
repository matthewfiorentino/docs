<a id="purpose-and-scope"></a>

## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the procedures for obtaining and documenting initial and ongoing informed consent and assent. This SOP also describes informed consent guidelines, and the roles of the legally appointed representative, the translator and the requirement for impartial witness for verbal consent.

## 2.0 Scope

This procedure concerns the research community (employees, investigators, physicians, management, consultants, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in preparing for, performing or documenting the informed consent process for any human research study at the MUHC/RI-MUHC.

This SOP includes the process of informing Medical Records of participants enrolled in studies with investigational product(s) (drug, medical device or natural health product).

All parts of this process are applicable, regardless of obtaining participant written informed consent with a paper or electronic informed consent form (<Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> or eICF) or verbal informed consent.

<a id="responsibilities"></a>

## 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator is responsible for providing the study-specific master ICF to the site(s) for revision into site-specific (customized) ICF(s) and submission to their local Research Ethics Board (<Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>). Refer to [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006) Informed Consent Form.

The Sponsor-Investigator or Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>)/ Principal Investigator (<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>) is responsible for ensuring that the informed consent process meets all of the applicable regulatory, [International Council on Harmonisation (ICH) Good Clinical Practice (GCP)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html)/ [International Standards Organization (ISO) Good Clinical Practice (GCP)](https://www.iso.org/standard/14155), [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html), Sponsor, and local REB requirements.

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor-Investigator or QI/PI.

## 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

<a id="standard-consent-process"></a><a id="3-1-requirements"></a>

### 5.1 Informed consent process before participant study protocol screening

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** The person obtaining informed consent should ensure that a participant’s informed consent is obtained prior to any protocol screening procedure being conducted. This person should be a qualified person who knows the study protocol well, and be available to explain it to the participant in simple lay terms. This person should not be the participant’s treating physician or other person with a long-standing relationship with the participant as this may be perceived as placing undue influence on the participant. However if the treating physician is considered the best person to obtain consent, they must explain to the REB why it is necessary. If the REB does approve this, the physician must disclose their dual role to the participant (FRQS, Part 2, 11).

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** Ensure that the person obtaining informed consent is qualified by education, experience and documented training to do so (i.e., trained on [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006) and this SOP), has trained on the protocol and this training is documented, is knowledgeable in the study procedures and has subsequently been delegated this task by the QI/PI as indicated on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>.

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** When recruitment of individuals may raise concerns (e.g., relatives of research team members), special measures may need to be put in place to ensure the following: 1) Autonomous participant decision-making; 2) Participant confidentiality; 3) Research integrity.

If the research team has concerns, contact the REB for a case-specific analysis and guidance.

<a id="5-1-4"></a>**5.1.4** Determine if a legally appointed representative (LAR) will be required (i.e., the participant is a minor or an incompetent adult). If an LAR is required, ensure there is a line on the ICF for the LAR to sign and date and ensure there is a line for the minor or incompetent adult to sign and date (if capable) on an assent portion of the ICF. See the [MUHC REB webpage](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms) for all ICF templates.

<a id="5-1-5"></a>**5.1.5** Document if the potential participant is not deemed competent to consent and requires an LAR. Also, document the name of the LAR and the relationship to the participant.

<a id="5-1-6"></a>**5.1.6** Determine if an impartial translator is required (e.g., if the participant/LAR does not speak English or French). If this is the case, ensure there is also a signature line on the ICF for the translator to sign and date.

<a id="5-1-7"></a>**5.1.7** If the REB determines that an impartial witness is required (e.g., if participant/LAR in a clinical trial cannot read and must provide verbal consent). If this is the case, an impartial witness must be present throughout the informed consent process and sign and date the ICF as well.

<a id="5-1-8"></a>**5.1.8** Ensure that the Informed Consent Form (ICF) and any participant-facing materials to be presented to the participant/LAR are the most recently REB-approved versions.

<a id="5-1-9"></a>**5.1.9** Prepare an Informed Consent Process Checklist ([Appendix 3](#appendix-3)) to be used to document the informed consent process.

<a id="3-2-consent-discussion"></a><a id="5-1-10"></a>**5.1.10** Allow the potential participant/LAR ample time to read the ICF (preferably in a quiet room) and determine their ability to read English or French.

<a id="5-1-11"></a>**5.1.11** Review the ICF with the potential participant/LAR, preferably in an environment that is private and free of distractions or interruptions. This may be done virtually or at the clinical research site. See section [5.2](#5-2) Remote Informed Consent using eICF. Fully inform the potential participant/LAR of all pertinent aspects of the research (i.e., all essential elements as described in the ICF), including any additional REB-approved written information, in non-technical language that is easy for the potential participant/LAR to understand.

<a id="5-1-12"></a>**5.1.12** As per [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html), do not coerce or unduly influence an individual to participate, or to continue to participate in a study.

<a id="5-1-13"></a>**5.1.13** Encourage the participant/LAR to ask any questions they may have and ask the potential participant/LAR questions to assess their understanding of the material presented. Document this discussion along with the questions that the participant/LAR asked.

<a id="5-1-14"></a>**5.1.14** Permit the participant/LAR to take the ICF home to consult with a family member, care provider or other trusted individual, as may be necessary. If the participant/

LAR chooses to take the paper ICF home for discussion, instruct participant/LAR that they should return to the clinic for dated signature in the presence of study staff.

<a id="5-1-15"></a>**5.1.15** Ascertain the potential participant’s (and, if applicable, LAR’s) willingness to participate. Document the decision of any potential participant who declines to participate/screen failed and reason why (if known) on the study Screening Log (Refer to [SOP-CR-009](/sops/cr-009)).

<a id="5-1-16"></a>**5.1.16** Inform the potential participant/LAR that they may withdraw their voluntary consent at any time. If applicable, as per [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html), inform the potential participant/LAR that this may include withdrawal of their data and human biological materials. Any circumstances that do not allow withdrawal of data or human biological materials once collected should be clearly explained to the potential participant/LAR.

<a id="3-3-signatures"></a><a id="5-1-17"></a>**5.1.17** If the participant voluntarily agrees to participate, obtain documentation of the participant’s/LAR’s written or verbal consent consistent with REB approval and applicable regulations. Have the participant/LAR sign and date the ICF. As previously stated, if verbal consent is approved by the REB and the REB requires an impartial witness be present (depending on type of study), the impartial witness must also sign the ICF and a written ICF is still required to be provided to the prospective participant/LAR.

<a id="5-1-18"></a>**5.1.18** Review the ICF for completeness, ensuring all required fields have been filled out by the participant/LAR including the date of signature and then sign and date the ICF as the person who conducted the informed consent discussion and obtained consent. Obtain any other signatures/dates/times/initials as indicated on the ICF (e.g., QI/PI initials, if required by the Sponsor).

<a id="5-1-19"></a>**5.1.19** Note: All required signatures must be obtained prior to conducting any study-related screening procedures and enrolling the participant into the research study. Electronic signatures or electronic consent may be implemented, if the software is fully validated and compliant with local, provincial, regulatory, and REB requirements. Electronic signatures must also be compliant. Refer to [5.2.2](#5-2-2) and [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015) regarding electronic records.

<a id="5-1-20"></a>**5.1.20** Provide the participant/LAR with a copy of the signed/dated/completed ICF, and any other REB-approved written information reviewed during the informed consent discussion.

<a id="5-1-21"></a>**5.1.21** For clinical trials, if not already asked for on the ICF, request the participant’s/LAR’s permission to notify the participant’s family physician about their participation in the research. If the participant either does not have a family physician or does not wish them to be notified of participation in the study, document this accordingly on the ICF or separate Informed Consent Process Checklist.

<a id="5-1-22"></a>**5.1.22** File the original signed ICF with the study-related essential documents (participant study chart or other). Document the consent process on the Informed Consent Process Checklist ([Appendix 3](#appendix-3)) and file this with the ICF. Note that the person who completes this checklist must be the same person who obtained informed consent.

### 5.2 Remote informed consent using an electronic informed consent form (eICF)

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** According to [Health Canada GUI 0100](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100.html), which references [FDA 21CRF Part11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11), the process for obtaining informed consent using an electronic informed consent form in Health Canada regulated studies should be well detailed in a written document or working instruction (WI).

The WI should include how the eICF will be explained and discussed with the clinical trial participant. For example:

- will the eICF include security questions to confirm an individual’s identity;
- ask additional questions during the eICF discussion to help educate as well as assess the participant’s understanding of the informed consent materials;
- is there an option to sign and obtain a paper copy).

Should a site wish to obtain trial participant consent electronically, they must ensure

- that the electronic software is fully validated and;
- compliant with electronic record guidelines and regulations;
- has documented procedures and appropriate training for users;
- all signed eICFs can be stored (and easily retrieved) securely to ensure confidentiality regarding the participant’s identity, study participation and personal information for the required time period (15 years for Health Canada regulated studies).

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** The electronic ICF should have the capability of obtaining at least two signatures (the participant’s signature and the signature of the person obtaining consent) and should have the capability of producing a signed hard copy for the participant. As stated in [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006), a compliant eSignature includes:

- The printed name of the signer;
- The date and time stamp that the signature was executed;
- A unique user ID code;
- A digital adopted signature;
- The meaning of the signature (signing reason);
- Audit trail.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** All study eICFs, eICF amendments or addenda, any participant-facing materials including any additional questions to assess a participant’s understanding, as well as a written Remote Informed Consent Process must first be submitted and acknowledged by the REB. Consideration should be made with regards to the population being recruited and if the method used to obtain and/or document consent would impede the participant’s capability to provide consent/assent.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** The eICF technology must be able to confirm an individual’s identity.

<a id="5-2-5"></a>**5.2.5** Document the informed consent process using [Appendix 3](#appendix-3), Informed Consent Process Checklist. Ensure there is clear documentation of all aspects of the study that were discussed with the potential participant.

<a id="ip-studies-medical-records"></a>

### 5.3 Additional safety procedures for newly consented participants on investigational product (IP)

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** When a participant begins participation in a clinical research study involving an <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> i.e., an investigational drug, natural health product or medical device, the following information must be sent to MUHC Medical Records Services to be filed in the Alert section (chart level, not visit level) of the participant’s electronic hospital medical record ([OACIS](/apps/oacis)).

- The following information submitted to medical records ensures that any caregiver providing care (emergency or other) will be aware of the patient’s participation in a study with IP and have access to medically relevant information for patient care including the QI/PI contact information to discuss patient safety:

  1. MUHC Form FMU-9997entitled: Confirmation of Participation in Research Form (with applied patient-specific bar code obtained from Medivisit, if using the paper form). Refer to [SOP-CR-009](/sops/cr-009);

  2. A copy of the signed and dated ICF/Assent Document (or a document summarising the project if required by the REB).

- It is highly recommended to use the electronic version of the Confirmation of Participation form that is available in the OWord section of the patient’s OACIS medical chart. Once in OWord, enter the code FMU-9997 to obtain the form. A copy of the paper ICF signed by the participant and person obtaining consent (via wet signature) or a document summarizing the project should still be hand delivered to Medical Records with an attached Medivisit printout of the patient-specific bar code in order for Medical Records to scan the ICF (or project summary) and Confirmation of Participation form into the patient’s medical record i.e., the Alert section of OACIS.

- If still using the paper version of the Confirmation of Participation form (FMU-9997) ensure it is the most recent version and that the bar code has not been degraded by photocopying. Obtain the patient-specific bar code from Medivisit, print and apply the bar code to the top right hand corner of the Confirmation of Participation form. Staple the Confirmation of Participation Form to the top of a copy of the participant’s signed ICF (or project summary) and bring to Medical Records for scanning into the patient’s medical record i.e., the Alert section of OACIS.

- The fact that the participation will be documented in the medical record must be included in the informed consent form.

- All OACIS MUHC medical records are kept for at least 15 years or longer.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** Additional to the information provided in the participant’s electronic medical record, the Investigator should give each study participant participating in an interventional clinical research study with IP, a wallet-size information card at the beginning of participation that provides the following:

- Study title;
- List of study drug(s), or medical device(s), or procedure(s);
- Emergency contact numbers.

Refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1) and 2 for examples of wallet size cards.

The wallet size cards should be REB approved prior to use.

### 5.4 Informed consent in a retrospective study

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** REB approval is required for a retrospective study. The informed consent requirement must be reviewed and approved by the REB. Where appropriate a waiver of informed consent can be granted by the REB in accordance with [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html).

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** If feasible, participants must give informed consent (as described above). If the REB has provided a waiver for consent (and an “évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée”, [EFVP](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed) form has been submitted to the Privacy Officer as per [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007)), the investigator must obtain MUHC DPS approval when participant’s medical records are used for a retrospective study.

<a id="5-5"></a>

<a id="7-3-research-risks"></a>

### 5.5 Research risk in all studies

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** All human research studies have the potential for participant risk and this risk should be stated in the ICF. In addition to the risk associated with study procedures and investigational products, the following potential risks also apply to non-interventional (as well as interventional) studies and are not limited to:

- Inconveniences related to study visits, procedures or questionnaires;
- Risk to privacy or confidentiality;

- Psychological or emotional stress or discomfort;
- Personal embarrassment or humiliation;
- Economic or social risks that might arise from involvement in the study;
- High risk because of the vulnerability of the participants;
- Influences that may adversely affect the participant in a significant way.

<a id="5-6"></a>

<a id="7-2-non-therapeutic"></a>

### 5.6 Informed consent in non-therapeutic studies

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Non-therapeutic research studies may be conducted with participants, with the consent of an LAR, provided the following conditions are fulfilled:

- The objectives of the study cannot be met by means of a study with participants who can give personal informed consent;
- The foreseeable risks to the participants is low;
- The negative impact on the participant’s well-being is minimized and low
- The study is not prohibited by law and;
- The approval/favourable opinion of the REB is expressly sought on the inclusion of such participants, and the written approval/favourable opinion covers this aspect.

<a id="5-7"></a>

<a id="ongoing-consent"></a><a id="6-1-maintaining-consent"></a>

### 5.7 Ongoing informed consent process

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** Ensure that the participant’s consent to participate in the study remains valid throughout the study by providing ongoing opportunities for the participant to ask questions about the study.

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** Communicate any important new information that becomes available, and that may be relevant to participant’s consent, in a timely manner. This should be documented.

<a id="6-2-icf-amendments"></a><a id="5-7-3"></a>**5.7.3** Revise the ICF (and any other written material) as applicable throughout the course of the study (refer to [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006)), and submit to the REB for approval (refer to [SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026) for ongoing REB submission process). It is recommended that a site have a system in place to ensure control over the re-consenting process, including documenting and tracking all versions of the ICF and all REB approvals as well as tracking which participants have been re-consented. Refer to the ICF Tracker in [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006).

<a id="5-7-4"></a>**5.7.4** According to [Health Canada Guide-0100](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100.html), a trial participant must be re-consented on any REB-approved amended ICF at their earliest visit to the clinical research site, unless there are specific recommendations/requirements from the Sponsor, REB and/or study/department internal SOP, if applicable. Re-consent the study participants affected after written REB approval of the amended ICF is obtained. Document the re-consent process on an Informed Consent Checklist ([Appendix 3](#appendix-3)).

<a id="5-7-5"></a>**5.7.5** Provide a copy of the signed REB-approved amended ICF and any REB-approved revised participant materials to each of the study participants.

<a id="5-7-6"></a>**5.7.6** File the original signed amended ICF, Informed Consent Checklist and any revised patient materials with the study-related source documents (participant research chart or other).

<a id="special-populations"></a>

### 5.8 Participants who are minors or adults that are incompetent to provide informed consent

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** The age of consent (full age) to participate in research in the Province of Quebec is 18 years of age. In the case of a minor, consent to participation in a research study may be given by the person having parental authority or the tutor ([CCQ, a.21](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:21)).

<a id="5-8-2"></a>**5.8.2** A minor that is 14 years of age or more can consent to participate in research that presents minimal risk, as defined by the [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html), only if the REB agrees that the research presents minimal risk and that it is acceptable. Otherwise the consent must be obtained by the person having parental authority or the tutor ([CCQ, a.21](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:21)).

<a id="5-8-3"></a>**5.8.3** A Legally Appointed Representative (LAR) may consent on behalf of participants who are unconscious, who are severely ill, cognitively impaired, minors or elderly that cannot provide informed consent. Under special emergency situations, when a person of full age suddenly becomes incapable of consent and the research does not permit, for lack of time, the designation of a LAR, consent may be given by the person authorized to give consent to any care the person requires; it is incumbent upon the competent REB to determine, when examining the research project, whether the research meets this condition (CCQ, a.21). If prior consent of the participant or his LAR is not possible, enrollment of the participant should require measures described in the protocol or other document approved by the REB.

<a id="7-1-assent"></a><a id="5-8-4"></a>**5.8.4** Obtaining assent from minors and incompetent adults for research participation is equivalent to the process of informed consent for competent adults and requires documentation of the informed consent process. In determining capacity to provide assent, a child’s maturity, and psychological status should be considered.

<a id="5-8-5"></a>**5.8.5** Inform the participant to the extent compatible with his or her understanding. If capable, the participant should also sign and date the ICF/Assent.

<a id="5-8-6"></a>**5.8.6** For participants whose participation is ongoing, obtain the legally effective signed consent from the participant as soon as possible after reaching the age of 18 years or if an incompetent adult recovers the capacity to give consent e.g., regains consciousness.

<a id="5-8-7"></a>**5.8.7** Distribute and file the ICF/Assent, Informed Consent Process Checklist and any patient-facing documentation, as described in section [5.1](#5-1).

<a id="5-9"></a>

### 5.9 Participants or LAR unable to read

<a id="5-9-1"></a>**5.9.1** If a participant or LAR is unable to read, an impartial witness must be present during the entire informed consent discussion.

<a id="5-9-2"></a>**5.9.2** Obtain verbal consent from the participant or LAR, after the ICF or any other written information is read and explained to the participant.

<a id="5-9-3"></a>**5.9.3** Obtain dated signatures from the participant (if capable) or LAR, and the impartial witness on the ICF, where applicable. The signature of the impartial witness attests that the information was accurately explained to, and understood by, the participant or LAR, and that informed consent was freely given by the participant or LAR.

<a id="5-9-4"></a>**5.9.4** Distribute and file documentation, as described in section [5.1](#5-1).

<a id="5-10"></a>

### 5.10 Participants unable to speak English/French

<a id="5-10-1"></a>**5.10.1** The requirement for free and informed consent should not disqualify research participants who are not proficient in either French or English from the opportunity to participate in research.

<a id="5-10-2"></a>**5.10.2** If the participant does not speak English or French (where applicable), the informed consent discussion must take place in the participant’s first/preferred language, using an impartial qualified interpreter/translator.

<a id="5-10-3"></a>**5.10.3** Produce a certified translation of the ICF in the language of the prospective participant, if possible.

<a id="5-10-4"></a>**5.10.4** Obtain the dated signatures of both the participant and the interpreter/translator on the REB-approved ICF. By signing the ICF, the interpreter/translator attests that the information in the ICF and any other written information was accurately explained to, and apparently understood by, the participant, and that informed consent was volunteered by the participant.

<a id="5-10-5"></a>**5.10.5** Distribute and file documentation, as described in section [5.1](#5-1).

<a id="5-11"></a>

### 5.11 Research directives in a legal document (will or power of attorney)

<a id="5-11-1"></a>**5.11.1** Where individuals have signed a legal document indicating their preferences about future participation in research in the event that they lose capacity or upon death, researchers and authorized third parties should be guided by these directives during the consent process.

<a id="5-12"></a>

<a id="documenting-consent"></a>

### 5.12 Documenting the informed consent process

<a id="5-12-1"></a>**5.12.1** Record evidence of the informed consent process in the source documentation using the Informed Consent Process Checklist confirming:

- The participant’s comprehension of the material reviewed;
- The participant having been given ample opportunity to read the informed consent form and to decide whether or not to participate in the research study;
- Adequate time having been given for all questions about the research study to be answered to the satisfaction of the participant;
- Informed consent having been obtained prior to initiating any study-related procedures and;
- Any other relevant information involving the process of informed consent.

Refer to [Appendix 3](#appendix-3) Informed Consent Process Checklist for complete documented capture of the informed consent process.

<a id="5-12-2"></a>**5.12.2** Best practice is to track the ICFs signed by each participant to ensure participant informed consent and re-consent in a timely fashion. Refer to [Appendix 4](#appendix-4).
