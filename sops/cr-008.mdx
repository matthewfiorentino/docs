# Informed Consent Process

Approved by Gilbert Tordjman, Chief Operating and Development Officer, RI-MUHC.

## 1 · Purpose and scope

This SOP describes how to obtain and document initial and ongoing informed consent and assent for human research studies at the MUHC/RI-MUHC. It covers written and electronic ICF processes, verbal consent, the roles of the Legally Appointed Representative (LAR) and impartial witness, translator requirements, and special populations including minors and incompetent adults.

It applies to all members of the research community — employees, investigators, physicians, coordinators, consultants, students, and volunteers — involved in preparing for, conducting, or documenting the informed consent process for any human research study at the MUHC/RI-MUHC. This SOP also covers the requirement to notify MUHC Medical Records when participants are enrolled in studies involving investigational products (drugs, medical devices, or natural health products).

---

## 2 · Responsibilities

The **Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator** is responsible for providing the study-specific master ICF to sites for local customization and REB submission. See SOP-CR-006 for ICF preparation requirements.

The **Sponsor-Investigator or QI/PI** is responsible for ensuring that the informed consent process meets all applicable regulatory requirements — ICH-GCP, ISO-GCP, TCPS2, Sponsor, and local REB requirements. Any part of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained team members, but the QI/PI retains ultimate accountability.

## 3 · Standard consent process

See Appendix 3 (Informed Consent Process Checklist) for the complete documentation record.

### 3.1 Determining requirements before meeting the participant

The person obtaining informed consent should be a qualified person who knows the study protocol well and is available to explain it to the participant in simple lay terms. This person should not be the participant's treating physician or other person with a long-standing relationship with the participant, as this may be perceived as placing undue influence on the participant. However, if the treating physician is considered the best person to obtain consent, they must explain to the REB why it is necessary. If the REB does approve this, the physician must disclose their dual role to the participant. _(FRQS, Part 2, 11)_

Before the consent discussion, confirm the following:

- The person obtaining consent must be qualified by education, experience, and documented training on SOP-CR-006, this SOP, and the study protocol; knowledgeable in the study procedures; and delegated this task by the QI/PI on the Task Delegation Log.
- The ICF and all participant-facing materials are the most recently REB-approved versions. _Check every version number before each use — do not rely on cached copies._
- Whether a **Legally Appointed Representative (LAR)** is required: if the participant is a minor or an incompetent adult, an LAR must sign. If an LAR is involved, the ICF must include a signature line for the LAR and, if capable, for the minor or incompetent adult (assent). Document whether the participant is not deemed competent to consent, and document the LAR's name and relationship to the participant. _See the MUHC REB webpage for all ICF templates._
- Whether an **impartial translator** is required: if the participant or LAR does not speak English or French, the ICF must include a translator signature line.
- Whether an **impartial witness** is required: if the REB determines that an impartial witness is required (e.g. if the participant or LAR in a clinical trial cannot read and must provide verbal consent), the witness must be present throughout the entire informed consent process and sign and date the ICF.
- Prepare the Informed Consent Process Checklist (Appendix 3).

### 3.2 Conducting the consent discussion

Review the ICF with the participant or LAR, preferably in an environment that is private and free of distractions or interruptions — at the clinical research site or virtually (see §4 for remote consent). Fully inform the participant or LAR of all essential elements of the research in plain, non-technical language.

- Give the participant or LAR ample time to read the ICF and assess their ability to read English or French. They may take the ICF home to consult with family, a caregiver, or a trusted person. _If taking the paper ICF home, instruct them to return to the clinic for a dated signature in the presence of study staff._
- Discuss all study risks with the participant or LAR, including **reproductive risks** where relevant to the study — the risk of becoming pregnant and risk to the unborn child — and the importance of using approved contraceptives. _If not applicable, document the reason on the Informed Consent Process Checklist (Appendix 3)._
- Encourage questions. Assess the participant's understanding by asking **study-specific questions** — document both the questions asked and the participant's answers in the study source files.
- Inform the participant that participation is voluntary and that they may withdraw at any time. Where applicable, inform them that withdrawal may include withdrawal of their data and biological materials, and explain any circumstances that prevent this. _(TCPS2)_
- Do not coerce or unduly influence an individual to participate, or to continue to participate in the study. _(TCPS2)_
- When recruitment raises concerns (e.g. relatives of team members), put special measures in place to protect autonomous decision-making, confidentiality, and research integrity. Contact the REB for case-specific guidance.

### 3.3 Obtaining signatures and filing

<Warning>
**Hard rule — signatures before screening:** All required signatures must be obtained **before** conducting any study-related screening procedure and before enrolling the participant. No exceptions. The consent obtainer signs and dates after reviewing the completed ICF for all required fields, confirming the discussion took place.
</Warning>

- Have the participant or LAR sign and date the ICF. If verbal consent is REB-approved and a witness is required, the witness must also sign — but a written ICF copy must still be provided to the participant.
- Review the ICF for completeness — all required fields, dates, and signatures. Sign and date the ICF and the Informed Consent Process Checklist (Appendix 3) as the person who conducted the discussion and obtained consent. The **Investigator must also sign and date the Checklist separately**. Obtain any other required initials or signatures (e.g. QI/PI initials on the ICF if required by the Sponsor).
- Provide the participant or LAR with a completed and signed copy of the ICF and all other REB-approved written information reviewed during the discussion.
- For clinical trials: if not already asked for on the ICF, request the participant's or LAR's permission to notify the participant's family physician about their participation in the research. If the participant either does not have a family physician or does not wish them to be notified, document this accordingly on the ICF or the Informed Consent Process Checklist (Appendix 3).
- File the original signed ICF with the study-related essential documents. Complete Appendix 3 and file it with the ICF. The person who obtained consent must complete the checklist.
- Document any participant who declines on the study Screening Log. → SOP-CR-009

<Note>
**Electronic signatures — compliance requirements:** Electronic consent may be used if the software is fully validated and compliant with local, provincial, regulatory, and REB requirements. A compliant eSignature must include: the signer's printed name · date and time stamp · unique user ID code · digital adopted signature · signing reason · audit trail. **(Health Canada GUI-0100; FDA 21 CFR Part 11; SOP-CR-015)**
</Note>

## 4 · Remote / electronic consent (eICF)

Remote consent using an electronic ICF (eICF) is permitted where the software is validated and compliant. The process must be documented in a written Working Instruction (WI) before use.

**The WI must describe:**

- How the eICF will be explained and discussed with the participant — including any security questions used to confirm identity
- How additional questions will be used to educate and assess participant understanding during the discussion
- Whether a paper copy option is available and how it will be provided

**Before using an eICF, the site must ensure:**

- The software is fully validated and compliant with applicable electronic record regulations
- Documented procedures and appropriate user training are in place
- All signed eICFs can be stored and retrieved securely, protecting participant confidentiality for the required retention period — **15 years for Health Canada-regulated studies**
- The eICF can capture at least two signatures (participant and consent obtainer) and can produce a signed hard copy for the participant
- The eICF technology can confirm an individual's identity

<Warning>
**REB submission required before use:** All eICFs, amendments or addenda, participant-facing materials, and the written Remote Informed Consent Process must be submitted to and acknowledged by the REB **before** use. Consideration must be given to whether the population being recruited could be impeded from providing consent by the electronic method chosen.
</Warning>

Complete the Informed Consent Process Checklist (Appendix 3) for all eICF-obtained consents.

## 5 · IP studies — Medical Records notification

When a participant begins an interventional clinical research study involving an investigational product (drug, natural health product, or medical device), the following must be filed in the **Alert section (chart level)** of the participant's OACIS electronic medical record at MUHC Medical Records Services:

- **MUHC Form FMU-9997** — Confirmation of Participation in Research Form. Use the electronic version in OWord (enter code FMU-9997). Apply a patient-specific bar code from Medivisit. _If using the paper version, ensure it is the most recent version and the bar code has not been degraded by photocopying._
- **A copy of the paper ICF signed by the participant and person obtaining consent (via wet signature)** (or a project summary if required by the REB) — hand-delivered to Medical Records with a Medivisit printout of the patient-specific bar code for scanning into OACIS.

<Warning>
**Why this matters — patient safety:** Filing in the OACIS Alert section ensures that any caregiver providing emergency or other care is aware of the participant's study enrolment and has access to medically relevant information, including the QI/PI contact details. **The ICF must state that study participation will be documented in the medical record.** OACIS MUHC medical records are retained for a minimum of 15 years.
</Warning>

Additionally, provide each participant with an **REB-approved wallet-size information card** at the beginning of their participation. The card must include: the study title · list of study drugs, devices, or procedures · emergency contact numbers. See Appendices 1 and 2 for templates (device and drug).

## 6 · Ongoing consent and re-consent

Consent is not a one-time event — it must remain valid throughout the study.

### 6.1 Maintaining ongoing consent

- Provide participants with ongoing opportunities to ask questions throughout the study.
- Communicate any important new information that may be relevant to a participant's continued consent in a timely manner. Document this communication.
- It is recommended to maintain a system to track ICF versions and re-consent status — see the ICF Tracker in SOP-CR-006 and Appendix 4 (Participant ICF Tracking Log).

### 6.2 ICF amendments and re-consent

<Warning>
**Re-consent timing:** Participants affected by an amended ICF must be re-consented at their **earliest visit to the clinical research site** after written REB approval of the amended ICF is obtained — unless the Sponsor, REB, or an applicable internal SOP specifies a different requirement. Do not re-consent before written REB approval is in hand.
</Warning>

- Revise the ICF as applicable throughout the study and submit revised ICFs and participant materials to the REB for approval. (→ SOP-CR-006, SOP-CR-026)
- Document the re-consent process on an Informed Consent Checklist (Appendix 3).
- Provide each participant with a copy of the signed, REB-approved amended ICF and any revised participant materials.
- File the original signed amended ICF, the Consent Checklist, and all revised participant materials with the study source documents.

## 7 · Special populations

| Situation | Who signs / requirement | Key rule / citation |
|---|---|---|
| Minor < 14 years | Person with parental authority or tutor signs. Minor signs assent if capable. | CCQ art. 21. Age of majority in Quebec = 18. |
| Minor 14–17 years, minimal risk | Minor may consent alone if REB agrees the study presents minimal risk (TCPS2 definition). Otherwise parental authority required. | CCQ art. 21. REB must expressly approve. |
| Incompetent adult (cognitively impaired, unconscious) | LAR signs. Participant signs assent portion if capable. | CCQ art. 21. Document LAR name and relationship. |
| Emergency — adult suddenly incapable, no time for LAR | Person authorized to consent to care may consent. REB must have determined in advance that the study meets this condition. | CCQ art. 21. Protocol or other REB-approved document must describe measures. |
| Ongoing participation — minor reaches 18 / incompetent adult recovers capacity | Obtain legally effective signed consent from the participant directly as soon as possible after reaching majority or recovering capacity. | File as described in §3.3. |
| Participant / LAR cannot read | Impartial witness must be present throughout. Read and explain the ICF. Obtain dated signatures from participant (if capable) or LAR and the witness. | Witness attests that the information was accurately explained and understood, and consent was freely given. |
| Participant does not speak English or French | Use an impartial qualified interpreter/translator. Produce a certified ICF translation if possible. Obtain dated signatures from participant and translator on the REB-approved ICF. | Translator's signature attests the ICF was accurately explained and apparently understood. Non-EN/FR participants cannot be excluded solely on language grounds. (TCPS2) |
| Legal directive (will / power of attorney) | Researchers and authorized third parties must be guided by the research directives in the legal document during the consent process. | Applies where individual has previously documented preferences about future research participation. |

### 7.1 Assent — minors and incompetent adults

Obtaining assent from minors and incompetent adults is equivalent to the process of informed consent for competent adults and requires the same documentation of the informed consent process. In determining capacity to provide assent, the child's maturity and psychological status should be considered. Inform the participant to the extent compatible with their understanding. If capable, the participant should also sign and date the ICF or Assent document.

Under emergency situations where a person of full age suddenly becomes incapable of consent and the research does not permit, for lack of time, the designation of a LAR, it is incumbent upon the competent REB to determine — when examining the research project — whether the research meets this condition (CCQ, a.21). If prior consent of the participant or LAR is not possible, enrolment of the participant must follow the measures described in the protocol or other document approved by the REB.

### 7.2 Non-therapeutic research

Non-therapeutic studies may be conducted with LAR consent if all of the following conditions are met:

- Study objectives cannot be achieved with participants who can personally consent
- Foreseeable risks are low
- The negative impact on participant well-being is minimized and low
- The study is not prohibited by law
- The approval or favourable opinion of the REB is expressly sought on the inclusion of such participants, and the written approval or favourable opinion covers this aspect

### 7.3 Research risks — all study types

All human research studies carry potential participant risk, which must be stated in the ICF. In addition to risks associated with study procedures and investigational products, the following potential risks apply to non-interventional studies as well:

- Inconveniences related to study visits, procedures, or questionnaires
- Risk to privacy or confidentiality
- Psychological or emotional stress or discomfort
- Personal embarrassment or humiliation
- Economic or social risks from study involvement
- Heightened risk because of the vulnerability of the participants
- Influences that may adversely affect the participant in a significant way

## 8 · Retrospective studies

REB approval is required for retrospective studies. The REB must review and approve the informed consent requirement, including any waiver of consent under TCPS2.

- Where feasible, participants must give informed consent as described above.
- If the REB grants a waiver of consent, and an _évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée_ (EFVP form) has been submitted to the Privacy Officer per SOP-CR-007, the investigator must obtain MUHC DPS (Direction de la protection de la sécurité) approval when participant medical records are accessed for the retrospective study.

---

## 9 · Documenting the informed consent process

Use the Informed Consent Process Checklist (Appendix 3) to record evidence of the consent process in the source documentation. The checklist must confirm:

- The participant's comprehension of the material reviewed
- That the participant or LAR was given ample time to read the ICF and decide whether to participate
- That adequate time was given for all questions to be answered to the participant's satisfaction
- That informed consent was obtained **before** initiating any study-related procedures
- Any other relevant information involving the consent process

<Note>
**Best practice — ICF tracking:** Track ICFs signed by each participant to ensure timely consent and re-consent throughout the study. Use Appendix 4 (Participant ICF Tracking Log). A site-level ICF Tracker is also available via SOP-CR-006.
</Note>
