## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the processes to be followed prior to, during, and following audits and regulatory inspections.

## 2.0 Scope

This procedure concerns the research community (employees, investigators, sponsor-investigators, physicians, management, consultants, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in the study conduct and/or preparing for, hosting and responding to audits and regulatory inspections of a Health Canada regulated clinical trial at the MUHC/RI-MUHC.

Regulated parties that may be subject to a clinical trial inspection by Health Canada include the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, the Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>) and if applicable, a contract Clinical Research Organization (CRO) and/or Site Management Organization (SMO).

Note that Health Canada regulations do not differentiate between a commercial and a non-commercial sponsor (e.g., Sponsor-Investigator) and as such, all ICH/ISO GCP Sponsor responsibilities apply.

Foreign clinical trials (e.g., regulated by the FDA or EMA) that are conducted in Canada are also subject to Health Canada regulations.

Health Canada’s site selection for inspection is on a risk-based approach taking into consideration many criteria and spans all levels of the regulatory process.

In preparation for a Health Canada Inspection, sponsors may conduct on site audits.

The RI-MUHC Quality Assurance department also conducts process audits across MUHC/RI-MUHC sites and may conduct study-specific requested or for-cause investigations or audits.

## 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and QI/<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> are responsible for ensuring that the study is in compliance with, and that all research team members are prepared to receive audits or inspections in compliance with regulatory, International Council on Harmonisation (ICH) Good Clinical Practice (GCP), International Standards Organization (ISO) Good Clinical Practice (GCP), Sponsor, and local requirements, where applicable.

Some parts of audit/inspection preparation may be delegated to appropriately trained human research team members, however responsibility differs between ICH-GCP and ISO-GCP. For drugs, the ultimate responsibility remains with the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, and QI/PI, whereas for medical device studies, responsibility is delegated with study-related tasks.

As per Health Canada POL—0030 (Nov2021), obstruction of inspectors is not tolerated. The Food and Drug Act exists, in part, to provide protection to Canadian study participants. Preventing an inspector from inspecting or knowingly providing false or misleading information (verbally or in writing) is a violation and subject to penalties. If convicted, a person would be liable to pay fines ranging from $500 to $5,000,000 and/or imprisonment of 3 months to 2 years.

## 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

### 5.1 Regulatory background

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** The Clinical Trial Compliance Program (CTCP) of Health Canada’s Regulatory Operations and Enforcement Branch (ROEB) has the authority to conduct inspections at Canadian Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/CRO/SMO and QI/PI sites participating in Health Canada approved studies to assess site compliance with the protocol, regulatory requirements, international guidelines and standard operating procedures (SOPs). The same principles apply to inspections whether conducted on site or conducted remotely. For routine inspections, a Health Canada Notice of Inspection is sent to the site typically, with approximately 4 weeks’ notice, however in the case of a for-cause inspection (where there may be an immediate risk to participants), inspectors may not give any advanced notice when conducting on-site inspections.

- Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/CRO/SMO Inspections conducted by Health Canada are referred to as system-based inspections and allow for the verification of the systems and procedures that describe how the trial is conducted and monitored as well as how data is generated, modified, processed and reported. Systems inspected may include the following (but not limited to) organization and personnel, project management, quality systems, training, Trial Master File (<Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip>)/archiving, regulatory and ethics approvals, contracts and agreements, pharmacovigilance/safety, investigational product management, computer system validation, data management and statistics, monitoring and protocol/GCP compliance and study reports. Sponsor Inspections may trigger inspections at the QI/PI clinical trial site level.

- Clinical Trial Site Inspections at the QI/PI’s site allow for verification of a clinical trial’s <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> regulatory documents, source documents, eCRF transcription, training and task delegation logs, informed consent, medical records, integrity of the data and record keeping, adverse event reporting, drug accountability, drug storage, monitoring activities and adequacy of corrective and preventative action (CAPA) implemented, validation of electronic systems used as well as compliance to the site SOPs and other written processes.

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) may conduct inspections at Canadian sites involved in studies conducted under a US-held Investigational New Drug (IND) or Investigational Device Exemption (IDE) application. In the event of an FDA inspection, the site should receive FDA form #482 “Notice of Inspection”. With this request, both parties will agree on a date that leaves a sufficient period for the research team to prepare for the visit. However, in the case of suspicion of fraud, FDA inspectors can arrive without notice.

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** Investigator sites may undergo audits by Sponsors, inspections by other regulatory authorities or be inspected by Health Canada as a result of delegation/partnership by/with another country’s regulatory authority (e.g., European Medicines Agency, EMA).

<a id="5-1-4"></a>**5.1.4** The QI/PI generally receives advance written notice of routine site audits/inspections however, auditors or inspectors may arrive with minimal (or no) notice especially a ‘for cause’ visit. Immediately inform the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, the research team and in the case of inspections, the Quality Assurance department, as required.

<a id="5-1-5"></a>**5.1.5** Retain documentation of all communications concerning the audit/inspection requests with the essential study documents.

<a id="5-1-6"></a>**5.1.6** The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/CRO/SMO may assist the clinical research team to prepare for QI site regulatory inspections. In many cases, the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator may want to conduct a pre-inspection audit/verification.

<a id="5-1-7"></a>**5.1.7** It is important to note that while drug clinical trials will be inspected for compliance with Health Canada Division 5 regulations and ICH-GCP, medical device clinical trials will be inspected for compliance with medical device regulations SOR/98-282, Part 3 and ISO-14155 GCP.

<a id="5-1-8"></a>**5.1.8** If your site will be hosting a Health Canada Inspection, refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1) Health Canada Inspection Guide for more information and templates you can use to facilitate the inspection process.

<a id="5-2"></a>

### 5.2 Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator audit/verification: routine or for cause

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** Clinical Research Organizations (CROs) and QI/PIs may be audited by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator. A routine audit/verification is carried out by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator to ensure that, the study complies with applicable standards; the data are accurate and of good quality and; participant safety is maintained. If carried out at the beginning of the study, an audit/verification can serve to correct errors before the study is completed; however, it can be carried out at any time, in the course of the study or at the end of the study.

- The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator should appoint monitors independent of the conduct of the human research study. The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator will ensure that the monitors are qualified by training and experience, their qualifications are documented and monitoring activities are carried out according to the Monitoring Plan.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** A ‘for cause’ audit/verification is carried out when there is reason to believe that there are problems with the site’s data, procedures and/or compliance with GCP or regulations. Some of the problems can become apparent because of weak compliance of the site with the protocol, an overly low or high rate of adverse reactions or adverse events compared to other sites, or a high rate of recruitment compared to other sites, among others.

<a id="5-3"></a>

### 5.3 Regulatory inspection: routine or for cause

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** Routine inspections by regulatory authorities (e.g., Health Canada or US FDA) may be carried out as part of a routine surveillance program, or as part of a pre-approval inspection (PAI) for a particular product (usually FDA). The inspection is an official review of documents, facilities, records, and any other resources that are deemed by the authority(ies) to be related to the clinical trial, and that may be located at the site of the trial, at the Sponsor's and/or CRO's facilities, or at other establishments deemed appropriate by the regulatory authority(ies) (e.g., 3rd party laboratories).

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** An investigation or inspection for cause is a specific response to known or suspected non-compliance or misconduct/fraud. Investigations are typically undertaken when there are reasonable grounds to suspect that noncompliance has occurred, and that enforcement measures may be necessary (e.g., product quality complaints, reports from other regulatory authorities, increased reports of adverse reactions).

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** Inspections by a regulatory agency such as Health Canada, the FDA, or another regulatory agency can extend from a few days to a week or more, if necessary or two weeks or more for a remote inspection. An FDA inspection may occur at the time of the New Drug Application (NDA) review.

<a id="5-4"></a>

### 5.4 RI-MUHC Quality Assurance audits

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** As part of Continuous Process Improvement (CPI) <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's Quality Assurance department, which checks investigators' credentials and training, audits research and maintains the SOPs. In GCP, quality assurance is the planned, systematic actions that ensure a trial complies with GCP." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> conducts process audits across the RI-MUHC/MUHC in order to assess the need for process improvement, facilitating SOP revisions and creation of tools/templates for the human research community as well as the resulting improved SOP training.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** QA may also conduct for-cause audits or investigations when there is reported non-compliance. The resulting audit report is reported to RI-MUHC upper management. Refer to [SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026) for a CAPA form and reporting major or critical deviations to QA.

<a id="5-4-3"></a>**5.4.3** Additionally, a QI/PI may request that an audit be conducted by QA.

<a id="5-5"></a>

### 5.5 Preparation for an audit/ verification or inspection

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** Typically, the notification letter and/or pre-inspection package will be sent to the attention of the QI. Ensure that these documents are shared with appropriate research team members. Verify the following, as appropriate (i.e., if not covered in the notification letter):

- Purpose of the audit/inspection;
- Whether the audit/inspection is to be in-person, virtual or a hybrid;
- Documents requested pre-audit/inspection;
- Audit/inspection plan and procedures;
- Auditor’s/Inspector’s name(s);
- Expected duration of the audit/inspection;
- Proposed agenda (e.g., document access, system access, tour of facilities);
- List of study personnel required during the audit/inspection;

- Audit/inspection reporting: documented observations and findings of the auditor(s);
- Follow-up requirements and;
- Procedures for non-compliance.

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** Confirm the agreed date(s) and time of the audit/inspection opening meeting and exit interview with the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and/or regulatory agency.

<a id="5-5-3"></a>**5.5.3** Arrange for a pre-audit/inspection meeting with the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, to review audit/inspection procedures and determine study personnel required during the audit/inspection.

<a id="5-5-4"></a>**5.5.4** Contact the RI-MUHC Quality Assurance department (QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) for assistance with the preparation and hosting of the Health Canada inspection, as required. The QI should promptly inform the RI-MUHC Quality Assurance department within 24 hours of the inspection notice. This prompt request will ensure that the QA department has enough time to ensure the site is familiar with [Appendix 1](#appendix-1), discuss/review the pertinent study documentation requested by the inspector and that the site has enough time to put corrective action in place prior to the inspection. Additionally, QA is available throughout the inspection and can assist with the Corrective and Preventative Action (CAPA) response to the Health Canada Inspection report, if required.

<a id="5-5-5"></a>**5.5.5** Determine the areas/facilities that the auditor/inspector will want to visit or tour, and ensure appropriate personnel are available to accompany the auditor/inspector.

<a id="5-5-6"></a>**5.5.6** Identify representatives (as potential interviewees) involved in the project and departments likely to be covered during the inspection.

<a id="5-5-7"></a>**5.5.7** Notify all relevant research team members and potential interviewees of the audit/inspection (e.g., investigator, sub-investigators, study coordinator(s), study nurse(s), pharmacist, laboratory technicians, other technical personnel, <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> members).

<a id="5-5-8"></a>**5.5.8** Review audit/inspection procedures with study personnel, and conduct a thorough review of the study procedures, study protocol, case report forms (CRFs), source data, and study documentation.

<a id="5-5-9"></a>**5.5.9** Ensure that all study files are up-to-date and available for review, such as:

- Written agreements with Sponsors and/or 3rd parties;
- Validation of any electronic systems used at the site level (e.g., electronic data capture, electronic inform consent, electronic participant diaries);
- Investigator Site File (ISF) essential documentation (regulatory binders or files);

- Case Report Forms (CRFs);
- All participant-specific source documentation to verify data reported in <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip>;
- Signed and dated Informed Consent Forms (ICFs) and documentation of the informed consent process for all participants;
- All REB approval letters (Initial, Continuous and Ongoing);
- CVs, licenses and training of research team members;
- Printed medical records and other source documents for all protocol procedures with documentation of medical oversight (e.g., all test results initialed and dated by QI/PI), including documentation of <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip>/MDI reporting;
- All study logs (Training, Task Delegation, Screening, Enrolment, Participant ID, <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> Accountability, <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">AE</Tooltip>, SAE, MDI, Biological Samples, Equipment Inventory, Protocol Deviation, as required);
- Monitoring log and QI/PI initialed and dated monitoring letters;
- QI/PI initialed and dated safety reports and a working instruction for review and distribution of <Tooltip tip="A serious adverse reaction to a drug or natural health product whose nature, severity or frequency the Investigator's Brochure or product label does not anticipate; the sponsor reports it to Health Canada, and the site reports it to the REB when it meets the REB's criteria." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip> reports (if applicable);
- Pharmacy Files (management of IP), if applicable, and;
- any other document related to the study.

<a id="5-5-10"></a>**5.5.10** Ensure that the information contained in all documents is complete, up-to-date, and in agreement with the raw data or source documents. Ensure that retrospective (late) entries or corrections are dated and initialed by the person who made the entry or the correction using the current date with reason and indicated “late entry” (no back dating).

<a id="5-5-11"></a>**5.5.11** Prepare the logistical aspects of this visit, for example:

- Reserve a confidential limited access office/working space for the visit (with washroom access and away from patient care areas or files of other studies), and ensure that wifi and required equipment is available. If applicable, block interior window views with paper to ensure confidentiality.
- Arrange the use of computer(s) and access to source documents and electronic CRF entries, if applicable to the study. It is recommended to have a team member be available to assist and even access required electronic information for an inspector.

- For virtual audits or inspections, come to an agreement with regards to the virtual meeting and a secure file sharing platform to be used.
- In the case of inspections, it is advisable for clinical research site personnel, Monitor, and QA personnel to be available to assist inspectors with the conduct of the inspection (e.g., to locate and view electronic files, document certified copies required). Ensure that all relevant members are notified. Note that Health Canada inspectors do not sign confidentiality agreements as this is already implied in their job functions and therefore will not typically want their own access to electronic medical records (e.g., [OACIS](/apps/oacis)). They will expect a team member to access information for them.

- Ensure an interpreter will be available for the inspector, if required.

- Review facilities likely to be inspected; determine any potential issues and put remedial action in place, where possible.

- Ensure the availability and access to all study documentation likely to be requested by the inspector.

- Ensure de-identified certified copies can be provided (have a grease pencil e.g., china marker), “Certified Copy” stamp, a pen to initial and date, and a photocopier). Ensure that no participant personal data will leave the institution.

- Instruct the research team on their responsibilities, best practices on how to handle an inspection, and remind them of available training accordingly. The staff should be informed/reminded that they must only answer questions from the Inspector(s) in relation to their job function or their direct involvement in the study. An optional course is available on [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms), Hosting a Health Canada Inspection and it is highly recommended that the entire study team complete this training prior to hosting an inspection.

- Time permitting; determine if a pre-inspection review of study documentation is required.

- Prepare and coordinate an action plan with the team.

- Confirm that clinical research site personnel, including the QI/PI, will be available for interviews/questions for at least part of the visit.
- Ensure that other departments involved in the study (REB, pharmacy, laboratory, IT/[REDCap](/apps/redcap) data management, if applicable) are available to answer questions, if needed.

- Obtain and review documents requested by the inspector, prepare answers to the inspector’s questions, brief staff prior to being interviewed by the inspector.

- Ensure that a tracking process will be employed for certified copies of study documents provided to the inspector. In the case of an on-site inspection the QA department is available to track this for the team, if required. Retain this documentation with the study essential documents.

- Ensure that arrangements will be made to translate documents, or to facilitate communication during the visit (if applicable).

<a id="5-6"></a>

### 5.6 Additional preparation for remote audit/inspection

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** In lieu of an in-person inspection visit, HC may proceed with a remote or hybrid inspection. In addition to full study inspections, Health Canada may conduct shorter remote Compliance Readiness Inspections (CRIs) earlier on in the study. In the case of a remote inspection, a checklist of required information/ documentation may be requested by the inspectors prior to the inspection. Health Canada has also begun conducting inspections of medical device studies.

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** The inspectors may communicate which software(s) they would like to be utilized for the conduct of the inspection. Different software may be used for different purposes (e.g., Teams may be used for communication, facility walk-throughs and meetings, however, this software is not considered confidential for study document external sharing).

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** Determine whether document sharing will be on a Health Canada platform or using an MUHC approved platform (e.g., NextCloud).

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** Ensure that the electronic platform(s) that will be utilized for confidential documents is 21 CFR, Part 11 compliant, it is validated and there is documentation to support this. Also, ensure that the electronic platform does not fall under the US Cloud Act.

<a id="5-6-5"></a>**5.6.5** Ensure that the computer used for remote sharing of documents is located in a closed, confidential area.

<a id="5-7"></a>

### 5.7 Facilitate audit or inspection

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** The regulatory inspection visit starts with the inspector’s credentials and an introductory meeting prepared by the regulatory inspector(s).

- Typically, the QI/PI will be asked about his/her experience, past and current research, including numbers of studies, as well as GCP and Health Canada Division 5 training (for drug studies). It is highly recommended to ensure training is up-to-date as per [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003) (e.g., CITI ICH-GCP and Health Canada Division 5 training in addition to RI-MUHC SOP training for drug studies).

- Note that for medical device study inspections, the expectation is that the QI and team have been trained on the basic principles of [Medical Device Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/) (refer to the Medical Device course on TalentLMS) as well as self-training on the ISO 14155 GCP document.

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** The opening meeting is followed by an evaluation of the conduct of the study, usually over a 5-day period. The auditors/inspectors will determine whom they would like to meet for interview, often on an as-needed basis. The following questions could be asked:

- What are the individual research staff roles?
- How has staff training been documented?
- Are delegations appropriate and clearly documented?
- Is there sufficient documented medical oversight?
- What written process is in place for distribution of SUSAR/MDI reports?
- How are study document versions being tracked?
- What equipment or material is being used and how is it maintained?
- Who collects and enters the data?
- Who monitors/quality controls the CRF entries?
- Is there active communication with the Sponsor/CRO?

- Do standard operating procedures and working instructions exist?

<a id="5-7-3"></a>**5.7.3** Ensure to take detailed notes throughout the audit/inspection which will guide your team with CAPA. It is recommended to appoint a scribe to take notes throughout the inspection.

<a id="5-7-4"></a>**5.7.4** Delegate a team member to act as runner during the inspection, collecting documents requested by the inspector.

<a id="5-7-5"></a>**5.7.5** If the inspector asks to see patient electronic medical records (e.g., OACIS), do not remain logged in and leave the inspector alone, as the inspector may have access to confidential information. Note that unlike monitors who sign a confidentiality agreement and are provided restricted access, inspectors will not have (nor want) their own access.

<a id="5-7-6"></a>**5.7.6** Ensure that no participant personal data (original or copy) will leave the institution to the extent permitted by law, unless required by authorized personnel, e.g., regulatory authorities. Black out information on audit/inspection copies, as required.

<a id="5-7-7"></a>**5.7.7** Ensure tracking of the de-identified certified copies prior to providing these documents to the inspector (e.g., create a log listing all certified copies). Refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1).

<a id="5-7-8"></a>**5.7.8** Accompany the inspector to other areas of the facility, including directing them to the washroom.

<a id="5-7-9"></a>**5.7.9** During an audit/inspection, never argue or have controversial conversations in the presence of the inspector. Step out of the room to discuss, come to a consensus and return to provide an answer to the inspector.

<a id="5-7-10"></a>**5.7.10** The auditor/inspector may want to hold an end of day meeting with key people on each of the inspection days, if deemed necessary. It is important to take notes and to quickly respond to all requests stemming from the findings made at the time of the visit. The more observations that are resolved during the visit, the less the report content and the less the amount of CAPA required in your written response afterwards.

<a id="5-7-11"></a>**5.7.11** On the final audit/inspection day, the auditor/inspector will then conduct an exit meeting, usually with the Sponsor, CRO (if applicable), RI-MUHC QA (in the case of an inspection) and the site, providing a summary of their observations and deficiencies. This meeting provides the site with the opportunity to ask for clarifications before the audit/inspection report is finalized.

### 5.8 Online Health Canada report of initial inspection deficiencies

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** Shortly after the regulatory inspection and before the Final Inspection Exit Notice is issued, Health Canada will post an initial report on the Initial Inspection Deficiencies (IID) noted during their inspections.

<a id="5-8-2"></a>**5.8.2** After the Exit Notice is issued, Health Canada will post the Inspection Report Card (IRC) to summarize the observations as well as the rating. These reports can be found on the Drug and health products inspections database (DHPID).

<a id="5-9"></a>

### 5.9 Final Health Canada inspection exit notice

<a id="5-9-1"></a>**5.9.1** Inspectors will note all deficiencies and/or deviations from regulations as observations in the Final Inspection Exit Notice. For remote CRI’s, observations may be communicated verbally only.

<a id="5-9-2"></a>**5.9.2** An observation is a deficiency or deviation from the regulations. For each observation made, a risk classification, based on the severity of the deviation, is assigned by the inspector. The observation can be classified as “critical” (risk 1), “major” (risk 2) or “minor” (risk 3).

<a id="5-9-3"></a>**5.9.3** Once the inspector has rated all observations, the overall information will be assessed in order to assign an overall rating of “C” or “NC”.

- A “C” (Compliant) rating indicates that the regulated party was in compliance with the Food and Drugs Act and its associated Division 5 regulations. This does not mean that there are no observations or corrective actions required.

- An “NC” (Non-compliant) rating indicates that the regulated party has not demonstrated that study conduct or documentation was in compliance with regulations. An NC rating could result in immediate suspension or “intent to suspend” the authorization to sell or import a drug for the purposes of a clinical trial (entire trial or at the clinical trial site).

<a id="5-9-4"></a>**5.9.4** The Final Inspection Exit Notice is submitted to the Sponsor/ Sponsor-Investigator (and CRO, if applicable) who then communicates the report to the site(s).

<a id="5-9-5"></a>**5.9.5** The site should forward a copy of the inspection report to the RI-MUHC QA department and the MUHC REB.

<a id="5-9-6"></a>**5.9.6** With the help of the Sponsor/CRO and the QA department, the site should address the deficiencies by describing the intended corrective actions, including projected timeframe for completion. Note: If significant non-compliance is discovered, the Sponsor or Sponsor/Investigator should perform a root analysis and implement corrective and preventive action (CAPA) and should inform the REB, regulatory authorities of a serious breach of protocol or GCP if required by applicable law or regulations.

<a id="5-9-7"></a>**5.9.7** Prepare a site response document. If a response form is not provided by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, refer to [Appendix 2](#appendix-2), Health Canada Inspection Response form.

<a id="5-9-8"></a>**5.9.8** Submit the site response to the Sponsor/CRO, or regulatory agency, within the requested time period, if applicable.

<a id="5-9-9"></a>**5.9.9** Ensure that any additional CAPA are completed, as promised. Follow up, as required.

<a id="5-9-10"></a>**5.9.10** The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator will then respond to all Inspection Exit Notice observations in writing to Health Canada (Sponsor/CRO and site observations) and the observations remain open until all requests have been met. Request to be copied on the final response from the Sponsor/CRO.

<a id="5-9-11"></a>**5.9.11** For observations cited during a remote CRI, best practice is to document all observations and implement CAPA to ensure compliance prior to a subsequent full inspection.

<a id="5-10"></a>

### 5.10 Repeat inspection observations

<a id="5-10-1"></a>**5.10.1** In the case of re-inspection, a repeat observation may be assigned by the inspector at a higher risk level than indicated on a previous Final Inspection Notice. This can occur if deviations or deficiencies observed during the previous inspection were not adequately addressed or if proposed CAPA was not implemented.

<a id="5-10-2"></a>**5.10.2** Repetition of most major observations reported during a previous inspection would most likely result in an “NC” rating on the current Final Inspection Notice.

<a id="5-10-3"></a>**5.10.3** If an inspector notes that serious or persistent cases of non-compliance are associated with a particular QI/Institution, the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator will be asked to end the QI/Institution’s participation in the study. When participation of a QI/Institution in the study is terminated for reasons of non-compliance, the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator should, in the 15 days following termination, inform the regulatory organizations (article HC C.05.015 of the regulation, ICH 5.202) and the REB that approved the study (ICH 4.12).

<a id="5-10-4"></a>**5.10.4** In cases of repeated disregard for the legislative and regulatory requirements, Health Canada may refer its investigation results to the Public Prosecution Service of Canada, recommending prosecution in relation to offences under the Act and Criminal Code of Canada where applicable.
