Accurate, complete, and retrievable records are the foundation of clinical research integrity. These two SOPs define how you collect and manage clinical trial data, and how you organize, maintain, and ultimately archive the documents that make up your Investigator Site File and Trial Master File. SOP-CR-014 applies to Levels I–IV; SOP-CR-015 applies to all levels, including Level V.

<Warning>
**Controlled documents — always use the online version.** Do not rely on downloaded or printed copies. Your first reading of each SOP must be completed through the SOP Reader on TalentLMS to count toward your training record.
</Warning>

## ALCOA+ data integrity principles

Every entry you make in a case report form, research database, or paper log must meet the ALCOA+ standard. These principles apply equally to paper and electronic records.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Attributable">
    It must be clear who created or changed an entry and when. Sign and date all entries. In electronic systems, your login credentials serve as your signature.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Legible">
    Records must be readable now and in the future. Never use correction fluid (white-out). Cross out errors with a single line, initial, date, and provide a reason.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Contemporaneous">
    Record data at the time observations are made — not reconstructed later from memory. Late entries must be clearly identified as such.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Original">
    The first record of data (source document) must be preserved. Certified copies are acceptable when originals cannot be retained, but the copy must be verified.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Accurate">
    Entries must reflect what actually occurred. Do not round, estimate, or summarize when exact values are required by the protocol.
  </Card>
  <Card title="+ Complete, Consistent, Enduring, Available">
    Data must be complete (no missing fields without explanation), consistent across documents, stored durably for the required retention period, and accessible to inspectors and monitors.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## SOPs in this group

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="SOP-CR-014 — Clinical Data Management · v01-May-2025 · Levels I–IV">
    This SOP defines the standards for collecting, entering, verifying, correcting, and querying clinical trial data at the RI-MUHC site. It covers source documents, case report forms (CRFs), electronic data capture (EDC), data correction procedures, query management, and the use of Notes to File for explaining discrepancies or late entries.

    **Key points:**
    - Source documents are the primary record of participant data. All CRF entries must be traceable back to a source document. "Source data" includes original paper records, electronic medical records (OACIS), lab reports, imaging, and other primary observations.
    - All data corrections must follow ALCOA+ principles: cross out the original entry, enter the correct value, initial and date, and provide a brief reason. Do not obliterate or overwrite original entries.
    - A **Note to File** must be used to document any clarification, explanation, or late entry that cannot be recorded directly on the source or CRF. Use Appendix 1 (electronic template) and file in the ISF.
    - Medical and medication histories must be documented using the dedicated logs (Appendices 2–4) unless the Sponsor provides equivalent CRF pages. Both the history log and the Sponsor CRF may be required; consult the protocol.
    - Query resolution must be documented promptly. Outstanding data queries are a common monitoring finding; review your EDC query dashboard regularly.

  <Accordion title="SOP-CR-015 — Investigator Site Files and Essential Documents · v19-Jun-2023 · Levels I–V">
    This SOP defines the essential documents that must be collected and maintained in the Investigator Site File (ISF) at the RI-MUHC site and, for Sponsor-Investigator studies, in the Trial Master File (TMF). It specifies the filing structure, content requirements at each stage of the study lifecycle (before, during, and after), and the rules for archiving and document retention.

    **Key points:**
    - The ISF must be organized according to the structure defined in **Appendix 4** (ISF Filing and Archiving Structure). For Levels III–V studies, use the highlighted version that identifies the subset of documents required for lower-risk studies.
    - Essential documents must be filed promptly — ideally within 48 hours of receipt or generation. A disorganized or incomplete ISF is the most common finding at monitoring visits and inspections.
    - Regulatory retention periods apply from the date of study completion or termination: typically 25 years for clinical trial records under Canadian regulations. Do not destroy any study records without completing an **Essential Document Destruction Request** (Appendix 5) and receiving authorization.
    - For Sponsor-Investigator studies, the **TMF Index** (Appendix 6) defines the full set of documents that must be maintained. Use the highlighted version for Levels III–V studies where applicable.
    - The **Trial Master File Plan** (Appendix 7) describes how the TMF will be organized, where documents will be stored (paper vs. electronic), and who is responsible for each section. Complete and file it at study initiation.

<Tip>
Review your ISF completeness before every monitoring visit. Use the ISF Filing and Archiving Structure (Appendix 4 of SOP-CR-015) as a self-audit checklist. An inspection-ready ISF is one that is complete and current at all times — not one that is assembled at the last minute.
</Tip>
