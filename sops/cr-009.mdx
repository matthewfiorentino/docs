## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the process of recruitment, screening and enrollment of participants for a human research study, within the framework of Good Clinical Practice (GCP) principles, published by the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) and the International Standards Organization (ISO) as well as all other applicable guidelines and regulations.

## 2.0 Scope

This procedure concerns the research community (employees, investigators, physicians, management, consultants, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in conducting, reviewing, and/or approving the recruitment, screening and enrollment processes of human research participants at the MUHC/RI-MUHC.

<a id="responsibilities"></a>

## 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>)/Principal Investigator (<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>) is responsible for:

- Ensuring that the study participant recruitment, screening and enrollment processes meet all of the applicable regulatory, ICH-GCP, ISO-GCP, Sponsor, Health Canada regulations and local requirements including:
  - Ensuring that in addition to the protocol, <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> and other important study documents, any study recruitment tools and advertising for any medium is <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approved prior to use. Public-facing and patient-facing advertisements, must also be approved by MUHC Communications prior to use.

  - Ensuring that informed consent is obtained as per [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) from every participant prior to screening activities (unless an REB waiver for informed consent is obtained and an [EFVP](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed) form is reviewed). Refer to [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008).

  - Ensuring that only participants who meet the inclusion/exclusion criteria of the most recently REB-approved protocol are enrolled into the study.

  - Ensuring that screening and enrollment are captured confidentially on logs using study participant numbers.

  - Ensuring that contact information for participants (and legally appointed representatives, if applicable) is captured on a separate log to facilitate safety updates if necessary.

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator or QI/PI.

## 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

<a id="5-1-developing-a-recruitment-plan"></a>

### 5.1 Developing a recruitment plan (if required)

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** The Sponsor, QI/PI, Clinical Research Coordinator (<Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip>) or delegate develops a study-specific recruitment plan prior to the initiation of a human research study.

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** Select the most appropriate activities related to each step in the recruitment process and describe in the recruitment plan.

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** Take into consideration the most effective strategies for recruitment for each of the potential population groups along the recruitment process: baseline, target, reviewed, identified, approached, screened, and eligible for enrollment.

<a id="5-1-4"></a>**5.1.4** Create the plan, as follows:

- Identify potential recruitment methods or strategies related to each area of the recruitment process;
- Assess the feasibility of these recruitment methods for each of the populations. Consider potential issues such as:
  - Ethical,
  - Financial,
  - Regulatory,

  - Privacy,
  - Timelines,
  - Clinic staffing/resources,
  - Treatment,
  - Trial design,
  - Health Emergency restrictions (if applicable).

<a id="5-1-5"></a>**5.1.5** Select the most suitable recruitment activities. Use several recruitment methods throughout the study (if possible), rather than depending on one single method e.g., Hospital TV monitors, websites, social media, posters, flyers, radio and newspapers.

<a id="5-1-6"></a>**5.1.6** Formalize and document the recruitment plan and strategies, including timelines.

<a id="5-1-7"></a>**5.1.7** Develop recruitment materials and submit advertisements in any medium (including text for emails or social media) to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, the MUHC Research Ethics Board (REB) for approval and then to MUHC Communications for approval. Refer to [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007).

<a id="5-1-8"></a>**5.1.8** To publicize a clinical trial (internally and/or externally) in order to enroll participants, follow the requirements below:

- The ad includes the RI-MUHC logo (or the MUHC logo if the PI/Sub-I is based at the MUHC without being a member of the RI-MUHC);
- Ensure correct use of the logo;
- Ensure bilingualism (French and English).

- Ensure correct use of language – (no grammar and spelling issues);
- Ensure language quality and message clarity;
- Ensure the text is written in laypersons terms and avoid complex scientific terms as much as possible;
- Ensure the advertisement is efficient and professional in terms of language and visual presentation;
- Full name and title of the QI/PI and affiliation should be specified;
- Full name of the Clinical Research Coordinator together with an MUHC/RI-MUHC phone number must be specified (do not use personal cell phones or personal email addresses such as Hotmail, Yahoo or Gmail);
- Refer to the MUHC Branding and Language guidelines. Click here for link or refer to the MUHC portal.

<a id="5-2"></a>

<a id="5-2-reb-approval-of-recruitment-materials"></a>

### 5.2 Research Ethics Board (REB) approval of recruitment materials

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** As per [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007), submit recruitment materials to the REB via the study submission platform (e.g., [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview)). The REB will review for undue influence or coercive tone of the content.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** Obtain REB approval of any recruitment materials that a patient may see including text for social media and then obtain approval from the MUHC Communications in order to streamline the process.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** File the REB approval of any recruitment materials with essential documents in the Investigator Site File (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>) and implement the recruitment methods once REB approval, MUHC Authorization and the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator’s Go letter/email has been received.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** Submit any amendments/revisions of recruitment materials to the REB as per [SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026). It is important to ensure there are version numbers on your documents and electronic file names. Obtain approvals prior to use, document approval on an REB Approval Tracking log and file in the ISF.

<a id="5-3"></a>

<a id="5-3-muhc-communications-approval"></a>

### 5.3 MUHC Communications approval of recruitment materials

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** After REB approval of recruitment materials, obtain MUHC Communications approval of any recruitment materials that a patient may see.

- Submit the recruitment material (formatted text) in English and French to MUHC Communications at communications@muhc.mcgill.ca.

- Once recruitment materials are approved by the REB and by MUHC Communications (usually within 3 days), this text may be adapted for use in any medium.

<a id="5-4"></a>

<a id="5-4-access-to-participant-medical-records"></a>

### 5.4 Access to participant’s Medical Records

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** As per [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007) and [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008), if patients’ MUHC health records (e.g., [OACIS](/apps/oacis)) will be accessed, the written informed consent for this access should be obtained from each participant or approval should be obtained from the MUHC Director of Professional Services (DPS), via the study submission platform (e.g., Nagano) prior to accessing their MUHC health records. The authorization must be granted for a limited period and may be subject to conditions. It may be revoked at any time if there is reason to believe that the authorized professional is violating the confidentiality of the information obtained or is not complying with the conditions imposed or with generally accepted standards of ethics and scientific integrity.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** If a patient is referred from outside the MUHC patient population (including the JGH), then certified copies of the source documents relevant to the study must be requested and obtained from the external institution and filed in a timely fashion. Ensure to document request and receipt, as there can be significant delays.

<a id="5-4-3"></a>**5.4.3** All participants recruited in an MUHC research study must have an MUHC hospital card.

<a id="5-4-4"></a>**5.4.4** Refer to section [5.10](#5-10) regarding notification of participant’s participation sent to Medical Records and [Appendix 1](#appendix-1) and 2.

<a id="5-5"></a>

<a id="5-5-gcp-required-participant-logs"></a>

### 5.5 GCP-required participant logs

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** The following logs related to the recruitment of participants are part of the essential study documents, (E6 R2 section 8 of the ICH-GCP and Annex E of ISO-14155 GCP):

- Participant Screening Log: This document identifies participants in screening, provides informed consent date and reasons for screen failures (if the Sponsor has not provided a screening log, refer to the template in [Appendix 3](#appendix-3)).

- Participant Enrollment Log: This document lists enrolled participants chronologically by study number and tracks visit dates (if the Sponsor has not provided an enrollment log, refer to the template in [Appendix 4](#appendix-4)).
- Participant Identification Log: In the event of an emergency, this document permits the rapid identification of all participants (and their legally appointed representatives, if applicable), who have taken part in the study, in the event follow-up of a participant is necessary. If the Sponsor has not provided a Participant Identification Log, refer to the template in [Appendix 5](#appendix-5)). Important: This list is confidential. Under no circumstances can it be provided to the Sponsor.

<a id="5-6"></a>

<a id="5-6-participant-screening"></a>

### 5.6 Participant screening

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Create a study Screening Log using the template in [Appendix 3](#appendix-3).

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** As per [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008), obtain participant’s written informed consent prior to starting any protocol screening activities using the latest REB-approved Informed Consent Form (ICF), (unless a written waiver has been obtained from the REB and an EFVP form has been reviewed).

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** Document the participants to be screened using sequential screening numbers in chronological order and record the date that the ICF was signed (or the reason, if it was not signed by the participant) on the Screening Log at the time of participant’s screening.

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** Schedule or perform the study-specific screening procedures in the protocol-specified order and timeline (if applicable).

<a id="5-6-5"></a>**5.6.5** Review the baseline screening test results, as applicable and have the QI/PI initial and date results to document medical oversight.

<a id="5-6-6"></a>**5.6.6** Confirm eligibility of participants with the QI/PI, who is ultimately responsible for the final medical decision on participant eligibility for the study and should sign off on a source document inclusion/exclusion checklist for each participant to confirm QI/PI oversight of eligibility. Eligibility is typically based on many source documents e.g., study-specific test assessments, medical records (e.g., OACIS), pharmacy records, external hospital/pharmacy records.

<a id="5-6-7"></a>**5.6.7** Indicate which participants screen failed along with the reason for screen failure and indicate which participants were enrolled in the study on the Screening Log.

<a id="5-7"></a>

<a id="5-7-participant-enrollment"></a>

### 5.7 Participant enrollment

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** Create a study Participant Enrollment Log from the template in [Appendix 4](#appendix-4).

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** The QI/PI must ensure a participant has met all inclusion criteria, that none of the exclusion criteria is met from the most recently REB-approved protocol and that the participant’s eligibility was documented in source documents (e.g., a signed inclusion/exclusion checklist in each participant’s file).

<a id="5-7-3"></a>**5.7.3** Enroll eligible participants into the study using the protocol-specified enrollment procedures. This may be accomplished using a validated Interactive Web/Voice Response System (IWRS/IVRS). The use of this validated system may be delegated to trained research team members (e.g., to a CRC or to a Pharmacist).

<a id="5-7-4"></a>**5.7.4** Confidentially document the enrollment of the participants on the Enrollment Log using approved study participant identifiers at the time of each participant enrollment. The participant identification numbers should be documented chronologically by approved study identifier for each participant only and should not include nominative information (e.g., no initials or date of birth unless required by the protocol and approved by the REB). The Enrollment Log also tracks participant visit dates and provides an overview of which study visits participants have completed.

<a id="5-8"></a>

<a id="5-8-participant-identification"></a>

### 5.8 Participant identification

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** Create a confidential Participant Identification Log from the template in [Appendix 5](#appendix-5).

<a id="5-8-2"></a>**5.8.2** The Participant Identification Log permits the identification of all participants who are taking part in the study along with other nominative information (e.g., address, MRN, phone number and email of all enrolled participants.

<a id="5-8-3"></a>**5.8.3** The purpose of this log is to have quick access to all participants on a study in the event urgent follow-up of a participant is necessary e.g., drug/device recall, notification of important new safety information that requires urgent action.

<a id="5-8-4"></a>**5.8.4** This list is one of very few documents that contains participant nominative information and must be kept confidential. In the event that a participant moves, the QI/PI or his delegate should obtain the new contact information to ensure follow-up of the participant. All contact with the participant in order to obtain this information should be documented and filed with essential documents (e.g., [Appendix 6](#appendix-6), Telephone/Video Conference Log).

<a id="5-8-5"></a>**5.8.5** In the event that the QI/PI leaves the institution, the required information must be transferred to the newly appointed QI/PI or the director / head of the department. The Sponsor and REB must also be informed of this transfer of information. Refer to [SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002) Appendix 5.

<a id="5-8-6"></a>**5.8.6** External Sponsors must not know the identity of study participants. Always protect participant confidentiality; do not provide the confidential Participant Identification Log to an external Sponsor and do not archive it with essential documents. Always obtain the Sponsor’s approval prior to its destruction (Typically, this log is kept at least one year after study closure).

<a id="5-9"></a>

<a id="5-9-participants-lost-to-follow-up"></a>

### 5.9 Participants lost to follow-up

<a id="5-9-1"></a>**5.9.1** In the event that a participant is unreachable, the site should make every effort to contact the participant. Within 30 days after any unattended scheduled visit, the site should leave a text (if cell contact available) and/or call and leave a message.

<a id="5-9-2"></a>**5.9.2** If the participant is still unreachable, within 5 calendar days, the site should send an email (or letter via regular mail).

<a id="5-9-3"></a>**5.9.3** If still unreachable, within 5 calendar days, the site should send another email (or letter via regular mail).

<a id="5-9-4"></a>**5.9.4** If the participant continues to be unreachable, repeat again within another 5 calendar days.

<a id="5-9-5"></a>**5.9.5** If after the third email/letter attempt, the participant continues to be unreachable, a registered letter (with confirmation of receipt) should be sent to the participant.

<a id="5-9-6"></a>**5.9.6** Unless additional requirements are specified in a protocol or Sponsor’s policy which would supersede this one, if there is still no response and the registered letter is returned to the site, the participant is considered Lost to Follow-up and this should be documented on the Enrollment Log and source document.

<a id="5-9-7"></a>**5.9.7** All documentation on attempt to contact the participant (including the registered letter and envelope) should be filed with essential documents.

<a id="5-9-8"></a>**5.9.8** If a participant is found after designation of Lost to Follow-up, the Sponsor must be notified without delay and documentation regarding this discussion should also be filed with essential documents.

<a id="5-10"></a>

<a id="5-10-notification-to-medical-records"></a>

### 5.10 Notification to Medical Records

<a id="5-10-1"></a>**5.10.1** As stated in [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008), the Medical Records Services must be informed of a participant participating in clinical research study with an Investigational Product (drug, natural health product or medical device) via the Confirmation of Participation in Research form ([Appendix 1](#appendix-1)) which must be submitted for every research participant. Refer to [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) for details on how to submit the ICF with this form to medical records.

<a id="5-10-2"></a>**5.10.2** The Medical Records Services must also be informed of a participant’s end of participation in a study with <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip>. Submit the End of Participation form to Medical Records for each research participant (Refer to [Appendix 2](#appendix-2)).

<a id="5-10-3"></a>**5.10.3** Always ensure that the patient specific bar code is applied to the top right corner of the form if not completing this form electronically in the electronic medical record (e.g., OACIS).

<a id="5-10-4"></a>**5.10.4** If submitting hard copies, ensure that only original copies of the Confirmation of Participation in Research and End of Participation in Research forms are sent to Medical Records for scanning as the bar code will degrade when photocopied.

<a id="5-11"></a>

<a id="5-11-ongoing-evaluation-of-the-recruitment-plan"></a>

### 5.11 Ongoing evaluation of the recruitment plan

<a id="5-11-1"></a>**5.11.1** Review recruitment goals and recruitment strategies periodically during the human research study.

<a id="5-11-2"></a>**5.11.2** Track recruitment at the MUHC site, and request information from the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator (if applicable) about recruitment at all sites involved in multi-centre studies, if not already obtained from Sponsor’s Newsletters.

<a id="5-11-3"></a>**5.11.3** If new medically important information is included in Sponsor’s Newsletters, ensure that the QI/PI signs and dates the Newsletters to document medical oversight.

<a id="5-11-4"></a>**5.11.4** Regularly assess the likelihood of the study completing within the projected study timelines e.g., consider the following: Is the overall targeted recruitment being achieved at study site(s)? Did the study start on schedule?

<a id="5-11-5"></a>**5.11.5** Keep the Monitor, or other designated Sponsor contact, informed about study progress and adherence to study recruitment timelines.

<a id="5-11-6"></a>**5.11.6** Motivate research team members to maintain an acceptable recruitment rate for the duration of the study (i.e., provide ongoing training or information sessions about the study).

<a id="5-11-7"></a>**5.11.7** Modify the recruitment plan/strategy, as necessary.

<a id="5-11-8"></a>**5.11.8** Prior to using any new or revised recruitment materials, ensure they have first been approved by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, the REB and MUHC Communications.
