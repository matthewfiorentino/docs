<a id="purpose"></a>

### 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the process for submitting a Clinical Trial Application (CTA) to Health Canada, requesting approval to carry out an investigational trial on a drug. This SOP does not include the CTA process for Natural Health Products.

<a id="scope"></a>

### 2.0 Scope

This SOP is applicable to all clinical studies undertaken at the site, and to those clinical research personnel responsible for preparing, reviewing, approving, and submitting CTAs and subsequent amendments/notifications for investigator-initiated trials.

This SOP does not apply to externally-sponsored trials.

<a id="responsibilities"></a>

### 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor-Investigator is considered to be the sponsor for investigator-initiated clinical trials, and therefore is responsible for submitting the Clinical Trial Application (CTA) to Health Canada.

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

### 4.0 Definitions

<a id="procedure"></a>

### 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

#### 5.1 Background

A CTA must be filed for clinical trials in Phases I through III of development (including comparative bioavailability trials). Approval must be received prior to study activation. This includes studies in which a marketed drug is used outside of conditions specified in the Notice of Compliance (NOC) or Drug Identification Number (DIN) application, i.e., if the indication/s and clinical use, target population/s, dosage regimen/s, route/s of administration, or dosage form/s differ from those described in the Product Monograph.

<a id="5-2"></a>

#### 5.2 Preparation of Clinical Trial Application

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** Request and attend a pre-CTA consultation meeting with Health Canada, if desired. Note: Meetings are useful for new active substances, or applications with complex issues.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** Main documents required for preparation of a CTA submission include:

- Drug Submission Application Form;
- Study Protocol;
- Protocol Synopsis (use appropriate Health Canada template, where applicable);
- Informed Consent Form;
- Research Ethics Board Attestation, signed by the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> (or equivalent form) and a signed Qualified Investigator’s Undertaking (QIU) form; not required for submission, must be retained in the essential study document file; and
- Investigator’s Brochure (<Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip>) OR Product Monograph (PM) AND Chemistry Manufacturing Control (CMC) information;\*\*
- Chemistry, Manufacturing, and Control (CMC) information\*\*\*(use appropriate Quality Overall Summary (QOS) template, where applicable)<br />If a controlled substance is to be used in the trial, a request for exemption must be made by completing Application Form for an Exemption to Use a Controlled Substance For Clinical Studies.

\*\* If the manufacturer of the product in a dosage form or strength not specified in the PM, has previously submitted information to Health Canada about the product in question, a letter authorizing cross-reference to their information may be submitted in lieu of the IB.

\*\*\* Chemistry Manufacturing Control (CMC) information: If using marketed product, the Product Monograph (PM) and Drug Identification Number (DIN) will suffice UNLESS the dosage form or strength is different from that specified in the PM. In the latter case, CMC information will be required.

Most investigator-initiated CTAs which require CMC information will use one of the following sources:

- A letter of cross-reference which allows Health Canada to access the CMC information for the product through a previously approved CTA on file at Health Canada.
- Letter from the drug manufacturer granting access to the Drug Master File (DMF) at Health Canada for the product.
- Cross-referenced CMC information, if using a Canadian marketed product. Cite DIN number of product.
- Full CMC information provided by the manufacturer for inclusion in the CTA.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** Submit the CTA, CTA-A, any CTA-notifications, Clinical Trial Site Information (CTSI) forms and Pre-CTA meeting information electronically to Health Canada,. Ensure that no trial activities begin before the No Objection Letter (<Tooltip tip="Letter from Health Canada confirming a CTA is satisfactory and authorizing the sponsor to proceed." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nol">NOL</Tooltip>) is received.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** Note: Detailed information and forms/templates to be used for a CTA are available on Health Canada’s web site at the following address: http://www.hc-sc.gc.ca

<a id="5-3"></a>

#### 5.3 Health Canada screening and review

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** Health Canada reviews the application, and if any deficiencies are detected, should inform the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator within 30 days. Submit further information, as/if requested.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** Submission Screening: Minor issues with documentation or deficiencies with the package will be addressed by either:

- Screening Rejection Letter, itemizing deficiencies/significant information lacking from the CTA or Clinical Trial Application Amendment (CTA-A). All information will require re-submission and will be processed as ‘new’ information, and hence will be assigned a new control number; or
- Request for Clarification, sent via fax, email, or phone. Response is required within 2 calendar days.

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** Receive the acknowledgement letter from Health Canada, to indicate that screening is complete, and the review has commenced (usually by fax, within 2-3 days of submission). Contact Health Canada, if acknowledgment letter is NOT received, to ensure that submission has been received safely. The default/target review period from receipt of a completed application package is 30 days.

<a id="5-3-4"></a>**5.3.4** Receive queries (if any) for specific issues with the protocol, consent, or the submission from Health Canada reviewer via fax, email, or phone (Information Request).

<a id="5-3-5"></a>**5.3.5** Respond to Health Canada (to any form of communication) within two (2) calendar days, in order to maintain the 30-day review period.

<a id="5-3-6"></a>**5.3.6** The reviewer may ask the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator to withdraw the CTA, to avoid receiving a Not Satisfactory Notice (NSN), if any deficiencies remain that Health Canada does not consider to have been satisfactorily addressed.

<a id="5-3-7"></a>**5.3.7** An NSN will be issued if a timely response is not received, or significant deficiencies are identified during the review, and the applicant does not withdraw the CTA. Note: A new 30-day review period will be applied to this new CTA. The resubmission will be reviewed without prejudice, if the CTA is withdrawn.

<a id="5-3-8"></a>**5.3.8** Note: Withdraw the CTA, if amendment-worthy modifications are desired by the Sponsor-Investigator, while the review is underway. Re-submit a new CTA for approval, as described above

<a id="5-3-9"></a>**5.3.9** Maintain all submission documents and correspondence in the essential study documents file.

<a id="5-4"></a>

#### 5.4 Trial commencement

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** Wait for No Objection Letter (NOL), indicating a satisfactory review and approval, before commencing trial activities. Submit copy of NOL to REB, if required.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** Complete and submit a completed Clinical Trial Site Information (CTSI) form (for each clinical trial site in Canada) via fax to Health Canada if not submitted with the CTA OR CTA-A. Ensure that the Health Canada control number and trial start date information are included on the form. Submit the CTSI form prior to the start of the study at the clinical site.

<a id="5-5"></a>

#### 5.5 Clinical Trial Application amendment (CTA-A)

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** Submit Clinical Trial Application Amendments (CTA-A), as/if required. Reasons for amendments include, but are not limited to, changes to the following:

- Clinical trial drug supplies (e.g., manufacturing process, dose strength
- Dosing regimen, inclusion/exclusion criteria;
- Selection, selection criteria, monitoring, or dismissal of a clinical trial subject;
- Evaluation of the clinical efficacy of the drug;
- Alteration of risk to the health of a clinical trial subject;
- Drug safety assessment (changed or additional);

- Extended clinical trial duration; and
- CMC information affecting safety or quality of the drug.

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** CTA amendments are subject to a 30-day review period, and require approval from Health Canada before implementing the changes.

<a id="5-5-3"></a>**5.5.3** Exception to 30-day review period: Implement protocol changes immediately only if there are serious safety concerns to study subjects (or others). Submit the changes to Health Canada immediately, as a Notification. Ensure that the reasons for immediate change are fully explained. Submit the full CTA-A, within 15 days of implementing the amended protocol.

<a id="5-5-4"></a>**5.5.4** Complete and submit a completed Clinical Trial Site Information (CTSI) form (for each clinical trial site in Canada) via fax to Health Canada for the CTA amendment. Ensure that the Health Canada control number issued to the CTA-A and trial start date information are included on the form.

<a id="5-6"></a>

#### 5.6 Notifications to CTA or CTA-A

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Inform Health Canada when implementing any of the following types of protocol changes:

- Changes to the protocol that do not compromise the safety of clinical trial participants, and are not classed as amendments (see CTA-A section);
- Information about a site closure or completion of a clinical trial;
- Premature discontinuation of a trial, at one or all of the study sites, for any reason, as specified in the protocol (e.g., safety, efficacy, administrative, recruiting issues, sponsor choice, etc.).

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** Notification information for continuing trials includes changes in data quality (chemistry and manufacturing) that do not affect drug quality or safety, such as:

- Pharmaceutical products: increase in production without any change in process;
- Narrowing of actual test specifications;
- Changes related to research laboratories under contract;
- Changes in packaging material;
- Pharmaceutical products: extension of shelf life; and
- Pharmaceutical products: all changes to the chemistry and manufacturing of the drug which do not affect its quality or safety (according to the criteria described elsewhere in this SOP).

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** Notification information for discontinued trials includes:

- A description of the effect of discontinuation on projected or ongoing trials of the drug in Canada;
- A statement confirming that each qualified investigator has been duly notified of the trial discontinuation and the reasons thereof, and that they have been sent a written notice regarding the potential health risks to participating subjects or others;
- Confirmation that the sale or importation of the drug at each involved trial site has been discontinued; and
- Confirmation that reasonable measures will be taken to ensure the return of all unused drug.

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** Other types of Notification include:

- All information regarding refusals to approve the study by other regulatory authority, or Research Ethics Boards (REBs), at any time before or during the clinical study.
- Discontinuation or partial/full Clinical Hold of a trial outside of Canada, when equivalent trials are being conducted in Canada.
- If the clinical trial drug is being imported: provide a No Objection Letter at importation to facilitate shipment, and to demonstrate regulatory compliance.
- If the product is imported by a third party: ensure that the Sponsor-Investigator has a written agreement, and systems in place for handling and storage of the product.
- If additional drugs are imported for the clinical trial: provide a list of these drugs in the CTA.

<a id="5-6-5"></a>**5.6.5 Suspension and Cancellation:**

- Division 5, Section C of the [Food and Drug Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/) allows for the suspension of the authorization to sell or import a drug for investigational purposes, in its entirety or at a clinical trial site. The conditions for a suspension are outlined in subsection C.05.016 (1) paragraphs (a) to (d).
- Prior to suspending an authorization, Health Canada shall send a notice to the Sponsor detailing the reason for the suspension, the date the suspension begins, and if the authorization to suspend is in its entirety or at a particular clinical trial site.
- The authorization for the clinical trial will be restored if, within 30 days after the day on which the suspension is effective, Health Canada is provided with information demonstrating that the situation causing the suspension either did not exist or has been corrected. If the information has not been provided to Health Canada within those 30 days, the authorization for the clinical trial will be cancelled in its entirety or at a particular site.

<a id="5-7"></a>

#### 5.7 Investigator brochure updates

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** Submit updated Investigator Brochure (IB) as a Notification, unless included as part of a planned CTA-A. Include complete safety data and a general overview of the trial.

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** Submit updated IB annually, or more often if needed. Highlight/summarize changes for ease of review and evaluation.

<a id="5-8"></a>

#### 5.8 Submission of adverse drug reactions (ARs)

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** See specific SOP on Adverse Event/Reaction Reporting.

<a id="5-9"></a>

#### 5.9 Ongoing Sponsor-Investigator responsibilities

### 1.0 Specific RI-MUHC policies and procedures

<a id="1-1"></a>

#### 1.1 Additional comments on compliance pertaining to CTA (drugs)

<a id="1-1-1"></a>**1.1.1** Refer to [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500) for mandatory investigator privilege to conduct research at the RI MUHC.

<a id="1-1-2"></a>**1.1.2** When completing the CTA form HC-SC 3011, Appendix 3 - Clinical Trial Application Information (for Clinical Trial Applications for human drugs only), the Senior Executive Officer signature should be that of the appropriate Department Head or the RI-MUHC Chief Scientific Officer.

<a id="1-1-3"></a>**1.1.3** Clinical Trials must be conducted in compliance with: Health Canada, Food and Drug Act: C.R.C., c 870 – Division 5, Part C, Clinical Trials Involving Human Subjects; ICH- GCP E6, R2: Provincial and local regulations; Tri-Council Policy Statement ([TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html)); Compliance to the RI MUHC Standard Operating Procedures.

<a id="1-2"></a>

#### 1.2 Suspension or cancellation of authorization from Health Canada C.05.016-017

<a id="1-2-1"></a>**1.2.1** Sections C.05.016 and C.05.017 allow for the suspension or cancellation of a CTA authorization to sell or import a drug for the purposes of a clinical trial, in its entirety or at a clinical trial site with or without the opportunity to be heard by the Minister.

<a id="1-3"></a>

#### 1.3 Health Canada, C.R.C., c 870 – Division 5, Part C and amendments training documentation

<a id="1-3-1"></a>**1.3.1** It is mandatory for site personnel involved in a clinical trial with Investigational Product and listed on the ‘Tasks Delegation Log’ to have documented training on Health Canada, Division 5, Part C and any related amendments. The Research Certification, mandatory for all personnel involved in human subject’s research at the MUHC, includes Division 5, Part C incorporated into the RI-MUHC SOPs, however, additional training is recommended.

<a id="1-3-2"></a>**1.3.2** In addition to the online CITI GCP and RI-MUHC SOP obtained through the Certification training, if conducting a study under a Health Canada Clinical Trial Application (CTA), it is highly recommended to obtain free online CITI training on Division 5, Part C of Health Canada Regulations available on the research portal under Research&gt;Clinical Research&gt;Continuing Education.

<a id="1-4"></a>

#### 1.4 Tri-Council Policy Statement (TCPS2) requirements

<a id="1-4-1"></a>**1.4.1 Responsible Conduct of Research Tri-Agencies Framework (CIHR, NSERC, SSHRC)**

“To be able to receive and administer research funds from the Agencies, institutions must agree to comply with a number of policies set as schedules to a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) between the Agencies and Institutions. Institutions must therefore ensure that research conducted under their auspices adhere to this Policy.” (Adapted from [TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html), 5).

<a id="1-4-2"></a>**1.4.2 Responsible Conduct of Research by Investigator**

- Sponsor-Investigators/Qualified Investigators, Sub-Investigators responsible for the design and the conduct of Agency funded projects OR projects funded by a private company under the auspices of an institution eligible to administer Agency funds must be in compliance with all applicable Agency requirements and legislation for the conduct of research, including, but not limited to:
  - Current edition of Tri-Council Policy Statement: Ethical Conduct of Research Involving Humans (TCPS2)
  - Agency policies related to the Canadian Environmental Assessment Act;
  - Licenses for research in the field;
  - Laboratory Biosafety Guidelines;
  - Controlled Goods Program;
  - Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission (CNSC) Regulations;
  - Health Canada Food and Drug Act: C.R.C., c 870 – Division 5, Part C, Clinical Trials Involving Human Subjects.
