## Purpose and scope

This SOP describes the process for submitting a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application</Tooltip> (CTA) to Health Canada, requesting approval to carry out an investigational trial involving a drug (pharmaceutical or biologic). It covers CTA preparation and submission, the Health Canada review process, post-authorization requirements, CTA amendments, and notifications.

This SOP applies to Sponsor-Investigators conducting investigator-initiated drug clinical trials at the RI-MUHC. It does not cover the CTA process for Natural Health Products (→ [SOP-CR-023](/sops/cr-023)) or the <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">Investigational Testing Authorization</Tooltip> for medical devices (→ [SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024)).

---

## Responsibilities

The **Sponsor-Investigator** is considered to be the sponsor for investigator-initiated clinical trials and is responsible for submitting the CTA to Health Canada (Therapeutic Products Directorate [TPD] or Biologics and Genetic Therapies Directorate [BGTD]).

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor-Investigator.

---

## Procedure

### 5.1 RI-MUHC compliance requirements

**5.1.1** Refer to [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500) for mandatory investigator privileges at the RI-MUHC. These must be in place before initiating a CTA submission.

**5.1.2** When completing the CTA form HC-SC 3011, Appendix 3 — Clinical Trial Application Information (for human drug CTAs), the **Senior Executive Officer signature** must be that of the appropriate Department Head or the RI-MUHC Chief Scientific Officer.

**5.1.3** Clinical trials must be conducted in compliance with:
- Health Canada, Food and Drug Act: C.R.C., c 870 — Division 5, Part C, Clinical Trials Involving Human Subjects
- ICH-<Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> E6(R2)
- Provincial and local regulations
- Tri-Council Policy Statement (TCPS2)
- RI-MUHC Standard Operating Procedures

### 5.2 When a CTA is required

A CTA must be filed for:

| Trial type | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Phase I, II, III drug trials | Submission required |
| Comparative bioavailability trials | Submission required |
| Marketed drug used outside approved NOC/DIN parameters (indication, population, route, dosage) | Submission required |
| Clinical trial with a drug that has received a Notice of Compliance with Conditions | Submission required |

### 5.3 CTA composition and submission

**5.3.1** The CTA is composed of three modules:
- **Module 1** — Administrative and clinical information
- **Module 2** — Quality information (chemistry and manufacturing)
- **Module 3** — Additional supporting quality information

**5.3.2** Submit the CTA directly to the appropriate directorate:
- **Pharmaceutical drugs** → Therapeutic Products Directorate (TPD)
- **Biologics/radiopharmaceuticals** → Biologic and Genetic Therapies Directorate (BGTD)

**5.3.3** Maintain all submission documents and correspondence in the Investigator Study File (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>) → [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015).

### 5.4 Health Canada CTA review

**5.4.1** The CTA first undergoes a **screening phase**. If acceptable, Health Canada sends a letter indicating the start of the review period.

**5.4.2** The standard review period is **30 days** from receipt of a complete application.

**5.4.3** At the end of the review:
- If authorized: Health Canada issues a **<Tooltip tip="Letter from Health Canada confirming a CTA is satisfactory and authorizing the sponsor to proceed." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#nol">No Objection Letter</Tooltip> (NOL)**
- If rejected: Health Canada issues a **No Satisfactory Notice (NSN)**

**5.4.4** No trial activities may begin until the NOL has been received.

**5.4.5** Respond to any Health Canada queries within the specified timeframe.

### 5.5 Post-authorization requirements

Once a CTA has received regulatory authorization, inform Health Canada of:
- Clinical Trial Site Information for additional sites
- Changes to a previously authorized CTA
- Premature discontinuation of the trial
- Adverse drug reactions
- <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> refusal
- Lot release information (for biologics/radiopharmaceuticals)
- Updated <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip>

### 5.6 CTA amendments (CTA-A) and notifications

**5.6.1** Submit a **Clinical Trial Application Amendment (CTA-A)** for changes that require formal amendment; submit a **Notification** for minor changes.

**5.6.2** Amendments are subject to the standard 30-day review period and require approval before implementing changes.

**5.6.3** Exception: Implement protocol changes immediately only if there are serious safety concerns to participants. Submit changes to Health Canada immediately as a Notification and submit the full CTA-A within 15 days.

### 5.7 Record retention

**5.7.1** Records related to the clinical trial must be maintained by the Sponsor-Investigator for **25 years**.

**5.7.2** Records must be made available to Health Canada within:
- **2 calendar days** if there is a safety/risk concern for patients
- **7 calendar days** in any other case

### 5.8 Training requirements

**5.8.1** It is mandatory for all site personnel listed on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> to have documented training on Health Canada Division 5, Part C and any related amendments.

**5.8.2** The RI-MUHC Research Certification (including CITI GCP and RI-MUHC SOP training) incorporates Division 5, Part C. Additional CITI online training on Division 5, Part C is highly recommended and available on the research portal under Research > Clinical Research > Continuing Education.

### 5.9 Suspension or cancellation of authorization

**5.9.1** Sections C.05.016 and C.05.017 of the Food and Drug Act allow Health Canada to suspend or cancel the authorization to sell or import a drug for clinical trial purposes, in its entirety or at a clinical trial site, with or without the opportunity to be heard by the Minister.
