### 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the management of biological specimens obtained from clinical research subjects. Processes include collection, processing, storage, and handling from collection to destruction, within the institution.

<a id="scope"></a>

### 2.0 Scope

This SOP applies to all clinical studies undertaken at the site, and to those clinical research personnel responsible for biological specimen handling.

<a id="responsibilities"></a>

### 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor-Investigator or Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>)/Investigator is responsible for ensuring that the specimen handling processes meet all of the applicable regulatory, International Conference on Harmonisation (ICH) Good Clinical Practice (GCP), sponsor, and local requirements.

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor-Investigator or Qualified Investigator (QI)/Investigator.

<a id="definitions"></a>

### 4.0 Definitions

See [Glossary of Terms](/kb/foundations/glossary).

<a id="procedure"></a>

### 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

#### 5.1 General

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** Obtain detailed instructions for specimen management from the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and/or central laboratory prior to study activation. Ensure that all supplies are available.

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** The protocol, laboratory manual, or other documentation should include:

- laboratory contact information (for central laboratories),
- requirements for specimen collection, labelling, processing and storage,
- supplies, and
- packaging and shipping specifications.

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** Ensure that equipment such as centrifuges, storage refrigerators and freezers are calibrated and checked on a regular maintenance schedule. Retain any documentation connected with maintenance of the equipment with the essential study documentation.

<a id="5-1-4"></a>**5.1.4** Prepare an emergency response plan to ensure the integrity of the biological specimens.

<a id="5-1-5"></a>**5.1.5** Arrange/perform specimen collection, as required by the protocol.

<a id="5-1-6"></a>**5.1.6** Identify specimens according to the specifications in the protocol and as required by the site <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>.

<a id="5-1-7"></a>**5.1.7** Document the collection, storage, and shipping (as applicable), and file records with the essential study documentation.

<a id="5-2"></a>

#### 5.2 Biological specimen storage

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** Ensure that biological specimens are stored in a secure and suitable environment, in compliance with the requirements of the protocol, or other study documentation. Establish and maintain controlled access for authorized personnel.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** Ensure adherence to the storage time for biological specimens, as defined in the protocol, or other study documentation.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** File storage records with the essential study documentation.

<a id="5-3"></a>

#### 5.3 Biological specimen destruction

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** Destroy biological specimens, according to the requirements of the protocol, and of the institution.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** File destruction records with the essential study documentation.

### 1.0 Specific RI-MUHC policies and procedures

<a id="1-1"></a>

#### 1.1 Additional comments on Investigator responsibilities of biological specimen collection

<a id="1-1-1"></a>**1.1.1** Ensure that staff is qualified and trained on the study specific tasks delegated to them.

<a id="1-1-2"></a>**1.1.2** Assess the health and safety risks involved in the collection and analysis of the biological specimens, as well as the precautionary measures required to prevent accidents

<a id="1-1-3"></a>**1.1.3** All laboratory abnormalities should be reported to the QI/Investigator for assessment and all assessments (clinical significance e.g. c.s. or n.c.s.) should be printed, initialed and dated by the Investigator. Laboratory abnormalities deemed clinically significant should be documented as described in the protocol.

<a id="1-1-4"></a>**1.1.4** Make the safety information available to the persons coming in contact or handling the biological specimens and ensure that these persons receive training in the appropriate safety practices. Contact the RI-MUHC Environmental Health and Safety Officer (ri.ehs@muhc.mcgill.ca) for biosafety training schedules

<a id="1-1-5"></a>**1.1.5** Ensure the safety and well-being of subjects during the collection of specimens.

<a id="1-1-6"></a>**1.1.6** Laboratory records to retain include normal ranges for study tests; laboratory certification and/or accreditation; established quality control and/or external quality assessment; laboratory results/reports; X-ray films, digital images, microfilms and compact disks.

<a id="1-1-7"></a>**1.1.7** Document specimen collection in a specimen collection log. Refer to Appendix 1 for an example of a log for collection, processing and shipment.

<a id="1-2"></a>

#### 1.2 Additional comments on Investigator responsibilities of biological specimen shipment

<a id="1-2-1"></a>**1.2.1** Ensure that appropriate packaging, labelling and shipping standards are applied, according to Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulation (TDG-for ground transportation) and International Air Transportation Association (IATA-for air transportation). The shipping process should be documented and the documentation kept with the essential documentation of the study.

<a id="1-2-2"></a>**1.2.2** Ensure that the person who is responsible for shipping biological specimens is trained and holds a valid certification from TDG or IATA. The certification is valid for 2 years for air transportation and 3 years for ground transportation. This certification must be kept with the training documents in the essential study documentation. Online training is available through the CITI link under Continuing Education in the RI portal at <span>http</span>://researchportal.muhc.mcgill.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106.251

<a id="1-2-3"></a>**1.2.3** Document specimen shipment in a specimen shipment log. Refer to Appendix 1 for an example of a log for collection, processing and shipment.

<a id="1-3"></a>

#### 1.3 Additional comments on Investigator responsibilities of biological specimen analysis

<a id="1-3-1"></a>**1.3.1** The Investigator should know, within a reasonable time, the results of the analysis related to the follow-up of a subject, with reference to the protocol.

<a id="1-3-2"></a>**1.3.2** The Investigator should be in a position to know all the results of the analysis by the end of the study or as specified in the protocol and in the subject informed consent form.

<a id="1-4"></a>

#### 1.4 Tasks delegated to the CRC or other research staff member include:

- Ensuring they have received training on the protocol specific task delegated to them.
- Ensuring their IATA/TDG certification is current and up-to-date.
- Ensuring the lab kits (if applicable) have not expired and if kits are expired, order replacements.
- Ensuring the correct lab kit (if applicable) corresponds to the study and visit.
- Drawing subject blood for processing
- Ensuring the samples are processed according the lab manual provided by sponsor.
- Ensuring a copy of the central lab requisition is stored with the samples in the freezer(s) and/or refrigerator.
- Ordering dry ice (if applicable).
- Shipping samples to the central lab in accordance with the Sponsor’s lab manual.
- Ensuring processed samples are stored as per study requirements.
- Ensuring the Lab Sample Processing and Storage Log (Appendix 1) is completed at the time of sample processing.
- Ensuring the Lab Sample Processing and Storage Log is filed with the subject’s research chart.
- Ensuring the Lab Sample Processing and Storage Log is complete and updated on an ongoing basis and available for monitor, audit or inspection.
