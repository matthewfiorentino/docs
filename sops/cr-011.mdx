## Key points

- All specimen collection, processing, and shipping must follow the Sponsor's laboratory manual and the protocol exactly. Deviations (wrong tube, wrong processing time, incorrect storage temperature) must be documented and reported to the Sponsor.
- All laboratory abnormalities must be reported to the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI for assessment; results must be printed, initialled, and dated by the Investigator. Clinically significant abnormalities must be documented as described in the protocol.
- Specimens must be labelled with the participant's study ID only — never with name, MRN, or date of birth — along with visit code, collection date/time, and any other Sponsor-required identifiers.
- Chain of custody must be maintained from collection through shipment. The **Human Sample Collection, Processing and Shipping Log** (Appendix 1) must be completed for every specimen batch.
- Ensure staff are qualified and trained on study-specific specimen handling tasks. Safety information about biological specimens must be accessible to all persons handling them; biosafety training is available through RI-MUHC Environmental Health and Safety (ri.ehs@muhc.mcgill.ca).
- The person responsible for shipping biological specimens must hold a valid TDG certification (3 years, ground) or IATA certification (2 years, air). Certification must be kept with training documents in the essential study file.
- Laboratory records to retain include: normal ranges for study tests; laboratory certification/accreditation; quality control documentation; laboratory results/reports; X-ray films, digital images, microfilms, and compact disks.

## 1 · Purpose {#purpose}

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the management of biological
specimens obtained from clinical research subjects. Processes include collection,
processing, storage, and handling from collection to destruction, within the institution.

---

## 2 · Scope {#scope}

This SOP applies to all clinical studies undertaken at the site, and to those clinical
research personnel responsible for biological specimen handling.

---

## 3 · Responsibilities {#responsibilities}

The Sponsor-Investigator or <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">Qualified Investigator</Tooltip> (QI)/<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">Principal Investigator</Tooltip> (PI) is responsible for ensuring that the specimen handling processes meet all applicable regulatory, ICH-<Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>, Sponsor, and local requirements.

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained study team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor-Investigator or QI/PI.

## 5 · Procedure {#procedure}
### 5.1 Specimen collection

**5.1.1** Ensure that staff are qualified and trained on the study-specific tasks delegated to them.

**5.1.2** Assess the health and safety risks involved in the collection and analysis of biological specimens, as well as the precautionary measures required to prevent accidents.

**5.1.3** All laboratory abnormalities must be reported to the QI/Investigator for assessment; all assessments (clinical significance, e.g. c.s. or n.c.s.) must be printed, initialled, and dated by the Investigator. Laboratory abnormalities deemed clinically significant must be documented as described in the protocol.

**5.1.4** Make safety information available to all persons who may come in contact with or handle biological specimens, and ensure they receive training in appropriate safety practices. Contact the RI-MUHC Environmental Health and Safety Officer (ri.ehs@muhc.mcgill.ca) for biosafety training schedules.

**5.1.5** Ensure the safety and well-being of subjects during the collection of specimens.

**5.1.6** Laboratory records to retain include: normal ranges for study tests; laboratory certification and/or accreditation; established quality control and/or external quality assessment; laboratory results/reports; X-ray films, digital images, microfilms, and compact disks.

**5.1.7** Document specimen collection in a specimen collection log. Refer to Appendix 1 for the Human Sample Collection, Processing and Shipping Log.

### 5.2 Specimen processing and storage

**5.2.1** Process specimens according to the Sponsor's laboratory manual and the protocol. Any deviation from specified processing procedures (wrong tube, wrong centrifuge speed, incorrect time or temperature) must be documented on the collection log and reported to the Sponsor without delay.

**5.2.2** Label all specimens with the participant's study ID only — never with name, MRN, or date of birth — along with visit code, collection date and time, and any other Sponsor-required identifiers.

**5.2.3** Store specimens under the conditions specified in the protocol or laboratory manual (temperature, light, duration). Document storage conditions and any temperature excursions.

**5.2.4** Maintain chain of custody from collection through final disposition. The Human Sample Collection, Processing and Shipping Log (Appendix 1) must be completed for every specimen batch.

### 5.3 Specimen shipment

**5.3.1** Ensure that appropriate packaging, labelling, and shipping standards are applied according to the *Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations* (TDG — ground transportation) and the *International Air Transport Association Dangerous Goods Regulations* (IATA — air transportation). The shipping process must be documented and documentation kept with the essential study file.

**5.3.2** Ensure that the person responsible for shipping biological specimens holds a valid certification from TDG or IATA. The certification is valid for 2 years for air transportation and 3 years for ground transportation. This certification must be kept with the training documents in the essential study file.

**5.3.3** Confirm successful receipt of specimens with the receiving laboratory and document confirmation (e.g. email or tracking record) in the collection log and/or <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>.

### 5.4 Specimen destruction

**5.4.1** Specimens may only be destroyed per the Sponsor's instructions, applicable regulations, and the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>-approved protocol.

**5.4.2** Obtain written authorization from the Sponsor before destroying any specimens. Document the destruction: date, quantity, method, and staff signature.

**5.4.3** Biological waste must be disposed of according to MUHC biosafety and environmental health and safety procedures. Do not dispose of biological specimens in general waste.
