# Management of Investigational Products

<Badge color="gray">SOP-CR-010_07</Badge> <Badge color="gray">Version 07</Badge> <Badge color="green">Effective 01 Sep 2018</Badge> <Badge color="blue">Levels I–III</Badge>

<Note>
This SOP describes the management of investigational products (IPs), regardless of source. It covers receipt, labelling, storage, distribution, accountability, and return or authorized destruction. Investigational products include drugs, biological products, natural health products, radiopharmaceuticals, and medical devices.
</Note>

## Key points

- In Quebec, all <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> dispensed to a participant must be handled by a licensed pharmacist — the MUHC Pharmacy must be informed and present at the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#siv">Site Initiation Visit</Tooltip> for drug studies. Although the pharmacist is delegated dispensing, overall IP accountability remains the responsibility of the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI.
- Only the QI/PI and Sub-Investigators (MDs) may prescribe medication. A research nurse or <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip> may dispense only if pharmacy has reviewed and approved a specific waiver, which must be kept in essential documents.
- IP must be received, logged, and stored according to the Sponsor's specifications and the study protocol before being administered to any participant.
- All IP dispensing must be documented with participant identifier, amount dispensed, lot number, expiry date, and date/time. Dispensing records must match accountability records exactly.
- Temperature excursions affecting stored IP must be documented immediately, assessed for impact on product integrity, and reported to the Sponsor. Do not administer IP that has experienced an unqualified excursion without Sponsor authorization.
- Unused, returned, or expired IP must be segregated, documented, and either returned to the Sponsor or destroyed per the Sponsor's instructions and applicable regulations. Do not dispose of IP through general waste.
- IP accountability records must be available for inspection at all times and filed in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>. The QI/PI must ensure the Pharmacy is provided with the IP Receipt Log and the IP Dispensing Log.
- All drugs dispensed by a trained staff member must carry a pharmacy-provided label identical to the IP label; a copy must be applied to the appropriate source document.
