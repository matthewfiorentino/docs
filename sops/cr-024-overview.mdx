# ITA for Medical Devices

<Note>
This SOP describes the process for submitting an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">Investigational Testing Authorization</Tooltip> (ITA) to Health Canada, requesting approval to carry out an investigational trial on a non-IVDD (non-in vitro diagnostic device) medical device. It also covers mandatory problem reporting and manufacturer/sponsor obligations. This SOP applies to Sponsor-Investigators at the RI-MUHC conducting investigator-initiated medical device trials. It does not apply to externally-sponsored device trials or trials involving in vitro diagnostic devices (IVDDs).

## Key points

- **Research privileges required:** Refer to [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500). Mandatory investigator privilege to conduct research at the RI-MUHC must be in place before submitting an ITA.
- An ITA is required for **Class II, III, and IV medical devices**. Class I devices (minimal risk) do not require an ITA. No ITA is required for IVDDs (in vitro diagnostic devices) — use the IVDD-specific process instead.
- Investigational testing must comply with the **Declaration of Helsinki**, **ISO 14155:2011(E)** (Clinical investigation of medical devices — <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">Good Clinical Practice</Tooltip>), and **ISO 14971:2007** (Medical devices — Application of risk management). Both ISO standards are Health Canada recognized.
- Device risk class is determined by degree of invasiveness, whether the device is implantable or active (powered), and the body structure affected. See the Device Accountability Log template (Appendix 1) for device tracking requirements.
- Changes made during an ITA that require notification include: protocol changes, investigator/institution changes, indication/treatment changes, and mandatory problem reporting events.
- **Mandatory Problem Reporting:** Report any serious incident involving a medical device (malfunction, deterioration, or inadequacy that has led to or could lead to serious injury or death) to Health Canada per applicable Medical Devices Regulations timelines → [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012).
- Health Canada may **cancel** an ITA authorization per Section 85 of the Medical Devices Regulations if conditions for investigational testing are no longer met.
- Records related to the clinical trial must be maintained by the Sponsor-Investigator for **25 years** and made available to Health Canada within 2 calendar days (safety concern) or 7 calendar days (any other case).
- Use the **Medical Device Accountability Log** (Appendix 1) to track device receipt, use, return, and disposition throughout the study → [SOP-CR-010](/sops/cr-010) for <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> management procedures applicable to medical devices.
