Monitoring, close-out, and inspection readiness are not events that happen at the end of a study — they are ongoing responsibilities throughout the study lifecycle. These SOPs define your obligations when a Sponsor's monitor or CRO visits the site, how to properly close out a completed study, and how to prepare for and respond to a regulatory audit or Health Canada inspection. They apply to Levels I–IV.

<Warning>
**Controlled documents — always use the online version.** Do not rely on downloaded or printed copies. Your first reading of each SOP must be completed through the SOP Reader on TalentLMS to count toward your training record.
</Warning>

  <Accordion title="SOP-CR-013 — Study Monitoring and Communication · v19-Jun-2023 · Levels I–IV">
    This SOP defines the requirements for hosting sponsor monitors and CRO representatives at the RI-MUHC, including access to source records, OACIS chart access, and communication between monitoring visits. It covers all visit types: site feasibility, site initiation, periodic monitoring, and close-out visits.

    **Key points:**
    - You must provide monitors with access to all study records, source documents, and facilities required to verify data against source. Access to OACIS requires completion of the Monitor OACIS Access Request (Appendix 2) before the visit.
    - For sites where DPS paper charts are still in use, the DPS Chart Request (Appendix 2b) must be submitted in advance to retrieve charts for the monitoring visit.
    - Before each monitoring visit, confirm the visit date and scope with the monitor and send (or receive) a pre-visit confirmation email using the template in Appendix 4.
    - All monitoring visit findings must be addressed within the timeframe specified in the monitoring visit follow-up letter. Track outstanding findings and escalate unresolved items to the QI/PI promptly.
    - The **Monitoring Visit Log** (Appendix 1) must be completed for every visit and filed in the ISF.

  <Accordion title="SOP-CR-016 — Study Close-Out · v01-May-2025 · Levels I–IV">
    This SOP defines the procedures for closing out a clinical research study at the RI-MUHC, whether the study has completed as planned, has been terminated early, or has been suspended. Close-out must be completed in a systematic and documented manner to satisfy GCP requirements and to protect the long-term retrievability of study records.

    **Key points:**
    - Close-out activities begin after the last participant visit is completed and the Sponsor (or QI/PI for Sponsor-Investigator studies) has confirmed that the database is locked or that all data queries have been resolved.
    - You must return or destroy all unused investigational product per the protocol and IP management SOP (SOP-CR-010) before close-out is complete.
    - The ISF must be reviewed for completeness before the close-out visit. Any missing documents must be obtained or a Note to File must explain why they are unavailable.
    - The REB must be notified of study completion by submitting a final report (Study Completion Report) through Nagano. Do not archive records before the REB acknowledges completion.
    - Study records must be archived for the required retention period (typically 25 years under Canadian regulations) in a secure, accessible location. Notify the Research Hub of the archiving location and access arrangements.

    <Info>
      No downloadable appendices are associated with this SOP. Close-out documentation uses the ISF structure defined in SOP-CR-015 and the site close-out visit report from SOP-CR-013 (Appendix 10).
    </Info>
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="SOP-CR-017 — Audits and Inspections · v01-May-2025 · Levels I–IV">
    This SOP defines how to prepare for, host, and respond to audits and regulatory inspections — including inspections conducted by Health Canada's Health Products and Food Branch Inspectorate (HPFBI). It covers both Sponsor-initiated audits and unsolicited regulatory inspections, and provides the complete inspection package that must be prepared before an inspection begins.

    **Key points:**
    - If you receive notification of a Health Canada inspection, immediately notify the RI-MUHC Research Hub Quality team and begin preparing your inspection package using the documents in Appendix 1.
    - Inspectors must be provided with a dedicated room, access to all study documents, and a study contact list. The **Room Sign** and **Study Contact List** (Appendix 1) must be ready before inspectors arrive.
    - All documents released to inspectors as certified copies must be tracked on the **Document Release Log of Certified Copies** (Appendix 1). Do not allow inspectors to take original documents.
    - An **Attendance Record** (Appendix 1) must be maintained throughout the inspection, logging all RI-MUHC personnel who enter the inspection room.
    - Inspection observations must be documented on the **Summary of Inspection Observations — Daily Report** (Appendix 1). At the close of each inspection day, review observations with the inspector to ensure accuracy.
    - After the inspection, you must respond to each observation using the **Health Canada Inspection Response Form** (Appendix 2) within the deadline specified by the inspector. Responses must be factual, specific, and include a corrective action plan for each finding.

## Preparing for a Health Canada inspection

A Health Canada inspection can be announced with as little as a few days' notice. The following steps outline the key preparation activities you should take as soon as you receive notification.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Notify the Research Hub immediately">
    Contact the RI-MUHC Research Hub Quality team as soon as you receive the inspection notice. They will coordinate institutional support and help you prepare.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Download and complete the Inspection Package">
    Download the Inspection Preparation Checklist (SOP-CR-017, Appendix 1) and begin working through it systematically. Confirm who will serve as the primary site contact during the inspection.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and organize your ISF">
    Use the ISF Filing and Archiving Structure (SOP-CR-015, Appendix 4) to confirm your ISF is complete and up to date. Identify and resolve any gaps with Notes to File.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare the inspection room">
    Set up a dedicated room with the Room Sign posted, a copy of the Study Contact List, and a printer for generating certified copies of documents as needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Brief your team">
    Ensure all team members who may speak to inspectors know their role, can locate documents in the ISF, and understand that they must answer questions accurately and refer queries they cannot answer to the QI/PI.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document everything during the inspection">
    Complete the Attendance Record and Summary of Inspection Observations — Daily Report throughout the inspection. Track all documents provided to inspectors on the Document Release Log of Certified Copies.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Respond to observations on time">
    After the inspection, complete the Health Canada Inspection Response Form (Appendix 2) within the required deadline. Provide factual, specific responses with a corrective action plan for each finding.
  </Step>
</Steps>
