## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the process for <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PIs to obtain MSSS required Privileges or [Researcher Status](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status) from the RI-MUHC in order to conduct human research at the MUHC/RI-MUHC.

This procedure describes how researchers (who are, in the context of this SOP, Qualified or Principal Investigators, Sub-Investigators delegated QI/<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> backup tasks on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>, and Sponsor-Investigators, as defined in the [Glossary of Terms](/kb/foundations/glossary)) obtain and maintain either Human Research Privileges (for MDs and Dentists listed on the MUHC CPDP) or Researcher Status (for non-MDs/Dentists). Privileges and Researcher Status are mandatory prior to accessing training to conduct clinical research (see [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003) for the procedure on human research training) and conducting a human research study.

<a id="scope"></a>

## 2.0 Scope

This procedure applies to any MUHC physicians or dentists (and their backup) who wish to obtain Privileges to be a QI/PI in order to conduct MUHC/RI-MUHC studies requiring medical oversight.

This procedure also applies to non-MDs e.g. PhDs, nurses, physiotherapists, radiotherapists, psychologists, pharmacists and other health care professionals (and their backup) who wish to obtain Researcher Status in order to be a PI conducting a human research study that does not require medical oversight.

The QI/PI or Sponsor-Investigator (and their backup) is required to obtain Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status to conduct any study that involves human participants’ participation, information or materials at the MUHC (including biological material human embryos, fetuses, fetal tissue, reproductive materials and stem cells from living and deceased individuals).

The aim of this SOP covers all studies, whether or not these require the submission of a CTA/<Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip> to Health Canada including observational, epidemiological and human health records research.

Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) exception:

- Although the MNI is part of the MUHC, MNI PIs who plan to conduct studies at the MNI must obtain McGill Research Privileges and institutional training as per MNI and McGill SOPs.

- MNI PIs who plan to conduct studies at other MUHC sites (e.g., RVH, MGH, MCH) must obtain RI-MUHC Research Privileges and institutional training as per RI-MUHC SOPs.

As per the 2022 MUHC Regulatory Framework, for the purposes of a study, a fellow, resident or medical student cannot conduct research independently and hence, cannot be Sponsor-Investigator, Principal/Qualified Investigator, nor Sub-Investigator delegated QI/PI responsibilities (i.e. cannot apply for Privileges or Researcher Status).

Note: All other research team members listed on the Task Delegation Log, other than the QI/PI and Sub-Investigator backup, are not required to obtain Privileges/Researcher Status for the same study.

## 3.0 Responsibilities (privileges/researcher status &amp; institutional training)

The Investigators are required to obtain either Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status, in order to submit a study to the MUHC <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> and conduct human research at the RI-MUHC and/or MUHC. In addition to overall study conduct at the site, investigators are also responsible for:

- Obtaining the appropriate level of institutional training as per [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003) as well as reading the associated [SOP Reader](/training/compliance-requirements#sop-reader-and-assessment) prior to submitting a study through the study submission platform (e.g., [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview)) as per [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007) and information on the MUHC REB website;
- Ensuring research staff who will be delegated protocol tasks have adequate institutional and study-specific training and documented task delegation by the QI/PI/Sponsor-Investigator prior to the start of any trial/study activities;
- Ensuring that a Sub-Investigator qualified to be a QI/PI backup (during vacation, absences, sabbaticals) has also obtained the same authorization (Privileges/Researcher Status) and institutional training as the QI/PI or better e.g., a practicing physician Sub-Investigator obtaining Privileges for a study that requires medical oversight;
- All other responsibilities as per RI-MUHC SOPs, ICH-GCP/ISO-GCP and Health Canada regulations (where applicable).

The RI-MUHC Executive Director and Chief Scientific Officer (ED/CSO) is responsible for ensuring that:

- The requirements of the International Conference of Harmonization Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP) for drugs, International Standards Organization (ISO) 14155 GCP for medical device, RI-MUHC Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and federal and provincial regulatory authorities are available to the MUHC/RI-MUHC research community;
- Appropriate measures are taken to secure and maintain compliance with the ICH-GCP E6, ISO-14155 GCP, RI-MUHC SOPs and federal and provincial regulatory authorities’ requirements (as required by the Ministry of Health);
- Each Qualified/Principal Investigator, or Sponsor-Investigator has valid RI-MUHC Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status (as applicable) to conduct human research.

The RI-MUHC Performance Office is responsible for:

- Defining, implementing and maintaining a process to attribute RI-MUHC Privileges and Researcher Status;
- Processing and issuing Privileges and Researcher Status certificates according to established criteria (described in Section 5).

The RI-MUHC Quality Assurance department is responsible for:

- Confirming the QI/PI or Sponsor-Investigator has Privileges or Researcher Status (as applicable) during the <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's Quality Assurance department, which checks the credentials and training of each investigator who submits a study, audits research and maintains the SOPs. In GCP, quality assurance is the planned, systematic actions that ensure a trial complies with GCP and the applicable regulatory requirements." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> feasibility portion of the study submission process for new studies (in addition to confirming the appropriate level of institutional training assessed per protocol as described in [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003)).

<a id="definitions"></a>

## 4.0 Definitions

The MSSS, via the Cadre de référence ministériel pour le recherche avec des participants humains (2020) defines two ways for a researcher to obtain an authorization to conduct research on human participants.

Human Research Privileges allow a member of the CPDP (physicians, or dentists) to conduct research on humans at the MUHC/RI-MUHC. Note: Privileges are required for QI/PIs conducting Health Canada regulated studies with <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> (drug, medical device or natural health product).

Researcher Status allows individuals who are not members of CPDP (e.g., PhDs, nurses, physiotherapists, psychologists, radiotherapists and pharmacists) to conduct research on humans at MUHC/RI-MUHC which does not require medical oversight. Unlike Human Research Privileges, an individual granted Researcher Status cannot conduct research involving human medical oversight.

## 5.0 Procedure

The sections below describe how to obtain either Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status. A flowchart in [Appendix 1](#appendix-1) describes the connection with human research training ([SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003)). Both Privileges/Researcher Status request and the RI-MUHC SOP training are available on the [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) platform. (To log onto the TalentLMS platform, first log onto the RI portal, click on Applications and then click on TalentLMS).

<a id="5-1"></a>

### 5.1 Evaluation of eligibility for research privileges or researcher status

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** Before initiating human research, researchers must receive their human research commitment certificate. This certificate confirms their Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status. To receive this certificate, the researcher must log onto TalentLMS and select the Privileges/Researcher Status “course” from the course catalog and complete a questionnaire on TalentLMS (under « Demande de privilèges de recherche - Research Privileges Request » or « Demande de statut de chercheur | Demande de statut de chercheur ».

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** To be eligible for either Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status, the individual must typically hold a McGill Faculty appointment and plan to conduct research at the MUHC. The eligibility of individuals who are requesting Researcher Status or Researcher Privileges and do not hold a McGill Faculty appointment will be evaluated on a case by case basis by the RI-MUHC Performance Officer.

<a id="5-2"></a>

### 5.2 Privileges/researcher status commitment certificate

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** Upon acceptance by the RI-MUHC Performance Office, the certificate (Privileges or Researcher Status) is automatically generated and available in TalentLMS where it can be downloaded. It outlines all commitments and is valid for 3 years. It is the investigator’s responsibility to ensure this certificate is filed in the Investigator Site File (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>) of each of their studies. A copy is maintained in TalentLMS as a backup.

<a id="5-3"></a>

### 5.3 Renewal

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** Thirty days before the expiration of Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status, a reminder email will be automatically sent to the Investigator to renew via TalentLMS. Ongoing eligibility will be verified by the Performance Office before the certificate is renewed.

<a id="5-4"></a>

### 5.4 Revocation

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** Research Privileges or Researcher Status will be revoked by the RI-MUHC ED/CSO for the following reasons (without being limited to this list):

- Suspicion of misconduct that puts the safety of the participants at risk and requires further investigation was reported;
- Evidence of fraud;
- Inspection and audit activities have identified fraud or misconduct by the Investigator;
- Loss of Council of Physicians, Dentists and Pharmacists (CPDP) rights or end of MUHC employment.

<a id="5-5"></a>

### 5.5 Reattribution of research privileges or researcher status

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** Reattribution of Research Privileges or Researcher Status to Conduct Research Depending on the situation, Research Privileges or Researcher Status may be reattributed by the RI-MUHC ED/CSO.
