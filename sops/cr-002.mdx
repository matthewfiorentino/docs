## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the necessary organizational elements and planning of clinical studies with regards to roles, responsibilities and delegation of study tasks. This SOP identifies all members of the research team, and defines their roles and responsibilities as well as how to document roles, responsibilities and delegation of tasks.

## 2.0 Scope

This procedure concerns the research community (employees, Sponsor-Investigators, Investigators, physicians, management, consultants, fellows, residents, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in conducting research with human participants at the MUHC/RI-MUHC.

## 3.0 Responsibilities

All human research team members are responsible for performing and documenting their roles, as described in this document.

The Sponsor-Investigator, Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>) / Principal Investigator (<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>) is responsible for ensuring that the research team under his/her supervision complies with all guidelines, regulations, policies, and procedures.

## 4.0 Definitions

Refer to the [Glossary of Terms](/kb/foundations/glossary) (in the Clinical Research SOP section of the RI portal)

For the purposes of this SOP, the term Investigator will be used to refer to Sponsor-Investigator, QI, and PI.

## 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

<a id="building-a-research-team"></a>

### 5.1 Creation of a human research team

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** Human research team members must understand the responsibilities of the Investigator and his/her human research site, and be appropriately qualified by education, general training, study-specific training and experience to perform their study-related task(s).

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** In preparation for a human research study, the Investigator or his/her delegates should:

- Conduct a feasibility assessment prior to agreement to conduct the study. (Refer to [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004)).

- Appoint a Sub-Investigator with the same qualifications as the Investigator who is qualified and trained to temporarily replace the Investigator if needed. This Sub-Investigator must also obtain RI-MUHC [Privileges/Researcher Status](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status) (Note: the backup Sub-I cannot be a fellow nor resident as they are not eligible for Privileges/Researcher Status). Fellows and longitudinal residents can be named as sub-investigators but cannot replace the Investigator, the Task Delegation log must reflect exactly what duties may be carried out by fellows and longitudinal residents. Note: Residents on rotation cannot be named Sub-Is.

- Appoint members of the research team who will be involved in the study (before submitting to the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>/IEC, where applicable);
- Determine, prior to start of the study, each team member’s role, and the availability of relief personnel (e.g. <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip> backup) as well as any 3rd parties required;
- Determine the level of RI-MUHC institutional training that is required for the types of studies conducted by the team (e.g. SOP Level I, ICH-GCP, Health Canada Division 5 if working on studies with drug). Note that since training is study level dependent, the whole team should be trained at the same level for a given study. Refer to [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003) regarding training.

- Identify team members who need to obtain general, task-specific and protocol-specific training.

- Determine who should be trained by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator at the Site Initiation Visit (<Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip>) and when appropriate, schedule study-specific training on protocol and study document content and application; and
- Maintain a Training Log and a <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>, listing appropriate qualified and trained persons to whom the Investigator has delegated significant study-related duties. Once trained, the training is documented on the Training Log and the study team members must sign and date the Task Delegation Log in order to accept delegation of protocol tasks on the Task Delegation Log. The Investigator must sign the Task Delegation Log to confirm that each staff member is qualified and trained to conduct the study-specific task(s) that the Investigator is delegating. (Refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1) for the Task Delegation Log).

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** The Investigator must ensure that all persons assisting with the study are adequately informed/trained throughout the study, on the protocol and any amendments, investigational products, their study-related duties and functions, and that associated training is documented, by way of a certificate or recorded on a Training Log on an ongoing basis.

<a id="5-2"></a>

### 5.2 Roles and responsibilities of the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator is responsible for ensuring that:

- The human research study is scientifically sound and clearly described in a protocol and that any changes to the study are clearly documented in a protocol amendment. It is recommended to have study-specific SOPs describing the Sponsor’s/Sponsor-Investigator’s operating procedures.
- No human research site may start conducting the research prior to REB approval, MUHC Authorization and the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator’s Go letter.

- The Sponsor’s Trial Master File (<Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip>) is consistent with ICH-GCP section 8 (for drug studies) and/or ISO-GCP annex E (for medical device studies) as well as the site’s Investigator Site File (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>) i.e. both sets of essential documents are consistent and according to guidelines.

- The human research study is conducted, and the investigational product, if applicable, is used, in accordance with the REB-approved protocol;
- Systems and procedures that assure the quality of every aspect of the clinical study are implemented; e.g. A monitoring plan has been created and a monitor is dedicated to quality control (QC) of a multi-centre study.

- The methods used to assure and control the quality of the trial are risk-based and should focus on activities that ensure human participant protection and the reliability of trial results;
- The approval of a Research Ethics Board (REB) is obtained before the human research study begins at the site;
- There is no more than one QI/PI at each human research team site;
- Medical care and medical decisions, in respect of the human research study, are under the supervision of the QI/PI;
- All reportable SAEs are reported to the regulatory authorities (e.g Health Canada) and SUSARs are reported to all sites (where applicable);
- Each individual involved in the conduct of the human research study is qualified by education, training and experience to perform his or her respective tasks;
- Any trial-related duties and functions carried out on its behalf (e.g. by the CIM/ARO/CRO; vendor) are specified in writing, and that there is Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator oversight of those activities.
- An Master Informed Consent Form (<Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip>) template may be provided by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator to all sites in order for sites to obtain written informed consent from each participant. If the participant is in agreement, informed consent is given in accordance with the applicable laws governing consent. It is obtained from every participant before participation in the human research study, but only after the participant has been adequately informed of, and understands, the risks and anticipated benefits to his or her health arising from participation in the human research study, and informed of all other aspects of the study that are necessary to make the informed decision to participate in the study;
- The requirements respecting information and records set out in ICH-GCP and Section C.05.012 of the Health Canada, Food and Drug regulations are met (if applicable);he drug(s) is (are) manufactured, handled and stored in accordance with the applicable good manufacturing practice (GMP) regulations of Health Canada (if applicable).

- Where applicable, the investigational medical device study is conducted and records are kept according to ISO-14155 GCP guidelines and Health Canada regulations.

- If necessary, the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator should establish a Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) to assess the progress of a human research study, including the safety data and the critical efficacy end points and intervals, and to recommend whether to continue, modify or stop a study. The DSMB should have written operating procedures and maintain written records of all its meetings (ICH, E6 5.5.2).

<a id="5-3"></a>

### 5.3 Roles and responsibilities of the Investigator

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** The Investigator is responsible for the well-being of research participants, the conduct and documentation of the study according to guidelines and regulations, administration of the investigational product, if applicable, team and space requirements, conformity with the requirements of the REB and ongoing Investigator and team training.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** The Investigator must be aware of, and comply with applicable Regulations, SOPs, Guidance, Policies and requirements:

- Health Canada Regulations (drug, device or natural health product)
- ICH-E6 R2 GCP: Good Clinical Practice
- ISO 14155 GCP, Clinical investigation of medical devices for human subjects – Good clinical practice, if applicable.

- Provincial regulations
- RI-MUHC/MUHC Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)
- Tri-Council Policy Statement ([TCPS2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/policy-politique_tcps2-eptc2_2022.html))

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** The Investigator must obtain RI-MUHC Institutional Privileges (or Researcher Status for non-MD studies) to conduct human research through the online [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) platform (Refer to [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500) and information in the Privileges/Researcher Status section of the RI-MUHC portal). Additionally, they must obtain the appropriate level of institutional training suitable for the types of studies conducted (Refer to [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003) and information in the Training section of the RI-MUHC portal). Any Sub-Investigator delegated as an Investigator back-up must also obtain Privileges (or Researcher Status for non-MDs) in addition to the institutional training.

Note: Fellows and residents must not conduct human research independently. If conducting medical assessments, they must be co-signed by the QI/PI or the qualified Sub-Investigator with Privileges/Researcher Status.

<a id="5-3-4"></a>**5.3.4** The Investigator must ensure that human research team members have also obtained the same level of institutional training deemed appropriate for the study being conducted as per the [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003) Training Matrix.

<a id="5-3-5"></a>**5.3.5** Prior to study start, the Investigator must:

- Be qualified by education, training, and experience to assume responsibility for the proper conduct of the study, meet all the qualifications specified by the applicable regulatory requirements and guidelines and provide evidence of such qualifications through up-to-date curriculum vitae (CV) (i.e. initialed and dated every 2 years), up-to-date license (if applicable) and all other relevant documentation requested by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, the REB and the regulatory authorities;
- Ensure that research team members have documented training and current CVs and licenses are on file and available for audit and inspection. Note that the CVs should not contain any personal/family information (e.g. do not list children or home phone number). Refer to [Appendix 6](#appendix-6) for a suggested Abbreviated CV template.

- Be thoroughly familiar with all protocol procedures. Although some tasks may be delegated to other trained and qualified research team members, the Investigator should ensure the individual or party is qualified and trained to perform those study tasks. The Investigator maintains oversight and responsibility for supervising any individual or party to whom tasks are delegated at the trial site.

- Assume ultimate responsibility for the overall conduct of a human research study at his/her site and for compliance with all applicable regulations and guidelines;
- Be thoroughly familiar with the appropriate use of the investigational products, if applicable, as described in the protocol and Investigational Brochure (<Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip>), in any IB amendment, in product information, investigational medical device manual and in other information sources provided by the Sponsor or Sponsor/Investigator;
- Make sure a written and dated approval/favourable opinion from the REB for the study protocol, written informed consent form, consent form updates, participant recruitment procedures (e.g., advertisements) and any other written information to be provided to participants has been granted before the study is begun at the site;
- Make sure the study does not start until a written and dated MUHC Authorization letter has been obtained (after REB approval) as well as Sponsor written authorization to start e.g. Sponsor’s Go letter.

<a id="5-3-6"></a>**5.3.6** During the course of the study, the Investigator must also ensure that:

- The qualified Sub-Investigator(s) maintains their RI-MUHC Privileges and institutional training appropriate for the study (refer to [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500) and [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003)) and other persons delegated tasks, maintain the same level of RI-MUHC institutional training and all remain adequately trained on the protocol and study document amendments, investigational product(s) and their study-related tasks and roles;
- The study is conducted in compliance with the protocol approved by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, the REB and, if applicable, appropriate regulatory authorities, such as Health Canada;
- Informed Consent Form(s) has been created as per [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006) (and CAE templates) and Informed Consent has been obtained from each participant as per [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) and that the Informed Consent Process is documented;
- All study-related medical decisions that are made are documented, including those delegated to a qualified and trained Sub-Investigator (e.g. review/assessment of laboratory/imaging results for clinical significance, and assessment/ determination of causality for adverse events/drug reactions and <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reporting). Note: This delegation of Investigator responsibilities must first be clearly documented on the Task Delegation Log prior to tasks being conducted;

- Each participant is explained, the appropriate use of the investigational product and should verify, at regular intervals, if all participants are following instructions appropriately;
- Adequate medical care and follow-up is provided to a participant in the case of any adverse event and is documented;
- All serious adverse events and protocol violations/reportable events are immediately reported to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and unexpected and possibly related SAEs are reported to the REB and applicable regulatory authorities within 7 days. (Refer to [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007) and [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012));
- All medical oversight is documented by way of initialing and dating external safety reports (e.g. <Tooltip tip="A serious adverse reaction to a drug or natural health product whose nature, severity or frequency the Investigator's Brochure or product label does not anticipate; the sponsor reports it to Health Canada, and the site reports it to the REB when it meets the REB's criteria." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip> reports), lab and test results and any other document that requires medical oversight (e.g. monitoring reports and newsletters). Best practice is to write a Working Instruction specific to your department regarding how the QI/PI medical oversight is achieved and how these documents are distributed to team members.

- The data reported to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator in the Case Report Forms (CRFs) and in all required reports are accurate, complete, and timely.
- All study documents are maintained up-to-date as specified in [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015), ICH-GCP section 8/ ISO-GCP annex E (for medical device if applicable) and as required by the applicable regulatory requirements;
- Necessary measures are taken to prevent accidental or premature destruction of essential study documents for a clinical study;
- Monitoring and auditing of the clinical study is permitted by the Sponsor-Sponsor-investigator, RI-MUHC auditors as well as inspection by the appropriate regulatory authorities;
- All deviations from the REB-approved protocol and/or GCP are documented and reported appropriately to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and REB (if applicable) and
- They are aware of and comply with, any other applicable regulations and guidelines.

<a id="5-3-7"></a>**5.3.7** Although, in Quebec, for investigational drug studies, the task of Investigational Product (<Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip>) Management is delegated to the Pharmacy, the responsibility for IP at the study site rests with the Investigator and the institution. The Investigator should ensure that the IP is used only in accordance with the approved protocol and the investigator brochure and/or product monograph. In Quebec, under normal circumstances, the Investigator delegates’ tasks for IP(s) storage, dispensing, accountability and IP return/destruction at the study site to an appropriately trained pharmacist(s).

<a id="5-3-8"></a>**5.3.8** For Division 5 trials only: The QI must complete and sign a Health Canada, Qualified Investigator Undertaking (QIU) Form for clinical trials conducted in Canada. There must be no more than one QI per study per site (individual MUHC research team per protocol). By signing this form, the QI agrees to conduct the study in accordance with Canadian regulations and guidelines, and acknowledges his/her responsibilities as defined by the regulatory bodies. Note that the QIU is the Canadian version of the US 1572.

Note that Form FDA 1572 is not to be signed by MUHC/RI-MUHC Investigators given it requires Investigators conducting studies in Canada to follow US laws (foreign laws are not to overrule Canadian laws when a study is conducted in Canada). Sponsors of trials under a US IND may request Investigators to complete an investigator commitment form similar to the 1572 (as the QIU does not ask for confirmation of IB review, nor asks what laboratories will be involved). It is recommended that if a Sponsor does not provide a Canadianized version, the RI-MUHC form, ICH GCP attestation form included in [Appendix 4](#appendix-4) be completed and signed in lieu of the FDA1572.

### 5.4 Roles and responsibilities of the clinical research coordinator (CRC)

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** The CRC works in collaboration with the Investigator, and with a multidisciplinary research team to ensure that rigorous human research study standards are maintained. The CRC assesses the skills required and prior to task delegation, obtains the necessary initial and ongoing institutional training, general training, training for each protocol and delegated tasks, filing his/her documented training and CV with essential documents.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** It is the responsibility of the trainee to ensure that all required training is appropriate for the types of studies conducted and maintained up-to-date at all times. If a trainee is on leave for more than 6 months, training should be renewed. Note that the TalentLMS platform for SOP training sends a reminder to renew SOP training and the CITI platform will send a reminder to renew CITI courses every 2 years from training dates. Refer to [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003). Ensure that the Task Delegation Log reflects that the person on leave is undelegated and replaced by a backup study member with appropriate qualifications and training. Upon the person’s return from leave, ensure training is updated prior to re-delegation on the Task Delegation Log. Ensure to update the study Contact List and notify the Sponsor of the change in staff.

<a id="5-4-3"></a>**5.4.3** The specific tasks for the role of the CRC are described in the procedures of each SOP. However, in general the CRC (alone or with the assistance of other research team members):

- Works closely with the Investigator to organize, plan and carry out the human research study in an efficient and timely manner including: preparing the REB submission; creating source document forms to capture protocol required procedures; providing participants with all pertinent information regarding the study; coordinating participant appointments and Sponsor’s monitoring visits; executing study-related procedures with the authorization of the Investigator; and completing CRFs in a timely fashion, ensuring that <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> entries are consistent with source documents;
- Liaises with hospital departments (laboratory, pharmacy, radiology etc.), 3rd Parties and REB;
- Adheres to the REB-approved protocol, ICH-GCP/ISO-GCP, other guidelines, regulations and SOPs, remaining current on guidelines, regulations and SOPs by attending or obtaining regular refresher training; and
- Acts as a participant advocate.

<a id="5-5"></a>

### 5.5 Roles and responsibilities of other parties

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** Other parties may be involved in generating human research study data (e.g., research and testing facilities, pharmacy, radiology, teams on rotation etc.). The Investigator should ensure the party is qualified to perform those study tasks and implement procedures to ensure the integrity of the study tasks performed and any data generated. At the request of the Investigator, these specialists or supervisors of a team on rotation must be added to the Task Delegation Log. (Refer to [Appendix 2](#appendix-2) and 3).

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** The MUHC Pharmacy has their own set of Pharmacy specific SOPs which may be found in the [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview) platform and include the process of creating and maintaining their own Pharmacy Task Delegation Log. This completed log must be obtained from pharmacy and added to the Study Task Delegation Log prior to archiving with essential documents.

<a id="5-5-3"></a>**5.5.3** For external 3rd party vendors, have a negotiated contract or agreements in place e.g. if using CIM services, a CIM agreement must be in place.

### 5.6 Documentation of task delegation

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** The Investigator must maintain a list of appropriately qualified persons to whom he/she has delegated significant study-related tasks. (Refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1) for the Task Delegation Log template). This log is to be completed for site team members who will be involved in the study (i.e. prior to conducting any study-related task). This log should be maintained up-to-date throughout the study.

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** It is important to document the delegation of a Sub-Investigator with same qualifications, training and Privileges/Researcher Status as the QI/PI in order to have a qualified QI/PI backup.

For the purposes of grant applications, fellows and residents can be listed as Sub-Investigators on the protocol or grant proposal. In general, they should not however, be named as Sub-Investigator on the Task Delegation Log and other study essential documentation. On the Task Delegation Log fellows, residents and medical students can be named as student, fellow, resident or research assistant.

- Exceptions may be made in some departments where fellows and longitudinal residents may have the title of “Sub-I (fellow)” or “Sub-I (longitudinal resident)” however these Sub-Is must not be delegated unsupervised QI/PI tasks and can not be the QI/PI backup. The QI/PI must ensure that any QI/PI tasks conducted by a fellow or longitudinal resident is under the direct documented supervision of a QI/PI (i.e. all medical assessments are co-signed by the QI/PI/Sub-I with Privileges) and this should be clearly indicated on the Task Delegation Log. Additionally, the department should write a department-specific SOP to detail which tasks will be delegated to a fellow or longitudinal resident and how they will be supervised.

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** The signatures and initials of all persons delegated significant study related tasks must be recorded to permit an evaluation of the conduct of the human research study and the quality of the data. All persons listed on the Task Delegation Log must have already been appropriately trained to conduct the task(s) they are delegated and this training must be documented prior to the individual and the investigator signing the Task Delegation Log.

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** In the event that the role of Investigator must be reassigned to another party (e.g. the Investigator is no longer working at the MUHC), a new Investigator should be named (e.g. A qualified Sub-I with RI-MUHC research privileges or the head of the department with the same qualifications), the Sponsor must be informed, the contract must be revised and signed by both outgoing and incoming Investigator, the change should be documented (refer to [Appendix 5](#appendix-5)) and the REB must be informed via an amendment to a study in the Nagano system (Form F1) without delay.

<a id="5-7"></a>

### 5.7 Agreements for delegation of protocol task(s) to a team on rotation or an MD/ specialist

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** When study protocol task(s) are delegated to a person in charge of other staff on rotation (for example, a nurse manager, imaging manager, manager of medical technologists drawing blood etc.) further sub delegation to individual staff does not need to be documented on the Tasks Delegation Log, provided that evidence of qualification of those individuals is available.

The Manager/Supervisor (of other staff on rotation) delegated a study protocol task should:

- Document the Manager/Supervisor’s training appropriate to the task delegated to them. The Investigator’s study team should provide a completed Task Delegation to a Manager/Supervisor of a Team on Rotation - Protocol Specific Task &amp; GCP Training form ([Appendix 2](#appendix-2)) which should consist of a protocol summary or briefing concerning the task delegated, GCP/GDP summary and a signature page to be signed and dated.

- Subsequently sign and date the Task Delegation Log.

- Provide their updated CV and license (if applicable) to the QI/ study team to file with the essential documents.

- Ensure that her/his staff is qualified to conduct the task(s) (have staff CVs and licenses in her/his clinic/HR files, accessible in the event of a regulatory inspection).

- Ensure that the training provided to her/him is passed down to her/his staff prior to conducting the task and that this training is documented. Note that this sub-delegated training documentation may be subject to a regulatory inspection.

The Investigator/study team must ensure:

- There is documented training of the Manager/Supervisor by obtaining a signed Task Delegation to a Manager/Supervisor of a Team on Rotation - Protocol Specific Task &amp; GCP Training form.

- The trainer has signed the Task Delegation to a Manager/Supervisor of a Team on Rotation - Protocol Specific Task &amp; GCP Training form.
- The Investigator has also signed the Task Delegation to a Manager/Supervisor of a Team on Rotation - Protocol Specific Task &amp; GCP Training form.

- The Manager/Supervisor has signed and dated the Task Delegation Log, and the Investigator has signed off on this delegation.

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** When a specific task is delegated to an MD Specialist it is best practice to have an MD/Specialist sign the Task Delegation Log prior to conducting the protocol specific task. In this case, the MD/Specialist would be required to obtain ICH-GCP/ISO-GCP (where applicable), Health Canada Division 5 (if applicable) and the appropriate level of RI-MUHC SOP training. Note that if the MD is delegated more than a specific task and has overall medical oversight of the study, then the MD should be indicated as a Sub-I.

<a id="5-7-3"></a>**5.7.3** An alternative is for the MD/Specialist to sign an agreement instead. MD/Specialist referrals are not required to be on a Task Delegation Log (e.g. patient’s own ophthalmologist). MDs/Specialists who are delegated a specific task considered to be standard of care in the study protocol are still associated with the research and should sign off on training by way of a protocol summary or briefing concerning the task delegated and a GCP/GDP summary. The template in [Appendix 3](#appendix-3) (Research Study Specific MD/Specialist Referral Protocol Specific Task &amp; GCP Training) should be filled in, provided to the MD/Specialist who will be seeing the referred study participants.

<a id="5-8"></a>

### 5.8 Obtaining external source documents from other institutions/clinics

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** When obtaining source documents (e.g. reports, nursing or physician’s notes regarding medical history or referrals) from external clinics or hospitals, these source documents should be certified copies to ensure they are exact copies. Each document should be indicated as “Certified Copy” (i.e. handwritten or stamped) signed and dated by the person authorized to make the copy.
