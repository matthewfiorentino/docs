## Key points

- The **<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI** holds ultimate responsibility for all study activities at the site and must hold RI-MUHC Institutional Privileges or Researcher Status before any study begins.
- No person may perform any study task before they appear on the **Task Delegation Log**, their training is documented, and the QI/PI has confirmed they are qualified.
- **Fellows and rotating residents** cannot conduct research independently or serve as QI/PI backup. All medical acts they perform must be co-signed by a QI/PI or qualified Sub-Investigator with Privileges.
- In Quebec, **<Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> management** (storage, dispensing, accountability) is delegated to a trained pharmacist. The Investigator retains responsibility for ensuring IP is used per the approved protocol.
- If the **QI/PI changes** during a study, both outgoing and incoming QI/PI must sign the Task Delegation Log, the Sponsor and <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> must be notified, and a <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> amendment (Form F1) must be submitted without delay.

## 1 · Purpose and scope {#purpose-and-scope}

This SOP describes how to structure a research team, assign roles, and document the delegation of study tasks for human research conducted at the MUHC/RI-MUHC. It applies to all members of the research community involved in conducting research with human participants — including employees, Sponsor-Investigators, Investigators, physicians, fellows, residents, students, and volunteers.

All human research team members are responsible for performing and documenting their roles as described here. The <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">Qualified Investigator</Tooltip> (QI) / <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">Principal Investigator</Tooltip> (PI) is ultimately responsible for ensuring their team complies with all applicable guidelines, regulations, policies, and procedures.

<Note>
Throughout this SOP, the term **Investigator** refers collectively to the Sponsor-Investigator, QI, and PI. For role-specific distinctions, see Section 3.
</Note>

## 2 · Building a research team {#building-a-research-team}

Every team member must be qualified by education, training, and experience to perform their assigned tasks. In preparation for a study, the Investigator or their delegates must complete all of the following before the study begins.

- Conduct a feasibility assessment before agreeing to conduct the study. → [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004)
- Appoint a qualified Sub-Investigator as QI/PI backup who also holds RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status. _Fellows and rotating residents are not eligible for Privileges/Researcher Status and cannot serve as QI/PI backup. Longitudinal residents may be named as Sub-I (fellow) or Sub-I (longitudinal resident) in some departments — see Section 4.3._
- Appoint all research team members before submitting to the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>, where applicable.
- Confirm availability of backup personnel (e.g. <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip> cover) and any required third parties.
- Determine the required training level for the study type. The whole team must train at the same level for a given study. → [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003)
- Identify team members requiring general, task-specific, and protocol-specific training. Determine who will be trained by the Sponsor at the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">Site Initiation Visit</Tooltip> (SIV). → [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005)
- Set up the Training Log and <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> before any study tasks begin. All team members must sign and date the Task Delegation Log — and the QI/PI must countersign confirming qualification — before performing any study task. → Appendix 1

Throughout the study, the Investigator must ensure all team members remain informed and trained on protocol amendments, investigational products, and any changes to their duties. This training must be documented on an ongoing basis, by way of certificate or recorded on the Training Log.

### 3.1 Sponsor / Sponsor-Investigator

The Sponsor holds overall responsibility for the scientific integrity, conduct, and documentation of the study. Before the study begins, the Sponsor must ensure:

- The study is scientifically sound and clearly described in a protocol. Changes must be documented in a protocol amendment.
- No site begins research before REB approval, <Tooltip tip="Institutional authorization allowing a study to proceed at the RI-MUHC. Issued after ethics approval and feasibility review." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, and the Sponsor's Go Letter have all been issued.
- The Trial Master File (TMF) is maintained consistently with ICH-<Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> section 8 (drug studies) and/or ISO-GCP Annex E (medical device studies).
- A monitoring plan is in place. Quality control procedures must be risk-based and focus on participant protection and the reliability of trial results.
- All reportable SAEs are reported to regulatory authorities (e.g. Health Canada) and SUSARs are distributed to all sites.
- Every person involved is qualified by education, training, and experience for their tasks.
- Any duties delegated to a CRO or third party are specified in writing, with documented Sponsor oversight.
- Informed consent is obtained from every participant before participation. → [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006), [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008)
- Investigational products are manufactured, handled, and stored in accordance with applicable Health Canada GMP regulations, where applicable.
- For medical device studies: the study is conducted and records are kept in accordance with ISO 14155 and Health Canada regulations.

<Note>
**Regulatory note:** If required, the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator must establish a <Tooltip tip="Independent committee that reviews interim safety and efficacy data and recommends whether to continue, modify, or stop the study." headline="Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#dsmb">Data Safety Monitoring Board</Tooltip> (DSMB) with written operating procedures and records of all meetings. **(ICH E6 5.5.2)**
</Note>

### 3.2 Qualified Investigator / Principal Investigator (QI/PI)

<Note>
**Privileges requirement:** The QI/PI must hold RI-MUHC Institutional Privileges (or Researcher Status for non-MD studies) and have completed the required level of institutional training before the study begins. Any Sub-I serving as QI/PI backup must also hold Privileges or Researcher Status. → [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500), [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003)
</Note>

The QI/PI is ultimately responsible for participant well-being and the overall conduct of the study at their site. **There must be no more than one QI per study per site.** The QI/PI must be aware of and comply with applicable regulations and guidelines including Health Canada Regulations, ICH E6 R2 GCP, ISO 14155 (if applicable), provincial regulations, RI-MUHC SOPs, and TCPS2.

**Before the study begins, the QI/PI must:**

- Have a current CV (initialled and dated within the past 2 years) and any required licences on file. _CVs must not include personal or family information. See Appendix 6 for the abbreviated CV template._
- Ensure all team members have documented training and current CVs and licences on file, available for audit and inspection.
- Be thoroughly familiar with the protocol and, where applicable, the Investigational Brochure (<Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip>), investigational product, and investigational medical device manual.
- Obtain written REB approval for the protocol, <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip>, recruitment materials, and all participant-facing documents before the study begins.
- Confirm that a written MUHC Authorization letter and the Sponsor's Go Letter are both in hand before the first participant is enrolled.
- Ensure that the Sub-Investigator appointed as backup also holds Privileges (or Researcher Status for non-MD studies) and appropriate institutional training. _Fellows and residents must not conduct human research independently. If conducting medical assessments, they must be co-signed by the QI/PI or a qualified Sub-I with Privileges/Researcher Status._

<Warning>
**Division 5 drug trials only:** The QI must complete and sign the Health Canada **Qualified Investigator Undertaking (QIU)** form. There must be no more than one QI per study per site. The QIU is the Canadian equivalent of the US FDA Form 1572. **Do not sign FDA Form 1572** — it requires investigators in Canada to follow US laws, which takes precedence over Canadian law. If a US Sponsor requires it, use the RI-MUHC ICH GCP Attestation form (Appendix 4) instead.
</Warning>

**During the study, the QI/PI must:**

- Maintain direct supervision and oversight of all delegated study activities. Although tasks may be delegated, the QI/PI retains accountability and responsibility for all study activities at the site.
- Ensure all Sub-Investigators maintain their RI-MUHC Privileges and institutional training appropriate for the study, and that all team members remain trained on protocol amendments and their study tasks. → [SOP-CR-500](/sops/cr-500), [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003)
- Ensure informed consent is obtained from every participant as per [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008), and that the process is documented.
- Document all study-related medical decisions, including those delegated to a qualified Sub-I (e.g. review of lab/imaging results, assessment of causality for AEs and SAEs). _This delegation must first be documented on the Task Delegation Log before the task is performed._
- Provide adequate medical care and follow-up for any participant adverse events, and document it.
- Report all SAEs and protocol violations to the Sponsor immediately. Report unexpected and possibly related SAEs to the REB and applicable regulatory authorities **within 7 days**. → [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007), [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012)
- Initial and date all external safety reports (e.g. SUSAR reports), lab and test results, and any other documents requiring medical oversight.
- Ensure data reported in CRFs and required reports is accurate, complete, and timely.
- Maintain all study documents up-to-date as per [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015), ICH-GCP section 8, and ISO-GCP Annex E (medical device studies). → [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015)
- Allow monitoring, auditing, and regulatory inspection of the study as required.
- Document and report all protocol deviations to the Sponsor and REB as applicable.

<Note>
**IP management — Quebec-specific:** In Quebec, for investigational drug studies, the QI delegates IP storage, dispensing, accountability, and return/destruction to an appropriately trained pharmacist at the study site. The Investigator and the institution retain ultimate responsibility for the investigational product. → [SOP-CR-010](/sops/cr-010)
</Note>

### 3.3 Sub-Investigator

The Sub-Investigator assists the QI/PI with study-specific tasks and may serve as QI/PI backup — but only if they hold RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status. Sub-Is must hold at minimum the same training level required for the study.

<Warning>
**Fellows and residents:** Fellows and longitudinal residents may appear on the Task Delegation Log as **Sub-I (fellow)** or **Sub-I (longitudinal resident)** in departments where this has been established, but cannot be delegated unsupervised QI/PI tasks and **cannot serve as QI/PI backup**. All medical assessments they perform must be co-signed by the QI/PI or a qualified Sub-I with Privileges. Departments that delegate tasks to fellows or longitudinal residents must have a department-specific SOP detailing which tasks are delegated and how supervision is documented. _Rotating residents cannot be named as Sub-Is._
</Warning>

### 3.4 Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)

The CRC works in collaboration with the Investigator and the multidisciplinary research team to ensure that rigorous study standards are maintained. The CRC is responsible for assessing the skills required and obtaining appropriate institutional, general, and protocol-specific training before tasks are delegated.

<Warning>
**Training currency:** It is the CRC's responsibility to ensure all required training remains current at all times. **If a CRC is on leave for more than 6 months, training must be renewed before returning to study tasks.** TalentLMS sends reminders for SOP training renewal; CITI sends reminders every 2 years for GCP training. → [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003)
</Warning>

When a CRC goes on leave, the Task Delegation Log must reflect that the person is undelegated and that a trained backup has been named. On return, training must be updated and the QI/PI must sign off before re-delegation. The Sponsor must be notified of staff changes and the study contact list must be updated.

Typical CRC responsibilities include:

- Preparing REB submissions and maintaining correspondence via <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>
- Creating source document forms and completing CRFs in a timely manner, ensuring <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> entries match source documents
- Providing study information to participants and coordinating participant appointments and Sponsor monitoring visits
- Executing study-related procedures with the authorization of the Investigator
- Liaising with hospital departments (laboratory, pharmacy, radiology) and third parties
- Collecting and reporting AEs/SAEs to the QI/PI for medical assessment
- Maintaining the ISF and essential documents
- Remaining current on applicable guidelines, regulations, and SOPs through regular training
- Acting as participant advocate

### 3.5 Other parties

Other parties may generate study data (e.g. research and testing facilities, pharmacy, radiology, rotating teams). The Investigator must ensure each party is qualified for their tasks and that procedures are in place to maintain data integrity. At the Investigator's request, specialists or supervisors of rotating teams must be added to the Task Delegation Log.

For external third-party vendors, a negotiated contract or agreement must be in place before work begins (e.g. a CIM agreement for CIM services).

| Responsibility | QI / PI | Sub-I | CRC | Pharmacy / 3rd party |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| AE / SAE assessment & reporting | **Accountable** | If delegated | Collects & reports | — |
| Informed consent | **Accountable** | If delegated | If delegated | — |
| IP management | **Accountable** | Coordinates | Coordinates | Pharmacy executes |
| ISF / essential documents | **Accountable** | Contributes | Maintains daily | Own logs to ISF |

## 4 · Task delegation {#task-delegation}

**The golden rule:** No person may conduct any study task before they appear on the Task Delegation Log, their training is documented, and the QI/PI has signed confirming they are qualified. The log must reflect the actual team at all times.

### 4.1 What must be on the Task Delegation Log

- Name, role, and title of each delegated person
- Specific tasks delegated to each person
- Delegation start date (and end date if applicable)
- Delegate's initials and signature
- QI/PI signature confirming the person is trained and qualified — _training must be documented before signing_
- CV and licence on file for each delegate
- Pharmacy Task Delegation Log appended before archiving (drug studies)

Use the Appendix 1 template. → Appendix 1 — Task Delegation Log

<Note>
**Signatures and inspection:** The signatures and initials of all delegated persons must be recorded to permit evaluation of the study's conduct and data quality. All delegated persons must have been appropriately trained and that training documented _prior to_ both the delegate and the QI/PI signing the log. If an inspection examines the log, undated, unsigned, or after-the-fact entries are findings.
</Note>

### 4.2 When to update the log

| Situation | Action required |
|---|---|
| Before day one | All team members must be on the log before performing any study task. No exceptions. |
| New staff joins | Add to the log and delegate before the person performs any task. Update the study contact list and notify the Sponsor. |
| Staff goes on leave | Undelegate the person, document the end date, and name a trained backup. On return, update training and verify qualification before re-delegating. |
| Tasks change | Update the log before new tasks are performed — not after. |
| QI/PI changes | Both outgoing and incoming QI/PI must sign. Notify the Sponsor, revise the contract, and notify the REB via a Nagano amendment (Form F1) **without delay**. → Appendix 5 |

### 4.3 Who can and cannot be delegated

<Note>
**Grant applications vs. study documents:** For grant applications, fellows and residents may be listed as Sub-Investigators on the protocol or grant proposal. However, they should generally not be named as Sub-Investigators on the Task Delegation Log or other essential study documentation. On the Task Delegation Log they may be listed as student, fellow, resident, or research assistant.
</Note>

| Category | Can be delegated study tasks? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-Investigators (with Privileges) | Yes — including QI/PI backup | Must hold RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status |
| CRCs and research nurses | Yes | Most common delegates for operational tasks |
| Research assistants / managers | Yes — for appropriate tasks | Qualify by education, training, and experience |
| MD specialists | Yes — specific tasks or Sub-I role | Sign Task Delegation Log or use Appendix 3 agreement |
| Fellows / longitudinal residents | Limited — cannot be QI/PI backup | All medical acts must be co-signed by QI/PI or Sub-I with Privileges. Departments must have a specific SOP for this. |
| Rotating residents | Limited — cannot be Sub-Is | Cannot conduct research independently; all medical acts require co-signature |
| Medical students | Very limited | All tasks require direct supervision and co-signature |

## 5 · Special delegation situations {#special-delegation-situations}
### 5.1 Teams on rotation

When a study task is delegated to a manager or supervisor of a rotating team (e.g. nurse manager, imaging manager, or phlebotomy team manager), individual staff members under that manager do not each need to appear on the Task Delegation Log — provided evidence of their qualifications is available in clinic or HR files.

The manager/supervisor must:

- Complete the protocol briefing and GCP/GDP summary — the study team provides the completed Appendix 2 form, which includes a protocol summary for the specific task and a GCP/GDP summary; both the trainer and the Investigator must sign this form
- Sign and date the Task Delegation Log after training is complete
- Provide an updated CV and licence (if applicable) to the study team for filing with essential documents
- Ensure their own staff are qualified to perform the task before it is conducted, and that protocol training is passed down and documented — _this sub-delegated training documentation may be subject to regulatory inspection_

→ Appendix 2 — Team on Rotation — Protocol Specific Task & GCP Training form

### 5.2 MD specialists

When a specific study task is delegated to an MD specialist, best practice is to have the specialist sign the Task Delegation Log before performing the task. In that case, the specialist must obtain appropriate ICH-GCP/ISO-GCP training (where applicable), Health Canada Division 5 training (if applicable), and the appropriate RI-MUHC SOP training level.

If the specialist has overall medical oversight of the study rather than one specific task, they should be listed as a Sub-Investigator.

Alternatively, the specialist may sign the Appendix 3 agreement form. Specialists performing tasks that are standard of care within the protocol must still receive a protocol briefing and GCP/GDP summary.

<Note>
Routine referrals to a participant's own specialist (e.g. their own ophthalmologist) who do not conduct protocol-specific tasks are not required to appear on the Task Delegation Log.
</Note>

→ Appendix 3 — MD/Specialist Referral — Protocol Specific Task & GCP Training form

### 5.3 External source documents

Source documents obtained from external clinics or hospitals (e.g. physician notes, referral letters, imaging reports) must be certified copies. Each document must be marked **"Certified Copy"** (handwritten or stamped), signed, and dated by the person authorized to make the copy, to confirm it is an exact copy of the original.
