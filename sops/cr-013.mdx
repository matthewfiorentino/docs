## 1 · Purpose {#purpose}
This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes:
- The responsibilities of the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator regarding the obligation to monitor the quality of all aspects of the human research study;
- The Sponsor-Investigator's monitoring processes to conduct, manage and document monitoring of all sites involved in the study.
- The responsibilities of the Qualified Investigator (QI)/Principal Investigator (PI) to prepare for and allow/facilitate monitoring at the site; and
- The QI/PI's processes to promote, manage and document monitoring visits, respond to monitoring issues and communicate with the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and/or their qualified assigned Monitor regarding a single site.
---

## 2 · Scope {#scope}
This procedure concerns the research community (employees, sponsor-investigators, investigators, physicians, management, consultants, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in preparation, facilitating, performing, documenting or responding to the monitoring visits and communication regarding the quality of the human research data collection at the MUHC/RI-MUHC.

This SOP procedure includes templates to facilitate the Sponsor-Investigator and delegated clinical research team (Project Manager, Monitor, Data Manager) responsible for ensuring quality control (QC) monitoring and Quality Assurance (QA) throughout the course of the research study. In addition, this SOP is meant as a general guide from which the Sponsor-Investigator may create their own SOPs/Working Instructions (WI).
---

## 3 · Responsibilities {#responsibilities}
The **Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator** and the **QI/PI** are responsible for ensuring their respective roles in the monitoring and communication processes meet all applicable regulatory, ICH-<Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>, ISO-GCP, Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, and local requirements.

Any or all parts may be delegated to appropriately trained team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator or QI/PI. Sponsor-Investigators at the RI-MUHC may delegate to a CRO or academic research organization (ARO), such as the CIM.

## 5 · Procedure {#procedure}
### 5.1 Monitoring visits — general

**5.1.1** The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator should develop a risk-based Monitoring Plan (MP) based on the study design. The MP provides the Monitor with a roadmap for quality control and <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qa">quality assurance</Tooltip>, based on the established site-specific SOPs, to ensure compliance with the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>-approved protocol, GCP, and regulations.

**5.1.2** A Sponsor's/Sponsor-Investigator's Monitor should be dedicated to the study to conduct and document the following monitoring visits and communications:
- **Feasibility Visit** — ensure site selection is appropriate → [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004)
- **Site Initiation Visit (SIV)** — final assessment of site readiness, staff training on study materials → [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005)
- **Ongoing monitoring visits** — assess compliance with protocol, SOPs, GCP, and regulations; may be in person or remote
- **Each communication** with the site (focused visit, VC, phone call)
- **Close-Out Visit (COV)** — ensure <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>/<Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip>, source data, and eCRF entries are complete → [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016)

<Note>
A monitoring report must be written after each monitoring visit, and a follow-up Monitoring Letter must be sent to the site. Documentation of all important communications must be filed in the ISF.
</Note>

**5.1.3** The frequency of ongoing monitoring visits (outlined in the MP) may increase based on: type and complexity of the study; enrollment rate; site experience and staff turnover; monitoring observations, protocol deviations, or data queries; and number of SAEs.

**5.1.4** Typically, the first post-SIV monitoring visit occurs after one or two participants have been enrolled, to confirm site understanding of protocol requirements. The monitoring schedule is generally confirmed on a visit-by-visit basis.

**5.1.5** The Monitor is usually the main point of contact between the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and the site, and acts as a key study resource. Any ambiguity in the protocol, study conduct, or logistics should be clarified by the Monitor.

**5.1.6** The Monitor's assigned tasks include (not limited to):
- Acts as main communication point between the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and QI/PI
- Verifies QI/PI and team qualifications and training throughout the study
- Verifies facilities and equipment remain adequate for safe study conduct
- Verifies <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> management: supplies, storage, dispensing to eligible participants only, return/destruction
- Verifies the QI/PI follows the REB-approved protocol and all amendments
- Verifies written informed consent was obtained before each participant's screening
- Ensures the QI/PI receives and has read the <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip> and all study documents
- Reports participant recruitment rate
- Verifies source documentation is accurate, complete, and up-to-date
- Verifies all required reports, notifications, and submissions are accurate, complete, timely, legible, dated, and identified
- Conducts Source Data Verification (SDV) to ensure <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> accuracy and completeness
- Informs the QI/PI of CRF entry omissions, errors, or illegibility and ensures corrections are made, dated, explained (if necessary), and initialled
- Determines if all AEs are reported per GCP, regulations, REB, and SOPs
- Determines if the QI/PI is maintaining the ISF per ICH-GCP or ISO-GCP
- Communicates observed deviations to the QI/PI in writing; ensures <Tooltip tip="Actions taken to resolve a compliance issue and prevent recurrence: identify, correct, prevent." headline="Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#capa">CAPA</Tooltip> is implemented
- Submits written monitoring report to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator after each site visit or communication

**5.1.7** If noncompliance is observed that significantly affects or has the potential to affect participant protection or data integrity, the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator must perform a root cause analysis and implement appropriate CAPA.

### 5.2 Sponsor-Investigator preparation of Monitoring Plan

**5.2.1** It is the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator's responsibility to develop a systematic, prioritized, risk-based approach to monitoring.

**5.2.2** Ensure a MP is in place describing the monitoring strategy (on-site, remote, or combined), rationale, and that an independent dedicated monitor is assigned.

<Note>
The Monitor is part of the Sponsor-Investigator team, not the site team — the Monitor is not listed on the site <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> and must remain independent of site operations. Site QC (e.g. QC of source document forms) should be conducted by the site, not by the Monitor.
</Note>

**5.2.3** The MP must include processes for addressing non-compliance and identify events that would require a revision to the MP.

**5.2.4** Per Health Canada GUI-0100, the MP should include:
- The monitoring strategy and monitoring responsibilities of all parties
- Various monitoring methods and the rationale for their use
- Monitoring of critical data and processes, with attention to non-routine clinical practices requiring additional training
- References to applicable policies and SOPs

**5.2.5** For a Monitoring Plan template, refer to Appendix 3.

### 5.3 Monitor's preparation for a site visit

**5.3.1** Preparation should be done in accordance with the MP and established Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator SOPs, appropriate to the type of visit.

**5.3.2** The Monitor should prepare protocol-specific tools to conduct the visit and capture observations.

**5.3.3** Before any visit (feasibility, SIV, ongoing monitoring, close-out) — whether in person or virtual — a pre-visit confirmation letter or email must be sent to the site to allow preparation time and confirm availability of required personnel. → Appendix 4.

### 5.4 Monitor's protocol feasibility visit

**5.4.1** Per [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004), the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator should confirm site feasibility — access to appropriate study population, adequate facilities, qualified and experienced staff.

**5.4.2** The Monitor writes a pre-study Protocol Feasibility Report (filed in the TMF). → Appendix 5.

### 5.5 Monitor's Site Initiation Visit

**5.5.1** Once the REB has provided written approval, the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and/or Monitor conducts an SIV to review all study materials and ensure compliant study conduct per their SOPs, GCP, and regulations. → [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005). Use the SIV Report template at Appendix 6.

### 5.6 Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator's Go Letter

**5.6.1** Once the SIV report has been approved and the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator determines the site to be compliant with start-up requirements, a Go Letter is sent to the site providing written authorization to commence the study. → Appendix 7.

### 5.7 Monitor's preparation for ongoing monitoring visits

**5.7.1** The Monitor prepares tools to capture all observations during the visit.

**5.7.2** Typically after the first enrolled participant (or 2+ screen failures), the Monitor prepares and sends a pre-visit confirmation letter/email. → Appendix 4.

### 5.8 MUHC/RI-MUHC site preparation for an ongoing monitoring visit

**5.8.1** The QI/PI must be available at the time of the monitoring visit. Schedule visits with all applicable parties (e.g. pharmacy).

**5.8.2** Ensure all documentation (paper and/or electronic) is up-to-date before each monitoring visit.

**5.8.3** Complete applicable CRFs on a timely basis.

**5.8.4** Review the SIV report or previous monitoring letters; ensure the QI/PI has initialled and dated the report/letter to document medical oversight.

**5.8.5** Ensure all outstanding issues from the last monitoring visit have been resolved. Complete any outstanding CRF corrections, additions, or deletions (with explanations if necessary). Obtain QI/PI or delegate initials and dates.

**5.8.6** Ensure the regulatory binder/file, participant data binders, and essential documents are up-to-date. Ensure data stored in other departments (e.g. pharmacy IP management documentation) is also current.

**5.8.7** Ensure Monitors receive documented OACIS training and appropriate access (if required) to verify participant existence and review source documents. → Section 5.9.

**5.8.8** Have all paper and/or electronic records available for review, along with corresponding source documents.

**5.8.9** Follow all institutional requirements for permitting third parties on site (security pass, escorting, providing confidential work space).

**5.8.10** The Monitor must have limited access to appropriate premises and must be accompanied by a site employee if entering areas where confidential material is stored.

**5.8.11** Update the Protocol Deviation (Exceptions and Violations) Log and make it available for Monitor review. Ensure all reportable violations have been reported to the REB. The site should conduct its own QC prior to and independent of the Monitor's QC.

**5.8.12** Prepare a Monitoring Log for the <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip> and Monitor to sign on the visit date. → Appendix 1.

### 5.9 Request EMR training and appropriate Monitor access

**5.9.1** The Monitor should have electronic medical record (EMR) access and training to verify participant source data.

**5.9.2** At all MUHC/RI-MUHC sites, the MUHC Medical Records department provides temporary restricted OACIS access to external Monitors. An initial request must be submitted to Medical Records with the following:
- MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement signed by the Monitor → [SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019)
- DPS Chart Request form, signed by all parties (monitor, QI/PI, supervisor), sent to DPS (cathy.martell@muhc.mcgill.ca)
- Letter from the QI/PI requesting access: reason, role, study number, and list of patient MRN numbers (must be updated regularly) → Appendix 2
- Copy of the study's <Tooltip tip="Institutional authorization allowing a study to proceed at the RI-MUHC. Issued after ethics approval and feasibility review." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> letter
- Copy of the study's initial REB Approval letter
- When a Monitor changes, notify the archivist to terminate the prior Monitor's access

**5.9.3** MUHC Medical Records will inform the OACIS team of approval and provide the Monitor's full name and duration of access.

**5.9.4** The OACIS Training and Support team creates the OACIS ID and, based on a list from the CRC, Medical Records creates a restricted patient list and arranges OACIS training for untrained Monitors.

**5.9.5** When the Monitor no longer works on the study, notify Medical Records to remove OACIS access.

**5.9.6** The CRC must renew the Monitor's OACIS ID annually by submitting the study's Annual Renewal REB approval to Medical Records.

**5.9.7** Medical Records requires **5 working days' notice** for all new OACIS ID requests, new patient list requests, or additions to a current patient list.

Medical Records generic email (MCH, MGH, RVH): Archiviste.MGH@muhc.mcgill.ca

### 5.10 Site actions to facilitate the monitoring visit

**5.10.1** When the Monitor sends a written confirmation of an upcoming visit, alert other departments as required (e.g. pharmacy) and file the confirmation letter in the monitoring section of the ISF.

**5.10.2** Be prepared to provide or verify the following during monitoring visits:
- Investigator and team qualifications, resources, and site facilities remain adequate
- Current team list with CVs, licenses, training records, and task delegation
- IP has been stored, handled, dispensed, and destroyed/returned per protocol and Sponsor requirements
- Study conduct is compliant with the currently approved protocol/amendments, GCP, and regulations
- Signed informed consent (with latest REB-approved <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> version) obtained before each participant's screening; only eligible participants enrolled
- Signed re-consent forms obtained in a timely fashion, if applicable
- Participant recruitment logs and original targets and timelines
- Source documents, other study records, and essential documents are accurate, complete, up-to-date, and filed appropriately
- All required REB documents, REB approval letters, and Sponsor's Go Letters are on file
- All SAEs, SUSARs, or Medical Device Incidents reported within required timeframes (Sponsor, protocol, REB, GCP, Health Canada/FDA/other)
- Protocol Deviation Log is up-to-date with CAPA taken for any deviations noted in previous communications
- All documents requiring QI/PI oversight have been initialled and dated by the QI/PI (protocols, amendments, eligibility criteria, <Tooltip tip="Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction — unexpected in nature, severity, or frequency; must be reported to Health Canada and the REB." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip>/<Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reports, monitoring reports, newsletters, medically sensitive information)

**5.10.3** The Monitor checks CRF accuracy and completeness, verifies entries against source documents, and informs the QI/PI and CRC of any discrepancies. Source data/CRF discrepancies are documented by the Monitor as queries sent to the CRC and QI/PI.

**5.10.4** Protocol deviations observed by the Monitor must be recorded on the Protocol Deviation Log, reported to the Sponsor within 24 hours, and reported to the REB per local procedures. At the MUHC REB: violations reported within 7 days; exceptions submitted at Annual Continuing Review.

**5.10.5** No study document with patient identifiers may leave the institution. De-identify documents if the Monitor requires a copy.

**5.10.6** Ensure the Monitor and site CRC sign the Monitoring Log before the Monitor departs. File the Monitoring Log in the ISF with monitoring letters.

### 5.11 Monitoring report, follow-up letter, and site follow-up

**5.11.1** The Monitor submits a written Monitoring Visit Report (MVR) to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator after each site visit (on-site and remote). The MVR is filed in the TMF. → Appendix 8.

**5.11.2** The Monitor forwards a summary of findings and required follow-up actions to the site as a monitoring letter. → Appendix 9.

**5.11.3** The QI/PI must initial and date the monitoring letter to document medical oversight. File the initialled and dated letter in the ISF.

**5.11.4** The QI/PI (or delegate):
- Follows up with the Monitor to obtain the monitoring summary if not received promptly
- Reviews findings, deviations, deficiencies, conclusions, and actions required
- Clarifies action items with the Monitor and implements remedial action
- Responds to each monitoring observation in writing and promptly to all requests

**5.11.5** File the initialled and dated monitoring letter and any CAPA response in the ISF.

### 5.12 Additional Monitor communication between visits

**5.12.1** The Monitor may contact the site by telephone or email for study status updates or to transmit information.

**5.12.2** A Remote Monitor may also contact the site with queries about eCRF data.

**5.12.3** Respond promptly to requests and retain copies of all correspondence in the ISF.

### 5.13 Site communication with the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/Monitor

**5.13.1** Define communication channels during study implementation; clarify each team member's function. Request a contact list from the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/CRO if not provided.

**5.13.2** Organize and facilitate meetings (in-person or virtual) and other communications with all parties throughout the study. Document all discussions.

**5.13.3** General guidelines for documenting communications:
- Maintain a Telephone Log for each study → [SOP-CR-009](/sops/cr-009); document all calls chronologically; follow up on calls where study decisions were made via email
- Document all communications: names of participants, meeting date, summary of discussions, and resolutions
- Record the date of dispatch or receipt of all documents. Print fax transmittal reports (if available) or record key information (time, date, sender)

### 5.14 Monitor's close-out visit

**5.14.1** Per [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016), once the last participant has completed the last visit and all CRFs are complete, the Monitor schedules the Close-Out Monitoring Visit to confirm accuracy and completeness of CRFs, source data, and regulatory documentation, and to reconcile and collect study materials.

**5.14.2** → Appendix 10 (Monitor's Close-Out Visit Report — TMF) and Appendix 11 (Site Notification of Close-Out — ISF).

### 5.15 Site quality control of monitoring documentation

**5.15.1** Ensure that for every monitoring visit on the Monitoring Log, there is a corresponding Follow-Up Letter that has been initialled and dated by the QI/PI, and that all queries and CAPA have been answered, resolved, and filed.

**5.15.2** Ensure all deviations from the approved protocol, GCP, or Health Canada regulations have been documented and reported per institutional and REB SOPs.

**5.15.3** MUHC/RI-MUHC violations must also be reported by the site to the RI-MUHC Quality Assurance Department: qaclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca
