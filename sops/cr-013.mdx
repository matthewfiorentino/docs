## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes:

- The responsibilities of the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator regarding the obligation to monitor the quality of all aspects of the human research study;
- The Sponsor-Investigator’s monitoring processes to conduct, manage and document monitoring of all sites involved in the study.

- The responsibilities of the Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>)/Principal Investigator (<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>) to prepare for and allow/facilitate monitoring at the site; and
- The QI/PI’s processes to promote, manage and document monitoring visits, respond to monitoring issues and communicate with the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and/or their qualified assigned Monitor regarding a single site.

## 2.0 Scope

This procedure concerns the research community (employees, sponsor-investigators, investigators, physicians, management, consultants, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in preparation, facilitating, performing, documenting or responding to the monitoring visits and communication regarding the quality of the human research data collection at the MUHC/RI-MUHC.

This SOP procedure includes templates to facilitate the Sponsor-Investigator and delegated clinical research team (Project Manager, Monitor, Data Manager) responsible for ensuring quality control (QC) monitoring and Quality Assurance (<Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's Quality Assurance department, which checks the credentials and training of each investigator who submits a study, audits research and maintains the SOPs. In GCP, quality assurance is the planned, systematic actions that ensure a trial complies with GCP and the applicable regulatory requirements." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip>) throughout the course of the research study. In addition, this SOP is meant as a general guide from which the Sponsor-Investigator may create their own SOPs/ Working Instructions (WI).

This SOP should be read in parallel with [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004) regarding the Feasibility Visit, [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005) regarding the Study Initiation Visit (<Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip>) and [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016) regarding the Close-Out Visit which are also usually conducted by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator’s Monitor.

## 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and Qualified Investigator (QI)/Principal Investigator (PI) are responsible for ensuring that their respective roles in the monitoring and communication processes meet all of the applicable regulatory, International Council on Harmonisation (ICH) Good Clinical Practice (GCP), International Standards Organization (ISO) Good Clinical Practice (GCP), Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, and local requirements.

Any or all parts of this procedure may be delegated to appropriately trained human research team members, but remain the ultimate responsibility of the Sponsor/ Sponsor-Investigator, or QI/PI, as defined in Health Canada regulations. Sponsor-Investigators at the RI-MUHC may delegate to a clinical research organization (CRO) or academic research organization (ARO) (eg CIM).

## 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

### 5.1 Monitoring visits - general

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1** The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator should develop a risk-based monitoring plan (MP) based on the study design which will provide their Monitor with a roadmap of how quality control and quality assurance will be conducted. The MP should be based on guidelines provided by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator’s established site-specific SOPs to ensure compliance with the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approved protocol, GCP and regulations.

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** A Sponsor’s/Sponsor-Investigator’s Monitor (from the Sponsor’s organization or a CRO/ARO) should be dedicated to the study in order to conduct and document the following Monitoring Visits/Communications:

- Feasibility Visit to ensure the site selection is appropriate for the study conduct. Refer to [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004).

- Site Initiation Visit (SIV) at each site where the Monitor does a last assessment of the site’s readiness to conduct the study and trains the site staff on study materials and conduct. Refer to [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005).

- Ongoing investigator site monitoring visits to assess compliance with the protocol, Sponsor’s/Sponsor-Investigator’s SOPs, GCP and applicable regulations. These visits may be in person or conducted remotely.

- Each communication with the site (focused monitoring visit, VC, phone call etc)
- Close-Out Visit (COV) to ensure that the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>/<Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip>, source data and eCRF entries meet all requirements. Refer to [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016).

Note: A monitoring report should be written after each monitoring visit and a follow-up Monitoring Letter should be sent to the site. Documentation of important communications should be filed in ISF.

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** Once the Feasibility Visit and SIV have occurred the frequency of ongoing monitoring site visits (or remote monitoring visits), outlined in the MP may increase based on many factors, including:

- The type and complexity of the study and need for additional site training,
- The rate of enrollment,
- The site experience and staff turnover,
- The number of monitoring observations, protocol deviations or data management queries,
- The number of SAEs.

<a id="5-1-4"></a>**5.1.4** Typically, Sponsors/Sponsor-Investigators/Monitors conduct the first post-SIV monitoring visit at each site after one or two participants have been enrolled into the study to ensure site understanding of the protocol requirements and proper conduct of the study. The MP (on-site/remote and expected frequency) is usually summarized during the SIV, however, the schedule is generally confirmed on a visit-by-visit basis, as the study progresses.

<a id="5-1-5"></a>**5.1.5** The Monitor is usually the main contact between the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and the QI/PI’s site, and acts as a key study resource for the site. Any ambiguity in the protocol, study conduct or study logistics should be clarified by the Monitor.

<a id="5-1-6"></a>**5.1.6** The Monitor’s assigned tasks include (and are not limited to) the following (note that ISO-GCP delegates responsibility with task):

- Acts as main point of communication between the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and the QI/PIs.

- Verifies QI/PI and team have adequate qualifications and training throughout the study.

- Verifies facilities and equipment remain adequate for safe study conduct.
- Verifies various aspects of <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> Management:
  - Supplies and storage are as per protocol/pharmacy manual.

  - IP is provided only to eligible study participants.

  - Participants receive necessary instruction on proper use, handling, storing and returning the IP.

  - IP receipt, use and return are controlled and documented adequately by the site.

  - IP disposition of unused IP complies with regulations and Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator instructions.

- Verifies that the QI/PI follows the REB approved protocol and all amendments
- Verifies that written informed consent was obtained prior to each participant’s participation in the study.

- Ensures the QI/PI receives and has read the Investigator’s Brochure and all study documents as well as receives all study supplies.

- Ensures the QI/PI and team are well informed about the study on an ongoing basis.

- Ensures that the QI/PI and team conduct all study procedures according to the REB approved protocol and have not delegated these functions to an unauthorized party.

- Reports the participant recruitment rate.

- Verifies that source documentation is accurate, complete and up-to-date throughout the study.

- Verifies that the QI/PI provides all required reports, notifications and submissions and these documents are accurate, complete, timely, legible, dated, and identify the study.

- Conducts Source Data Verification (SDV) to ensure accuracy and completeness of <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> entries.

  - Data required by protocol is reported accurately on CRFs and consistent with source data.

  - Any modification in treatment is well documented for each participant.
  - AEs, concomitant meds and intercurrent illnesses are reported as per protocol.

  - Visits or tests not conducted are clearly reported as such in the CRF.
  - All participant withdrawals and dropouts are reported and explained in the CRF.

- Informs the QI/PI of any CRF entry omissions, error or illegibility and ensures appropriate corrections and additions are made, dated, explained (if necessary) and initialed by the QI/PI or delegate authorized to do so on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>.

- Determines if all AEs are appropriately reported according to GCP, regulations, REB and SOPs.

- Determines if the QI/PI is maintaining the ISF as per ICH-GCP (for drug and NHP) or as per ISO-GCP (for medical device).

- Communicates any deviations observed from the protocol, SOPs, GCP and regulations to the QI/PI and team in writing and ensures they implement corrective and preventative (CAPA) actions.

- Submits a written report to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator after each trial-site visit or trial-related communication in a timely fashion. Reporting of remote/centralized monitoring activities may be independent from site visits.

<a id="5-1-7"></a>**5.1.7** If noncompliance is observed that significantly affects or has the potential to affect human participant protection or data integrity, the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator should perform a root cause analysis and implement appropriate corrective and preventative (CAPA) action.

<a id="5-2"></a>

### 5.2 Sponsor-Investigator preparation of monitoring plan

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** It is the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator’s responsibility to develop a systematic, prioritized, risk-based approach to monitoring human research studies.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** Ensure that a monitoring plan (MP) is in place describing the monitoring strategy (on-site monitoring, remote monitoring or a combination) and providing the rationale for the strategy as well as ensuring an independent dedicated monitor(s) will be assigned the task of conducting QC.

Note: The Monitor is part of the Sponsor-Investigator team and not the Investigator’s site team, hence the Monitor is not listed on the site-specific Task Delegation Log. Any site-specific Quality Control (QC), e.g. QC of site source document forms) should be conducted (initialed and dated) by the site, not the Sponsor-Investigator’s Monitor who should remain independent of operations.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** In addition to identifying risks and how to control the risks, it is also critical to include the processes that will be used to address any non-compliance as well as what events would constitute the need for a revision to the MP.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** According to the [Health Canada GUI 0100](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100.html), the MP should include the following:

- The monitoring strategy
- The monitoring responsibilities of all parties involved
- The various monitoring methods to be used
- The rationale for their use
- The monitoring of critical data and processes with particular attention to non-routine clinical practices that require additional training.

- The MP should reference the applicable polices and SOPs.

<a id="5-2-5"></a>**5.2.5** For a template of a MP, refer to [Appendix 3](#appendix-3)

<a id="5-3"></a>

### 5.3 Monitor’s preparation for a site visit

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** The preparation for a monitoring visit should be done in accordance with the MP and established Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator SOPs and be appropriate for the type of visit to be scheduled.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2** The monitor should prepare protocol-specific tools in order to effectively conduct the monitoring visit and capture any observations.

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3** Prior to any monitor’s visit to a site or a virtual visit (whether a Feasibility visit, an SIV visit, an Ongoing monitoring visit or a Study Close-Out visit), a pre-visit confirmation letter (usually in the form of an email) should be sent to the site in order to allow the site time to prepare and ensure availability of required personnel. Refer to [Appendix 4](#appendix-4) for a confirmation letter template.

<a id="5-4"></a>

### 5.4 Monitor’s protocol feasibility visit

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** As per [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004) Clinical Research Protocol Feasibility and Site Selection, the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator should confirm that it is feasible for the QI/PI and team to conduct the study e.g. the QI/PI has access to the appropriate study population, the appropriate facilities and has suitably qualified and experienced staff available. Refer to [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004) for more information on Feasibility.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** The Monitor writes up a pre-study Protocol Feasibility Report to document if the site is suitable for study conduct. This report is filed in the TMF as per GCP. Refer to [Appendix 5](#appendix-5) for a Protocol Feasibility Report template.

<a id="5-5"></a>

### 5.5 Monitor’s site initiation visit (SIV)

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** Once the institution and the institution’s REB has provided written approval to conduct the study protocol at the site, the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator and/or Monitor conducts an SIV to review all study materials, procedures and ensure study conduct will be compliant with their SOPs, GCP and all applicable regulations. Refer to [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005) Site Initiation/Activation. The Sponsor-Investigator/Monitor should refer to [Appendix 6](#appendix-6) in this SOP for the SIV Report template.

<a id="5-6"></a>

### 5.6 Sponsor/Sponsor-investigator’s go letter

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Once the SIV report has been approved and sent to the site and the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator determines the site to be compliant with start-up requirements, a Go Letter is sent by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/Monitor to the site providing them with written approval to commence the study at their site. Refer to [Appendix 7](#appendix-7) for Go Letter template.

<a id="5-7"></a>

### 5.7 Monitor’s preparation for an ongoing monitoring visit

<a id="5-7-1"></a>**5.7.1** The Monitor prepares tools in order to capture all observations during the monitoring visit.

<a id="5-7-2"></a>**5.7.2** Typically after the 1st participant is enrolled or there are 2 or more screen failed participants, the Monitor prepares the pre-visit confirmation letter (or confirmation email) and sends it out to the sites for confirmation of dates as well as availability of staff and study documentation required for a particular monitoring visit. Refer to [Appendix 4](#appendix-4) for a pre-visit confirmation letter template.

<a id="5-8"></a>

### 5.8 Muhc/ri-muhc site preparation for an ongoing monitoring visit

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** The QI/PI should be available at the time of the monitoring visit. Ensure the availability of the QI/PI and schedule visits with all applicable parties from different services (e.g. pharmacy).

<a id="5-8-2"></a>**5.8.2** Ensure that documentation (paper and/or electronic) is kept up-to-date in preparation for each monitoring visit, as described below.

<a id="5-8-3"></a>**5.8.3** Complete the applicable case report forms (CRFs), on a timely basis.

<a id="5-8-4"></a>**5.8.4** Review the SIV report or previous monitoring letters (if applicable) and ensure that the QI/PI has initialed and dated the report/letter to show medical oversight.

<a id="5-8-5"></a>**5.8.5** Ensure all outstanding issues from the last monitoring visit have been resolved and perform any outstanding CRF corrections, additions, or deletions, including explanations (if necessary). Obtain initials/dates of the QI/PI or delegate.

<a id="5-8-6"></a>**5.8.6** Ensure that the regulatory binder/file as well as participant data binders and essential documents, are up-to-date. Also ensure that all other data stored in other departments is kept up-to-date (e.g. pharmacy IP management documentation).

<a id="5-8-7"></a>**5.8.7** Ensure that the Monitor(s) receive documented training (if not already trained) and appropriate access to the MUHC [OACIS](/apps/oacis) electronic medical records (if required) in order to verify participant existence across all sites as well as access additional source documents. Organize and document OACIS training for the Monitor or obtain documentation. Refer to section [5.9](#5-9) below.

<a id="5-8-8"></a>**5.8.8** Have all paper and/or electronic records available for review, along with the corresponding source documents in order to facilitate source data review.

<a id="5-8-9"></a>**5.8.9** Ensure that research team members follow all institutional requirements for permitting third parties onto site, e.g., security pass, pandemic viral testing (if applicable), escorting Monitors, providing a confidential work space for the Monitors.

<a id="5-8-10"></a>**5.8.10** Ensure that the Monitor has limited access to appropriate premises where the study takes place and is accompanied by a site employee if they enter into an area where confidential material is stored.

<a id="5-8-11"></a>**5.8.11** Update the study Protocol Deviation (Exceptions and Violations) Log and make it available to the Monitor for review. Ensure all reportable violations have been reported to the REB. Note the site should be conducting their own quality control (QC), prior to and independent of the Sponsor’s/Monitor’s monitoring QC.

<a id="5-8-12"></a>**5.8.12** Prepare a Monitoring Log for the <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip> and Monitor to sign on the visit date. Refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1) if a log was not provided by the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator.

<a id="5-9"></a>

### 5.9 Request EMR training and appropriate monitor access

<a id="5-9-1"></a>**5.9.1** The monitor should ensure that electronic medical record (EMR) access (for adult or pediatric studies) and training is provided in order to verify study participant source data and existence.

<a id="5-9-2"></a>**5.9.2** At all MUHC/RI-MUHC sites, the MUHC Medical Records department can provide temporary restricted access to external Monitors. An initial request must be submitted to Medical records along with the following information:

- MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement signed by the Monitor (refer to RI-MUHC [SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019))
- A DPS Chart request form must be filled out, signed by all parties (monitor, QI/PI, supervisor) then sent to DPS (cathy.martell@muhc.mcgill.ca) for approval and signature. Refer to Appendix 2b.

- A letter from the QI/PI requesting access for whomever needs temporary restricted access to OACIS; indicating the reason the person needs access, their role and the study number as well as a list of patient MRN numbers. ([Appendix 2](#appendix-2)). Note: the list of MRNs must be updated regularly.
- A copy of the study’s MUHC Authorization letter
- A copy of the study’s Initial REB Approval letter
- When there is a change of monitor, the archivist must be notified in order to terminate a monitor’s access.

<a id="5-9-3"></a>**5.9.3** The MUHC Medical Records will in turn inform the OACIS team of approval. Medical Records will provide:

- First and last name of Monitor
- Duration for which the Monitor will have access to OACIS.

<a id="5-9-4"></a>**5.9.4** The OACIS Training and Support team creates the OACIS ID in the OACIS ID management tool with the following information:

  - Name: Last name , First name
- System Log in ID: Create OACIS ID
- OACIS Role:
- Termination date: As provided by Medical Records
- Default Password
- If possible, include site and location where Monitor will work

- OACIS training and support team will advise the Medical Records team of the new OACIS ID.

- Based on a list provided by the CRC, Medical Records will create a restricted OACIS patient list with the designated patients recruited into the study.

- Medical Records will advise the OACIS training and support team when completed and the OACIS training and support team will arrange for OACIS training for the untrained Monitor.

- Retain the Monitor’s documented training in the monitoring section of the ISF.

<a id="5-9-5"></a>**5.9.5** When the Monitor no longer works on the specific study, notify Medical Records and request that they remove the Monitor’s access from OACIS.

<a id="5-9-6"></a>**5.9.6** The CRC or delegate must renew the Monitor’s OACIS ID annually, by submitting the study’s REB Annual Renewal approval to Medical Records.

<a id="5-9-7"></a>**5.9.7** Medical Records requires a five working day notice for all Monitor requests for a new OACIS ID; or access to a new patient list (provide Medical Records with patient’s MRNs); or require additional patients on their current patient list, as it does take time to build these lists and to train a Monitor prior to their first on-site monitoring visit.

### 5.10 Site action to facilitate the monitoring visit

<a id="5-10-1"></a>**5.10.1** Typically, the Monitor will send a written confirmation of an upcoming monitoring visit. Alert other departments, as required e.g. Pharmacy if the Monitor will be visiting them. File the confirmation letter in the monitoring section of the ISF.

<a id="5-10-2"></a>**5.10.2** Be prepared to provide or verify the following during monitoring visits, as applicable:

- Investigator and team qualifications, resources, and site facilities (e.g., laboratories, equipment, staff) remain adequate to safely and properly conduct the study;
- Current list of research team members, CVs, licenses, training records and delegation of tasks;
- Investigational product/s have been stored, handled, dispensed, and destroyed or returned, according to the protocol and Sponsor requirements;
- Conduct of the study is in compliance with the currently approved protocol/amendment/s, GCP, applicable regulatory requirement(s), and site SOPs;
- Signed informed consent (with the most recently REB-approved <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> version) was obtained before each participant’s screening in the study, and only eligible participants have been enrolled;
- Signed re-consent forms were obtained in a timely fashion, as applicable;
- Participant recruitment logs and original recruitment targets and timelines;

- Source documents, other study records, and essential documents are accurate, complete, up-to-date and filed appropriately;
- All required Research Ethics Board (REB) documents, REB approval letters and Sponsor’s Go Letters are on file;
- All serious adverse events (SAEs) and suspect unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs) or Medical Device Incidents (MDIs) have been reported within the time periods required by Sponsor, protocol, REB, GCP (ICH E6/ISO 14155) and Health Canada/ FDA/ other regulatory agency, as required; and
- The Protocol Deviation Log is up-to-date and Corrective and Preventative Action (CAPA) has been taken to prevent the recurrence of deviations (if any) from the protocol, SOPs, GCP, or applicable regulations that were noted in previous communications by the Monitor.

- All study documents requiring QI/PI oversight have been initialed and dated by the QI/PI. (e.g. all protocols and amendments, all eligibility criteria, all SUSARs and <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reports, all monitoring reports, newsletters and any other medically sensitive information or memorandums).

<a id="5-10-3"></a>**5.10.3** The Monitor checks the accuracy and completeness of the CRF entries, verifies the CRF entries against the source documents and other trial-related records, and informs the QI/PI and the Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC) of any CRF entry errors, omissions, or illegibility. Any source data/CRF discrepancies should be documented by the Monitor who will then issue a query sent to the CRC and QI/PI.

<a id="5-10-4"></a>**5.10.4** Ensure that any protocol deviations observed by the Monitor are recorded on the Protocol Deviation Log. Deviations should be reported to the Sponsor within 24 hrs and REB as per local REB procedures. In the case of MUHC/RI-MUHC REB, violations are reported within 7 days whereas protocol exceptions can be submitted to the REB at the time of Annual Continuing Review, in accordance with RI-MUHC SOPs and MUHC REB SOPs.

<a id="5-10-5"></a>**5.10.5** Ensure that no study document with patient identifiers leaves the institution. These documents should be de-identified if the Monitor requires a copy for their files.

<a id="5-10-6"></a>**5.10.6** Ensure that the Monitor and site CRC sign the Monitoring Log prior to the Monitor’s departure. File the Monitoring Log in the ISF with the Monitoring letters.

<a id="5-11"></a>

### 5.11 Monitor’s monitoring report (for the TMF), monitoring follow-up letter (for the ISF and TMF) and site follow-up

<a id="5-11-1"></a>**5.11.1** The Monitor submits a written monitoring visit report (MVR) to the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, after each site visit (for both on-site and remote visits). The MVR is then filed in the TMF. Refer to [Appendix 8](#appendix-8) for a monitoring report template.

<a id="5-11-2"></a>**5.11.2** The Monitor forwards a summary of the visit findings, and any follow-up actions required, to the site in the form of a monitoring letter. Refer to [Appendix 9](#appendix-9) for a monitoring letter template.

<a id="5-11-3"></a>**5.11.3** Ensure that the QI/PI initials and dates the monitoring letter to document medical oversight. The initialed and dated Monitoring Letter is then filed in the ISF.

<a id="5-11-4"></a>**5.11.4** The QI/PI (or delegate):

- Follows up with the Monitor to obtain a summary of the monitoring visit, if not received promptly after the visit;
- Review the summary for significant findings, deviations, deficiencies, conclusions, actions taken or to be taken, and/or actions recommended;
- Clarify action items with the Monitor as needed, and implement remedial action at the site to ensure compliance with the protocol, GCP, applicable regulations and SOPs; Respond to each monitoring observation in writing. Refer to contract timelines.

- Respond promptly to requests, and discuss plans for any subsequent visits.

<a id="5-11-5"></a>**5.11.5** File the initialed and dated monitoring letter as well as any CAPA response in the ISF.

<a id="5-12"></a>

### 5.12 Additional monitor communication between visits

<a id="5-12-1"></a>**5.12.1** The Monitor may contact the site by telephone, or e-mail to obtain study status updates, obtain information on various study matters, or transmit information to the site.

<a id="5-12-2"></a>**5.12.2** Additionally, there could be a Remote Monitor who may contact the site with additional queries regarding data transcribed into the eCRF.

<a id="5-12-3"></a>**5.12.3** Respond promptly to requests, and retain copies of all correspondence in the ISF.

<a id="5-13"></a>

### 5.13 Site communication with the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator/monitor

<a id="5-13-1"></a>**5.13.1** Define channels of communication, during the study implementation process, and clarify the function of each team member. Ask the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator or CRO for a contact list, if one was not provided.

<a id="5-13-2"></a>**5.13.2** Organize/facilitate meetings (in-person or virtual), and other means of communication, with all parties involved in the study, throughout the course of the study and document discussions.

<a id="5-13-3"></a>**5.13.3** Apply the following general guidelines for documenting such communication:

- Maintain a Telephone Log for each study (refer to [SOP-CR-009](/sops/cr-009)), and document all telephone calls chronologically to the Sponsor, Monitor, project manager, study participants, investigator, etc.; follow up on telephone calls in which study decisions were made via email.

- Document all communications, including the names of participants, meeting date, summary of discussions, and resolutions adopted; and
- Record the date of dispatch or receipt of, all documents sent or received. Print fax transmittal reports (if available), or record key information, such as time and date of fax transmission, and name of sender.

<a id="5-14"></a>

### 5.14 Monitor’s close-out visit

<a id="5-14-1"></a>**5.14.1** As per [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016) Study Close-Out, once the last study participant has completed the last scheduled study visit and all CRFs are complete, the Monitor will schedule the study Close-Out Monitoring Visit in order to ensure accuracy and completeness of CRFs, source data and regulatory documentation and reconcile and collect study-related materials.

<a id="5-14-2"></a>**5.14.2** Refer to [Appendix 10](#appendix-10) for the Monitor’s Close-Out Report (for the TMF) and refer to [Appendix 11](#appendix-11) for the Site Notification of Close-Out (for the ISF).

<a id="5-15"></a>

### 5.15 Site quality control (QC) of monitoring documentation

<a id="5-15-1"></a>**5.15.1** Ensure that for every monitoring visit logged on the Monitoring Log, there is a monitoring Follow-Up letter corresponding to that visit and each monitoring Follow-Up letter has been initialed and dated by the QI/PI. Also ensure that all queries and CAPA requested by the Monitor has been answered, resolved and filed.

<a id="5-15-2"></a>**5.15.2** Ensure that all deviations from the approved study protocol, GCP or Health Canada regulations has been documented and reported according to Institutional and REB SOPs.

<a id="5-15-3"></a>**5.15.3** Any MUHC-RI-MUHC violations should also be reported by site to the RI-MUHC Quality Assurance Department qaclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca
