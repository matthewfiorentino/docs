## 1.0 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) describes the required communication for Ongoing Study Submission between the Sponsor-Investigator or Qualified Investigator (<Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>) / Principal Investigator (<Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>) and the MUHC Research Ethics Board (<Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>) and feasibility reviewers throughout the duration of human research studies. It also describes the upkeep of the web-accessible registration of the study. For Initial and Annual Continuing Review and initial study registration, refer to [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007).

## 2.0 Scope

This procedure concerns the research community (employees, investigators, sponsor-investigators, physicians, management, consultants, students, volunteers or other persons) involved in the ongoing study submission process. Ongoing REB review must continue after Initial REB review (and between annual Continuing Reviews) to obtain approval/acknowledgment of any new information that might affect the rights, safety and well-being of research participants and/or for which a preventative or corrective action is possible. This includes amendments, reportable events and relevant reports during the course of a human research study. This ongoing review ensures participant rights, safety and well-being.

This procedure also concerns research team members who are updating their publicly accessible study registration.

## 3.0 Responsibilities

The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator is responsible to ensure that all study document amendments and safety information are promptly distributed to investigators for REB approval prior to implementation and that all reportable events have been reported by the QI/PI to the REB.

The Sponsor-Investigator or Qualified Investigator (QI) / Principal Investigator (PI) is responsible for ensuring that all updated information (e.g., amendments to the protocol, informed consent form (<Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip>), participant facing materials and investigational brochure (<Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip>)/product monograph (PM)) required for ongoing study approval is forwarded to the REB in a timely manner and that all reportable events (including reportable deviations and SAEs) are submitted to the REB as per applicable timelines.

Tasks required for effective communication may be delegated, but the responsibility to ensure compliance with this SOP and the REB policies and procedures rests with the Sponsor-Investigator or QI/PI.

The Sponsor-Investigator is responsible for both Sponsor and QI/PI responsibilities as well as the upkeep of study registration records (i.e., on clinicaltrials.gov).

## 4.0 Definitions

The term Investigator is used throughout this document to refer to Sponsor-Investigator, QI or PI.

The term Sponsor is used throughout the document to refer to Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator.

See [Glossary of Terms](/kb/foundations/glossary)

## 5.0 Procedure

<a id="5-1"></a>

<a id="5-1-sponsor-responsibilities-regarding-ongoing-study-submissions"></a>

### 5.1 Sponsor responsibilities regarding ongoing study submissions and communication

<a id="5-1-1"></a>**5.1.1 Sponsor responsibilities regarding ongoing regulatory submissions to Health Canada:**

- Provide regulatory agencies (e.g., Health Canada) with CTA-amendments or notifications where applicable (Sponsor-Investigators should refer to [SOP-CR-018](/sops/cr-018), [SOP-CR-023](/sops/cr-023) or [SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024), where applicable), obtain written approval from Health Canada, file this in the study Trial Master File (<Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip>) and forward a copy of the No Objection Letter/Notice of Authorization/Investigational Testing Authorization (<Tooltip tip="Letter from Health Canada confirming a CTA is satisfactory and authorizing the sponsor to proceed." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nol">NOL</Tooltip>/NOA/<Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip>) to the Investigators to file in their respective ISFs.

- Provide regulatory agencies with all reportable Serious Adverse Events (SAEs) and all reportable Medical Device Incidents within required timelines. Refer to [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012).

<a id="5-1-2"></a>**5.1.2** Sponsor responsibilities regarding oversight of ongoing REB submissions:

- Provide the Investigators with all amendments/revisions to study documents in a timely fashion and request that the Investigator sign and date the amendments and obtain REB approval/acknowledgment where appropriate in a timely fashion (within 30 days of receipt). Where applicable, provide the site with a Go Letter (approval of implementation) for an amendment.
- Provide the Investigator(s) with all external Serious Unexpected Adverse Reactions (SUSARs) or Medical Device Incident (MDI) reports in a timely fashion and request that the Investigator document their review and distribution to team members and to the REB (where applicable) within the required timelines. Refer to [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012) regarding reportable SUSARs.
- Ensure that all reportable protocol/GCP deviations are reported to the REB by the site in a timely fashion as per REB SOPs.

<a id="5-1-3"></a>**5.1.3** Collect documentation of Investigator oversight and file in the Trial Master File (TMF):

- Documentation of Investigator’s amendment review e.g., protocol or IB amendment signature page;
- Documented and dated REB approvals of any amendments of study documents and submission form e.g., protocol, informed consent form (ICF), investigational brochure (IB), participant materials (diaries, questionnaires and participant information);
- Copy of the modifications requested by the REB before approval and the date approval was given by the REB;
- Documentation and dates of any REB re-approvals and any withdrawals or suspensions of study approval.

<a id="5-1-4"></a>**5.1.4** Provide Investigators with an <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reporting process and tools (e.g., SAE reports for submission to Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator) and collect all SAEs from sites.

MUHC/RI-MUHC Sponsor-Investigators should refer to [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012) for a report template.

<a id="5-1-5"></a>**5.1.5** Expedite <Tooltip tip="A serious adverse reaction to a drug or natural health product whose nature, severity or frequency the Investigator's Brochure or product label does not anticipate; the sponsor reports it to Health Canada, and the site reports it to the REB when it meets the REB's criteria." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip>/MDI reports and document Investigator oversight throughout the study:

- Expedite and document the reporting to all concerned Investigators and regulatory authorities of all adverse events/incidents that are both serious and unexpected and possibly, probably or definitely related to investigational product, in compliance with regulations and ICH Guidelines for Clinical Safety Data Management: Definitions and Standards for Expedited Reporting or ISO 14155 GCP.

- Ensure that Investigators report all reportable SUSARs or MDIs to their respective REBs according to their respective institutional guidelines.
- Ensure that the Investigator has a process in place to review and distribute SUSAR/MDI reports to their study team members.

<a id="5-1-6"></a>**5.1.6** Document any revisions to study agreements

- Changes in duties transferred and assumed by a Contract Research Organization (CRO), or Academic Research Organization (ARO) and duties that are retained by the Sponsor.

<a id="5-1-7"></a>**5.1.7** Throughout the trial, the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator must demonstrate overall study oversight with emphasis on quality control measures as per ICH-GCP/ ISO-GCP.

- Conduct Quality Control (QC) of all amended and added documents and file all study essential documents in a Trial Master File (TMF) in compliance with ICH-GCP, section 8/ ISO-GCP, annex E.

- Ensure that the study is monitored to confirm that all Investigators conduct the trial in strict compliance with the most recent REB approved study protocol and materials.

- Update the study information on the publicly accessible registry (e.g., clinicaltrials.gov).

<a id="5-2"></a>

<a id="5-2-investigator-responsibilities-regarding-ongoing-reb-submissions"></a>

### 5.2 Investigator responsibilities regarding ongoing study submissions to the REB:

<a id="5-2-1"></a>**5.2.1** If the study is regulated by Health Canada (HC), the Investigator should obtain any amendments and revisions to study documents as well as the associated HC NOL, NOA or ITA approval letter from the Sponsor. In this case, for each protocol amendment, there should be an NOL/NOA/ITA approval for the CTA-A or documentation regarding a Clinical Trial Application notification (CTA-N) submitted to the REB and filed in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>.

<a id="5-2-2"></a>**5.2.2** For any human research study (regulated by HC or not), the Investigator is responsible for submitting any revisions/amendments to a previously approved study to the MUHC REB (e.g., revised protocol, IB/PM) and appropriate MUHC/RI-MUHC department where applicable (e.g., revised contract, laboratory manual, pharmacy manual). If applicable, an addendum to a Pharmacy Agreement or Laboratory Agreement should be submitted for approval by the appropriate department.

<a id="5-2-3"></a>**5.2.3** The Investigator must not implement any deviation from the approved protocol without prior agreement by the Sponsor and approval from the REB except where necessary to eliminate immediate hazard(s) to study participants(s) or for an administrative aspect of the study (e.g., changes that are minor, and may involve grammar, rephrasing, punctuation or small editorial changes). All protocol deviations (minor, major and critical) must be assessed, addressed and documented. Refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1) for the Protocol/GCP/SOP Deviation Log and [Appendix 2](#appendix-2) for the Corrective and Preventive (CAPA) Form. In the event of a public health emergency where it is necessary to deviate from the protocol, refer to the most recent MUHC and RI-MUHC guidelines regarding flexibility of this regulation.

<a id="5-2-4"></a>**5.2.4** The investigator must submit to the Sponsor and REB, updates and information that impact or may impact the conduct of the study. Provide written reports to the Sponsor and the REB on any changes or new information significantly affecting the conduct of the study (research efficacy), data integrity, or affecting the rights, safety and well-being of the participants. File all approvals/acknowledgments in the ISF.

<a id="5-2-5"></a>**5.2.5** Inform the Sponsor and REB of study completion, suspension, or termination.

- Inform the REB if the Sponsor completes, terminates or suspends a trial for any reason and provide a detailed written explanation for termination and suspension. All communication should be filed in the ISF.

- Inform the Sponsor if the REB terminates or suspends a trial for any reason and provide a detailed written explanation. Suspensions of recruitment should also be communicated and documented in the ISF.

- Refer to [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016) on Study Close-Out.

<a id="5-2-6"></a>**5.2.6** Update the Investigator Site File (ISF) with a printed copy of regulatory and REB approvals, acknowledgments, and correspondence until the MUHC/RI-MUHC has obtained a compliant electronic document management system.

<a id="5-3-ongoing-study-submission-review-of-an-approved-study"></a>

### 5.3 Ongoing study submission review of an approved study

<a id="5-3-1"></a>**5.3.1** Over the course of the study, the Investigator must inform the REB about the following ongoing events prior to their implementation via specific forms in the study submission platform (e.g., [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview)). For information regarding the specific forms to use, consult the MUHC REB webpage document, “Nagano Forms” or contact your REB coordinator.

- Any significant protocol changes or deviation (e.g., deliberate action required to avert an immediate hazard to a participant, post facto discovery of dosing error). Major and critical deviations (report immediately) and minor deviations (report annually);
- Amendments to an approved study:
  - Modifications to the consent form(s), other study materials or communication/advertising tools destined to participants,
  - Modifications to the IB or PM,
  - Change of QI/PI or Sponsor (requiring a revised contract),
  - Other revision/modification to an approved study, for example:
- Procedural changes that might increase participant risk;
- Change in sample size;
- Dropping of a control or treatment arm;
- Change in investigational product exposure, and;
- Any other change that could influence the scientific validity of the study results;
- Newly translated study documents e.g., revised ICFs, participant materials;
- SAE or MDI that is unexpected and at least possibly related to investigational product (<Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip>) or study conduct and poses greater risk of harm to participants (Refer to [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012) and/or [SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024));
- Premature participant withdrawal from the study;
- Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) reports;
- Privacy breaches involving participants;
- Complaints regarding study conduct;
- Change in study status (e.g., Sponsor or Investigator places study on hold);
- Additional information and/or miscellaneous communications;
- Study completion/suspension/termination;
- Request to convert to a Québec multi-centrique study or add a new RSSS site;

- Changes to a multi-centrique study reported by an external site.

Forward REB-requested revisions to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator for review (if applicable).

The Investigator is to:

- Revise the documents, as required;
- Ensure that all revised documents contain a new version number and date indicated in the footer of the document (best practice is to use the date submitted to the REB) and also ensure that the file name is clear and has been updated with the version number and date. Note it is the electronic file name that is documented on the REB approved form;
- Document and file in the ISF all approvals, acknowledgements and communications with the REB during the course of the study;
- Inform Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, where required;
- Not implement any changes until REB written approval and Sponsor’s Go Letter/confirmation (if applicable) are received unless under emergency circumstances where it is important to deviate for participant safety reasons. The Sponsor-Investigator and the REB should be notified ASAP afterwards.

<a id="5-3-2"></a>**5.3.2 SAE/MDI Reporting to the MUHC REB:**

The Investigator is to report to the REB:

- Any serious and unexpected adverse reaction or medical device incident involving a participant enrolled at an MUHC/RI-MUHC site which is at least possibly related to participation in the study and poses greater risk of harm to participants. When reporting, always ensure that all research participant identifiers have been removed and replaced by the participant’s enrollment number. Note: SAEs/MDIs should also be forwarded to the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator without delay (within 24 hours) and to the REB within 7 days (if life threatening) or 15 days of becoming aware of the event as per [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012)/[SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024).

- For multi-centrique Quebec studies where the MUHC is the Quebec lead REB, any suspect unexpected serious adverse reaction (SUSAR) or MDI report involving a participant at any Quebec site which is at least possibly related to participation in the study and poses greater risk of harm to participants. Per the harmonized study submission platform, each participating site is responsible for submitting their own SAEs to the MUHC-REB directly via the appropriate form. For external sites not using the study submission platform, the MUHC research team must submit on behalf of the external team.

- For multicentre (other provinces/countries), any external SUSAR or MDI report which is at least possibly related to participation in the study and would require an amendment to the study.

- Any new information that may have an impact on the safety or conduct of the study, safety of the participant, or his/her willingness to continue participating in the study. Such new information should also be reflected in an amended ICF.

The Investigator must ensure that the site has a written process describing how documents requiring medical oversight (e.g., SUSARs) are reviewed by the Investigator, and how they are distributed to the appropriate human research team members.

For more information on Local Serious Adverse Events (SAEs) and Non-Local (External) SAE reporting, refer to RI-MUHC [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012).

For more information on MDI reporting, refer to RI-MUHC [SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024).

<a id="5-3-3"></a>**5.3.3 Protocol and SOP/GCP Deviations :**

- Any excursion from the current REB approved study documents, RI-MUHC SOPs, ICH-GCP or ISO-GCP (if applicable) is considered a deviation.
- Deviations are categorized as Minor, Major or Critical.
  - Minor: a deviation that is classified as not critical or major, but which indicates a deficiency and/or deviation from protocol/GCP/regulations.
  - Major: a deviation describing a marked deviation or deficiency, other than a critical one, that may result in undue health risks to the clinical trial participants, in other persons or could invalidate data.
  - Critical: a deviation describing a situation that results in fatal, life threatening or unsafe conditions for participants enrolled in a clinical trial. It presents an immediate or latent undue risk to the rights, health and safety of participants. The conduct or unauthorized trials, adulteration, misrepresentation and falsification of records are also critical deviations from SOPs and GCP.

- Protocol deviations are identified as any unplanned or unforeseen excursion from the REB approved study protocol, consent(s) or study addenda/protocol procedures that are not meant to be implemented systematically to the study.
  - Note: Protocol deviations are different from amendments in that they generally apply to a single occurrence or participant and are not intended to modify the conduct of the protocol.

  - Intentional deviations/waiver should not be implemented without prior agreement from the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and approval from the REB (as required) unless they eliminate an immediate hazard to trial participants or when the change is administrative or logistical in nature.

  - Note: In the case of emergency measures (e.g., a pandemic), the Sponsor should provide emergency measures to the QI/PI and instructions from the REB on their submission should be followed.
  - If the Sponsor allows minor changes to the protocol, confirmation of this should be provided in writing by the Sponsor. The documentation of this waiver should be filed in the ISF and available to the REB for review upon request.

- All protocol, GCP and SOP deviations observed by the site (intentional or not) or detected by a monitor, auditor or inspector should be documented on the Protocol/GCP/SOP Deviation Log, assessed in order to identify trends and take corrective and preventive action regarding process change (as applicable). Refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1).

- All major and critical deviations should have corrective and preventive action (CAPA) implemented which should be captured on a CAPA from. It is best practice to document what has already been implemented (not to be implemented in future). The CAPA number should be entered on the Deviation Log (Refer to [Appendix 1](#appendix-1)) as well as the CAPA form (Refer to [Appendix 2](#appendix-2)) in order to facilitate tracking and closure.

- All major or critical deviations should be reported to the evaluating REB within 7 days (if life-threatening) or 15 days of becoming aware of the event along with CAPA implemented by the Investigator to avoid repetition of the event. Ensure that no participant nominative information appears (i.e., participant study number only). The CAPA form for major or critical violations should also be sent to RI-MUHC Quality Assurance department for trend tracking and further process improvement.

  - Submit the appropriate study submission form to the REB via the study submission platform (e.g., Nagano);
  - Send the CAPA form to QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca.;
  - Inform the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator of all deviations.
- The REB’s view on the adequacy of the corrective measures will result in a decision to accept or reject the action taken, and will be communicated in writing to the Investigator.

- If the protocol deviation introduces new information that may affect a participant’s willingness to continue in the study, all study participants must be informed, and the REB may require the participants to renew their informed consent in writing. Refer to [MUHC REB SOP 405](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops).

- All deviations assessed as minor occurring since the previous REB review may be collected and submitted to the REB at the time of annual Continuing Review. Refer to [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007) regarding Continuing Review.

<a id="5-3-4"></a>**5.3.4 Other Reportable Events and Information**

The Investigator must report to the REB any change in risks or benefits to study participants, including: An interim analysis indicating that participants have an unexpected rate of response to study treatment; Data indicating that a particular adverse event is more severe or more frequent than expected; Other published research that indicates that an arm of the research is of no therapeutic value; A change in Health Canada or FDA safety labeling, approval status or withdrawal from market of a drug, device, natural health product, genetic therapy or biologic used in the project; Any unanticipated problem or other event that could significantly impact the conduct of the research at the site (e.g., concerns of non-compliance, institutional feasibility issues); Inspection or audit report observations relevant to the safety and well-being of the participants. The Investigator must report to the REB a summary of the relevant findings following a Health Canada inspection, an FDA or other regulatory audit, Sponsor’s audit or an internal RI-MUHC Quality Assurance audit. Refer to MUHC REB SOP 405 for further information on reportable events.

<a id="5-3-5"></a>**5.3.5** Under certain circumstances Delegated REB review may be conducted in lieu of Full Board Review. For more information on Delegated REB review refer to MUHC REB SOP 402.

### 5.4 Multi-centrique research in Quebec and multi-center research across Canada

<a id="5-4-1"></a>**5.4.1** Multi-centrique Research is research involving more than one QI/PI from the Quebec public health care network conducting the study under the same Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator. Refer to the REB flowchart located on the MUHC CAE website for REB reporting, https://muhc.ca/cae/multicentre-study.

<a id="5-4-2"></a>**5.4.2** Each QI/PI is responsible for submissions of reportable events to the evaluating REB. The local researcher must submit in accordance with this SOP.

<a id="5-4-3"></a>**5.4.3** Requests to convert a study into a multi-centrique project or to add at least one site to a multi-centrique study must be done via the study submission platform (e.g., Nagano), using the appropriate Form.

<a id="5-4-4"></a>**5.4.4** If an external multi-centrique QI/PI does not have access to the MUHC study submission platform (e.g., Nagano), they must instead complete the relevant PDF form (equivalent to the study submission forms, available online at https://muhc.ca/cae/page/multicentre-study) and the MUHC research team must submit PDF forms through the study submission platform appropriately. These changes could include:

- Amendment request for external sites;
- Other notifications from external sites;
- Serious, unexpected reportable event that occurred at an external site under the jurisdiction of the MUHC as reviewing REB;
- Other reportable events: deviations, breach of confidentiality, complaints, that occurred at an external site;
- Request for annual renewal for external sites at same time for all sites;
- Notification of study closure, a suspension, a termination, or any interruption to a research project at an external site.

<a id="5-4-5"></a>**5.4.5 Multi-centrique Reportable Events - Quebec sites under MUHC REB study review:**

- Each Quebec site enters the reportable event on their individual log (e.g., SAE log, Deviation log);
- Each site must notify the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator ASAP (within 24 hrs for an SAE/serious MDI or protocol violation);
- Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator confirms the reportable event (correspondence is kept in the ISF and Sponsor’s TMF);
- All external Quebec sites with access to Nagano, report their events directly to the MUHC REB (if lead REB);
- For external Quebec sites with no access to the study submission platform, the lead MUHC Sponsor-Investigator reports external reportable events and corrective actions on their behalf to the MUHC REB (if the lead) within 7 days (if life threatening) or 15 days of becoming aware of the event.

<a id="5-4-6"></a>**5.4.6 MUHC Sponsor-Investigator Studies with Multi-center Reportable Events – Canadian sites other than Quebec sites:**

- Each Canadian site enters the reportable event on their individual log (e.g., SAE log, Deviation log);
- Each site must notify the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator ASAP (within 24 hrs for SAE/serious MDI or protocol violation);
- Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator confirms the reportable event (correspondence is kept in the ISF and Sponsor’s TMF);
- Each Canadian site reports their individual reportable event to their individual local REB.

<a id="5-4-7"></a>**5.4.7** The Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator should conduct a risk analysis for each of the deviations identified and rate them according to impact on the study and probability of reoccurrence. Track the number and type of deviations that have occurred across all sites in order to identify trends that may trigger a need for a protocol amendment.

### 5.5 Suspension or termination of REB approved studies

<a id="5-5-1"></a>**5.5.1** The Investigator is responsible for promptly notifying the REB and the organization of any suspensions or terminations of the research by the Sponsor, the investigator himself or health authorities and for providing a detailed explanation for the action, filing this correspondence with essential documents (ISF/TMF).

<a id="5-5-2"></a>**5.5.2** The REB may suspend or terminate a study (or suspend recruitment) if any concerns are raised including:

- Continuation of the research does not permit an acceptable potential risk/benefit ratio;
- The research is not being conducted in accordance with the REB-approved protocol or REB requirements;
- The research is associated with unexpected serious harm to participants (i.e., as may be determined following REB review of reportable events or DSMB reports);
- Falsification of research records or data;
- Failure to comply with prior conditions imposed by the REB (i.e., under a suspension or approval with modifications);
- Failure to apply for annual renewal of ethics approval prior to the expiry date; such failure entails de facto suspension. Failure to submit a request for continuing review within 30 days following the expiry date may result in the termination of the research project;
- Repeated or deliberate failure to properly obtain consent from research participants or to properly document the consent process;
- Repeated or deliberate failure to limit administration of the investigational drug or device to those research participants under the researcher’s supervision;
- Repeated or deliberate failure to comply with conditions placed on the research by the REB, by the Sponsor, or by regulatory agencies;
- Repeated or deliberate failure to obtain prior REB review and approval of amendments or modifications to the research, or;

- Repeated or deliberate failure to maintain accurate research records or submit required reportable event reports to the REB.

<a id="5-5-3"></a>**5.5.3** When the REB suspends or terminates ethics approval of the research, no further study activities can take place other than the submission of an amendment or reportable events.

<a id="5-5-4"></a>**5.5.4** If REB approval or conduct of a research study has been suspended, the suspension may be lifted after corrective and preventive actions are completed to the satisfaction of the REB and other third parties involved, if any.

<a id="5-5-5"></a>**5.5.5** Refer to [MUHC REB SOP 407](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) for further information.

<a id="5-6-completion-of-a-clinical-study"></a>

### 5.6 Completion of a clinical study

<a id="5-6-1"></a>**5.6.1** Once study close-out has occurred as per [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016), submit a Closure of Research Project form to the REB when there are no further participants on study, all data collection is complete and the Sponsor’s Close-Out report has been completed.

<a id="5-6-2"></a>**5.6.2** The QI/PI must report to the REB the change in study status in a timely fashion (ideally within 30 days of the Close-Out visit).

<a id="5-6-3"></a>**5.6.3** Once the REB has reviewed the submission, the QI/PI will receive a letter of acknowledgement from the REB.

<a id="5-6-4"></a>**5.6.4** Once the study status is changed to “Closed” in the study submission platform, no further submissions are permitted. If required, additional relevant documents for REB acknowledgement may be still be submitted after study closure through the study submission Discussion feature.

<a id="5-7"></a>

### 5.7 REB notification timelines

| REB notification of… | Time delay (calendar days) | Follow up required |
|---|---|---|
| Amendments |  |  |
| Undertaken immediately to protect participants | Immediately | Submit and obtain REB review and approval of amendments. File approvals and documentation in the ISF. |
| All other amendments | Report prior to introducing any change | Submit and obtain REB review and approval prior to initiation of amendment. File approvals in the ISF and implement amendment at next participant visit. |
| All Reportable events as described herein |  |  |
| Reportable events in context of death and/or life-threatening reactions | Within 7 days of researcher becoming aware of the event | Submit a detailed report containing an analysis of the event, its consequences and corrective measures taken within 8 days of the first report. File all correspondence and acknowledgments in the ISF. |
| All other reportable events | Within 15 days of researcher becoming aware of the event | Submit and file all correspondence and acknowledgments in the ISF. |
| Any new information that may adversely affect the safety of the research participants or the conduct of the research | Within 15 days of researcher becoming aware of this info | Submit and file all correspondence and acknowledgments in the ISF. |
| Other Reports |  |  |
| Scheduled, routine or random reports without identified impact on participant safety or well-being or required changes | Submit at time of annual renewal | Submit and file REB acknowledgement letter in the ISF. |

Note that [REB-SOP 501](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) addresses REB Review during publicly declared emergencies.

<a id="5-8"></a>

### 5.8 Submission of complaints or breaches of privacy to the REB

<a id="5-8-1"></a>**5.8.1** The QI/PI is responsible for notifying the REB of any complaints related to research for which they are responsible, that are brought to their attention, including those under investigation by the MUHC ombudsman.

<a id="5-8-2"></a>**5.8.2** The QI/PI is responsible for notifying the REB of any unauthorized collection, use, or disclosure of personal information.

<a id="5-8-3"></a>**5.8.3** For additional information on submitting complaints or breaches of privacy to the REB, refer to [MUHC REB SOP 405](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) and [SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019).

<a id="5-9"></a>

### 5.9 Informing the REB of inspections and audits

<a id="5-9-1"></a>**5.9.1** In addition to internal RI-MUHC Quality Assurance audits, external bodies such as Health Authorities (Health Canada, FDA, EMA), Sponsor’s or funding agencies (e.g., CIHR) may conduct inspections or audits of studies. The Sponsor-Investigator or Investigator is responsible for notifying the REB of any planned audits or inspections of research projects overseen by the REB. Refer to [MUHC REB SOP 701](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) for further information.

For more information on MUHC REB SOPs refer to the MUHC REB webpage (link provided in RI-portal under SOPs).

<a id="5-10"></a>

### 5.10 Public clinical trial registration updating by Sponsor-Investigators

<a id="5-10-1"></a>**5.10.1** All approved public web-based registries (e.g., clinicaltrials.gov) require appropriate updating for the duration of the study. The Sponsor-Investigator (named Principal Investigator on the US DHHS site) responsible for registering the study is also responsible for the upkeep and all approvals of the record.

<a id="5-10-2"></a>**5.10.2** Reminder: As specified in [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007), for insurance purposes, in the clinicaltrials.gov record, the MUHC-RIMUHC should be entered for “Sponsor”.

<a id="5-10-3"></a>**5.10.3** Only one account is opened per Sponsor-Investigator. If a Sponsor-Investigator would like to register several studies, they may do so under their same account. Note that if a Sponsor-Investigator is unable to approve their own record in clinicaltrials.gov, the set-up of the record on the site was not completed as required. Revise the set-up and indicate that the PI is the “Responsible Party” (not the Sponsor, MUHC-RIMUHC). For all other issues, please consult the Problem Resolution Guide posted on the clinicaltrial.gov site.

<a id="5-10-4"></a>**5.10.4 Other requirements:**

- In order to publish the results of a Randomized Clinical Trial (RCT) in member journals of the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (http://www.icmje.org), the trial must be registered with an ICMJE-approved electronic registry.

- The World Health Organization (WHO) requires RCT (phase 1 to phase IV) to be registered.

The Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR) requires registration of clinical trials as a condition of CIHR funding
