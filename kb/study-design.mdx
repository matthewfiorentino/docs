This page maps who at the Institute can support you across each area of study design, and when to engage them. Most design-stage delays are avoidable — the common thread is getting the right people involved before decisions are hard to reverse.

For study type classification, randomization, blinding, and comparator design — see [Trial Design and Study Types](/kb/trial-design) in the 101 series.

## Support by design area

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Methodology and biostatistics">
    Methodology support is most useful before the protocol is written — sample size, statistical analysis planning, and research question framing are all harder to revisit once data collection has started.

    **Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)**

    The BCU supports researchers across the full study lifecycle — from grant applications and sample size calculations through to analysis, manuscript support, and <Tooltip tip="Independent committee that reviews interim safety and efficacy data and recommends whether to continue, modify, or stop the study." headline="Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#dsmb">DSMB</Tooltip> reports. The unit handles Phase II–IV clinical trials, epidemiological studies, and large administrative database analyses (RAMQ, Medicare, StatCan), working in R, SAS, and STATA.

    BCU operates on a fee-for-service basis through iLab ($80/hr for RI-MUHC researchers). Early Career Researchers receive the first four hours per grant submission free of charge (up to two grants per year). Full details on services, fees, and the iLab request process are on the [Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)](/kb/bcu) page.

    - BCU — general inquiries and iLab requests: [bcu@rimuhc.ca](mailto:bcu@rimuhc.ca)
    - Ramanakumar V. Agnihotram, PhD — Associate Director, BCU: [raman.agnihotram@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:raman.agnihotram@muhc.mcgill.ca)

This is distinct from biostatistics: BCU handles the analytical and quantitative side, Philippe works at the design and conceptual level.

  <Accordion title="Data management and technology">
    Data infrastructure involves two distinct needs that are often conflated: **building the system to capture your data**, and **accessing data that already exists**. Both need to be addressed at the design stage — not after your protocol is written.

    **CORD — building your data capture system**

    The CORD (Capture and Optimization of Research Data) unit handles everything related to how your study will collect, store, and manage its data. REDCap is the standard eCRF platform at the RI-MUHC and is validated for 21 CFR Part 11 compliance. CORD builds and tests REDCap databases, reviews <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> designs and data dictionaries before they are built, and provides ongoing data management support through the study lifecycle — extraction, cleaning, integration, and version tracking. Database architecture decisions are difficult to change after data collection begins — engaging CORD early avoids that.

    CORD also reviews data management plans in protocols and grant applications, which is increasingly a funder expectation. The unit operates on a fee-for-service basis ($200 initial REDCap project setup; $55/hr for expert support with the first hour free per initiative). Full details are on the [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/cord) page.

    - CORD Unit — REDCap, CRF support, data management: [cord@rimuhc.ca](mailto:cord@rimuhc.ca)

    **MUHC Data Warehouse — accessing existing MUHC patient data**

    If your study involves retrospective analysis of patient data — admissions, labs, pharmacy dispensations, imaging reports, surgical records, cancer registry — the Data Warehouse (DW) is the route in. The DW integrates data from over 18 MUHC hospital systems across five sites (MGH, RVH, MCH, Lachine, MNI), updated within 24 hours, with most domains going back to 2003–2008.

    Consulting the DW team **before finalizing your protocol** is strongly recommended. They can help you define patient selection criteria, confirm variable availability, and advise on sample size — and they can flag ÉFVP requirements early. If data access is not clearly defined in your Nagano submission, it routinely causes delays at both the ethics review and data request stages. Full process, requirements by data type, and tools are on the [Accessing MUHC Patient Data](/kb/data-warehouse) page.

    - RI-MUHC Data Warehouse: [info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca)

    **Technology feasibility**

    If your study involves any electronic platform beyond REDCap — e-consent systems, mobile apps, external databases, wearables — IT feasibility must be confirmed with the Digital and IT Division before submission. Post-submission security reviews go to MUHC Security and Governance.

    - Pascal St. Onge — Director, Digital and IT: [pascal.st-onge@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:pascal.st-onge@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - Security reviews post-submission: [securitygovernance@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:securitygovernance@muhc.mcgill.ca)

  <Accordion title="Funding and pre-awards">
    Funding timelines and submission timelines do not align neatly. Major CIHR and FRQS grant competitions have application deadlines that can fall months before a study is ready to submit to <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>. This means the design stage often runs in parallel with grant preparation — and the Pre-Awards Office needs to be in the loop before the protocol is finalized, not after the grant is submitted.

    Funding status also has regulatory implications. If you are initiating the study yourself — as happens frequently at the RI-MUHC with investigator-initiated trials — you are the Sponsor-Investigator and bear all Sponsor obligations under Health Canada Division 5, regardless of whether funding is secured. These include filing the <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">CTA</Tooltip>, arranging independent monitoring, and managing investigational product supply.

    **Pre-Awards Office**

    The Pre-Awards Office supports grant applications, funding strategy, and budget development across all major funders. For full detail on the process, see the [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/budgets-contracts) page.

    - Pre-Awards Office: [pre-awards.rimuhc@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:pre-awards.rimuhc@muhc.mcgill.ca)

    **RI-MUHC Clinical Trial Budget Template**

    The budget template (launched January 2026) is the recommended starting point for all budget builds. It includes staff salary lookup tables aligned with institutional salary scales, time and effort matrices for each study visit, and MUHC service costs — and produces both a project cost summary and a CIHR-format budget. Available on the RI-MUHC Portal.

    - Research Facilitator: [Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca)
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Participant considerations, PWLLE and EDI" id="participant-considerations">
    **Recruitment feasibility**

    Slow or failed recruitment is one of the most common reasons studies are delayed or underpowered. Before finalizing your eligibility criteria, confirm that the target population exists at the proposed site(s) in the required numbers — the Data Warehouse team can help with this for retrospective or feasibility queries. Confirm that your recruitment strategy is realistic within the study timeline and budget, and that the screening-to-enrolment ratio in your statistical plan reflects actual site conversion rates.

    **Consent design**

    Consent design should happen at the protocol stage, not as a final pre-submission task. For studies involving vulnerable populations — minors, adults incapable of consenting for themselves, or emergency research situations — the consent framework has legal implications under Quebec's Civil Code (Articles 21–21.1) that the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> will scrutinize carefully. These requirements affect your Informed Consent Form design, the authorization workflow, and in some cases the institutional feasibility review.

    **Patient perspectives — PWLLE**

    Integrating the perspectives of persons with lived or living experience (PWLLE) is increasingly expected by major funders. The MUHC Patient Engagement Office (Centre for Applied Ethics) can help identify PWLLE collaborators, advise on appropriate forms of engagement (advisory roles, co-investigators, recruitment partners), and support onboarding throughout the study lifecycle. Engaging PWLLE at the design stage gives their input more weight than consulting at the end.

    - Patient Engagement Office (MUHC): [contactpp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:contactpp@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - Research Facilitator: [research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca)

    **Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI)**

    EDI considerations have moved from encouraged to required for most funded research. CIHR requires GBA+ / sex and gender considerations in study design and EDI attestation for team composition; FRQS and CFI have similar requirements. In 2023–24, 85% of CIHR training and career-support research integrated sex and gender considerations.

    The RI-MUHC EDI team provides GBA+ consultation for grant proposals, inclusive recruitment and retention support, Tri-Agency-aligned self-assessment tools, and workshops on unconscious bias and inclusive leadership. EDI consultation is time-sensitive relative to grant deadlines — engage early. Full detail on funder requirements, programs, and contacts is on the [Equity, Diversity and Inclusion](/kb/edi) page.

    - EDI Team (RI-MUHC): [edi.ri@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:edi.ri@muhc.mcgill.ca)
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Regulatory and ethics">
    The regulatory pathway should be resolved at the design stage — not at submission.

    **Health Canada authorization**

    The most important determination is whether your study requires a Health Canada authorization. Drug trials require a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">CTA</Tooltip> under Division 5; device studies require an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip>. Both run on their own review clocks — Health Canada has a default 30-day review period — and must be resolved before a study can be activated. The CTA or ITA submission runs **in parallel** with REB review, not after it.

    **REB approval and institutional authorization**

    Every study at the RI-MUHC requires both REB approval **and** institutional authorization from the <Tooltip tip="Individual formally authorized to issue the final written authorization for each research project at a Quebec RSSS institution." headline="Personne mandatée (PFM)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip>. These are separate approvals — REB approval alone does not authorize research activities. No study procedure may begin until both are in your <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>.

    - MUHC Research Ethics Board: [reb@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - Personne mandatée Office: [personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca)

    The full regulatory framework — Health Canada authorization types, the ÉFVP process, and the three-framework model (TCPS2 + ICH GCP + Division 5) — is covered in [Regulations and Guidelines](/kb/regulations). For the ÉFVP decision tree and full privacy obligations, see [Privacy and Confidentiality in Quebec](/kb/privacy).

    - Research Facilitator: [Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca)
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Team and operations">
    **Credentials and affiliation**

    Before any study can be submitted to Nagano, the <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip> must hold valid Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status at the RI-MUHC, and all team members on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> must hold current compliance training appropriate to their study role. Missing privileges or lapsed training will hold up submission regardless of where the rest of the file stands.

    Beyond credentials, **every team member must have a valid employment or affiliation agreement with the RI-MUHC before performing any study tasks**. This applies to students, postdocs, trainees, volunteers, and observers — not only paid staff. Individuals with existing clinical roles at the MUHC still require a separate research agreement. No individual without a valid agreement can be delegated study tasks or appear on the TDL.

    **Hiring and HR — People & Culture**

    If your study team needs to grow, engage People & Culture early. All positions must be posted bilingually for a minimum of five business days before an offer can be extended — allow additional time for the Talent Acquisition team to review and publish the posting before the clock starts. Work permit renewals for international staff are currently taking up to nine months through IRCC — if anyone on your team holds a work permit approaching expiry, start the renewal process at least six months early.

    For full hiring procedures, contract templates, summer worker SOPs, and trainee affiliation agreements, see the [Hiring and HR support](/kb/hiring-hr-support) page.

    - People & Culture — HR Service Centre: [ri.hr.centre@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:ri.hr.centre@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - Talent Acquisition — hiring and posting: [research.talent@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:research.talent@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - Training and Learning Solutions — SOP Reader, TalentLMS support: [training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - Quality Assurance — SOP content, QA feasibility review: [QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca)

  <Accordion title="Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)">
    CIM engagement is not required for every study, but the cost of evaluating it late is high. The McConnell Centre for Innovative Medicine provides dedicated clinical infrastructure, specialized nursing, <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> services, and technical platforms at both the Glen and Montreal General Hospital sites.

    **When CIM is typically the right path**

    For Phase I studies, oncology protocols, industry-sponsored trials requiring IV administration, and Sponsor-Investigator studies, CIM involvement is typically the default — not an option to evaluate after REB submission. CIM's Functional Testing Platform (CPET, PFT, ECG, body composition) and Containment Level II Processing Laboratory are also available for studies requiring those specific capabilities.

    **The budget timing rule**

    CIM costs must be built into your initial budget before the CTA is filed. Adding CIM after the Site Agreement is signed requires a budget amendment, which delays activation. The feasibility conversation and budget estimate must happen in parallel with protocol development — before REB submission and before sponsor confirmation.

    Phase I capacity is finite and pre-booking is common. If your Phase I study has a tight activation timeline, contact CIM during protocol development — not after REB submission.

    [Planning Your Study with the CIM](/cim/planning) walks through study types, the engagement process, and budget timing.

    - CIM — general inquiries: [clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca)
  </Accordion>
