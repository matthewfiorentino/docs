In Quebec, the [Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991) decides who may consent to research, including who consents for a minor or for an adult who can't consent. The international good clinical practice guideline leaves those questions to local law, so in a sponsored trial the Civil Code still decides.

## Obtain consent before any study procedure

Before anyone is asked to consent, [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-006#5-3-8) require the study to have:
- Written approval from the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip>.
- [MUHC Authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview).
- A site initiation visit that has taken place.
- The sponsor's go letter, if the sponsor issues one. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email.

Then follow the steps that [the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-008#5-1) require:
1. Give the participant the current REB-approved consent form.
2. Explain the study in plain terms.
3. Answer the participant's questions.
4. Leave them time to decide.
5. If they agree, have everyone required sign and date the form.
6. Give the participant a copy.

## Who can consent

Who consents depends on the participant's age and whether they can consent.

| Participant | Who consents |
|---|---|
| An adult (18 or over) who can consent | The participant. |
| A minor under 14 | The person with parental authority, or the tutor. |
| A minor aged 14 to 17 | The person with parental authority, or the tutor. The minor may consent alone only if the REB finds that the research involves only minimal risk and that the circumstances justify it. |
| An adult who can't consent | Their mandatary or tutor. If the adult has neither, the person qualified to consent to their care may consent, but only to research with minimal risk. When the REB reviews the project, it decides whether the research involves only minimal risk. |
| An adult who suddenly can't consent, in research that must start promptly | The person qualified to consent to their care, if there's no time to designate a legal representative. When the REB reviews the project, it decides whether the research must start promptly and leaves no time to designate a legal representative. If no prior consent from the participant or a representative is possible, enrolment should follow the measures in the protocol or another REB-approved document. |

A minor of 14 or over can consent alone to the care their health requires. Consent to research follows the stricter rules in the table.

A minor or an adult who can't consent cannot take part if they understand the nature and consequences of the research and object to it, even if someone else has consented for them. Explain the study to them in words suited to their age and understanding. If they are able, they should also sign and date the form or an assent form.

In Quebec, research that could interfere with the integrity of a minor or of an adult who can't consent must also meet these conditions:
- An REB formed or designated by Quebec's health minister must approve and monitor it. [The MUHC REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) is a designated one.
- If the participant is the only subject of the research, it must have the potential to benefit their health.
- If the research is on a group, it must have the potential to produce results that benefit people in the same age group or with the same disease or disability.
- The risk must not be disproportionate to the benefit that may reasonably be anticipated, given the participant's state of health and personal condition.

On the RI-MUHC's forms, the person who signs for a participant who can't consent is the <Tooltip tip="The person with legal authority to consent for a participant who cannot consent." headline="Legally Appointed Representative (LAR)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#lar">legally appointed representative (LAR)</Tooltip>.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA; master doc, QA, "One name for the representative"): whether the person qualified to consent to care, who signs in the table's last two rows, is an LAR; SOP-CR-008_08 §5.8.3 sets that person apart from the LAR, while the REB's adult clinical-trial template has its "legal representative" sign "in cases of sudden incapacity" as "spouse, close relative, or person close to the participant" (blind check 7). */} When a participant can't consent, record that fact, the representative's name and their relationship to the participant. The representative cannot be the researcher or any other member of the research team. [Pediatric Research Considerations](/kb/recruitment/pediatric-research) and [Vulnerable Populations in Research](/kb/recruitment/vulnerable-populations) cover children and other participants who need added protection.

## Choose who obtains consent

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-008#5-1-2) require the person obtaining consent to:
- Be qualified by education and experience.
- Have documented training on [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006), [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) and the protocol.
- Know the study procedures.
- Have been delegated the task by the principal investigator on the Task Delegation Log.

The person obtaining consent should not be the participant's treating physician or anyone else with a long-standing relationship with them, because a patient who depends on that person may feel unable to say no. In practice, the clinical research coordinator or another member of the research team often leads the consent discussion.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): whether this describes practice (14-open-questions.md, QA block, item (b)). */} The principal investigator may be present to answer questions.

Patients asked by their own doctor to join a study may assume it is part of their treatment, and not realize that research aims mainly to produce knowledge and may bring them no therapeutic benefit. This misunderstanding is called [therapeutic misconception](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html).

When participants are also a clinician-researcher's patients, [the REB requires measures to minimize it](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops). Recruitment and consent should make clear which parts of the study are done for research and how they differ from the care the patient would otherwise receive. Clinicians must take care not to create unrealistic expectations about the research's potential benefits.

Involving the patient's own clinician in recruitment and consent can sometimes be in the patient's best interests. The proposal must then describe other measures to minimize therapeutic misconception. For example, the clinician may lead the consent discussion while someone not involved in the patient's care asks for their agreement.

If the treating physician is considered the best person to obtain consent, they must explain to the REB why it is necessary. If the REB approves, the physician must tell the participant about their two roles.

## Prepare the consent form

Start from the [Centre for Applied Ethics consent form templates](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms). To choose the right template and find the wording you may not change, use [Consent and Assent Templates](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates). You should always submit the consent form and other participant-facing material to the REB as editable Word files, not PDFs.

If the sponsor provides its own form, insert word for word the paragraphs required by Quebec's health ministry, the Ministère de la Santé, de la Prévention et des Aînés. [The required paragraphs](/sops/cr-006#5-3-2) cover:
- Voluntary participation and the right to withdraw.
- Confidentiality.
- What happens should the participant suffer any harm.
- Contact information.
- Signatures.

[The REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) rejects a form without these paragraphs. Check the finished form against the [Verification of an ICF Document](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249038753110964987) checklist.

The form needs:
- The version number or date on each page, also shown in the file name of an electronic version.
- Signature and date lines for the participant and the person obtaining consent, and for the investigator, the representative, a witness or an interpreter where they apply.
- An English and a French version, as for all participant-facing material, unless the REB accepts a justification.
- For a clinical trial, the registry's name and web address and the trial's registration number, as [the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-007#5-8-5) require. The template's registration paragraph has no space for the number, so add it.
- For a study of an investigational product (a drug, medical device or natural health product), MUHC letterhead with the Medical Records bar code FMU-9997, so that the form is filed in the Alert section of the participant's electronic medical record. [Not every template carries the bar code](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates#edit-the-template). The form must also state that participation will be recorded in the participant's medical record.
- Letterhead and logos that follow the MUHC's logo guidelines, if the form uses them. The guidelines are listed on the MUHC's [Media contact page](https://muhc.ca/media-contact).
- Information about any payments, such as the indemnity for loss and inconvenience, reimbursement of participation-related expenses and compensation for injury. When applicable, the information includes the method, amounts and schedule of payment.

Once the REB approves the form in one language, [have it translated and submit the translation to the REB](/sops/cr-007#5-4-8). The REB requires your team to attest to the translation's accuracy, or a translator's attestation certifying that the translation accurately reflects the REB-approved form. Use the translated form only once the REB has approved it.

When required, the translation should be done by a qualified or certified translator, and a certificate of translation should be obtained.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): what SOP-CR-006_08 section 5.3.7's "when required" covers (the blind review, batch 88, finding 7). */} If the translator doesn't provide one, you can use the [Certificate of Translation](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249039073901978848) template. A sponsor-investigator without funds for professional translation may translate the form or delegate its translation.

## Adapt the consent process when needed

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| The participant or their representative can't read | An impartial witness must be present for the whole consent discussion. Read the form aloud and explain it, then obtain verbal consent from the participant or the representative. The witness and the participant sign and date the form. If the participant isn't capable, the representative signs in their place. |
| The participant asks to have the form read to them | An impartial witness must be present during the reading and the whole consent discussion. The witness attests that the information was accurately explained and that the participant gave consent. |
| The participant doesn't speak English or French | Hold the consent discussion in the participant's preferred language with an impartial, qualified interpreter. If possible, provide a certified translation of the form in the participant's language. The participant and the interpreter sign and date the REB-approved form. |

### Obtain consent remotely or electronically

As of November 2026, the RI-MUHC doesn't have complete guidance on remote consent. Consult the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you set up a remote consent workflow.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 14 to 34", answer 19: "Put Research Facilitator. We don't really have complete guidance for this yet"). */}

Submit a [written remote consent procedure](/sops/cr-008#5-2-3) to the REB with the electronic consent form and any participant-facing material. The REB must acknowledge them before you use them.

The procedure must name the software and confirm that its installation has been validated and meets the rules on electronic records. For a drug trial, those include Health Canada's [guidance on Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations (GUI-0100)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100.html), which refers to the US Food and Drug Administration's rule, Part 11 of Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR Part 11).

For a Health Canada-regulated study, the procedure should also describe how the form will be explained and discussed with the participant. The software must be able to confirm the participant's identity. The site must also have documented procedures, trained users and secure storage for the signed forms.

In its [interim policy for clinical trials](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/announcements/interim-policy-modernization-clinical-trial-framework-notice.html), Health Canada accepts consent obtained remotely or electronically in drug trials when the process meets these conditions:
- The process is appropriately documented.
- The process follows good clinical practice.
- Consent is informed and given in accordance with the laws that govern consent, such as the Civil Code's rule on written consent in Quebec.

## Compensate participants within the limits

Quebec limits what participants may be paid. In research that could interfere with a participant's integrity, [Quebec's Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:25) allows an indemnity for the loss and inconvenience the participant suffers, and no other financial reward. Participants should receive [compensation for the loss and restriction they suffer](/sops/cr-007#5-7-4), on condition that it is not an undue inducement and is paid pro rata. The compensation should also comply with both ethics and the law, including Quebec's limit.

Before approving any study, [the REB reviews](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) the amount paid to participants for their losses and constraints, and the method of payment, to check that neither coerces nor unduly influences participants.

National and international ethics guidance allows payment for taking part, but at the MUHC it doesn't override the Civil Code's limit:

| Guidance | Applies to | Payment for taking part |
|---|---|---|
| The [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html), the federal research agencies' ethics policy | Research under the auspices of institutions that the agencies fund, the RI-MUHC included | Incentives for taking part are neither recommended nor discouraged. Where incentives are offered, they should not be so large or attractive as to encourage reckless disregard of risks. The limit on incentives matters particularly for healthy volunteers in the early phases of clinical trials. |
| The good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) | Interventional clinical trials of investigational products that are intended to be submitted to regulatory authorities | If participants are compensated for taking part, payments should be timely, prorated and not wholly contingent on completing the trial. |

A parent, tutor or other representative should not receive an incentive for arranging the participant's involvement. The representative may accept reasonable compensation suited to the circumstances on the participant's behalf, within Quebec's limits.

For amounts and reimbursement in practice, use [Pay and reimburse participants](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#pay-and-reimburse-participants).

## Keep consent current

Keep each participant's consent valid throughout the study by giving them ongoing chances to ask questions. Tell them in a timely way about any important new information that may be relevant to their consent. You should document that you told them.

Participants can withdraw their consent at any time, even verbally, and can ask for their data or human biological materials to be withdrawn too. You should clearly explain any circumstances in which data or materials can't be withdrawn once collected.

Do not use an amended consent form until [the REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) has approved it in writing. If the sponsor issues a go letter for the amendment, wait for that letter too. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-006#5-7-1) allow an exception in an emergency that poses an immediate safety hazard to participants, and in some cases that the [consent process SOP](/sops/cr-008) covers.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): which cases SOP-CR-006_08 section 5.7.1 means by "as covered under exceptions to informed consent process (Refer to SOP-CR-008)". Until QA answers, the page says only that such cases exist (REVIEW-HARNESS-HUB §2: never drop an exception). */} In an emergency, a documented conversation with the participant or their representative is required.

[Re-consent each affected participant](/sops/cr-008#5-7-4) on the amended form at their first visit to the site after the REB's written approval and the sponsor's go letter, if there is one. The sponsor, the REB or a study or department SOP may recommend or require something different. If a participant who is still taking part turns 18 or regains the capacity to consent, obtain their own signed consent as soon as possible.

Record each consent and re-consent on the [Informed Consent Process Checklist](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249391094414134256). The person who obtained the consent must complete it. Give the participant a copy of each signed amended form. File the originals and the checklists with the study's source documents.

When a participant starts a study of an investigational product, [send MUHC Medical Records Services](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#notify-medical-records-and-give-a-wallet-card) the Confirmation of Participation in Research form (FMU-9997) and a copy of the signed consent form.{/* TO CONFIRM (the Research Facilitator): the address or contact page research teams should use for MUHC Medical Records Services (STYLE-HUB H, contacts in the section).
