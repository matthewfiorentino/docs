In Quebec, the Civil Code sets who may consent to research and who may consent for someone who can't, and Quebec courts interpret it; good clinical practice guidelines leave these questions to local law. The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) on [consent forms](/sops/cr-006) and the [consent process](/sops/cr-008) set how your team obtains consent, records it and keeps it current through the study.

## Obtain consent before any study procedure

The person obtaining consent gives the participant the consent form approved by the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip>, explains the study in plain terms, answers questions and leaves time to decide. Once everyone required has signed and dated the form, the participant receives a copy.

For research that could interfere with a person's integrity, consent is given in writing. The REB may accept consent given another way when the circumstances justify it, and then decides how it's recorded.

Do not consent anyone until the study has the REB's written approval and [MUHC Authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview), the site initiation visit has been held and you have the sponsor's go letter (its written permission to start, which often arrives by email), if the sponsor issues one.

<Warning>
All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes early is a deviation.
</Warning>

The procedures most often done too early, before the consent form is signed, are drawing blood for screening lab tests and recording medical history in the electronic case report form. If a procedure happens before consent, [record and report the deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events). Repeated or deliberate failure to obtain consent properly, or to document the consent process, is among the reasons the REB may suspend or end a study, or suspend its recruitment.

To check eligibility in health records before someone consents, you need the REB's approval to use the records without consent and MUHC authorization to access them. You ask for both in your Nagano submission, where you answer the questions on access to information for pre-screening. The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, also completes the short version of the [évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP)](/kb/governance/privacy), the MUHC's privacy impact assessment.

Based on: [SOP-CR-008, section 3](/sops/cr-008#standard-consent-process); [SOP-CR-006, section 5.3](/sops/cr-006#5-3-icf-content) (no consent before the approvals); [SOP-CR-026, section 5.5](/sops/cr-026#5-5-suspension-or-termination-of-reb-approved-studies) (suspension); for pre-screening, [SOP-CR-007, section 5.4](/sops/cr-007#5-4-initial-reb-and-institutional-review) and the [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1), section 44; [Civil Code of Québec](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991), article 24.

## Who can consent

Research that could interfere with a person's integrity needs an REB's approval and monitoring, and its risk must not be disproportionate to the benefit that can reasonably be expected.

Research with minors, or with adults who can't consent, must be approved and monitored by an REB that the Minister of Health and Social Services has formed or designated; the MUHC REB is a designated one. That research must also have the potential to benefit the participant's health or, for research on a group, people in the same age group or with the same disease or disability.

| Participant | Who consents |
|---|---|
| An adult (18 or over) who can consent | The participant. |
| A minor under 14 | The person with parental authority, or the tutor. |
| A minor aged 14 to 17 | The person with parental authority, or the tutor. The minor may consent alone only if the REB finds that the research involves only minimal risk and that the circumstances justify it. |
| An adult who can't consent | Their mandatary or tutor. If the adult has neither, the person qualified to consent to their care may consent, but only to research with minimal risk, as the REB decides when it reviews the project. |
| An adult who suddenly can't consent, in research that must start promptly | The person qualified to consent to their care, when there's no time to designate a legal representative, as the REB decides when it reviews the project. Enrolment then should follow the measures set out in the protocol or another REB-approved document. |

A minor or an adult who can't consent, and who understands the nature and consequences of the research and objects to it, cannot take part, whoever has consented for them. Tell the participant about the study as far as they can understand it; if able, they should also sign the form or an assent.

The RI-MUHC's SOPs and forms call the person who consents for someone else the legally appointed representative (LAR). When a participant can't consent, record that, and record the representative's name and their relationship to the participant. The person who consents for a participant cannot be the researcher or any other member of the research team. For children and other participants who need added protection, use [Pediatric Research Considerations](/kb/recruitment/pediatric-research) and [Vulnerable Populations](/kb/recruitment/vulnerable-populations).

Full age in Quebec is 18. A minor of 14 or over can consent alone to the care their health requires, but research follows the stricter rules for minors.

Based on: [Civil Code of Québec](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991), articles 14, 20, 21 and 153; [SOP-CR-008, sections 3.1](/sops/cr-008#3-1-requirements) and [7.1](/sops/cr-008#7-1-assent); the MUHC REB's [SOP 404.001-2](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops), section 5.7; the [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html), article 3.9.

## Choose who obtains consent

The person obtaining consent must be qualified by education and experience, trained on [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006), [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) and the protocol, with that training documented, and knowledgeable about the study procedures. The principal investigator must have delegated the task to them on the Task Delegation Log.

It should not be the participant's treating physician, or anyone else with a long-standing relationship with them: a patient who depends on that person may feel unable to say no. In practice, the clinical research coordinator or another member of the research team often leads the consent discussion, and the principal investigator may be present to answer questions.

When participants are also a clinician-researcher's patients, measures must be taken to minimize therapeutic misconception. Participants have that misconception when they don't understand that research aims mainly to produce knowledge and may bring them no therapeutic benefit. Recruitment and consent should make clear which parts of the study are done for research and how they differ from the care the patient would otherwise receive, and clinicians must take care not to create unrealistic expectations about the research's benefits.

Where involving the patient's own clinician in recruitment and consent serves the patient's best interests, the proposal must say what other measures will minimize therapeutic misconception. For example, the clinician-researcher may lead the consent discussion, and someone not involved in the patient's care may then ask for their agreement.

<Note>
If the treating physician is the best person to obtain consent, they must explain why to the REB. If the REB approves, the physician must tell the participant about their two roles.
</Note>

Based on: [SOP-CR-008, section 3.1](/sops/cr-008#3-1-requirements); the MUHC REB's SOP 404.001-2, sections 5.1 and 5.2; TCPS 2, [chapter 11](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html) (its sections on therapeutic misconception and on clinician-researchers' dual roles, and article 11.5).

## Prepare the consent form

Start from the [Centre for Applied Ethics consent form templates](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms), and use [Consent and Assent Templates](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates) to choose the right one and find the wording you may not change.

If the sponsor provides its own form, insert word for word the paragraphs that Quebec's health ministry, the Ministère de la Santé, de la Prévention et des Aînés, requires: voluntary participation and the right to withdraw; confidentiality; what happens should the participant suffer any harm; contact information; and signatures. The REB rejects a form without them. Check the finished form against the [Verification of an ICF Document](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249038753110964987) checklist (sign-in required).

The form needs:
- The version number or date on each page, also shown in the file name of an electronic version.
- Signature and date lines for the participant and the person obtaining consent, and for the investigator, the representative, a witness or an interpreter where they apply.
- An English and a French version, as for all participant-facing material, unless the REB accepts a justification.
- For a clinical trial, the registry's name and web address and the trial's registration number, as the RI-MUHC's SOPs require. The template's registration paragraph names the registry but has no space for the number, so add it.
- For a study of an investigational product (a drug, medical device or natural health product), MUHC letterhead with the Medical Records bar code FMU-9997, which [not every template carries](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates#edit-the-template), and a statement that participation will be recorded in the participant's medical record.
- Letterhead and logos, where the form uses them, that follow the MUHC's logo guidelines, listed on its [Media contact page](https://muhc.ca/media-contact).
- Information about any payments, such as the indemnity for loss and inconvenience, reimbursement of participation-related expenses and compensation for injury, and, for a clinical trial, the methods, amounts and schedule of payment.

Once the REB approves the form in one language, have it translated and submit the translation to the REB. Translations should be done by a qualified or certified translator when required, and a translation certificate should be obtained; use the [Certificate of Translation](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249039073901978848) template (sign-in required) if the translator doesn't provide one.

A sponsor-investigator without funds for professional translation may translate the form, or delegate its translation. Submit the consent form and other participant-facing material to the REB as editable Word files, not PDFs.

Based on: [SOP-CR-006, sections 5.1](/sops/cr-006#5-1-general-overview), [5.2](/sops/cr-006#5-2-icf-guidelines) and [5.3](/sops/cr-006#5-3-icf-content); [SOP-CR-007, section 5.8](/sops/cr-007#5-8-clinical-trial-registration) (the registry details); [SOP-CR-008, section 5](/sops/cr-008#ip-studies-medical-records); for payment information, the [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html), article 3.2, and the International Council for Harmonisation's guideline [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), section 1.2.9.

## Adapt the consent process when needed

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| The participant or their representative can't read | An impartial witness must be present for the whole discussion. The form is read aloud and explained, and the participant or representative gives verbal consent. The participant (if able) or the representative, and the witness, sign and date the form. |
| The participant asks to have the form read to them | An impartial witness must be present during the reading and the whole consent discussion. The witness attests that the information was accurately explained and that the participant gave consent. |
| The participant doesn't speak English or French | Hold the discussion in the participant's preferred language with an impartial, qualified interpreter and, if possible, give them a certified translation of the form. The participant and the interpreter sign and date the REB-approved form. |
| Research using health records without consent | The REB may approve it. At the MUHC, it also needs an EFVP, approved by the MUHC's Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality, and MUHC authorization to access the records. |

### Obtain consent remotely or electronically

For drug trials, Health Canada's interim policy accepts consent obtained remotely or electronically when the process is documented, follows good clinical practice and meets the laws that govern consent, including the Civil Code's rule on written consent.

Submit a written remote consent procedure to the REB with the electronic form and any participant-facing material; the REB must acknowledge them before you use them. The procedure names the software and confirms that it is fully validated and complies with electronic records guidance and regulations. For a Health Canada-regulated study, it should also describe how the form will be explained and discussed.

The software must also be able to confirm the participant's identity, and the process needs documented procedures, trained users and secure storage of the signed forms.

For a drug trial, Health Canada's [guidance on Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations (GUI-0100)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100.html) applies; for more on electronic records, it refers to the US Food and Drug Administration rule, Part 11 of Title 21 of the Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR Part 11).

Based on: [SOP-CR-006, section 5.2](/sops/cr-006#5-2-icf-guidelines); the MUHC REB's SOP 404.001-2, section 5.4 (witnesses and interpreters); [SOP-CR-008, sections 4](/sops/cr-008#remote-electronic-consent), [7](/sops/cr-008#special-populations) and [8](/sops/cr-008#retrospective-studies); [SOP-CR-007, section 5.4](/sops/cr-007#5-4-initial-reb-and-institutional-review) and the [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1), section 44 (health records without consent); Health Canada's [interim policy](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/announcements/interim-policy-modernization-clinical-trial-framework-notice.html) (Sept. 14, 2026); [Civil Code of Québec](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991), article 24.

## Compensate participants within the limits

Participants in research that could interfere with their integrity may receive an indemnity for the loss and inconvenience they suffer, and no other financial reward.

Quebec's rule is stricter than national and international ethics guidance. Researchers following the [*Tri-Council Policy Statement*](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html) (TCPS 2) or the good clinical practice guideline (E6) of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) may pay participants for taking part, for example healthy volunteers in Phase I trials, as long as the payment isn't so large that it unduly influences the decision.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-007#5-7-conflict-of-interest-coi) add that the amount must not be an undue inducement and is paid pro rata, in proportion to the participation. For amounts and reimbursement in practice, use [Recruitment, Screening and Enrolment](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment).

A parent, tutor or other representative should not receive an incentive for arranging the participant's involvement. They may accept, on the participant's behalf, compensation suited to the circumstances, within the same limits.

Based on: [Civil Code of Québec](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991), article 25; [SOP-CR-007, section 5.7](/sops/cr-007#5-7-conflict-of-interest-coi); [TCPS 2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html), article 3.1, and [chapter 11](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html); [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), section 1.2.8.

## Keep consent current

Consent continues for the whole study. Participants can withdraw at any time, and can ask that their data or biological material be withdrawn too. Give them chances to ask questions, and tell them promptly about any important new information that could affect their decision to stay; this should be documented.

Do not use an amended consent form until you have the REB's written approval and the sponsor's go letter for the amendment, if the sponsor issues one. Then re-consent each affected participant on it at their first visit after that, unless the sponsor or the REB asks for something different. A participant who turns 18, or who regains the capacity to consent, signs their own consent as soon as possible.

Record each consent and re-consent on the [Informed Consent Process Checklist](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249391094414134256) (sign-in required); the person who obtained the consent must complete it. Give the participant a copy of each signed amended form, and file the originals and the checklists with the study's source documents.

When a participant starts a study of an investigational product, send MUHC Medical Records Services the Confirmation of Participation in Research form (FMU-9997) and a copy of the signed consent form, for the Alert section of the participant's electronic medical record (OACIS), so that any caregiver knows about the study. Follow [the steps for notifying Medical Records](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#notify-medical-records-and-give-a-wallet-card).

If your situation is different, ask the REB about consent rules, or the Research Facilitator about RI-MUHC processes.

Based on: [SOP-CR-006, section 5.4](/sops/cr-006#5-4-ongoing-amendments); [SOP-CR-008, sections 5](/sops/cr-008#ip-studies-medical-records), [6](/sops/cr-008#ongoing-consent) and [9](/sops/cr-008#documenting-consent); [TCPS 2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html), articles 3.1 and 3.3.
