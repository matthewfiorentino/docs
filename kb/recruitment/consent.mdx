In Quebec, the [Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991) decides who may consent to research, including who consents for a minor or for an adult who can't consent. The international good clinical practice guideline leaves those questions to local law, so in a sponsored trial the Civil Code still decides.

## Obtain consent before any study procedure

Before anyone is asked to consent, [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-006#5-3-8) require the study to have:
- Written approval from the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip>.
- [MUHC Authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview).
- A site initiation visit that has taken place.
- The sponsor's go letter, if the sponsor issues one. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email.

Then follow the steps that [the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-008#5-1) require:
1. Give the participant the current REB-approved consent form.
2. Explain the study in plain terms.
3. Answer the participant's questions.
4. Leave them time to decide.
5. If they agree, have everyone required sign and date the form.
6. Give the participant a copy.

Study teams sometimes start a screening procedure before the form is fully signed. Most often, they draw blood for screening lab tests or enter the participant's medical history in the electronic case report form. If a procedure is done before consent, follow the steps for [recording and reporting a deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations). If consent is repeatedly or deliberately obtained or documented improperly, the REB may [suspend or end the study, or suspend its recruitment](/sops/cr-026#5-5-2).

[Quebec's Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:24) also requires written consent for research that could interfere with a person's integrity. [The REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) may accept consent given another way if the circumstances justify it, and then decides how the consent is documented.

Before your team [checks eligibility in health records ahead of consent](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent) or does research entirely from health records without anyone's consent, the study needs the REB's approval to use the records without consent, an approved <Tooltip tip="The privacy impact assessment, or évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée, that Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information requires with a researcher's request for access to health and social services information without consent, including access before consent for pre-screening." headline="EFVP (privacy impact assessment)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#efvp">privacy impact assessment (EFVP)</Tooltip> and then MUHC Authorization, which includes access to the records.

### Ask the REB to alter the consent requirements

Some research questions can't be answered without an alteration to the usual consent requirements of the [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#7a). An alteration may include telling participants only part of the study's purpose, deceiving them entirely about its purpose, or not telling them that they, their data or their biological materials are involved in a study.

[Quebec's Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:24) requires that consent to research that could interfere with a person's integrity be given in writing. If [the REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) judges that the circumstances justify it, the consent may be given otherwise than in writing, in the manner the REB determines for evidential purposes.

The REB may approve research that involves an alteration to the consent requirements if it is satisfied, and documents, that all of these apply:
- The research involves no more than minimal risk to the participants.
- The alteration is unlikely to adversely affect the welfare of participants.
- It is impossible or impracticable to carry out the research and to address the research question properly, given the research design, if the participants' prior consent is required.
- For a proposed alteration, its precise nature and extent are defined.
- Any plan to debrief participants, which may also offer them the possibility of refusing consent or withdrawing their data or human biological materials, follows [TCPS 2's rules on debriefing](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#7b).

Debriefing must be part of all research that involves an alteration to the consent requirements whenever it is possible, practicable and appropriate. Participants in research that involves an alteration must have the opportunity to refuse consent and to request the withdrawal of their data or human biological materials whenever possible, practicable and appropriate.

Report to the REB any concerns about the conduct of the project that participants raise at the time of debriefing. If your collection methods don't allow data or human biological materials to be withdrawn once collected, provide the REB with a rationale for using them.

If you ask [the REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) for an exception to the requirement to debrief, provide a plan to disseminate information about the study to participants or their communities, for example through local media or direct mail.

Impracticable means an undue hardship or onerousness that jeopardizes the conduct of the research. Mere inconvenience doesn't make research impracticable.

Justify the need for any alteration to the REB's satisfaction. If the research can achieve its aims with a design that allows full or fuller prior disclosure, that design must be adopted.

When the conditions for an alteration are met, the REB may allow an exception to the requirement to seek participants' consent before collecting their data or human biological materials. If you ask for this exception, demonstrate that the alteration is necessary to address the research question and that the lack of prior consent will not adversely affect the participants' welfare. Also demonstrate that the direct, indirect or societal benefits of the research justify any risks of not seeking prior consent.

[The MUHC REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) may approve a consent procedure that leaves out or alters some or all of the elements of informed consent, provided that the researcher submits a satisfactory justification. The REB must also find and document that:
- The regulatory guidance framework for research and all previously expressed wishes support the waiver.
- It is impossible, practically impossible or inopportune to seek consent or to obtain fully informed consent.
- The waiver or alteration is unlikely to adversely affect the well-being of the participants or of their relations.
- The information is used in a manner that will ensure its confidentiality.
- Whenever appropriate, participants will be provided with additional pertinent information after participation.

TCPS 2's conditions for an alteration do not cover the secondary use of identifiable information, the use in research of information first collected for another purpose. Secondary use has [its own conditions](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html#5a).

### Obtain consent in a trial regulated by Health Canada

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span></div>

In a drug trial regulated by Health Canada, the sponsor must ensure that [informed consent given in accordance with the applicable laws governing consent is obtained from every person](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100/good-clinical-practices.html#c.05h) before that person takes part in the trial.

In a natural health product trial, the sponsor must ensure that [written informed consent given in accordance with the applicable laws governing consent is obtained from every person](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2003-196/section-74.html) before that person takes part in the trial.

For a natural health product trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, the consent form should outline the participant's responsibilities before, during and after the trial. The form should also include the other items that [Health Canada lists as required and frequently omitted from consent forms](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/clinical-trials.html#a3.2.4).

## Who can consent

Who consents depends on the participant's age and whether they can consent.

| Participant | Who consents |
|---|---|
| An adult (18 or over) who can consent | The participant. |
| A minor under 14 | The person with parental authority, or the tutor. |
| A minor aged 14 to 17 | The person with parental authority, or the tutor. The minor may consent alone only if the REB finds that the research involves only minimal risk and that the circumstances justify it. |
| An adult who can't consent | Their mandatary or tutor. If the adult has neither, the person qualified to consent to their care may consent, but only to research with minimal risk. When the REB reviews the project, it decides whether the research involves only minimal risk. |
| An adult who suddenly can't consent, in research that must start promptly | The person qualified to consent to their care, if there's no time to designate a legal representative. When the REB reviews the project, it decides whether the research must start promptly after the appearance of the condition that gives rise to the research, and whether that leaves no time to designate a legal representative. If no prior consent from the participant or a representative is possible, enrolment should follow the measures in the protocol or another REB-approved document. |

A minor of 14 or over can consent alone to the care their health requires. Consent to research follows the stricter rules in the table.

A minor or an adult who can't consent cannot take part if they understand the nature and consequences of the research and object to it, even if someone else has consented for them. Explain the study to them in words suited to their age and understanding. If they are able, they should also sign and date the form or an assent form.

In Quebec, research that could interfere with the integrity of a minor or of an adult who can't consent must also meet these conditions:
- An REB formed or designated by Quebec's health minister must approve and monitor it. [The MUHC REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) is a designated one.
- If the participant is the only subject of the research, it must have the potential to benefit their health.
- If the research is on a group, it must have the potential to produce results that benefit people in the same age group or with the same disease or disability.
- The risk must not be disproportionate to the benefit that may reasonably be anticipated, given the participant's state of health and personal condition.

On the RI-MUHC's forms, the person who signs for a participant who can't consent is the <Tooltip tip="The person with legal authority to consent for a participant who cannot consent." headline="Legally Appointed Representative (LAR)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#lar">legally appointed representative (LAR)</Tooltip>. When a participant can't consent, record that fact, the representative's name and their relationship to the participant. The representative cannot be the researcher or any other member of the research team. [Pediatric Research Considerations](/kb/recruitment/pediatric-research) and [Research with Participants in Vulnerable Circumstances](/kb/recruitment/vulnerable-populations) cover children and other participants who need added protection.

## Choose who obtains consent

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-008#5-1-2) require the person obtaining consent to:
- Be qualified by education and experience.
- Have documented training on [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006), [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) and the protocol.
- Know the study procedures.
- Have been delegated the task by the <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip> on the Task Delegation Log.

The person obtaining consent should not be the participant's treating physician or anyone else with a long-standing relationship with them, because a patient who depends on that person may feel unable to say no. In practice, the clinical research coordinator or another member of the research team often leads the consent discussion. The principal investigator may be present to answer questions.

Patients asked by their own doctor to join a study may assume it is part of their treatment, and not realize that research aims mainly to produce knowledge and may bring them no therapeutic benefit. This misunderstanding is called [therapeutic misconception](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html).

When participants are also a clinician-researcher's patients, [the REB requires measures to minimize it](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops). Recruitment and consent should make clear which parts of the study are done for research and how they differ from the care the patient would otherwise receive. Clinicians must take care not to create unrealistic expectations about the research's potential benefits.

Involving the patient's own clinician in recruitment and consent can sometimes be in the patient's best interests. The proposal must then describe other measures to minimize therapeutic misconception. For example, the clinician may lead the consent discussion while someone not involved in the patient's care asks for their agreement.

If the treating physician is considered the best person to obtain consent, they must explain to the REB why it is necessary. If the REB approves, the physician must disclose their dual role as clinician and researcher to the participant.

## Prepare the consent form

Start from the [Centre for Applied Ethics consent form templates](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms). To choose the right template and find the wording you may not change, use [Consent and Assent Templates](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates). You should always submit the consent form and other participant-facing material to the REB as editable Word files, not PDFs.

If the sponsor provides its own form, insert word for word the paragraphs required by Quebec's health ministry, the Ministère de la Santé, de la Prévention et des Aînés. [The required paragraphs](/sops/cr-006#5-3-2) cover:
- Voluntary participation and the right to withdraw.
- Confidentiality.
- What happens should the participant suffer any harm.
- Contact information.
- Signatures.

[The REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) rejects a form without these paragraphs. Check the finished form against the [Verification of an ICF Document](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249038753110964987) checklist.

The form needs:
- The version number or date on each page, also shown in the file name of an electronic version.
- The signature and date fields, and any initial fields, required for the participant, the person obtaining consent and, if applicable, the principal investigator.
- The signature and date fields, and any initial fields, that may be required for the legally appointed representative, a witness, or an interpreter or translator.
- An English and a French version, as for all participant-facing material, unless the REB accepts a justification.
- For a clinical trial, the registry's name and web address and the trial's registration number, as [the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-007#5-8-5) require. The template's registration paragraph has no space for the number, so add it.
- For a study of an investigational product (a drug, medical device or natural health product), MUHC letterhead with the Medical Records bar code FMU-9997, so that the form is filed in the Alert section of the participant's electronic medical record. [Not every template carries the bar code](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates#edit-the-template). The form must also state that participation will be recorded in the participant's medical record.
- Letterhead and logos that follow the MUHC's logo guidelines, if the form uses them. The guidelines are listed on the MUHC's [Media contact page](https://muhc.ca/media-contact).
- Information about any payments, such as the indemnity for loss and inconvenience, reimbursement of participation-related expenses and compensation for injury. When applicable, the information includes the method, amounts and schedule of payment.
- How the researcher informs the participant's treating physician of their participation, if applicable, and how the participant consents to having the physician informed. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-501#5-1-2) require the form to contain this wording: “I hereby authorize the researcher to inform my treating physician of my participation in this project”. There should be boxes to check for “YES”, “NO” or “I do not have a treating physician actively following me.”
- A space for the treating physician's name and contact information. If there is no treating physician, the person obtaining consent should indicate N/A there.

When you develop or adapt a master consent form, [also make sure that](/sops/cr-006#5-3-5):
- The form is consistent with the protocol, for example in its objectives, number of visits, study procedures, expected risks and alternative treatments.
- The form includes the sponsor's and the investigator's names.
- Every page is numbered, for example "page 1 of 15".

Make sure that [all the information in the consent form accurately reflects the protocol](/sops/cr-006#5-3-2) that the REB will review, whether the information comes from a sponsor or a sponsor-investigator. If the site finds that the form doesn't accurately reflect the protocol, for example because it leaves out procedures the protocol requires, the site should inform the sponsor or sponsor-investigator before the REB submission.

Before you submit a revised consent form to the REB, [have someone else at the site review it for quality control](/sops/cr-006#5-3-3).

For a clinical trial, if the participant agrees, the investigator [should obtain consent to contact the participant's primary caregiver](/sops/cr-006#5-3-4) about the participant's involvement in the study and any [clinically useful information in the context of continued care](/sops/cr-501).

In a clinical trial, if the consent form doesn't already ask for it, request the permission of the participant or their <Tooltip tip="The person with legal authority to consent for a participant who cannot consent." headline="Legally Appointed Representative (LAR)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#lar">legally appointed representative</Tooltip> [to notify the participant's family physician of their participation in the research](/sops/cr-008#5-1-21). If the participant has no family physician or doesn't want them notified, document it on the consent form or on the Informed Consent Process Checklist.

Once the REB approves the form in one language, [have it translated and submit the translation to the REB](/sops/cr-007#5-4-8). The REB requires your team to attest to the translation's accuracy, or a translator's attestation certifying that the translation accurately reflects the REB-approved form. Use the translated form only once the REB has approved it.

When required, the translation should be done by a qualified or certified translator, and a certificate of translation should be obtained. If the translator doesn't provide one, you can use the [Certificate of Translation](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249039073901978848) template. A sponsor-investigator without funds for professional translation may translate the form or delegate its translation.

## Adapt the consent process when needed

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| The participant or their representative can't read | An impartial witness must be present for the whole consent discussion. Read the form aloud and explain it, then obtain verbal consent from the participant or the representative. The witness and the participant sign and date the form. If the participant isn't capable, the representative signs in their place. |
| The participant asks to have the form read to them | An impartial witness must be present during the reading and the whole consent discussion. The witness attests that the information was accurately explained and that the participant gave consent. |
| The participant doesn't speak English or French | Hold the consent discussion in the participant's preferred language with an impartial, qualified interpreter or translator. If possible, provide a certified translation of the form in the participant's language. The participant and the interpreter or translator sign and date the REB-approved form. |
| The participant's representative doesn't speak English or French | [Determine whether an impartial interpreter or translator is needed](/sops/cr-008#5-1-6). If one is, make sure the form has a line for the interpreter or translator to sign and date. |
| The participant can't express consent by signing | Consent may be given verbally or with a written mark, provided that a witness is present and attests to it in writing. Document the consent in a way [consistent with the REB's approval and the applicable regulations](/sops/cr-008#5-1-17). If the REB approves verbal consent and requires an impartial witness, the impartial witness must sign the form, and a written form must still be given to the participant or their representative. |

### Obtain consent remotely or electronically

Some studies hold the consent discussion at a distance, by video for example, or have participants sign an electronic informed consent form (eICF). Electronic consent (eConsent) follows [every part of the consent process](/sops/cr-008#2-0-scope) that a paper form follows. Electronic consent adds conditions on the software, the signatures, the REB's review and the storage of the signed forms.

The RI-MUHC doesn't have complete guidance on remote consent. Consult the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you set up a remote consent workflow.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 14 to 34", answer 19: "Put Research Facilitator. We don't really have complete guidance for this yet"). */}

[Electronic signatures or electronic consent may be used](/sops/cr-008#5-1-19) if the software is fully validated and compliant with local, provincial, regulatory and REB requirements. The electronic signatures must also comply with local, provincial, regulatory and REB requirements. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs count an electronic signature as compliant with regulations](/sops/cr-015#5-1-9) only if it is made in a system validated to Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR Part 11).

A site that obtains consent electronically [must ensure that](/sops/cr-008#5-2-1):
- The software is fully validated.
- The software complies with the guidelines and regulations on electronic records.
- The site has documented procedures and appropriate training for users.
- Every signed eICF can be stored securely and easily retrieved, so that the participant's identity, study participation and personal information stay confidential for [the required retention period](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).

The eICF technology [must be able to confirm a person's identity](/sops/cr-008#5-2-4). The eICF [should be able to obtain at least two signatures](/sops/cr-008#5-2-2), the participant's and that of the person obtaining consent, and to produce a signed hard copy for the participant. An electronic signature that complies with regulations includes:
- The signer's printed name.
- The date and time stamp of the signature.
- A unique user ID code.
- A digital adopted signature.
- The meaning of the signature (the reason for signing).
- An audit trail.

Submit these to [the REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca), which [must acknowledge them](/sops/cr-008#5-2-3) before you use them:
- The eICFs, and their amendments and addenda.
- Any participant-facing material, including any added questions to assess the participant's understanding.
- A written remote informed consent process.

If the eICF uses electronic signatures, make sure that [the remote consent procedure you submit with it](/sops/cr-006#5-2-9) names the software and confirms that its installation has been validated and complies with the guidance and regulations on electronic records.

Not every participant can consent easily on a screen. [Consideration should be made](/sops/cr-008#5-2-3) of the population being recruited, and of whether the method used to obtain or document consent would impede a participant's capability to consent or assent.

[The REB usually requires written consent](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) on an REB-approved form, dated and signed by the participant or their representative and by the person obtaining consent. The REB may approve other means of obtaining consent in certain circumstances. [Document the consent process](/sops/cr-008#5-2-5) on the [Informed Consent Process Checklist](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249391094414134256), with clear documentation of every aspect of the study discussed with the potential participant.

A study regulated by Health Canada is [a drug, natural health product or medical device study conducted under Health Canada's regulations](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study), including a study that needs no authorization. For electronic consent, such a study follows the rules for every study, and some rules differ:

| | Every study | Studies regulated by Health Canada |
|---|---|---|
| How consent is recorded | In research that could interfere with a person's integrity, [Quebec's Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:24) requires consent in writing, unless the REB finds that the circumstances justify another way. The REB then decides how the consent is documented. The [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#12) requires evidence of consent in a signed consent form, or in the researcher's documentation of another appropriate means of consent. | The Civil Code's rule on written consent still applies at the MUHC. Under its interim policy, Health Canada no longer limits consent in a drug trial to written consent. In a natural health product trial, the sponsor must ensure that written informed consent is obtained from every person before that person takes part. In a drug trial with decentralized elements, there must be [a documented, retrievable and attributable record](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/clinical-trials-decentralized-elements/regulatory-ethics-approval-informed-consent.html) of each participant's consent. |
| Keeping the signed eICFs | Keep them for [the retention period that applies to the study](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving), which depends on whether Health Canada regulates it. | Keep them for the retention period of a study regulated by Health Canada, which applies once at least one participant has signed a consent form, screen failures included. |
| Platforms, REDCap included | Every platform must meet the conditions for electronic consent that apply to every study. Whether the RI-MUHC's REDCap may be used depends on whether Health Canada regulates the study. | The RI-MUHC's REDCap can't be used for electronic consent in these studies. Its compliance with the guidelines and regulations on electronic records and electronic signatures hasn't been validated. |

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs consider REDCap unfit for use as an electronic consent form](/sops/cr-014#5-8-1), because it allows only one signature per form and source data should be filed apart from the eCRF. The RI-MUHC nonetheless allows REDCap or another platform to be used for electronic consent in a study that Health Canada doesn't regulate, if the basic guidelines that [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](mailto:cord@rimuhc.ca) has been preparing with Quality Assurance are followed.

Before you plan electronic consent in a study that Health Canada doesn't regulate, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) for these guidelines. Among its REDCap services, CORD configures consent workflows. Requests go through its [Service Request Form](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/cim/redcap/surveys/?s=LPCYP8KP4JHP8C79).

#### Meet the added conditions for a study regulated by Health Canada

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span></div>

In a study regulated by Health Canada, the process for obtaining consent with an eICF [should also be well detailed in a written document or work instruction](/sops/cr-008#5-2-1). The work instruction should include how the eICF will be explained and discussed with the participant. For example, it can say whether the eICF includes security questions to confirm the person's identity, which questions will be asked to educate the participant and assess their understanding, and whether the participant can sign and obtain a paper copy.

For a drug trial, the rules on electronic records include Health Canada's [guidance on Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations (GUI-0100)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100/labelling-records.html). In a drug trial, any electronic system used to capture, process, manage or archive clinical trial information should be adequately validated. For more information on computerized system validation and electronic records, Health Canada refers to several documents, including the US Food and Drug Administration's rule, 21 CFR Part 11.

Through its [interim policy for clinical trials](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/announcements/interim-policy-modernization-clinical-trial-framework-notice.html), Health Canada considers the consent requirements of a drug trial met where the processes:
- Are appropriately documented.
- Comply with good clinical practices and the principles of the good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), ICH E6.
- Ensure that consent is informed and given in accordance with the applicable laws governing consent.

In a drug trial, the sponsor must also appropriately document consent processes and keep the records for verification by Health Canada's inspectors.

In a drug trial, Health Canada's move from written to documented consent allows new approaches, such as:
- Remote or electronic platforms for real-time, interactive consent discussions by audio and video.
- Electronic signatures, and the documentation of consent given orally, in person or remotely.

At the MUHC, the laws governing consent include [Quebec's rule on written consent](#obtain-consent-before-any-study-procedure) for research that could interfere with a person's integrity.

Participants may face added challenges in giving consent remotely. In a drug trial with decentralized elements, [sponsors and qualified investigators should be careful not to create a disadvantage](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/clinical-trials-decentralized-elements/regulatory-ethics-approval-informed-consent.html) for participants who face barriers to remote consent or prefer not to use virtual platforms. The barriers include reliable private internet access, digital literacy and comfort with virtual tools. Health Canada suggests offering other methods of consent to those who ask, including in-person consent meetings and physical copies of the consent form.

## Compensate participants within the limits

Quebec limits what participants may be paid. In research that could interfere with a participant's integrity, [Quebec's Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:25) allows an indemnity for the loss and inconvenience the participant suffers, and no other financial reward. Participants should receive [compensation for the loss and restriction they suffer](/sops/cr-007#5-7-4), on condition that it is not an undue inducement and is paid pro rata. The compensation should also comply with both ethics and the law, including Quebec's limit.

Before approving any study, [the REB reviews](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) the amount paid to participants for their losses and constraints, and the method of payment, to check that neither coerces nor unduly influences participants.

National and international ethics guidance allows payment for taking part, but at the MUHC it doesn't override the Civil Code's limit:

| Guidance | Applies to | Payment for taking part |
|---|---|---|
| The [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html), the federal research agencies' ethics policy | Research under the auspices of institutions that the agencies fund, the RI-MUHC included | Incentives for taking part are neither recommended nor discouraged. Where incentives are offered, they should not be so large or attractive as to encourage reckless disregard of risks. The limit on incentives matters particularly for healthy volunteers in the early phases of clinical trials. |
| The good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) | Interventional clinical trials of investigational products that are intended to be submitted to regulatory authorities | If participants are compensated for taking part, payments should be timely, prorated and not wholly contingent on completing the trial. |

A parent, tutor or other representative should not receive an incentive for arranging the participant's involvement. The representative may accept reasonable compensation suited to the circumstances on the participant's behalf, within Quebec's limits.

For amounts and reimbursement in practice, use [Pay and reimburse participants](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#pay-and-reimburse-participants).

## Keep consent current

Keep each participant's consent valid throughout the study by giving them ongoing chances to ask questions. Tell them in a timely way about any important new information that may be relevant to their consent. You should document that you told them.

Participants can withdraw their consent at any time, even verbally, and can ask for their data or human biological materials to be withdrawn too. You should clearly explain any circumstances in which data or materials can't be withdrawn once collected.

Do not use an amended consent form until [the REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) has approved it in writing. If the sponsor issues a go letter for the amendment, wait for that letter too. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-006#5-7-1) allow an exception in an emergency that poses an immediate safety hazard to participants, and in some cases that the [consent process SOP](/sops/cr-008) covers. In an emergency, a documented conversation with the participant or their representative is required.

[Re-consent each affected participant](/sops/cr-008#5-7-4) on the amended form at their first visit to the site after the REB's written approval and the sponsor's go letter, if there is one. The sponsor, the REB or a study or department SOP may recommend or require something different. If a participant who is still taking part turns 18 or regains the capacity to consent, obtain their own signed consent as soon as possible.

Record each consent and re-consent on the [Informed Consent Process Checklist](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249391094414134256). The person who obtained the consent must complete it. Give the participant a copy of each signed amended form. File the originals and the checklists with the study's source documents.

When a participant starts a study of an investigational product, [send MUHC Medical Records Services](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#notify-medical-records-and-give-a-wallet-card) the Confirmation of Participation in Research form (FMU-9997) and a copy of the signed consent form. Medical Records files them in the Alert section of the participant's electronic medical record (OACIS), so that any caregiver knows about the study.

If your situation is different, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) about consent rules, or the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) about RI-MUHC processes.
