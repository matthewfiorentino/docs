Recruitment, screening and enrolment at the MUHC follow the RI-MUHC's [standard operating procedure (SOP) on recruitment](/sops/cr-009), which applies to everyone involved. It sets how you have recruitment materials approved, starting with the sponsor and the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip>, how you screen and enrol participants, and the logs you keep.

## Before you recruit

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

Recruitment also waits for every other [start condition](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid), including a clinical trial's [registration in a public registry](/kb/close-out/publications#trial-registration) and, where there is one, the sponsor's go letter, its written permission to start.

To look in health records for possible participants before they consent, request [the approvals you need](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-before-any-study-procedure) in your <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> submission.

Based on: [SOP-CR-007, sections 5.4](/sops/cr-007#5-4-initial-reb-and-institutional-review) (section 5.4.11) and [5.8](/sops/cr-007#5-8-clinical-trial-registration) (section 5.8.3); [SOP-CR-009, sections 5.2](/sops/cr-009#5-2-reb-approval-of-recruitment-materials) (section 5.2.3) and [5.4](/sops/cr-009#5-4-access-to-participant-medical-records) (section 5.4.1).

## Plan your recruitment

If the study needs a recruitment plan, it's written before the study starts, by the sponsor, the principal investigator (who leads the study at the MUHC), the clinical research coordinator or a delegate.

For each step, from the population the study targets to the people found eligible, choose the methods that suit it, and weigh the issues each method raises, such as ethics, cost, regulations, privacy, staffing and timelines. Where you can, use several methods rather than one, such as hospital TV screens, websites, social media, posters, flyers, radio and newspapers. Write the plan down, with its timelines.

Any part of recruitment, screening and enrolment may be delegated to trained team members, but it remains the ultimate responsibility of the sponsor or the principal investigator. [People who are not affiliated with the RI-MUHC or the MUHC](/training/compliance-requirements), volunteers included, may not contact or recruit participants or perform procedures on them.

You should be inclusive in choosing whom you recruit. Do not exclude people because of [attributes such as their language, disability, gender or age](/kb/recruitment/consent#include-participants-fairly) unless there is a valid reason.

If your study doesn't fit these steps, ask the Research Facilitator for help.

Based on: [SOP-CR-009, sections 3](/sops/cr-009#responsibilities) (delegation) and [5.1](/sops/cr-009#5-1-developing-a-recruitment-plan); [SOP-CR-003, section 2](/sops/cr-003#scope); the [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter4-chapitre4.html), article 4.1.

## Set a realistic recruitment target

Recruitment often falls short of plans, and poor recruitment is the most common reason trials stop early. A 2014 study followed about 1,000 randomized trials approved from 2000 to 2003 by ethics committees in Switzerland, Germany and Canada. A quarter were discontinued, most often for poor recruitment, and investigator-sponsored trials were discontinued for poor recruitment far more often than industry-sponsored ones.

In a 2022 study, researchers, most of them at McGill's Biomedical Ethics Unit, asked principal investigators soon after their trials were registered how likely the trial was to reach its recruitment target. The average answer was 79 per cent; 43 per cent of the 281 trials whose recruitment had ended reached it.

Before you commit to a target, in a sponsor's feasibility questionnaire or a grant application, base it on data. In its good clinical practice guideline, the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) says the investigator should be able to show, for example from past or current data, a potential for recruiting the proposed number of eligible participants within the recruitment period agreed with the sponsor.

To set a target you can meet, work from your own numbers:
- Start from the potentially eligible people you can actually screen, not from everyone with the condition, and confirm your numbers with the [Study Feasibility Checklist](/kb/planning/feasibility#feasibility-checklist).
- For counts from MUHC data, ask the [Data Warehouse](/kb/design-support/data-warehouse) for aggregate data. Unlike other data requests, an aggregate request can be made at any time, for example for a feasibility check, taking into account the Data Warehouse's *Guidelines on Aggregate Data Requests*, on the RI-MUHC Portal.
- Commit to a number you can deliver. In a survey of people who choose trial sites in Europe, an investigator's record of recruiting and retaining participants was given the most weight among five investigator criteria. During a trial, monitors report recruitment rates.

### Estimate how long recruitment will take

Start from your target, the number your site will enrol. For a single-site study, it's the protocol's sample size; in a multicentre study, it's your site's target. If the sample size isn't set yet, the [Biostatistics Consulting Unit](/kb/design-support/bcu) can calculate it. Then work out the time:
1. Divide your target by the share of the people you screen whom you expect to enrol, from your own screening data where you have them. To enrol 30 participants when you enrol 1 in 3 of the people you screen, you need to screen 90.
2. Divide the number to screen by the potentially eligible people you can screen in a month when you're recruiting. At 10 a month, screening 90 people takes 9 recruiting months.
3. Add the time you won't recruit during that period, such as holidays, conferences and clinical commitments.

Then check whether the estimate still fits the recruitment period in your protocol or grant if recruitment runs at half that pace. In this example, that's 18 recruiting months instead of 9, plus the time you won't recruit. Half the pace is a cautious planning assumption rather than a measured figure or a worst case.

If the slower estimate doesn't fit, negotiate with the sponsor or funder before you commit, for a longer recruitment period or a smaller target, or find more recruitment sources.

## Have recruitment materials approved

Recruitment materials include advertisements in any medium, such as posters, flyers, press releases, social media posts, the text of emails, MUHC monitor screens, websites and videos. Before you use one, have it approved in this order:
1. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator approves it.
2. The REB approves it. You should submit it in Nagano with the initial study application; the REB reviews it for undue influence or a coercive tone.
3. MUHC Communications approves any material the public or patients may see. Send Communications the formatted text, in English and French, at [communications@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:communications@muhc.mcgill.ca). Once the REB and Communications have approved the text, you may adapt it for any medium.

File the REB's approval of the materials in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file</Tooltip>.

Every research advertisement must be available in English and French, and must follow the MUHC Communication policy and the MUHC Branding and Language Guidelines (on the MUHC portal). Before you send an ad to Communications, make sure that:
- It names the principal investigator or sub-investigator at the MUHC, with their affiliation.
- It carries the RI-MUHC logo, or the MUHC logo if that investigator is based at the MUHC without being a member of the RI-MUHC.
- It uses the logo in line with the MUHC's logo guidelines, listed on its [Media contact page](https://muhc.ca/media-contact).
- Its message is clear and well written in both languages.

MUHC Communications does not accept ads for studies that are not conducted at the MUHC or the RI-MUHC. If another institution's REB reviews your study, Communications should still review announcements about unpublished MUHC research.

To advertise a clinical trial, inside or outside the MUHC, write the ad in plain words that a layperson understands, with as few scientific terms as possible, correct grammar and spelling, and a clear, professional message and layout.

The ad must give the clinical research coordinator's full name and a phone number at the MUHC or the RI-MUHC, never a personal cell phone number or a personal email address, such as a Gmail address. It should also give the principal investigator's title.

Materials you add or revise after the REB's first approval need the same approvals before you use them. Submit them to the REB as amendments, and put a version number on each document and in its file name. Record each approval on an REB Approval Tracking log in the investigator site file.

Based on: [SOP-CR-009, sections 3](/sops/cr-009#responsibilities) (who approves which materials), [5.1](/sops/cr-009#5-1-developing-a-recruitment-plan) (sections 5.1.7 and 5.1.8), [5.2](/sops/cr-009#5-2-reb-approval-of-recruitment-materials), [5.3](/sops/cr-009#5-3-muhc-communications-approval) and [5.11](/sops/cr-009#5-11-ongoing-evaluation-of-the-recruitment-plan) (section 5.11.8); [SOP-CR-007, section 5.4](/sops/cr-007#5-4-initial-reb-and-institutional-review) (section 5.4.10); [SOP-CR-006, sections 5.2](/sops/cr-006#5-2-icf-guidelines) (section 5.2.1, the logo) and [5.3](/sops/cr-006#5-3-icf-content) (sections 5.3.6 and 5.3.7).

## Screen candidates

<Warning>
All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes early is a deviation.
</Warning>

One exception is a study with a written waiver of consent from the REB and a reviewed form for the [évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP)](/kb/recruitment/privacy), the MUHC's privacy impact assessment.

In research that must start promptly with an adult who suddenly can't consent, when there's no time to designate a legal representative, [the person qualified to consent to their care may consent for them](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent), as the REB decides when it reviews the project. If your study may need the waiver or this emergency rule, ask the REB before you submit.

Record each person on the [Screening Log](#enrol-participants-and-keep-the-logs) when you screen them. Do the screening procedures in the order and on the timeline the protocol sets. Review the baseline screening results, as applicable, and have the principal investigator initial and date them, to document medical oversight.

If a candidate is referred from outside the MUHC, including from the Jewish General Hospital, request certified copies of the source documents the study needs from that institution, and file them promptly. Record when you asked and when you received them, because there can be long delays.

The principal investigator must make sure that each participant meets every inclusion criterion and none of the exclusion criteria of the most recently REB-approved protocol, and that eligibility is documented in the source documents.

The principal investigator is ultimately responsible for the final medical decision on eligibility, and should sign an inclusion and exclusion checklist for each participant.

Based on: [SOP-CR-008, sections 3](/sops/cr-008#standard-consent-process) and [7](/sops/cr-008#special-populations) (the emergency); [Civil Code of Québec](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991), article 21; [SOP-CR-009, sections 5.4](/sops/cr-009#5-4-access-to-participant-medical-records) (section 5.4.2, referrals), [5.6](/sops/cr-009#5-6-participant-screening) and [5.7](/sops/cr-009#5-7-participant-enrollment) (section 5.7.2).

## Enrol participants and keep the logs

Enrol each eligible participant using the protocol's enrolment procedure. It may use a validated interactive web or voice response system, whose use may be delegated to trained team members, such as a coordinator or a pharmacist. Every participant recruited in an MUHC study must have an MUHC hospital card.

<Warning>
If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk.
</Warning>

If approval lapses, [submit the continuing review request at once and follow the other steps for a lapse](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid).

Keep three logs, which are essential documents. If the sponsor provides none, use the templates in [SOP-CR-009's appendices](/sops/cr-009#appendices).

| Log | What it records | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Screening Log (Appendix 3) | Everyone screened: the date they signed the consent form, or the reason they didn't; whether they failed screening, and why; and whether they were enrolled. | Record each person at the time of screening, with a sequential screening number, in chronological order. |
| Enrollment Log (Appendix 4) | Each enrolled participant, in order of enrolment, by study number, and the study visits they've completed. | It should list participants by study number only, and should not include initials or a date of birth unless the protocol requires them and the REB approves. |
| Participant Identification Log (Appendix 5) | Each enrolled participant's identity and contact details, such as their address, medical record number, phone number and email, and, where there is one, the contact details of the person who consents for them, so that participants can be reached quickly, for example after a product recall. | Keep it confidential. Never give it to the sponsor, and do not archive it with the other essential documents. If a participant moves, you should get their new contact details and file a record of the contact with the essential documents, for example on the Telephone Contact Log (Appendix 6). Destroy the log only with the sponsor's approval; it's typically kept at least a year after the study closes. |

If the principal investigator leaves the MUHC, transfer the Participant Identification Log to the new principal investigator or the department head, and tell the sponsor and the REB.

Based on: [SOP-CR-007, section 5.5](/sops/cr-007#5-5-annual-renewal-continuing-review) (a lapse in approval); [SOP-CR-009, sections 5.4](/sops/cr-009#5-4-access-to-participant-medical-records) (section 5.4.3, the hospital card), [5.5](/sops/cr-009#5-5-gcp-required-participant-logs), [5.6](/sops/cr-009#5-6-participant-screening), [5.7](/sops/cr-009#5-7-participant-enrollment) and [5.8](/sops/cr-009#5-8-participant-identification).

## Notify Medical Records and give a wallet card

For a study of an <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip> (a drug, natural health product or medical device), you must notify MUHC Medical Records Services when each participant starts and ends their participation.

When a participant starts:
- Complete the Confirmation of Participation in Research form (FMU-9997). The electronic version, in the OWord section of the participant's chart in OACIS, the MUHC's electronic medical record (enter the code FMU-9997), is highly recommended.
- Even with the electronic form, a copy of the signed consent form, or a project summary if the REB requires one, should still be brought to Medical Records, with a Medivisit printout of the participant's bar code, for scanning into the Alert section of the participant's OACIS record.
- If you use the paper form, use its latest version, with the participant's Medivisit bar code, and staple it to the top of the consent form copy.

The investigator should also give each participant in an interventional study of an investigational product a wallet-size card at the start, with the study's title, the study drugs, devices or procedures, and emergency contact numbers. The card should have the REB's approval before you use it.

When a participant's participation ends, send Medical Records the End of Participation in Research form (FMU-9907). On any paper form, apply the participant's bar code at the top right corner, and send only originals, because the bar code degrades when photocopied. Both forms are in [SOP-CR-009's appendices](/sops/cr-009#appendices).

Based on: [SOP-CR-008, section 5](/sops/cr-008#ip-studies-medical-records); [SOP-CR-009, section 5.10](/sops/cr-009#5-10-notification-to-medical-records).

## Pay and reimburse participants

Pay participants only within [Quebec's limits](/kb/recruitment/consent#compensate-participants-within-the-limits). The REB reviews the amount and the method of payment, to check that neither coerces nor unduly influences participants. If you're unsure about an amount, or your participants are in a vulnerable situation, ask the REB before you submit.

For a clinical trial, the ICH recommends that payments be timely, prorated and not wholly contingent on the participant completing the trial. Reimbursing participants' reasonable expenses, such as travel and lodging, is separate from any incentive.

If a participant withdraws, you should not withhold any payment that was due before they withdrew.

Based on: [Civil Code of Québec](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991), article 25; [TCPS 2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html), article 3.1; [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), section 1.2.8.

## If a participant can't be reached

If a participant misses a scheduled visit and can't be reached, the site should make every effort to contact them. Unless the protocol or the sponsor's policy requires more, the steps are:
1. Within 30 days of the missed visit, send a text, if you have a cell number, or call and leave a message, or both.
2. If the participant is still unreachable, send an email, or a letter by regular mail, within 5 calendar days.
3. Repeat it twice, each time within another 5 calendar days.
4. After the third email or letter, send a registered letter with confirmation of receipt.
5. If the registered letter comes back and there's still no response, consider the participant lost to follow-up, and record it on the Enrollment Log and in the source documents.

You should file every attempt, including the registered letter and its envelope, with the essential documents. If you find the participant afterwards, you must tell the sponsor without delay, and you should file a record of that discussion with the essential documents.

Based on: [SOP-CR-009, section 5.9](/sops/cr-009#5-9-participants-lost-to-follow-up).

## Review recruitment during the study

During the study, keep recruitment under review in these ways:
- Review the recruitment goals and strategies periodically, and change the plan when you need to.
- Track recruitment at the MUHC. In a multicentre study, ask the sponsor about recruitment at the other sites if its newsletters don't say. If a newsletter brings new medically important information, make sure the principal investigator signs and dates it, to document medical oversight.
- Check regularly whether the study is likely to finish on time, for example whether it started on schedule and is meeting its recruitment target.
- Keep the monitor, or the sponsor's contact, informed of progress against the recruitment timelines.
- Keep the team motivated, for example with ongoing training or information sessions about the study.

Based on: [SOP-CR-009, section 5.11](/sops/cr-009#5-11-ongoing-evaluation-of-the-recruitment-plan).
