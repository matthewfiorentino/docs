## Before you recruit

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

Recruitment also waits for every other [start condition](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid), including the sponsor's go letter, if the sponsor issues one. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email. A clinical trial must also be [registered in a public registry](/kb/close-out/publications#trial-registration) before the first participant is recruited.

To check eligibility in health records before someone consents, the study needs approval from the Research Ethics Board (REB) to [use the records without consent](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1#se:44), and [MUHC Authorization](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent), which includes access to the records. Request both in your <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> submission, and answer its [questions on access to information for pre-screening](/sops/cr-007#5-4-3).

The principal investigator who leads the study at the MUHC must also complete a short version of the privacy impact assessment, the <Tooltip tip="The privacy impact assessment that Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information (Law 5) requires when a researcher asks for access to health records without consent." headline="Évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP) (Quebec)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#efvp">évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP)</Tooltip>. The MUHC's [Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) reviews and approves it. MUHC Authorization is given only once the EFVP is approved.

## Plan your recruitment

If the study needs a [recruitment plan](/sops/cr-009#5-1), it is developed before the study starts by the sponsor, the principal investigator, the <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">clinical research coordinator</Tooltip> or a delegate.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): who decides whether a study needs a recruitment plan, and on what grounds. SOP-CR-009_08 section 5.1 is titled "Developing a Recruitment Plan (If required)" and doesn't say who requires one (the blind review of recruitment V3, batch 88, round 1, missing item 5). */}

Choose the methods that suit each step of recruitment, from the population the study targets to the people found eligible. Weigh the issues each method raises, such as ethics, cost, regulations, privacy, staffing and timelines. Where you can, use several methods throughout the study rather than relying on one. Examples include hospital TV screens, websites, social media, posters, flyers, radio and newspapers. Write the plan down, with its timelines.

[Any part of recruitment, screening and enrolment may be delegated](/sops/cr-009#responsibilities) to trained team members. The ultimate responsibility stays with the sponsor or the principal investigator. Volunteers and other [people who are not affiliated with the RI-MUHC or the MUHC](/training/compliance-requirements) may not contact or recruit participants or perform procedures on them, because they are not covered by professional or academic liability insurance.

If your situation is different, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) for help.

## Include participants fairly

You should be [inclusive in choosing participants](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter4-chapitre4.html), taking into account the scope and objectives of the research. Do not exclude people because of attributes such as their culture, language, religion, race, disability, sexual orientation, ethnicity, linguistic proficiency, gender or age, unless there is a valid reason. Do not exclude women because of their reproductive capacity or because they are pregnant or breastfeeding, unless there is a valid reason.

In a sponsor's trial, the protocol sets who is eligible. Where you write the protocol, excluding people who are pregnant or breastfeeding doesn't have to be the default. You and the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) must take into account the foreseeable risks and potential benefits:
- Of the research, for them and for the embryo, fetus or infant.
- Of excluding them from the research.

The national team [PregTrial](https://pregtrial.ca/) works to include pregnant and lactating people in clinical trials. PregTrial's [Rapid Response Consultation Service](https://pregtrial.ca/pregtrial-rapid-response-consultation-service-rrcs/) connects investigators with experts across Canada who advise on trial design, eligibility criteria and ethics.

## Set a realistic recruitment target

Recruitment often falls short of plans. [A 2014 study in *JAMA*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24618966/) followed about 1,000 randomized trials approved from 2000 to 2003 by ethics committees in Switzerland, Germany and Canada. A quarter were discontinued, most often for poor recruitment. Investigator-sponsored trials were discontinued for poor recruitment far more often than industry-sponsored ones.

In a [2022 study in *PLOS ONE*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0262862), a research team mostly from McGill's Biomedical Ethics Unit surveyed principal investigators within seven months of their trial's registration. Each was asked how likely the trial was to reach its recruitment target. Among the 281 trials whose recruitment had ended, the average answer was 79 per cent, and 43 per cent reached their target.

Study teams commit to a recruitment target in a sponsor's feasibility questionnaire or a grant application. In its good clinical practice guideline, the [International Council for Harmonisation (ICH)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) recommends that the investigator be able to show, for example from past or current data, a potential for recruiting the proposed number of eligible participants within the recruitment period agreed with the sponsor.

To set a target you can meet, work from your own numbers:
- Start from the potentially eligible people you can actually screen, rather than from everyone with the condition. You should confirm your numbers with the [Study Feasibility Checklist](/kb/planning/feasibility#feasibility-checklist).
- For counts from MUHC data, you can [request aggregate data](/kb/design-support/data-warehouse#the-request-process) from the [Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) at any time, for example for a feasibility check. Other data requests can be made only once the study has MUHC Authorization. Take into account the Data Warehouse's [*Guidelines on Aggregate Data Requests*](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:360).{/* The Guidelines' address on the RI-MUHC Portal: MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS). */}
- In a [2013 survey in *BMJ Open*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3831096/), people who choose trial sites in Europe gave an investigator's record of recruiting and retaining participants the most weight among five investigator criteria. During a trial, the sponsor's monitors generally review and report recruitment and retention rates.

### Estimate how long recruitment will take

Start from your target, the number your site will enrol. For a single-site study, the target is the protocol's sample size. In a multicentre study, it is your site's accrual target. If the sample size isn't set yet, the [Biostatistics Consulting Unit](mailto:bcu@rimuhc.ca) can calculate it. Then estimate the time:
1. Divide your target by the share of the people you screen whom you expect to enrol, from your own screening data where you have them, or from [published screen failure rates](#screen-failure-rates). To enrol 30 participants when you enrol 1 in 3 of the people you screen, you need to screen 90. The sponsor may assess the number you need to screen at its site feasibility visit.
2. Divide the number to screen by the potentially eligible people you can screen in a month when you're recruiting. At 10 a month, screening 90 people takes 9 recruiting months.
3. Add the time you won't recruit during that period, such as holidays, conferences and clinical commitments.

Then check whether the estimate still fits the recruitment period in your protocol or grant if recruitment runs at half that pace. In the example, half the pace means 18 recruiting months instead of 9, plus the time you won't recruit. Half the pace is a cautious planning assumption rather than a measured figure or a worst case.

If the slower estimate doesn't fit, negotiate a longer recruitment period or a smaller target with the sponsor or funder before you commit, or find more recruitment sources.

## Have recruitment materials approved

Recruitment materials include advertisements in any medium. Examples are posters, flyers, press releases, social media posts, the text of emails, MUHC monitor screens, websites and videos.

Before you use a recruitment material, [have it approved in this order](/sops/cr-009#5-1-7):
1. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator approves it.
2. The [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) approves it.
3. [MUHC Communications](mailto:communications@muhc.mcgill.ca) approves any material the public or patients may see.

You should submit materials to the REB in Nagano with the initial study application. The REB reviews them for undue influence or a coercive tone. File the REB's approval of the materials in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file</Tooltip>.

Every research advertisement must be available in English and French. Each document should carry a version number, in the document and in its file name. Record each approval on an REB Approval Tracking log, and file the log in the investigator site file.

## Screen candidates

<Warning>
All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes before all the required signatures are on the form is a deviation.
</Warning>

Screening without consent needs a written waiver of consent from the REB and a reviewed [EFVP form](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed).

Some emergency research must start promptly after an adult suddenly becomes unable to consent. If there's no time to designate a legal representative, [the person qualified to consent to the adult's care may consent for them](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent). When the REB reviews the project, it decides whether the research must start promptly and leaves no time to designate a legal representative.

If no prior consent from the participant or a representative is possible, enrolment should follow the measures in the protocol or another REB-approved document. If your study may need the waiver or this emergency rule, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you submit.

If a candidate is referred from outside the MUHC, request [certified copies of the source documents](/sops/cr-009#5-4-2) the study needs from the referring institution. Referrals from the Jewish General Hospital count as outside referrals. File the copies in a timely fashion. Record when you asked for them and when you received them, because there can be long delays.

Record each person on the [Screening Log](#enrol-participants-and-keep-the-logs) when you screen them. Do the screening procedures in the order and on the timeline the protocol sets. Review the baseline screening results where applicable. Have the principal investigator initial and date them to document medical oversight.

The final medical decision on eligibility is ultimately the principal investigator's responsibility. The principal investigator [must make sure that each participant meets every inclusion criterion and none of the exclusion criteria](/sops/cr-009#5-7-2) of the most recently REB-approved protocol, and that eligibility is documented in the source documents. For each participant, the principal investigator should also sign an inclusion and exclusion checklist, to confirm their oversight of eligibility.

## Enrol participants and keep the logs

Enrol each eligible participant using [the protocol's enrolment procedure](/sops/cr-009#5-7-3). The procedure may use a validated interactive web or voice response system. Use of the system may be delegated to trained team members, such as a coordinator or a pharmacist. Every participant recruited in an MUHC study must have an MUHC hospital card.

<Warning>
If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk.
</Warning>

If approval lapses, [submit the continuing review request and follow the other steps for a lapse](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid).

Keep a Screening Log, an Enrollment Log and a Participant Identification Log, which are essential documents. If the sponsor provides none, use the templates in [SOP-CR-009's appendices](/sops/cr-009#appendices).

| Log | What it records | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Screening Log (Appendix 3) | Everyone screened. The date each person signed the consent form, or the reason they didn't. Whether they failed screening, and why. Whether they were enrolled. | Record each person at the time of screening, with a sequential screening number, in chronological order. |
| Enrollment Log (Appendix 4) | Each enrolled participant, in order of enrolment, by study number. The study visits they've completed. | Participants should be listed by study number only. Initials or a date of birth should not be included unless the protocol requires them and the REB approves. |
| Participant Identification Log (Appendix 5) | Each enrolled participant's identity and contact details, such as their address, medical record number, phone number and email. The contact details of their <Tooltip tip="The person with legal authority to consent for a participant who cannot consent." headline="Legally Appointed Representative (LAR)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#lar">legally appointed representative</Tooltip>, where there is one. | Keep the log confidential. Never give it to the sponsor. External sponsors must not know who the participants are. Do not archive it with the other essential documents. Destroy the log only with the sponsor's approval. The log is typically kept for at least a year after the study closes. |

The Participant Identification Log lets the team reach participants quickly, for example after a product recall. If a participant moves, you should get their new contact details to ensure their follow-up. Every contact with the participant to get the new details should be documented and filed with the essential documents, for example on the Telephone Contact Log (Appendix 6).

If the principal investigator leaves the MUHC, transfer the Participant Identification Log to the new principal investigator or the department head. Tell the sponsor and the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) about the transfer.

## Pay and reimburse participants

[Quebec limits what participants may be paid](/kb/recruitment/consent#compensate-participants-within-the-limits). In research that could interfere with a participant's integrity, [Quebec's Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:25) allows an indemnity for the loss and inconvenience the participant suffers, and no other financial reward. Participants should receive [compensation for the loss and restriction they suffer](/sops/cr-007#5-7-4), on condition that it is not an undue inducement and is paid pro rata. The compensation should also comply with both ethics and the law, including Quebec's limit.

[National and international ethics guidance](/kb/recruitment/consent#compensate-participants-within-the-limits) allows payment for taking part, but at the MUHC it doesn't override the Civil Code's limit.

Before approving any study, [the REB reviews](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) the amount paid to participants for their losses and constraints, and the method of payment, to check that neither coerces nor unduly influences participants. The REB also checks that participants are given information on the method, amounts and schedule of payment, where applicable.

When some or all of the participants are likely to be vulnerable to coercion or undue influence, the REB looks for additional safeguards in the research. If you're unsure about an amount, or your participants are in a vulnerable situation, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you submit.

The Centre for Applied Ethics' [consent templates](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates) for clinical trials and for research with minors provide for two kinds of payment, which a study may combine:
- An amount per visit, as compensation for the costs of taking part. If the participant withdraws or is withdrawn before the end of the study, the compensation is in proportion to the length of their participation.
- Reimbursement of expenses such as travel, meals and parking, on presentation of receipts or by coupon.

The [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html) distinguishes reimbursement from incentives, which are anything offered to participants for taking part. ## Contact a participant who misses a visit

If a participant misses a scheduled visit and can't be reached, [the site should make every effort to contact them](/sops/cr-009#5-9-1). Unless the protocol or the sponsor's policy requires more, the site should take these steps:
1. Within 30 days of the missed visit, call and leave a message, send a text if you have a cell number, or do both.
2. If the participant is still unreachable, send an email or a letter by regular mail within 5 calendar days.
3. Repeat the email or letter twice, each time within another 5 calendar days.
4. After the third email or letter, send a registered letter with confirmation of receipt.
5. If the registered letter comes back and there's still no response, the participant is lost to follow-up. Record the status on the Enrollment Log and in the source documents.

You should file a record of every attempt with the essential documents, including the registered letter and its envelope. If you find the participant after they have been declared lost to follow-up, tell the sponsor without delay. Your discussion with the sponsor should also be documented and filed with the essential documents.

## Review recruitment during the study

During the study, [keep recruitment under review](/sops/cr-009#5-11) in these ways:
- Review the recruitment goals and strategies periodically. Change the plan when you need to.
- Track recruitment at the MUHC. In a multicentre study, ask the sponsor about recruitment at the other sites if its newsletters don't say. If a newsletter brings new medically important information, make sure the principal investigator signs and dates it to document medical oversight.
- Check regularly whether the study is likely to finish on time, for example whether it started on schedule and is meeting its recruitment target.
- Keep the monitor or another sponsor contact informed of progress against the recruitment timelines.
- Keep the team motivated to maintain an acceptable recruitment rate, for example with ongoing training or information sessions about the study.
