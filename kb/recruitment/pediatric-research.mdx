The MUHC includes the Montreal Children's Hospital, and the RI-MUHC conducts research with children. In research that could interfere with a child's integrity, [Quebec's Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991) decides who may consent for them.

## Obtain consent and assent for a child

[The person with parental authority, or the tutor, consents for a minor](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent) in research that could interfere with the minor's integrity. A minor aged 14 to 17 may consent alone only if the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> finds that the research involves only minimal risk and that the circumstances justify it.

<Warning>
A minor who understands the nature and consequences of the research and objects to taking part cannot take part, even if someone else has consented for them. A child who has some ability to understand the research also cannot take part if they dissent, in words or by their behaviour. Record the objection, and do not perform any study procedure.
</Warning>

Ask the child for their own agreement to take part, called assent. In their [*Tri-Council Policy Statement*](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html) (<Tooltip tip="The federal research agencies' policy on the ethical conduct of research involving humans, 2022 edition." headline="TCPS 2" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tcps2">TCPS 2</Tooltip>), the federal research agencies require researchers to learn the wishes of a child who can't yet consent but has some ability to understand the research. [The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-008#5-8-4) treat obtaining assent as equivalent to the informed consent process, and require the process to be documented.

The Centre for Applied Ethics' [pediatric templates](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates#choose-the-consent-form-template) record assent on the consent form's signature page, with no separate assent form. If the child is able, they should also [sign and date the form](/sops/cr-008#5-8-5). Quebec's Civil Code sets no age for assent. A child's maturity and psychological state should be considered in judging whether the child can give assent. If you are unsure of a child's ability to give assent, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca).

All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes before all the required signatures are on the form is a deviation.

If a minor has been emancipated, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) who may consent.

## Confirm who has parental authority

Both parents [exercise parental authority together](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:600). When one parent acts alone, the Civil Code presumes, for anyone dealing with that parent in good faith, that the parent acts with the other's consent. If the protocol or the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) asks for both parents' signatures, get both.

Whether one parent or someone else has custody, both parents keep the right to supervise the child's maintenance and education. If one parent dies, is deprived of parental authority by a court or can't express their will, the other parent exercises it. If the child has a tutor, the tutor may consent.{/* TO CONFIRM (the REB): when neither the protocol nor the REB asks for both parents' signatures, is one parent's signature enough, and does a custody order change who may consent for the child? ## Explain the study in words the child understands

How you explain the study depends on the child. Use these ages as rough guides, not cut-offs:
- Around ages 7 and 8, explain what will happen simply and in concrete words, and ask whether the child agrees. Record the child's answer, who explained the study and what was said.
- From around 9 to 12, a short written explanation read through with the child, with pictures where they help, can help the child decide. Consider a reading level of about Grade 3 to 4.
- From around 13, explain the study as fully as you would to an adult, in words suited to the teenager. Consider a reading level of about Grade 6 to 8.

{/* TO CONFIRM (the Centre for Applied Ethics): are these age bands for explaining a study (around 7 and 8, 9 to 12, 13 and over) and these reading levels (Grade 3 to 4, Grade 6 to 8) what the Centre and the REB expect? A mature 8-year-old may understand a written explanation, and a teenager with a developmental delay may need a spoken one.

Whatever the child's age, [explain the study in words they understand](/sops/cr-008#5-8-5). Consider answering these questions:{/* TO CONFIRM (the Centre for Applied Ethics; master doc, the REB): are these four questions what an explanation for a child should answer? The choice of the four is the Hub's own. Behind the explanation itself: SOP-CR-008_08, section 5.8.5 ("Inform the participant to the extent compatible with his or her understanding."); for drug studies, ICH E11(R1), section 2.6.3, on what children should be made aware of (not re-read on Nov. 14, 2026). */}
- What will happen to me? Describe the procedures in concrete words. For example, “We will take a small amount of blood from your arm with a needle” is clearer to a child than “A venipuncture will be performed”.
- How long will it take? Give the number of visits, how long each lasts and the total time in the study.
- Will anything hurt or be uncomfortable? Say so honestly, without playing down the unpleasant parts.
- Can I say no or change my mind? Say that taking part is voluntary, that the child can stop at any time and that saying no won't change their care.

Like all participant-facing material, a written explanation or a visual aid for children needs the REB's approval. The material also needs [an English and a French version](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form), unless the REB accepts a justification.

## Show the REB what it looks for

The MUHC REB's [Pediatrics panel](https://muhc.ca/cae/research-ethics) reviews all projects involving minors. [A new project that could interfere with a minor's integrity cannot go through delegated review at its first review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#how-a-study-is-reviewed). [Children must not be inappropriately excluded from research](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter4-chapitre4.html#4) solely because of their age or developmental stage. When you set your eligibility criteria, follow the [rules on including participants fairly](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#include-participants-fairly).

Your submission should explain:
- Why the research needs children, and how the risk, given each child's health and condition, is not disproportionate to the benefit that can reasonably be expected.
- If the research is on a group and can't benefit the participating child directly, how it can benefit others of the same age or with the same disease or disability, how it exposes the child to no more than minimal risk and burden, and how you will protect the child's welfare throughout their participation.
- How you will obtain consent and assent, with the consent form and any written explanation for children attached at the first submission.
- How you will check each child's understanding, and respect and record an objection.
- How you will check at later visits that the child still wants to take part, and act on signs of reluctance or distress.
- If adults also take part, how each group consents.
- For a study of an investigational product, how you will monitor safety in children.
- For a study of a drug, how you will report adverse events using laboratory values and clinical measurements suited to the children's age, and how you will watch for effects on growth and development.

## Recruit children

[The REB reviews](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) how, when, where and by whom children and families are contacted, and every material you use. Consider approaching the parent before the child, and recruiting the child directly only after a parent has agreed to let the team discuss the study with the child.{/* TO CONFIRM (the REB): must a parent agree before the team approaches a child, and does that hold for a minor aged 14 to 17 whom the REB has allowed to consent alone? No source read states the parents-first practice (14-open-questions.md, batch 76, rounds 1 and 2). */}

When the child is a patient, first contact with the family should come from someone at the health care facility where the child receives care. A clinician-researcher may approach the families of their own patients directly, but the family should in no way feel pressured or obliged to take part.

[The treating physician should not be the one who obtains consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#choose-who-obtains-consent). If the treating physician is considered the best person to obtain consent, they must explain to the REB why it is necessary. If the REB approves, the physician must disclose their dual role as clinician and researcher to the participant.

In schools, daycares and other community settings, children may feel they must take part when a teacher or another adult in charge is involved. Consider addressing recruitment materials to parents, and leaving the decision with the family. If a school or daycare hands out your materials, consider arranging for the team to receive no information that identifies the families who don't respond.{/* TO CONFIRM (the REB, with the Privacy Office): what does the REB expect of recruitment through schools and daycares: materials addressed to parents, and no identifying information on families who don't respond? No source read states either (14-open-questions.md, batch 76, round 1). */}

Parents of children with a serious or chronic condition may confuse research with care. Your materials and the consent discussion [should make clear what is standard care and what is research](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html#5).

Clinicians must take care not to create unrealistic expectations about the research's potential benefits. The materials and the discussion should not suggest a guaranteed benefit or priority access to care. When the child is a clinician-researcher's own patient, [the REB requires measures to minimize the confusion of research with care](/kb/recruitment/consent#choose-who-obtains-consent).

Consider suiting the materials that children see, such as posters in a waiting room, to their age. The materials should not use toys, prizes or other rewards to persuade children.{/* TO CONFIRM (the REB): are toys, prizes and other rewards ruled out in materials that children see, should those materials be suited to the children's age (the Hub's own advice), and is "priority access to care" a fair example of an unrealistic expectation? ICH E11(R1), section 2.6.2, asks only that recruitment be "free from inappropriate inducements", for drug studies; the examples are the Hub's (14-open-questions.md, batch 76: "toys and prizes in children's materials"). */}

Payment for a child's participation follows the [limits on compensation](/kb/recruitment/consent#compensate-participants-within-the-limits), including what a parent or tutor may accept on the child's behalf. A sponsor's global protocol doesn't change Quebec's limits.

## Staff the team for children

The principal investigator who leads the study at the MUHC [is responsible for making sure the team is qualified](/sops/cr-002#5-3-5). Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>.

At the RI-MUHC, the principal investigator of a pediatric study is an experienced pediatric specialist. A study that includes both adults and children has a pediatric specialist among its investigators.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 14 to 34"). */}

When the principal investigator delegates obtaining assent, consider delegating it to someone experienced in talking with children of the participants' age.{/* TO CONFIRM (Quality Assurance, or the REB; master doc, QA, "Two pieces of advice on the pediatric page"): is experience in talking with children of the participants' age an RI-MUHC expectation for whoever obtains assent? SOP-CR-008_08, section 5.1.2, asks that the person obtaining consent be "qualified by education, experience and documented training", trained on the protocol and delegated the task; no source read says more. The sentence is the Hub's own advice. */} A coordinator who works well with adults may not be the best person to explain a study to a 7-year-old.

For a study of a drug, [those conducting it should be properly trained and experienced in studying children](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), including in evaluating and managing adverse events in children.

Repeated invasive procedures can be painful or frightening for a child. For drug studies, discomfort can be minimized when investigators experienced in treating children design and conduct the study. The International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) lists practical measures that minimize discomfort and distress:
- Staff knowledgeable and skilled in dealing with children and their age-appropriate needs, including in performing pediatric procedures.
- A setting with furniture, play equipment, activities and food suited to the child's age.
- Visits in a place the child knows, such as the hospital or clinic where they receive their care.
- Topical anesthesia before an intravenous line is placed.
- An indwelling catheter for blood sampling, to spare the child repeated needle sticks.
- Some protocol blood samples taken at the same time as routine ones.

For procedures that may distress a child, such as blood draws, imaging or long visits, consider involving a child life specialist. A child life specialist prepares children for medical procedures and supports them during and after.{/* TO CONFIRM (the Research Facilitator, with the Montreal Children's Hospital's Child Life Services): do child life specialists support research visits at the Montreal Children's Hospital, and how does a team ask for one? ## Re-consent a participant who turns 18

When a participant who is still taking part turns 18, [obtain their own signed consent as soon as possible](/kb/recruitment/consent#keep-consent-current). The consent of the person with parental authority or the tutor no longer covers them. The Centre for Applied Ethics' [pediatric templates](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates#choose-the-consent-form-template) include an addendum for a participant who has become an adult. Record the re-consent in the source documents, [using the Informed Consent Process Checklist](/sops/cr-008#5-12-1).

Some children, such as infants, can't assent when the study begins. [Ask for their assent to keep taking part](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#3) once they can understand the research's purpose, risks and benefits. The team should plan from the start of the study how it will notice when a participant turns 18 or becomes able to assent.

{/* Sources: Civil Code of Québec, article 153 ("Full age or the age of majority is 18 years. On attaining full age, a person ceases to be a minor and has the full exercise of all his civil rights"); TCPS 2 (2022), article 3.3, its Application ("the researcher must seek their assent to continue their participation once they are able to understand the purpose of the research as well as its risks and benefits"; "Mechanisms should be in place from the outset to identify and address such changes"); [SOP-CR-008, section 5.8](/sops/cr-008#5-8) (5.8.6: "For participants whose participation is ongoing, obtain the legally effective signed consent from the participant as soon as possible after reaching the age of 18 years"; 5.8.7) and section 5.12.1 ("Record evidence of the informed consent process in the source documentation using the Informed Consent Process Checklist"); the Centre for Applied Ethics' pediatric templates (Sept. 30, 2024: "ADDENDUM TO CONSENT FORM / Participant who has now become an adult (18)"). */}
