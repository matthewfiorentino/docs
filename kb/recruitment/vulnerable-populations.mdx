## Recognize what may make participants vulnerable in your study

Vulnerability in research depends on a person's circumstances in the particular study. Belonging to a group doesn't by itself make someone vulnerable.

[The agencies' examples](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter4-chapitre4.html#7) include people who live in an institution, people in dependent situations and people whose poverty or poor health could make even a modest payment an inducement to take risks they would otherwise refuse. Limited capacity to decide whether to take part is another such circumstance. For clinical trials, the [International Council for Harmonisation (ICH)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) also names students and junior staff in a hierarchy, people in detention or nursing homes, and patients in emergencies.

As you design the study, look for these circumstances and plan a protection for each one you find:
- Participants' condition or its treatment may affect their understanding or judgment, and so their capacity to consent.
- Participants may depend on a member of the team, as patients, students or employees. Plan who will recruit them and who will obtain consent.
- A payment could be hard to refuse for people in financial hardship. [Keep payment from becoming an inducement](#keep-payment-from-becoming-an-inducement).
- The setting where you recruit may limit people's choice, as in an emergency department, a psychiatric unit, a long-term care residence or a detention facility.
- Participants may not be able to read, to sign or to speak English or French. [Adapt the consent process](/kb/recruitment/consent#adapt-the-consent-process-when-needed) for them, and for a representative who can't read or doesn't speak English or French.

[The REB requires special precautions](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) when you recruit people in these circumstances, to protect their freedom to choose. Describe and justify every recruitment and consent procedure in your submission.

In a clinical trial, the consent discussion and the consent materials should explain the alternative procedures or treatments that may be available to the participant, and their important potential benefits and risks, as applicable. Consider also explaining why the person is being invited and which added protections apply to them. In research that is not a clinical trial, consider also explaining what alternatives they have to taking part.

People whose circumstances may make them vulnerable should not be inappropriately included or automatically excluded on the basis of those circumstances. Participation should be based on inclusion and exclusion criteria that the research question justifies. When you set those criteria, follow the [rules on including participants fairly](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#include-participants-fairly).

Circumstances can change during a study, for example when a student finishes your course, an employee changes jobs or a resident leaves the residence. Researchers and the REB should watch for changes that create, increase or reduce a participant's vulnerability, and adjust the participant's protections. If you think a participant's consent needs to be renewed, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca).

### Include participants who are pregnant or breastfeeding

[The rules on including participants fairly](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#include-participants-fairly) also cover people who are pregnant or breastfeeding. If a participant in an interventional study becomes pregnant, [collect and follow the exposure in utero reports](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#track-a-pregnancy-during-a-trial).

## Obtain consent for an adult who cannot consent

[The Civil Code sets who may consent for an adult who can't consent, and what the REB must decide when it reviews the project](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent). The person who consents for the participant is their representative. [The representative cannot be the researcher or any other member of the research team](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#9).

If the participant signed a research directive while they could still decide, setting out their wishes about taking part in research, you and the representative [should be guided by it](/sops/cr-008#5-11-1) during the consent process.

Involve the participant in the decision as far as possible, and document their assent. The consent form [needs a line](/sops/cr-008#5-1-4) for the <Tooltip tip="The person with legal authority to consent for a participant who cannot consent." headline="Legally Appointed Representative (LAR)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#lar">legally appointed representative (LAR)</Tooltip> to sign and date. The form also needs a line on an assent portion where the participant signs and dates, if capable.

The Centre for Applied Ethics' [adult clinical-trial template](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates#choose-the-consent-form-template) has no assent line. The Centre's pediatric templates include an assent-and-consent page for an adult who can't consent, with an assent line. Ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) which form to use.

All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes before all the required signatures are on the form is a deviation.

<Warning>
An adult who cannot consent cannot take part if they understand the nature and consequences of the research and object to taking part, even if someone else has consented for them. A participant who has some ability to understand the research also cannot take part if they dissent, in words or by their behaviour.
</Warning>

[The REB examines](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) how the representative's consent will be obtained and documented. [When a participant can't consent, record that fact, the representative's name and their relationship to the participant](/sops/cr-008#5-1-5). Consider also noting whether the representative consents as mandatary, as tutor or as the person qualified to consent to the participant's care. If the representative changes during the study, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) whether the new one needs to consent.

[A study with no therapeutic purpose](/sops/cr-008#5-6-1) may include participants who consent through a representative only if:
- Its objectives can't be met with participants who can consent for themselves.
- The foreseeable risks are low.
- The negative effect on the participant's well-being is minimized and low.
- The law doesn't prohibit it.
- You expressly seek the REB's approval to include these participants, and its written approval covers them.

## Assess capacity and plan for changes

[Capacity to consent](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#c) is the ability to understand the relevant information about a study and to appreciate the potential consequences of deciding to take part or not. Capacity can vary with how complex the decision is, the circumstances and the moment. A person may be unable to make some decisions yet still able to decide about some research.

[The research design should take into account factors that may affect participants' capacity to consent](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter4-chapitre4.html#6). Consider planning how you will assess capacity whenever a condition or its treatment may affect it, as in cognitive impairment, a psychiatric condition, an acquired brain injury or a neurodegenerative disease.

Mechanisms to identify and address changes in a participant's capacity [should be in place from the start of the study](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#3). Where capacity may be affected, consider setting out in the protocol:
- Who will assess capacity, by role. Consider having someone other than the person who obtains consent do it, to keep the assessment separate from recruitment.
- How capacity will be assessed, naming any structured tool, such as the MacArthur Competence Assessment Tool for Clinical Research (MacCAT-CR).
- How often you will reassess capacity that may change during the study, as in delirium, a relapsing psychiatric illness or early dementia.
- What will prompt a reassessment, such as a clinical deterioration, a change in treatment or a study milestone.
- What happens if a participant loses capacity during the study, for example consent from a representative, withdrawal or continued participation on conditions the REB approves.

In the source documents, consider recording the date of each assessment, who assessed capacity, the method used and the result.

Consider adapting consent materials to participants' cognitive and communication abilities, for example with plainer language, large print or an audio version. Like all participant-facing material, adapted materials need the REB's approval. The adapted materials also need [an English and a French version](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form), unless the REB accepts a justification.

When a participant who is still taking part regains capacity, [obtain their own signed consent as soon as possible](/kb/recruitment/consent#keep-consent-current). Their consent is a condition of staying in the study. The Centre for Applied Ethics' adult clinical-trial template has a signature page for a participant who has regained capacity. Consider noting the reason for the re-consent in the source documents.

## Protect the free choice of participants who depend on the team

An employee recruited by their employer, a student by their teacher or a patient by their physician may feel unable to say no, even when no one pressures them. [The influence of such relationships should be judged from the participant's point of view](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#1). The risk of undue influence may be greater when a participant's dependency is ongoing or substantial.

[The REB reviews](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) how, when, where and by whom potential participants will be contacted, and any power relationship, trust or dependency that could unduly influence them. Describe these in your submission.

As far as possible, researchers should keep their role as researcher separate from their other roles, such as the participants' physician, teacher, supervisor or employer. If you act in both roles, you must always disclose that fact to the participants. Where a teacher, supervisor or employer is on the team, consider having someone with no authority over the participants recruit them and obtain consent.

Saying no or withdrawing later should not affect a participant's care, studies or other services. The information generally required for informed consent includes an assurance that participants are under no obligation to take part and are free to withdraw at any time without prejudice to what they are already entitled to. A physician should make sure that continued care isn't linked to taking part. Students who decline research for course credit should be offered a comparable alternative.

A clinician-researcher may approach their own patients about a study, but the patients should in no way feel pressured or obliged to take part. Clinician-researchers [should take all necessary measures to keep their role as researcher separate from their role as clinician](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html), for example by having associates recruit participants.

The person obtaining consent [should not be the participant's treating physician](/sops/cr-008#5-1-1) or anyone else with a long-standing relationship with them. If the treating physician is considered the best person to obtain consent, they must explain to the REB why it is necessary. If the REB approves, the physician must disclose their dual role as clinician and researcher to the participant.

The rules on [who may obtain consent and on therapeutic misconception](/kb/recruitment/consent#choose-who-obtains-consent) apply to participants who depend on the team as to any other participant.

If you plan to recruit relatives of team members or others whose recruitment raises similar concerns, you [may need special measures](/sops/cr-008#5-1-3) to protect their free decision, their confidentiality and the research's integrity. If you have concerns, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) for guidance on your case.

In long-term care residences, psychiatric units and similar settings, consider recruiting in a way that doesn't draw attention to the people approached or make a refusal visible to staff or other residents.

## Keep payment from becoming an inducement

Payment follows the [limits on compensation](/kb/recruitment/consent#compensate-participants-within-the-limits), including what a representative may accept on the participant's behalf. Researchers and the REB [should be sensitive to participants' economic circumstances](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#1). Justify the amount and the method of payment in your submission, giving the amounts, what each one compensates and when it's paid.

## Plan for emergency research

When an adult suddenly can't consent and the research must start promptly, [the Civil Code sets who may consent for them](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent). Conduct research in a medical emergency only if it addresses the emergency needs of the people involved, and only in accordance with the criteria the REB sets before the research starts. The REB may also allow deferred consent when no one can consent before a participant is enrolled. With deferred consent, the participant or their representative consents after enrolment.

Before you plan for deferred consent, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) whether and how it applies alongside the Civil Code's rule in your study. [The REB may allow deferred consent](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) only if you submit a satisfactory justification and the REB finds and documents the following:
- The regulatory guidance framework for research supports the waiver of prior consent.
- A serious threat to the prospective participant requires immediate intervention.
- Either no standard efficacious care exists, or the research offers a real possibility of direct benefit to the participant compared with standard care.
- Either the risk of harm is no greater than that of standard therapeutic care, or the potential for direct benefit to the participant clearly justifies it.
- The prospective participant is unconscious or lacks the capacity to understand the risks, methods and purpose of the research.
- A representative's authorization cannot be obtained in time, despite diligent and documented efforts to obtain it.
- No relevant prior directive by the participant is known to exist.

In your Nagano submission, [confirm that the study meets these conditions and give your justification](/apps/nagano/guide-06#deferred-consent-emergency-context).

Seek the participant's consent as soon as they regain capacity, or a representative's consent as soon as one is found. This consent is a condition of the participant continuing in the study.

[Enrolment without prior consent should follow the measures](/sops/cr-008#5-8-3) in the protocol or another REB-approved document. Consider describing the measures in detail, for example which procedures will be done before consent, how soon consent will be sought and what happens if it can't be obtained.

[Participants' welfare should be protected by added safeguards](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html#8), where feasible and appropriate. These safeguards might include:
- Additional scientific, medical or REB consultation.
- Identifying likely participants in advance, so that their consent can be sought before an emergency.
- Consulting former and prospective participants.
- Having a data safety monitoring board follow special monitoring procedures.

## Show the REB your safeguards

Before approving a study in which some or all of the participants are likely to be vulnerable to coercion or undue influence, [the REB considers](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) whether additional safeguards have been included in the research. Describe the safeguards in your first submission. Adding them later takes an amendment.

[A new project that could interfere with the integrity of a minor or of an adult who can't consent cannot go through delegated review at its first review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#how-a-study-is-reviewed). For other research, the REB chair or their designee decides whether the project is eligible for delegated review, considering the recruitment practices and the participant population, among other things.

If participants who can't consent will take part, show the REB in your submission:
- Why the research question can be addressed only with them.
- If the research can't directly benefit them, how it exposes them to only minimal risk and minimal burden, and how you will protect their welfare throughout their participation.

Describe how you will obtain and record each representative's consent.

Consider also describing:
- How your inclusion and exclusion criteria follow from the research question.
- How you will identify each participant's representative.
- The protection you planned for each circumstance of vulnerability, including your capacity assessment plan where capacity may be impaired.
- Any monitoring of the consent process you propose.

The consent form [should also state any risks that come from participants' vulnerability](/sops/cr-008#5-5-1).

## Keep the safeguards working during the study

For research with more than minimal risk, the REB may require [more extensive continuing review](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter6-chapitre6.html#14). That review can include monitoring and reviewing the consent process. [The REB may require continuing review more often than once a year](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops). To decide how often, it considers the vulnerability of the population, among other things. The REB sets the frequency when it first approves the study and may revise that frequency later.

When the REB renews its approval, it may impose special precautions. Before each renewal, consider noting for the REB how the safeguards are working, for example:
- How many participants consented through a representative.
- Whether a reassessment changed who consents for a participant.
- Whether anyone objected or withdrew.
- Whether any safeguard was missed.

It is harder to be sure that consent is free in some studies, for example with residents of an institution or participants whose capacity fluctuates. In such a study, you may propose that someone not otherwise involved in the study observe the consent discussion, check that it was done properly and, where appropriate, talk with the participant separately. Consider recording what the observer notes and resolving any concern they raise before you enrol the participant.

For an amendment, the REB chair or their designee decides whether full-board review is needed, considering the participant population and recruitment practices, among other things. In the amendment, consider explaining why the change is needed and how it affects participants' protections.

A lapse in a safeguard the protocol describes is a [protocol deviation](/sops/cr-026#5-3-3), for example a missed capacity reassessment or consent obtained by someone other than the person the protocol designates. [Record and report the deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations) as its severity requires. [If consent is repeatedly or deliberately obtained or documented improperly](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-before-any-study-procedure), the REB may suspend or end the study, or suspend its recruitment.

If your situation is different, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) about consent and review, or the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) about RI-MUHC processes.
