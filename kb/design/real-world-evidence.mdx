Much of the data about patients' health is collected in routine care: hospital records, insurance claims, registries and the records of Quebec's health insurance plan. Studies can draw evidence from these data, and trials can run within routine care. Getting access to Quebec's data takes several approvals in a set order, so it shapes a study's timeline from the start.

## Know the terms

- **Real-world data.** Data on patients' health status or on the delivery of health care, routinely collected from sources other than traditional clinical trials, such as electronic health records, claims databases, registries, data patients generate and wearables.
- **Real-world evidence.** Evidence on the use, and the potential benefits or risks, of a medical product, drawn from the analysis of real-world data.
- **Pragmatic trial.** A clinical trial that tests interventions in the conditions of routine clinical practice, often with broader eligibility criteria, the comparators used in practice, and outcomes that matter for clinical decisions.

## Know how Health Canada uses real-world evidence

Health Canada considers real-world evidence in its regulatory decisions, and the quality of the evidence decides how far it is considered. Its two main documents are:

- [*Elements of Real World Data/Evidence Quality throughout the Prescription Drug Product Life Cycle*](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/health/publications/drugs-health-products/real-world-data-evidence-drug-lifecycle-report.html) (2019), which sets out principles for generating real-world evidence consistent with Canada's regulatory standard of evidence.
- [*Optimizing the Use of Real World Evidence to Inform Regulatory Decision-Making*](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/announcements/optimizing-real-world-evidence-regulatory-decisions.html) (2019), Health Canada's notice on submitting real-world evidence.

Health Canada encourages real-world evidence to expand indications to populations often left out of clinical trials, such as children, older adults and pregnant women. It also encourages it where trials are not feasible, as with rare diseases, or would be unethical. Health Canada has accepted real-world evidence in Notice of Compliance with Conditions (NOC/c) decisions and orphan drug approvals.

Health Canada sets requirements for a sponsor's drug submission that contains real-world evidence. When the real-world evidence is pivotal evidence, the sponsor is required to:

- Identify in the cover letter that the submission contains real-world evidence, and in which modules.
- Explain in the cover letter why real-world evidence is used rather than more conventional approaches to show the product's benefits and risks.
- Explain in detail in the cover letter how the real-world evidence was gathered and analyzed.
- Show how any actual or potential biases or confounding in the data collection were mitigated.
- Reach out to Health Canada to determine whether a pre-submission meeting would be beneficial.

When the real-world evidence is supporting evidence, the sponsor is required to identify it in the cover letter, and in which modules.

Canada's Drug Agency (CDA-AMC, formerly CADTH) published the [*Guidance for Reporting Real-World Evidence*](https://www.cda-amc.ca/guidance-reporting-real-world-evidence) (2023) in partnership with Health Canada and INESSS. ## Choose a Quebec data source

Quebec has several large administrative databases for real-world evidence studies. Access to them is governed by Quebec's privacy law and needs ethics and institutional approval.

| Database | What it holds | Who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| [RAMQ](https://www.ramq.gouv.qc.ca/en/data-statistics) (Régie de l'assurance maladie du Québec) | The registration file (demographics), the medical services file (physicians' billing claims) and the pharmaceutical services file (outpatient prescriptions). | RAMQ |
| MED-ÉCHO (Maintenance et Exploitation des Données pour l'Étude de la Clientèle Hospitalière) | Hospital discharge summaries from all of Quebec's acute care institutions, with in-hospital procedures and diagnoses. | Quebec's health ministry |
| [INESSS](https://www.inesss.qc.ca) (Institut national d'excellence en santé et en services sociaux) | Linked, de-identified data under tripartite agreements between Quebec's health ministry, RAMQ and INESSS. | INESSS |
| SISMACQ (Système intégré de surveillance des maladies chroniques du Québec) | A dataset linking RAMQ's registration file, MED-ÉCHO, the death registry, medical services and pharmaceutical services, for surveillance of chronic diseases. | The Institut national de santé publique du Québec (INSPQ) |

RAMQ's data have been widely validated for pharmacoepidemiological studies and are among the administrative datasets most used for real-world evidence research in Canada.

## Get access to RAMQ and MED-ÉCHO data

Access to RAMQ and MED-ÉCHO data is not direct. It follows a set order, governed by Quebec's privacy law and agreements between institutions. Since July 1, 2024, the Act respecting health and social services information (Law 5) governs researchers' access to this information, including RAMQ's data.

1. **Get the research ethics board's decision.** The protocol says which databases are requested, the variables needed, how the data will be linked and the privacy safeguards. You can send the request to the Institut de la statistique du Québec (ISQ) while the ethics review is under way: its form asks for the review's status and expected date. The ISQ decides whether to wait for the approval.
2. **Ask the MUHC to authorize access.** A researcher attached to the MUHC asks the person exercising the highest authority within the MUHC, with a detailed presentation of the project, a privacy impact assessment (EFVP) and the decision of the research ethics board (REB). The MUHC's Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality reviews and approves the EFVP. An authorization is made official by a written agreement, and the Commission d'accès à l'information receives a copy. Confirm the route for RAMQ data with the [EFVP office](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca).
3. **Work with the data through the ISQ.** The [Institut de la statistique du Québec](https://statistique.quebec.ca/en/institut/services-for-researchers) manages secure access to linked administrative data for researchers. Researchers work with de-identified linked datasets in the CADRISQ secure research data centres.
4. **Sign the data holders' agreements.** Depending on the databases requested, agreements with RAMQ, Quebec's health ministry or other holders may be needed.

How long access takes varies with the request's complexity and the number of databases it links, so start the requests early.

Data access delays are the most common cause of delays in Quebec real-world evidence studies. File the request for authorization as soon as you have the REB's decision, since the request includes it.

## Check access to INESSS and SISMACQ data

INESSS has its own access route, separate from RAMQ's and the ISQ's.

INESSS gives access to linked, de-identified data under its tripartite agreements with Quebec's health ministry and RAMQ. Requests go directly to [INESSS](https://www.inesss.qc.ca), not through the ISQ and CADRISQ. REB approval is required before a research data request goes to INESSS.

SISMACQ is held by the [INSPQ](https://www.inspq.qc.ca) (Institut national de santé publique du Québec) and already links several RAMQ files, MED-ÉCHO and the death registry. As of 2017, the INSPQ reserved SISMACQ's data for developing and carrying out the surveillance of the chronic diseases in the surveillance plan, and only a restricted team dedicated to that surveillance could access them.

## Combine Data Warehouse and provincial data

A study that draws on both the [MUHC Data Warehouse](/kb/design-support/patient-data) and Quebec's provincial administrative data needs two separate authorizations. They can run at the same time. Teams often run them one after the other when they don't need to, which adds months to the timeline.

| Authorization | What it needs | When it can start |
|---|---|---|
| A Data Warehouse data request | REB approval, MUHC Authorization and, where the data are used without consent, an EFVP (form F17 for pre-screening, F18 for other access without consent). | The EFVP is filed with the Nagano F11. The data request is submitted once both approvals are in. |
| Access to provincial health and social services information | The REB's decision and an EFVP. | After the REB's decision. The MUHC authorizes access, and the Commission d'accès à l'information receives a copy of the agreement. |
| Access through the ISQ and CADRISQ | The access authorized under the Act respecting health and social services information. | Once access is authorized. |

File the EFVP with the Nagano F11, ask for authorization to access provincial information as soon as you have the REB's decision, and start a consultation with the Data Warehouse before you submit in Nagano.

## Plan a pragmatic trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

Pragmatic trials differ from traditional explanatory trials in ways that affect their design, their ethics review and their regulatory submission.

| Feature | Explanatory trial | Pragmatic trial |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | Specialized research centres. | Routine care settings. |
| Participants | Narrow eligibility, highly selected. | Broad eligibility, reflecting real practice. |
| Comparator | Placebo or an active control set by the protocol. | Usual care or standard of care. |
| Outcomes | Surrogate or biomarker endpoints. | Clinically meaningful outcomes, such as hospital admissions, deaths or quality of life. |
| Follow-up | Driven by the protocol, with frequent visits. | Routine clinical follow-up, which may use administrative data. |

Annex 2 of the international good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation, ICH E6(R3), which Health Canada implemented on Nov. 4, 2026, covers decentralized elements, pragmatic elements and the use of real-world data.{/* Closed in b161 by the second reader (b154's question for the coordinator, the fact check's row R33), for Annex 2's scope: ich.org, read by the coordinator in the built-in browser on Nov. 16, 2026 (saved, _review/2026-11/evidence/web/ich-efficacy-guidelines_read-2026-11-16/ich-efficacy-guidelines.txt): Annex 2 provides "additional GCP considerations tailored to the evolving clinical trial landscape, covering decentralised elements, pragmatic elements and the use of real-world data". The Innovative Clinical Trials (iCT) Initiative of the Strategy for Patient-Oriented Research (SPOR) of the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR) funds innovative trial designs, including pragmatic, adaptive and platform trials. The Clinical Trials Fund also supports pragmatic approaches. Both require patient engagement and open-access publication of results.

### Get Health Canada authorization when the trial needs it

A pragmatic drug trial still needs Health Canada's authorization through a Clinical Trial Application (CTA) if it uses a drug regulated under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, unless it uses a marketed drug within the conditions of its notice of compliance or drug identification number. [Types of Study](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study) says which studies need Health Canada's authorization. Consider discussing the regulatory route early in the design with the sponsor or with [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca).

## Address the design points of a real-world study

When a study uses real-world evidence or a pragmatic design, these points apply:

- **Data quality.** Real-world data should be assessed against the specific research question, for completeness, accuracy and relevance. Health Canada treats the scientific question as integral to assessing the level of evidence.
- **Confounding.** Observational real-world evidence studies face the potential for bias, such as confounding. Protocols should describe any potential sources of confounding or other bias, and address them through design (for example, an active comparator or a new-user design) and statistical methods. Protocols should also discuss any limitations on the ability to draw conclusions from the data, including any residual or unmeasured confounding, and methods to reduce their impact.
- **Privacy.** Using participants' health information held by the MUHC comes under Law 5, and access without consent needs an EFVP. [Privacy and the EFVP](/kb/governance/privacy) says what applies.
