Some researchers want to collect participants' biological materials or data and keep them for research that isn't yet defined, so that they or other researchers can use them in projects to come. A collection kept for that purpose is a bank. A biobank holds human biological materials, such as blood, tissue or DNA. A data registry, or databank, holds participants' data.

Creating a bank is research with human participants in its own right. Quebec's health ministry, in its [2020 framework for research with human participants](https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/document-002696/) (French only), counts the creation or use of a databank or a biobank as research. A bank collects, keeps and distributes its content with the participants' consent. Like a research project, it needs the ethics approval of a research ethics board (REB) in Quebec's health network.

The MUHC has adopted a *Policy on Biobanks and Databases for Research Purposes*. The policy covers the creation, management, use, oversight and destruction of the banks under the MUHC's responsibility.

Under that policy, the creation of a new bank is to be exceptional. The sustainability of existing banks, and the use of their materials and data in value-added studies, must guide the creation of any new one. The RI-MUHC's technology platforms include a Biobank platform, and the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM) offers a biobank.

## Know when a bank is a project of its own

In Nagano, a collection of biospecimens or data is a bank if it is collected, stored and distributed for use in future research. The bank or registry project type, with its form F11-BR, is for creating a new bank or registry. If a study also creates a biobank or a databank, [the bank is submitted as its own project](/apps/nagano/guide-03), on form F11-BR. If you aren't sure whether your study creates a bank, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca).

The [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter12-chapitre12.html) uses the word biobank in a wider sense. In TCPS 2's sense, the materials held in a biobank may be intended only for use in a specific project, or a biobank may be established to provide access to biological materials for numerous projects over many years.

| Situation | What applies | Where the steps are |
|---|---|---|
| A study keeps the specimens it collects for the storage time its protocol or other study documentation sets. | The study's own protocol, consent and REB approval. The specimens are destroyed as the protocol and the institution require. | [Management of Biological Specimens](/kb/conduct/study-operations/biological-specimens#store-specimens-and-monitor-their-temperature) |
| A study plans secondary uses of the data and samples it obtains, such as optional specimen banking. | The study's consent form, which the REB reviews. The information it discloses generally includes the planned or anticipated secondary uses of the data and samples obtained in the course of the study. | [Choose the consent model](#choose-the-consent-model) |
| A study or a team creates a bank, which collects, stores and distributes materials or data for use in future research. | Consent to storage and use in future research, the REB review that the creation of a bank requires, the bank's continuing review and its management framework. | [Get the bank approved](#get-the-bank-approved) and the sections after it |
| A new project uses materials or data from an earlier study or from a bank that already exists. | The rules for secondary use, and the bank's own conditions of access. | [Retrospective and Biobank Studies](/paths/retrospective#decide-whether-the-study-needs-consent) |

Not every registry is a bank. The Glossary of Terms of the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) counts a patient registry as a type of [observational study](/kb/foundations/glossary#observational-study).

## Get the bank approved

A bank needs the MUHC's authorization, as other research does. At the MUHC, a research project involving human participants [must receive three favourable reviews to be authorized](/kb/governance/institutional-structure#who-reviews-and-authorizes-your-study): a scientific review, an ethics review and an institutional feasibility review. The final step is <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, which the <Tooltip tip="The person whom an establishment of Quebec's health network formally mandates to authorize the research conducted under its auspices." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> issues. No solicitation, participant recruitment or data collection may begin until MUHC Authorization has been received by the principal investigator, who leads the project at the MUHC.

The creation of a bank [requires REB review](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html). New and existing data repositories and tissue banks to be used potentially for human research [must be reviewed by the REB before they are conducted](/sops/cr-007#5-3-1) at the MUHC or the RI-MUHC. For biological materials, the REB's review comes before any are collected. In its review, the REB should address the risks and potential benefits of collecting and storing the materials, the nature and governance of the bank, and the consent process.

Submit the bank in Nagano as its own project, of the bank or registry type. For a bank, [the REB's submission requirements](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) accept the bank's management framework in place of a research protocol. For a biobanking consent form or a management framework for a biobank or a registry, the [Centre for Applied Ethics](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms) asks teams to consult the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca).

The Director of the RI-MUHC's Centre for Translational Biology (CTB) assesses the institutional suitability and relevance of biobanks, in accordance with the MUHC's policy on banks.

The bank is recorded in the MUHC's registry of authorized research in Nagano. Its entry includes:

- The bank's title.
- The name of the person responsible for the bank.
- The date of the REB's ethics approval.
- The nature of its content, data or biological material, and the types of research projects that use it.
- The date of each annual renewal of the bank's ethics approval.
- The date the bank's activities end or the bank is transferred, where that applies.
- The name of the REB or REBs that review and follow the research projects that use the bank.

## Write a management framework for the bank

A bank's management framework sets how the bank is governed: who is responsible for it, how its content is collected, kept and released, and what happens when it closes. Quebec's health ministry requires every bank to have one, with at least the basic elements its 2012 guide on bank management frameworks sets (French only).

The management framework must say which REB reviews the bank when it is created and follows it afterwards. The management framework must also say how the REB that reviews and follows each project using the bank is chosen. When a bank is located in more than one establishment, the establishments concerned decide each one's role.

The ministry also requires each establishment's rules on banks to cover the approvals needed to create and maintain a bank, the end of its activities, the ownership of its infrastructure, intellectual property, and participation and contribution. At the MUHC, the establishment's rules on banks are in its *Policy on Biobanks and Databases for Research Purposes*.

Institutions and researchers that maintain a biobank [must have or use appropriate facilities](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter12-chapitre12.html), equipment, policies and procedures to store the materials safely and in accordance with applicable standards. Those institutions and researchers must also establish physical, administrative and technical safeguards to protect the materials and any information about participants from unauthorized handling. Procedures for storage and record keeping must include effective measures to protect participants' identities.

The bank's governance should clearly set out mechanisms and procedures to ensure that each later use of its materials and data follows the terms of the participants' original consent.

## Choose the consent model

Consent to store materials or data for future research can take several forms:

- Specific consent: consent for one defined research project only. Using the materials or data for other purposes then needs further consent or [the REB's approval of their use without consent](/paths/retrospective#decide-whether-the-study-needs-consent).
- Broad consent: consent to storage and use in future research that isn't yet specified, always within specific restrictions, for example to a field of study or a disease, or excluding use by private industry.
- Tiered consent: participants choose the kinds of research they consent to, for example cancer research only, publicly funded research only or specific institutions only.

Blanket consent, which is typically unrestricted, is not permitted under the [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html). Broad consent is permitted.

When consent to a specific project and consent to storage for future research are sought at the same time, prospective participants must be given the option to consent to each separately, on separate forms or in separate sections of the same form. The REB may require a separate consent form for specimen banking.

[Quebec's Civil Code](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991#se:24) requires that consent to research that could interfere with a person's integrity be given in writing. If [the REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) judges that the circumstances justify it, the consent may be given otherwise than in writing, in the manner the REB determines for evidential purposes. [Who may consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#who-can-consent) to research that could interfere with a person's integrity depends on the participant's age and situation.

The Civil Code also requires that the alienation by a person of a part or product of their body be gratuitous. An alienation that involves a risk to the person's health may not be repeated.

<Note>
In Quebec, a part of the body, whether an organ, tissue or other substance, removed from a person during their care may be used for research only with the person's consent, or that of the person qualified to consent on their behalf. After the person's death, the consent of the person who could have consented to their care is needed. Ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) how a broad consent under TCPS 2 meets the Civil Code's requirement before you rely on it.
</Note>

## Tell participants what they are consenting to

To seek broad consent, researchers [shall give prospective participants](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html), or authorized third parties, the information that TCPS 2 sets for every consent. Researchers shall also give these details, as appropriate to the project:

- The type, identifiability and amount of the data and materials collected and stored for re-use, and for what potential purpose.
- The voluntariness of the participant's consent, including any limits on the feasibility of withdrawal.
- A general description of the nature and types of future research that may be conducted, including whether it might be conducted outside Canada, if that is known.
- The risks and potential benefits of storing the data and materials and of their use in future research, including areas of uncertainty where the risks can't be estimated.
- Access to a general description of the bank and its governance.
- A statement of the participant's preference about being contacted again for future research.
- Whether the data or materials could be shared with researchers who are not subject to TCPS 2.
- Whether the research will or might include whole genome sequencing or similar technologies that may pose a substantial risk of re-identifying the participant or of identifying material incidental findings, where appropriate.
- Whether linking the data with other data about participants, in public or personal records, is anticipated.
- Separate options for consenting to a specific project and for consenting to storage for future research.

Not every item is needed for every project. The REB considers which items, or which other items, the consent process needs.

For biological materials, researchers [shall also give](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter12-chapitre12.html) these details:

- The type and amount of biological materials to be taken.
- How they will be taken, and the safety and invasiveness of the procedures.
- Their intended uses, including any commercial use.
- The measures used to protect participants' privacy and minimize their risks.
- How long the materials will be kept, how they will be preserved, where they will be stored, in Canada or outside Canada, and how they will be disposed of, if that applies.
- Any anticipated linkage of the materials with information about the participant.
- The researchers' plan for handling results and findings, including clinically relevant information and incidental findings.

The process for requesting the withdrawal of biological materials shall be clearly explained, with the conditions under which researchers could not remove a participant's data from the project. Information already derived from the materials and aggregated into findings can't be withdrawn. Anonymizing the materials may also prevent their later withdrawal.

When biological materials are collected for research, a clear distinction should be made during the consent process between consent to research participation and consent for any clinical procedure or test. In practice, the distinction may mean separate consent information and forms. In any event, the different uses must be clearly explained.

During the consent process for biological materials collected for research, researchers and authorized third parties must take into account any research directive in which the participant expressed preferences for future research participation before losing decision-making capacity.

Where the research uses participants' data, researchers and authorized third parties should be guided during the consent process by any research directive that a person signed to indicate their preferences about future participation in research, in the event that they lose capacity or upon death.

Researchers or banks that intend to make their data or materials available to researchers who are not subject to TCPS 2 [should consider the repercussions](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html) for participants. Participants must be informed of this possibility. Researchers must also comply with all applicable legal and regulatory requirements on [the protection of privacy](/kb/governance/privacy) and on consent for the collection, use or disclosure of information about participants. The requirements may vary from one jurisdiction to another.

Check the finished consent form against the [Verification of an ICF Document](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=249038753110964987) checklist, the RI-MUHC's checklist for informed consent forms (ICFs). Its Tissue Storage items ask the form to:

- Explain whether there will be biobanking.
- Explain the storage, safekeeping and confidentiality of genetic material, coded or anonymized.
- Explain how the samples are identified.
- State how long the samples are stored.
- State whether the samples may be used for other research projects.
- Explain the physical risks of obtaining the samples.
- Explain the socio-economic risks.
- State which third parties need access to the samples or results, and why.
- State whether genetic counselling will be offered.
- State whether other family members need to be recruited for genetic testing.

## Keep the materials and data safe

Participants are told how long the bank will keep their materials, how it will preserve them and where it will store them ([what participants are told](#tell-participants-what-they-are-consenting-to)).

A registry's unpublished data on individuals are in the sensitive security class. [Store them, send them and give access to them](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#apply-the-controls-for-each-kind-of-data) under the controls for that class. Linking the bank's data with other datasets [needs the REB's approval](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#know-how-identifiable-your-data-are) before it is done.

When the bank's content has been de-identified, the researcher, the bank's responsible authority and future researchers share the responsibility to abstain from attempting to re-identify participants, unless the law requires it.

## Run the bank over time

The bank's ethics approval is renewed each year. The REB reviews research at intervals suited to its degree of risk, and at least once a year.

Where future contact is planned, such as contacting participants again or returning results, researchers should invite participants to keep their contact information up to date with a designated person. Banks and researchers have a duty to give participants who wish it the information relevant to their consent, for as long as their data and materials are stored and used.

A bank that holds biological materials must respect the preferences participants expressed about the future use of their materials. When a participant does not want their materials used for future research, the bank should remove them from any collection used or made available for research.

## Release materials or data to other researchers

Other researchers use the bank's materials or data in projects of their own. A bank may have its own policies on access, and researchers should be aware of them. Researchers may be required to apply to the bank for permission and to sign an agreement that sets the conditions of access and use.

Biobank specimens held at the RI-MUHC are institutional assets. Releasing specimens for external research requires:

- A formal access request reviewed by the biobank's governance committee or its designate.
- The REB's approval of the proposed use, or a documented REB waiver where it applies.
- A Material Transfer Agreement (MTA) when specimens leave the institution, managed by the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).
- A record of the consent's scope, confirming that the proposed use falls within what participants consented to.

A research agreement is required whenever human samples or human data will be received or transferred for a study. [All agreements for studies conducted at the MUHC or the RI-MUHC must be drafted](/sops/cr-007#5-1-2) by the Research Agreements Office. The Office drafts data and material transfer agreements among the others.

[Upload every research agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#submit-the-agreement-in-nagano-and-my-contracts) both to Nagano and to My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal. The upload starts the Research Agreements Office's review. Researchers should never sign a binding contract or agreement before the Research Agreements Office has reviewed it. Investigators must not sign on behalf of the RI-MUHC.

An MTA governs the transfer of biological materials between institutions. The agreement sets the permitted uses, intellectual property rights, liability, biosafety requirements and what happens to the remaining material when the research ends, such as its return or destruction. Investigators should not transfer materials without an executed MTA. Before data go to a site outside the MUHC or come from one, [a data sharing or transfer agreement must be in place](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#put-an-agreement-in-place-before-data-leave-or-arrive).

Where the data or materials come from a specific community or group, researchers and banks may be required to consult the group further, seek its permission or respect existing agreements. Data or materials identifiable as coming from a First Nations, Inuit or Métis community or People follow [Indigenous Research Ethics](/kb/recruitment/indigenous-research-ethics).

## Use a bank that already exists

Most projects that involve a bank use one that already exists, rather than create one. A project that only uses an existing bank or registry is non-interventional research, and uses the F11-NIR in Nagano.

[Nagano asks](/apps/nagano/guide-03) where the bank is located, its name and the person responsible for it, and for a copy of its management framework and consent form. The [Centre for Applied Ethics](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms) publishes a protocol template for projects that use data or samples already stored in a research biobank.

The steps for such a project, from consent to the REB's review, are on [Retrospective and Biobank Studies](/paths/retrospective).

If you aren't sure whether your project needs a bank of its own, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca).
