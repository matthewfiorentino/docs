When a study runs at more than one public institution of Quebec's health and social services network (<Tooltip tip="Quebec's public health and social services network (Réseau de la santé et des services sociaux), including the RI-MUHC." headline="RSSS" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#rsss">RSSS</Tooltip>), one research ethics board (<Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>) reviews it for every site. Each institution still reviews the study's feasibility for itself and authorizes it at its own site. The order of submissions, from the first one to the closure, follows from that split.

## Check whether the study is a multi-centric study

A study at more than one Quebec site within the public health network is [reviewed by one Quebec evaluating REB](/sops/cr-007#5-6-multi-centrique-research-in-quebec). The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) and Nagano call it a multi-centric study.

The single review is set out in a framework of Quebec's health ministry, the [*Cadre de référence des établissements publics du réseau de la santé et des services sociaux pour l'autorisation d'une recherche menée dans plus d'un établissement*](https://publications.msss.gouv.qc.ca/msss/document-003975/) (the Framework, in French only). The ministry issued it under its former name, the Ministère de la Santé et des Services sociaux, and updated it on April 1, 2016. The Framework is a ministerial directive. It applies to every new study conducted, in part or in whole, at more than one public institution of the network since February 1, 2015. It doesn't apply to research under Quebec's act on assisted procreation.

The ministry began the work that led to the Framework in January 2013, together with the Fonds de recherche du Québec – Santé (FRQS) and the four integrated university health networks (RUIS). The MUHC belongs to the McGill network. The Framework aims to protect participants and to promote excellence in research across the network. It lets an REB put its expertise at the service of every public institution taking part in the same study, and lets each institution's patients take part safely in more research.

The MUHC REB is designated by Quebec's health minister, so it can act as the reviewing REB for a multi-centric study.

## Know which sites the Framework does not cover

The Framework covers only public institutions of Quebec's health and social services network. It doesn't apply to sites outside Quebec, or to sites in Quebec that are not part of the network.

A study with one Quebec site and sites in other provinces or countries is not a multi-centric study. [Each site submits to its own REB](/sops/cr-007#5-6-multi-centrique-research-in-quebec). Submit the sites outside Quebec under their own jurisdictions' rules, and submit the Quebec site in Nagano. For example, if the MUHC takes part in a study that also has sites in Toronto and Boston, the MUHC submits to the MUHC REB on its own, and no Quebec site acts as lead under the Framework. [International and Cross-Border Studies](/kb/design/cross-border-studies) covers the sites outside Quebec.

Private institutions and university faculties are outside the Framework unless they have a formal agreement with a network institution. The McGill University IRB has inter-institutional agreements with the MUHC and other affiliated hospital REBs, but McGill itself is not a network institution. If a study uses McGill University resources as well as an affiliated hospital's, it needs a separate McGill feasibility review and authorization letter.

## Know what the reviewing REB and each site do

In a multi-centric study, one REB of the network does the single ethics review for every participating public institution, and every institution recognizes its approval. The RI-MUHC's SOPs call the site whose REB reviews the study the lead site, and the others participating sites.

The researcher chooses which REB to ask, by the Framework's rules. A researcher who holds researcher status at network institutions asks the REB of one of those institutions, if participants will be recruited there. Otherwise, the researcher asks the REB of one of the institutions where participants will be recruited. When a sponsor offers the same study to several institutions, the sponsor and one of the researchers agree on which network REB to ask.

Every institution where the study runs does its own institutional feasibility review (*examen de convenance*). Its <Tooltip tip="The person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research: the Director of Innovation, Performance and Quality, who issues MUHC Authorization for each study." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> authorizes the study at that site only once the scientific review, the ethics review and the feasibility review have all had a positive result. The single ethics review does not replace this local authorization.

<Warning>
An authorization to conduct the research must be obtained from each participating institution before any research procedures are carried out at that site.

Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

## Practical implications

A multi-centric study changes how several routine submissions work. In the table, the lead site is the site whose REB reviews the study.

| Submission | How it works in a multi-centric study |
|---|---|
| Initial ethics submission | The lead site [submits the study documents in Nagano for all participating network sites](/sops/cr-007#3-0-responsibilities). Each participating institution runs its own feasibility review. |
| Amendments | The lead site submits amendments to the reviewing REB. Once the reviewing REB approves an amendment, the amended documents apply to every participating network site, which doesn't resubmit them for ethics review. The Personne mandatée of each participating institution receives a copy of the reviewing REB's decision and must adopt it or withdraw the authorization given to the researcher. A Personne mandatée who refuses to adopt a decision must suspend or withdraw that authorization and give the reviewing REB the reasons. When an amendment changes the network consent form, each site completes [the steps for the new form](#prepare-the-consent-form-for-every-site). When the MUHC is a participating site, the Personne mandatée creates an F1-MEO form in Nagano for each amendment the reviewing REB approves. Complete and submit it. If the amendment affects a feasibility review at the MUHC, wait for that review's approval before implementing the amendment at the MUHC. |
| EFVP | For a study that needs an EFVP, [the lead site must submit one EFVP form for the project](/sops/cr-007#5-4-3). [Privacy and the EFVP](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed) says when a study needs one. |
| Annual renewal | When the MUHC is the lead site, participating sites that use Nagano [submit their own renewal](/sops/cr-007#3-0-responsibilities). Sites without Nagano send it, on the F9PDF form, by email to the RI-MUHC or MUHC research coordinator assigned to the study's REB submissions, who submits it to the MUHC REB. Renewal requests for external sites go in at the same time for all sites. |
| Reportable events | Each site enters its events on its own log, such as the SAE log or the deviation log. [Each principal investigator submits their own site's reportable events to the reviewing REB](/sops/cr-026#5-4-2). When the MUHC REB is the reviewing REB, each Quebec site [must notify the sponsor or sponsor-investigator](/sops/cr-026#5-4-5) as soon as possible, and within 24 hours for a serious adverse event, a serious medical device incident or a protocol violation. Quebec sites with Nagano report their events directly to the MUHC REB. For a Quebec site without Nagano, the lead MUHC sponsor-investigator reports the site's events and corrective actions to the MUHC REB on the site's behalf, within 7 days of becoming aware of an event that involves a death or a life-threatening reaction, and within 15 days of becoming aware of other events. The MUHC research team submits the site's PDF form in Nagano (F3a for a serious, unexpected event, F3b for other events). |
| Adding a site | Converting a single-site study to a multi-centric one, or adding a site, uses the Nagano form F1MP (Request to convert to an MP study or add a new site), separate from the regular F1 amendment. A conversion also needs network versions of the consent forms. |

## Answer the reviewing REB when the MUHC is the lead site

When the MUHC is the lead site, the MUHC REB reviews the study, and the MUHC team submits the study in Nagano for all participating network institutions. The initial F11 covers every site, and the study-wide amendments and renewals are submitted from the MUHC.

The MUHC team also submits for the participating sites that don't use Nagano. Those sites send their renewal requests and PDF forms to the RI-MUHC or MUHC research coordinator assigned to the study's REB submissions. In practice, the MUHC coordinator often becomes the coordination hub for the Quebec sites: tracking each site's authorization, sending amended documents to the sites, aligning renewals and routing reportable events from sites without Nagano. The [SOP on initial review](/sops/cr-007#5-6-multi-centrique-research-in-quebec) and [the SOP on ongoing REB review](/sops/cr-026#5-4-multi-centrique-research-in-quebec-and-multi-center-research-across-canada) give the full procedure.

## Submit an MEO file when the MUHC is a participating site

When another network institution is the lead site, the MUHC's file in Nagano is an MEO file (*mis en œuvre*), a participating site's submission. The reviewing REB is the lead institution's REB, and the MUHC REB does not do its own ethics review of the study.

The MUHC team receives the reviewing REB's forms and notifications in Nagano only once two things are done. The reviewing REB confirms the MUHC as a participating site in Nagano, and the MUHC's MEO project is synchronized with the reviewing REB's. For the synchronization, the reviewing REB sends the MUHC team an invitation by email. Answer it, so that you receive the project's decisions and can open the reviewing REB's forms.

The MUHC still does its own institutional feasibility review, and the study needs <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> from the Personne mandatée before any research activity at the MUHC. An ethics review done elsewhere doesn't shorten this review. It covers <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's Quality Assurance department, which checks the credentials and training of each investigator who submits a study, audits research and maintains the SOPs. In GCP, quality assurance is the planned, systematic actions that ensure a trial complies with GCP and the applicable regulatory requirements." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">Quality Assurance (QA)</Tooltip> for Privileges or Researcher Status and training, contracts, the Director of Medical and Professional Services (DMPS), nursing, laboratory and pharmacy, where applicable, as for a single-site study.

When a serious, unexpected adverse event that is related or possibly related to the study happens at the MUHC, report it to the MUHC REB and the Personne mandatée on an F3-MEO form, and to the reviewing REB by its own procedure, without waiting for the reviewing REB's decision. The [Personne mandatée](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca) creates or reopens the other MEO forms without notice by email, so check the project in Nagano regularly.

Consider writing to the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) early if you coordinate an MEO study. The MEO workflow in Nagano differs from a standard F11 submission, and the arrangements with the lead site for exchanging documents, timing renewals and reporting events are best settled when the study is activated.

## Prepare the consent form for every site

The reviewing REB examines the consent form in French. The Framework sets that the consent form must be in French, and it may come with a translation into another language. The approved form names no institution and shows clearly where each site inserts its own administrative details, such as its complaints commissioner.

Each participating site's researcher then gives its Personne mandatée a French consent form with the site's details inserted in those spaces. When the MUHC is the lead site, prepare a network version of each consent form, in both English and French, unless the MUHC is the only site recruiting participants. A network version is an editable, site-adaptable version of the final approved form, titled "NETWORK ICF" followed by the form's title, which the MUHC team sends to the other Quebec sites. When the MUHC is a participating site, the Personne mandatée validates the local versions adapted from the reviewing REB's network version before they are used.

Once the local versions are validated, you may need to make an administrative change that doesn't affect the network version, such as correcting a typo or updating the research team's contact details. For such a change, notify the [Personne mandatée](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca) by email, then complete and submit the F5-MEO form that the Personne mandatée creates in the project in Nagano.

When the reviewing REB approves a change to the network consent form, it attaches to its decision the amended network version, with the date of its approval, and a copy of the old version annotated to show the changes. For the study to continue at each site, the following steps must be completed as soon as possible after the decision:

1. On receiving the decision, the site's Personne mandatée checks whether the site's researcher has already filed a version of the new form with the site's administrative details inserted. If not, the Personne mandatée sets the researcher a deadline for it.
2. The researcher gives the Personne mandatée the final version of the new form that the site will use, with a copy annotated to show the details inserted into the network version. The Personne mandatée confirms that the changes made to the network version are administrative and, on the researcher's request, confirms in writing the date from which the site authorizes the form's use.
3. The Personne mandatée sends the new form and the annotated copy to the reviewing REB, and to the sponsor if there is one.

The reviewing REB may suspend its approval for a site if it finds that changes other than administrative ones were made to the network version.

## CATALIS FAST TRACK

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Industry-sponsored studies</span></div>

[CATALIS](https://catalisquebec.com/en/), a not-for-profit organization mandated and funded by the Government of Quebec, offers the [FAST TRACK Evaluation Service](https://catalisquebec.com/en/fast-track-evaluation-service/) for industry-sponsored clinical trials. [CATALIS's aim](https://catalisquebec.com/en/resources-for-health-institutions/) is to have clinical trials approved through the service in no more than eight weeks. Clinical Trials Quebec publishes the service's performance figures, with their date, on [its FAST TRACK page](https://sponsors.clinicaltrialsquebec.com/accelerate-clinical-research-projects/fasttrack-evaluation-service/). Several Quebec institutions have been activated first in Canada or first in the world for studies run through FAST TRACK.

A sponsor that wants the service goes to CATALIS and pays a fee to run its study through it. CATALIS then contacts the RI-MUHC, which is one of the service's partner institutions. The service is open to pharmaceutical companies and contract research organizations that are members of the CATALIS Network.

FAST TRACK does not bypass any review. Its shorter timeline comes from running the ethics and feasibility reviews in parallel, with standardized document packages, to tighter deadlines. Each review still takes place.

## Close a multi-centric study in the right order

The order of closure matters, because the lead site's closure ends the study for every site. When the MUHC is the lead site, each participating network site in Quebec submits its own Closure of Research Project form in Nagano first. The MUHC submits its own closure only once every participating site has submitted theirs. Once the lead site submits its form, the sites can no longer create new forms or submit documents.

[The sponsor-investigator or the principal investigator should submit a site's Closure of Research Project form](/sops/cr-016#5-5-2) when all of these conditions are met:
- No participants remain involved at the site.
- Participants are no longer exposed to immediate research risks.
- All data collection is complete.
- All study supplies and investigational product have been returned or destroyed on site, if applicable.
- The close-out activities of the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, the investigator and the monitor, where there are any, are complete.

A participating site that hasn't reached that point delays the lead site's closure, so plan the sites' closures together.

When the MUHC is a participating site, submit the Closure of Research Project form for the MUHC site on the lead site's form, in a timely manner, for the reviewing REB's acknowledgement. [The SOP on study close-out](/sops/cr-016#5-5-2) gives the full procedure, including studies with sites in other provinces, and [Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out/close-out) covers the rest of closing a study.
