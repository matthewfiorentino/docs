You may be building an app, an algorithm or an artificial intelligence (AI) model for health care, as a researcher, an engineer, a data scientist or a clinician. Software can be a medical device. When its intended use is a medical purpose, such as diagnosing or treating a disease, Health Canada regulates it as one. Testing such software in people is investigational testing of a medical device, and Health Canada may have to authorize it.

Software that is not a medical device can still be research involving humans. A project is research when it is intended to extend knowledge through a disciplined inquiry or systematic investigation. A research project that develops or tests software with human participants, their data or their biological material goes through ethics review and MUHC Authorization like any other study. Where it uses identifiable health information without consent, it also goes through a privacy review.

## Know when software is a medical device

Software is a medical device when its intended use is a medical purpose. Health Canada calls it <Tooltip tip="Software intended to be used for one or more medical purposes that performs those purposes without being part of a hardware medical device." headline="Software as a Medical Device (SaMD)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#samd">software as a medical device (SaMD)</Tooltip> when it performs that purpose without being part of a hardware medical device. [Health Canada considers that software is a medical device](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/software-medical-device-guidance-document.html) when both of these are true:

- It is intended to be used for one or more of the medical purposes in the definition of a device in the Food and Drugs Act.
- It performs these purposes without being part of a hardware medical device, so that a hardware medical device does not need it to achieve its intended medical purpose.

The medical purposes in the Act's definition include diagnosing, treating, mitigating or preventing a disease, disorder or abnormal physical state, or any of their symptoms. They also include restoring, modifying or correcting the body's structure or the functioning of any part of the body, diagnosing pregnancy, caring for people during pregnancy and at or after birth, and preventing conception.

For software, Health Canada generally interprets a medical purpose as one of these:

- Acquiring, processing or analyzing a medical image, a signal from an in vitro diagnostic device, or a pattern or signal from a signal acquisition system or imaging device.
- Supporting or providing recommendations to health care professionals, patients or caregivers who are not health care professionals about the prevention, diagnosis, treatment or mitigation of a disease or condition.

Software that fits these criteria is broadly called decision support software. Clinical decision support software is intended for health care professionals, and patient decision support software for patients and caregivers. Some decision support software is regulated as a medical device, including software that provides prognostic and predictive information. Other decision support software is not subject to the Regulations if it meets [the exclusion criteria](#know-which-software-is-not-a-medical-device).

Mobile apps that meet the definition are SaMD. SaMD can run on ordinary computers, tablets and phones, and can be used with other products or connected to other medical devices. Software whose intended purpose is to drive a hardware medical device is not SaMD.

The software's functionality and how it is represented or labelled for use decide whether it is a medical device. The software's intended use normally shows in the manufacturer's labelling, instructions for use, website and promotional material. A software developer who produces SaMD under their own name or trademark is a manufacturer under the Medical Devices Regulations. An academic institution can also be a manufacturer, if it designed or owns the device.

Health Canada regulates only software that is sold within the meaning of the Food and Drugs Act, which generally requires the transfer of ownership of a device from one party to another. Downloading software from an online store to a mobile device is one example of such a sale.

An AI tool with a medical purpose can be SaMD. Health Canada calls a medical device that uses machine learning, in part or in whole, to achieve its intended medical purpose a [machine learning-enabled medical device (MLMD)](/kb/planning/ai-in-clinical-research#test-an-ai-tool-that-may-be-a-medical-device), and its class can range from Class I to Class IV.

A licence application for a Class III or IV machine learning-enabled device should include clinical evidence, such as clinical validation studies, which may use retrospective or prospective evaluations. The participants of such a study should be independent of the data used to develop, train and tune the software.

## Know which software is not a medical device

Software that has no direct impact on the diagnosis, treatment or management of a person's disease, disorder, abnormal physical state or symptoms is not subject to the Medical Devices Regulations. A mobile app that monitors daily calorie intake and energy expenditure, so that a person can manage their own weight, is Health Canada's example.

Health Canada has long held that these types of software are not medical devices:

- Software for the administrative support of a health care facility.
- Software for clinical communication and workflow, such as patient registration, scheduling visits, voice calls and video calls.
- Software for maintaining or encouraging a healthy lifestyle, such as general wellness apps.
- Software that serves as electronic patient records, or that lets patients access their personal health information.

Clinical or patient decision support software may also fall outside the Regulations. [Such software must meet all four of these criteria](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/software-medical-device-guidance-document.html) to be excluded:

1. It is not intended to acquire, process or analyze a medical image, a signal from an in vitro diagnostic device, or a pattern or signal from a signal acquisition system.
2. It is intended to display, analyze or print medical information about a patient, or other medical information such as clinical guidelines, studies or recommendations.
3. It is only intended to support a health care professional, a patient or a caregiver in making decisions about the prevention, diagnosis or treatment of a disease or condition.
4. It is not intended to replace a health care professional's clinical judgement in making a diagnosis or a treatment decision about an individual patient.

The intended user should be able to review the basis for the software's recommendation, so that they can rely on their own judgement rather than mainly on the software. Software used to treat, diagnose or drive clinical management does not generally meet the third criterion. The criteria are a foundation for the analysis, not a rigid test, and other factors may need to be considered.

Health Canada gives examples of software that is not subject to the Regulations, among them:

- Apps that give patients simple tools to log, track or trend their measurements and share them with their health care provider, without alerts or recommendations to change a prescribed treatment.
- An electronic health record. Software modules within an electronic health record that meet the definition of a medical device are still regulated.
- Software that only transfers, stores, converts the format of or displays medical device data, without controlling or altering a connected medical device.
- A risk calculator based on well-established, publicly available models, or software for simple medical calculations routinely used in clinical practice.
- Software that coaches or educates patients to help them manage their health, such as by promoting a healthy weight or prompting them to follow a set medication schedule.

The same tracking app changes category when it does more. In Health Canada's walkthrough, an app that only lets patients track their health information is not a medical device. When the app also analyzes that information with a novel algorithm to predict the risk of diabetes, giving the health care professional a diagnostic output they would otherwise not have, it is a Class II medical device.

Software that is not a medical device can still be part of research that needs [ethics review and MUHC Authorization](#test-software-that-is-not-a-medical-device).

## Know how Health Canada classes software as a medical device

The Medical Devices Regulations class every medical device from Class I, the lowest risk, to Class IV, the highest, by the classification rules in their Schedule 1. For software, the class depends mainly on its intended use. The class decides what Health Canada requires for a licence and, in a study, [whether testing the software needs an investigational testing authorization](#know-what-each-class-means-for-a-study).

The manufacturer [must determine the software's risk class](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/software-medical-device-guidance-document.html) from its intended use and the applicable rules in Schedule 1. Health Canada confirms the class when it reviews a licence application, and it has the final decision when it and the manufacturer disagree. If a device can be classified into more than one class, the class representing the higher risk applies. Each software function counts, so software intended for several health care situations or conditions takes the highest class among them.

### Describe the intended use on two factors

Health Canada classes software as a medical device on two factors in its intended use statement: what the software's information does for the health care decision, and how serious the health care situation or condition is. The statement should also describe the software's core functionality, the features essential to that use.

| What the software's information does | What it means |
|---|---|
| Treats or diagnoses | The information is used to take an immediate or near-term action: to treat, prevent or mitigate by connecting to other medical devices, medicinal products or other means of providing therapy, or to diagnose, screen for or detect a disease or condition. |
| Drives clinical or patient management | The information is used to triage or identify early signs of a disease or condition that will guide the next diagnostic or treatment interventions, to aid in diagnosis by analyzing relevant information to help predict the risk of a disease or condition or as an aid to making a definitive diagnosis, or to aid in treatment by providing enhanced support to the safe and effective use of medicinal products or a medical device. |
| Informs clinical or patient management | The information does not trigger an immediate or near-term action. It informs of options for treating, diagnosing, preventing or mitigating a disease or condition, or aggregates relevant clinical information. |

| Health care situation or condition | What it means |
|---|---|
| Critical | Accurate or timely diagnosis or treatment is vital to avoid death, long-term disability or other serious deterioration of a patient's health. The condition is life-threatening or needs major therapeutic interventions, action may be time-critical, or the target population is fragile, such as children. |
| Serious | Accurate diagnosis or treatment is of vital importance to avoid unnecessary interventions, such as a biopsy, or timely intervention is important to mitigate long-term irreversible consequences. The condition is moderate in progression and often curable, and intervention is not normally time-critical. The condition does not need major therapeutic interventions, and the target population is not vulnerable with respect to it. |
| Non-serious | Accurate diagnosis and treatment are important but not critical. The condition progresses slowly and predictably, needs only minor therapeutic interventions, and the interventions are normally non-invasive. |

### Find the class the two factors suggest

Health Canada's guidance combines the two factors in a chart, for software other than in vitro diagnostic software. The chart suggests a class and the rule that might apply. The chart is a guide only, and Health Canada keeps the final decision.

| Situation or condition | Treats or diagnoses | Drives management | Informs management |
|---|---|---|---|
| Critical | Class III | Class III | Class I or II |
| Serious | Class II or III | Class II or III | Class I or II |
| Non-serious | Class I or II | Class I or II | Class I or II |

The choice within a cell comes from the rules of Schedule 1 that Health Canada applies to software. Health Canada treats software as an active device, because it relies on a source of energy other than energy generated by the human body or gravity:

- **Rule 10(1), Class II.** An active diagnostic device, including any dedicated software, that supplies energy for imaging or monitoring physiological processes. For software, monitoring a physiological process means helping patients and health care professionals observe, track and record medical parameters, such as physiological and anatomical measurements, over time or at one point in time.
- **Rule 10(2), Class III.** A device under Rule 10(1) that is intended to monitor, assess or diagnose a disease, a disorder, an abnormal physical state or a pregnancy, if erroneous readings could result in immediate danger.
- **Rule 12, Class I.** Any other active device. For software, it is the fallback rule.

Health Canada expects most software apps to be classified under Rule 10. Other rules of Schedule 1 may be used as the technology progresses. Software that is an in vitro diagnostic device is classified by the rules for in vitro diagnostic devices in Part 2 of Schedule 1, except the rule that makes near-patient devices Class III.

Health Canada's examples show how the factors play out:

| Class | Examples of software in Health Canada's guidance |
|---|---|
| Class I | Software that analyzes camera images to measure joint mobility and posture, as a clinical aid in rehabilitation. |
| Class II | Software that acquires blood pressure, heart rate and weight from connected devices for remote patient monitoring and alerts the clinician when a signal is out of range. Software that analyzes previously recorded physiological signals to stage sleep, so that a physician can identify obstructive sleep apnea. Software that uses AI to tailor mental health treatment plans, including medication dosing, for use during a routine appointment. |
| Class III | Software that analyzes images to support treatment decisions in acute stroke. Software that receives readings from an implantable device and alerts a health care professional when immediate intervention is needed. Radiation treatment planning software whose plan serves as input for medical linear accelerators. |

An example doesn't mean that every software of its type is in that class. The same mental health software would be Class III if it were intended for use in hospital for patients in an acute psychiatric event, because the condition would then be critical.

If you are unsure of your software's class, Health Canada's [Medical Devices Bureau](mailto:hc.devicelicensing-homologationinstruments.sc@canada.ca) answers questions about how a rule applies.

## Know which kind of software study yours is

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Retrospective and biobank studies (Level V)</span></div>

What your study does with the software decides its steps. A study that uses software as a medical device in participants' care tests a medical device in people. A study that develops or validates the software on data or samples that already exist, without affecting anyone's care, is a study of existing data.

|---|---|---|
| What happens | Participants are diagnosed, treated or managed with the help of the software during the study. | The software runs on records, images, signals or samples collected earlier, for care or for another study. |
| Health Canada | Investigational testing of a medical device. | Ethics review and MUHC Authorization | Required before the study begins. | Required before the study begins. |
| Privacy review | A privacy impact assessment may be required for software that the study acquires, develops or alters to collect, keep, use, communicate or destroy participants' information, or that sends it outside Quebec. | A privacy impact assessment and MUHC Authorization are required to use identifiable health records without consent. |
| Training level | Level II, as a medical device study. | Ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) which level applies. |

## Test software as a medical device in participants

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span></div>

Testing software as a medical device in participants is a medical device study, at the RI-MUHC's [training Level II](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs). Every member of the team should hold Level II training, or a higher level, before signing the study's Task Delegation Log. [Medical Device and Surgery Studies](/paths/level-ii) gives the steps from planning to close-out.

Before the study begins, it needs the approval of the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>. Where the study needs Health Canada's authorization, the Personne mandatée waits for it before issuing MUHC Authorization.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

Research with medical devices must comply with Health Canada's Medical Devices Regulations and should follow the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) standard ISO 14155, on good clinical practice in clinical investigations of medical devices. Privileges are required to lead a study that Health Canada regulates with an investigational medical device.

### Know what each class means for a study

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span></div>

Health Canada regulates the import or sale of a medical device for investigational testing involving human subjects. A device that a manufacturer provides to an investigator free of charge counts as sold. The <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">investigational testing authorization (ITA)</Tooltip> it needs depends on its class:

| Class | Health Canada's authorization | Before the study starts |
|---|---|---|
| Class I | No ITA. The manufacturer or importer must still possess the records the Medical Devices Regulations require, including a written undertaking from each qualified investigator. | The REB's approval and MUHC Authorization. |
| Class II | An ITA if the software is not licensed in Canada, or if licensed software is used outside its licensed indications in a study that the manufacturer sponsors or that is meant to support a licence application. | The REB's approval, which must be obtained before the study starts but need not be sent to Health Canada. MUHC Authorization after the ITA. |
| Class III or IV | An ITA in the same cases as Class II, with a fuller application, including a risk assessment. Health Canada may issue the authorization before the REB has approved the study. | The REB's approval, which must be obtained and sent to Health Canada before the testing begins. MUHC Authorization after the ITA. |

[Check which applies to your study](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization), including the cases that need no ITA. Licensed software used within its licensed indications needs none. If you are unsure whether your study needs an ITA, ask Health Canada's [Investigational Testing division](mailto:it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca).

[Only the manufacturer or the importer can apply for an ITA](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#only-the-manufacturer-or-importer-can-apply). If you or the MUHC developed the software, [whoever designed, owns or modifies a device can become its manufacturer](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#consequences-for-academic-device-research). If you sponsor the study yourself, [add the device steps](/kb/planning/iit-startup#add-the-steps-for-a-device-trial) to its set-up.

No ITA is needed where there is no sale of the device.

Health Canada considers that no sale occurs if the development, manufacture and testing of the device take place within a single corporate entity. The testing must then be limited to use on-site, at that entity, at a single institution and solely by the legal manufacturer. A physician who is the legal manufacturer of a device has a [similar case](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization). Ask the Investigational Testing division whether your study fits either case before you rely on it.

## Develop or validate software on existing data or samples

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Retrospective and biobank studies (Level V)</span></div>

Many software projects start from data that already exist: health records, images, signals or samples collected for care or for another study. The *Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2) defines research as an undertaking intended to extend knowledge through a disciplined inquiry or systematic investigation. A project of this kind that is research is research involving humans.

TCPS 2 does not count quality assurance studies, quality improvement studies or program evaluation activities as research when they are used only for assessment, management or improvement. TCPS 2 counts as human participants the people whose data or biological materials are relevant to answering the research question, as well as the people who respond to the researcher's interventions or questions.

Research involving living human participants or human biological materials requires the approval of a research ethics board before it begins. At the MUHC, [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) require REB approval for a retrospective study](/sops/cr-008#5-4-1). Elsewhere, TCPS 2 requires no REB review for research that relies only on the secondary use of anonymous information or anonymous biological materials, as long as data linkage, recording or the dissemination of results does not generate identifiable information.

When in doubt about whether TCPS 2 applies to your project, you must seek the opinion of the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca), which makes the final decision on exemption from review.

Using information first collected for another purpose to develop or train software is a [secondary use](/kb/planning/ai-in-clinical-research#plan-a-project-that-develops-or-tests-an-ai-tool). To use identifiable health records held by the MUHC without consent, [the study needs the REB's approval, a privacy impact assessment and MUHC Authorization](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent), which includes access to the records. [Retrospective and Biobank Studies](/paths/retrospective) gives the steps of a study of existing data.

Health Canada's investigational testing rules cover devices imported or sold for testing involving human subjects. Ask Health Canada's [Investigational Testing division](mailto:it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca) whether a study that only runs software on existing data needs an ITA. Which training level such a study needs is a question for the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca).

## Test software that is not a medical device

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Retrospective and biobank studies (Level V)</span></div>

Software that is not a medical device, such as a wellness or coaching app, can still be the intervention or the data source of a study with human participants. A study of that software needs the REB's approval and MUHC Authorization like any other. A prospective study of such software, which Health Canada does not regulate, is at [training Level IV](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs).

Software that a study acquires, develops or alters to collect, keep, use, communicate or destroy participants' information [may require a privacy impact assessment](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed). So may sending participants' information outside Quebec. Ask the MUHC's [Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) whether your study needs a privacy impact assessment, the EFVP (évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée).

If your study will use study-specific software, the study team should ask [RI-MUHC IT](mailto:ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca) to evaluate it, through IT Rescue on the RI-MUHC Portal. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-004#5-1-3) require the sponsor or sponsor-investigator to ensure that all electronic software used in the study has been validated and complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR Part 11), the US rule on electronic records.
