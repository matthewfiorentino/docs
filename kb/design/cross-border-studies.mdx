Multi-centric studies increasingly span national borders — a Canadian site participating in a US-sponsored trial, a European collaboration, or a study with sites across multiple regulatory jurisdictions. This page covers the regulatory, privacy, and logistical considerations specific to cross-border research. For multi-site studies within Quebec, see [Multi-Centric Studies in Quebec](/kb/design/multi-centric).

## Regulatory requirements

### Canada and the United States

The sponsor must file a Clinical Trial Application (CTA) with Health Canada for a Canadian site, whether or not it holds a US Investigational New Drug application (IND) with the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA). The two are independent regulatory submissions:

- **CTA to Health Canada:** Required under Part C, Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations. Health Canada reviews it within a 30-day default period and typically issues a No Objection Letter (NOL).
- **IND to the FDA:** A separate submission governed by 21 CFR Part 312. Canadian data generated under a Health Canada CTA can be submitted to the FDA in support of a US IND.

At the MUHC, the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) have the sponsor obtain Health Canada's authorization first and send its letter to the site, which includes it in the Nagano submission for ethics review, although the order can differ for natural health products and some devices.

### Canada and the European Union

Studies involving EU sites operate under the EU Clinical Trials Regulation (CTR 536/2014), which differs from the Canadian framework in several ways — centralized authorization through CTIS (Clinical Trials Information System), a single assessment process across member states, and distinct timelines. Canadian sites are not subject to EU regulation, but the sponsor may impose EU-aligned requirements across all sites to maintain consistency.

### Quebec within Canada

Quebec's ethics review system is distinct from other provinces. The MSSS multicentre research ethics review mechanism (in effect since 2015) provides a single ethics review by a CER évaluateur (REB of Record) for all public health network sites in Quebec, with each site conducting a separate site-specific assessment (examen de convenance).

Clinical Trials Ontario (CTO) operates a streamlined single-REB review model for multi-site studies within Ontario. CTO and the Quebec MSSS mechanism are not interoperable — a study spanning both provinces requires separate ethics processes for Ontario sites (CTO) and Quebec sites (MSSS mechanism).

## Data transfer and privacy

Cross-border data transfer is one of the most complex aspects of international studies. Three privacy frameworks commonly intersect:

### PIPEDA (federal)

Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA), the federal private-sector privacy law, generally doesn't apply to hospitals, which provincial laws cover, though it may apply in certain situations, such as to a private sponsor or service provider. Where it applies, it requires organizations to ensure "comparable" protection through contractual measures when transferring personal information outside Canada, and participants must be informed that their data may be processed abroad.

### Quebec's privacy laws

Quebec's privacy legislation sets its own requirements for transfers outside Quebec:

- A **privacy impact assessment (PIA)** may be required before personal information is transferred outside Quebec; at the MUHC, write to efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca to learn whether your study needs one
- A **written agreement** with the recipient organization is required
- The **adequacy of the receiving jurisdiction's legal framework** must be assessed on a case-by-case basis — Quebec does not maintain a list of "adequate" jurisdictions
- These requirements apply even for transfers to other Canadian provinces

<Warning>
Do not assume PIPEDA compliance covers Quebec-specific requirements. Quebec's privacy laws have their own rules on transfers outside Quebec, and for health information held by the MUHC, Law 5 sets them. When planning data transfers outside Quebec, write to efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca.
</Warning>

### GDPR (EU collaboration)

When collaborating with European sites, both GDPR and Canadian privacy requirements must be satisfied. Canada's PIPEDA has GDPR adequacy status, meaning EU-to-Canada transfers under PIPEDA are generally permitted. However, Quebec's Law 25 has not separately received EU adequacy status — the practical impact of this distinction depends on the specific data flows and should be assessed with legal counsel.

## Investigational product shipment

Importing clinical trial drugs into Canada is regulated under the Food and Drugs Act. Requirements for cross-border IP shipment:

- A copy of the **No Objection Letter (NOL)** from Health Canada must accompany the drug shipment
- The shipment may only be sent to a **qualified investigator** for use in an authorized clinical trial
- Documentation accompanying the shipment must include: the NOL, product labelling, and shipping records
- Diversion into the commercial market is prohibited
- CBSA Memorandum D19-9-1 governs the administration of health product imports at the border

For shipments involving temperature-sensitive IP, ensure the cold chain is maintained throughout customs clearance. Plan for potential customs delays — particularly for first-time shipments to a new site or when using a new shipping route.

### Exporting specimens

Biological specimens shipped internationally are subject to both Canadian export requirements (Transportation of Dangerous Goods Act for ground transport, IATA regulations for air transport) and the importing country's requirements. See [Management of Biological Specimens](/kb/conduct/study-operations/biological-specimens) for TDG/IATA certification requirements.

## Practical considerations

| Area | What to plan for |
|---|---|
| **Time zones** | Monitoring visits, sponsor calls, and safety reporting deadlines may reference a different time zone. Confirm which timezone governs reporting clocks. |
| **Language** | Quebec consent forms must be available in French. International sponsors may not have French translations ready — build translation time into the timeline. |
| **Currency** | Clinical trial budgets with international sponsors are often denominated in USD or EUR. Address currency risk and conversion timing in the clinical trial agreement. |
| **Insurance** | Confirm the sponsor's clinical trial insurance covers Canadian sites and is valid under Quebec law. |
| **Regulatory reporting** | The sponsor reports to Health Canada and to other jurisdictions' regulators; the site reports to the sponsor and the REB, and in a trial of a device sold or imported for investigational testing, the qualified investigator also reports certain incidents to Health Canada and to the manufacturer or importer ([Reportable Events](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines)). Confirm that the sponsor's safety reporting covers all jurisdictions. |
