When the MUHC is one site of a study that also runs in other provinces or countries, each country's regulator, each site's research ethics board (REB) and each jurisdiction's privacy law still applies to its own part. A US-sponsored trial needs Health Canada's authorization for its Canadian sites, and participants' information that leaves Quebec needs Quebec's own safeguards. For several sites within Quebec's public network, see [Multi-Centric Studies in Quebec](/kb/design/multi-centric).

## Get Health Canada authorization for the Canadian sites

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

For a drug trial, the sponsor files a <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip> with Health Canada for the Canadian sites, unless the trial uses a marketed drug within the conditions of its notice of compliance or its drug identification number. The CTA is needed whether or not the sponsor holds an Investigational New Drug application (IND) with the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA). The two are separate submissions:

- The CTA is made under [Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/page-111.html). The sponsor may sell or import the drug for the trial at a site once all of these conditions are met:
  - Health Canada has not sent a notice of objection within 30 days after it received the application. Health Canada typically issues a No Objection Letter (NOL) when it raises no objection.
  - For that site, an REB has approved the protocol and the informed consent form.
  - Health Canada has the site's information, such as its qualified investigator and its REB, if the sponsor didn't give it with the application.
- The IND is a separate submission to the FDA, governed by Part 312 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR). Canadian data generated under a Health Canada CTA can be submitted to the FDA in support of a US IND.

The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) have the sponsor [obtain Health Canada's authorization first](/sops/cr-007#5-1-1) and send its letter to the site, which includes it in the Nagano submission for ethics review. The order can differ for natural health products and some devices: [Study Submission and Review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization) gives the order for each.

## Know how EU rules reach a Canadian site

Studies with sites in the European Union come under the EU's Clinical Trials Regulation (Regulation (EU) No 536/2014). It authorizes trials through a central portal, the Clinical Trials Information System (CTIS), with one assessment across member states. Canadian sites are not subject to the EU regulation, but a sponsor may apply EU-aligned requirements to every site for consistency.

## Submit the Quebec sites in Nagano

Quebec's ethics review differs from other provinces'. In a pan-Canadian study, submit the sites outside Quebec under their own jurisdictions' rules, and submit the Quebec sites in Nagano. A streamlined review elsewhere, such as Clinical Trials Ontario's for Ontario sites or [CanReview](https://canreview.ca/)'s for the sites that take part in it, doesn't replace the Nagano submission.

Several sites within Quebec's public network share one ethics review by a reviewing REB, and each site does its own institutional feasibility review: [Multi-Centric Studies in Quebec](/kb/design/multi-centric) covers it.

## Protect participant information that leaves Quebec

Participants' information held by the MUHC is health and social services information, and Law 5, the [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1), sets the rules for sending it outside Quebec. These rules apply to other Canadian provinces too.

- A privacy impact assessment (*évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée*, EFVP) is done before the information leaves Quebec. It weighs, among other things, the legal framework of the place it goes to.
- The information goes outside Quebec only if the assessment shows that it will be adequately protected there.
- A written agreement sets the terms. For research access to records without consent, it takes the assessment's results into account.

Quebec does not maintain a list of jurisdictions it considers adequate, so each transfer is assessed case by case.

[Privacy and the EFVP](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed) says when a study needs an EFVP. Write to the [EFVP office](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) when you plan to send participants' information outside Quebec.

Canada's federal private-sector privacy law, the [Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) (PIPEDA), generally doesn't apply to hospitals, which provincial laws cover. It may apply in certain situations, such as to a private sponsor or service provider.

Where PIPEDA applies, it requires organizations to use contractual or other means to provide a comparable level of protection when a third party processes personal information, including outside Canada. Where PIPEDA applies, organizations must also make it plain to participants, in clear and understandable language, that their information may be processed in a foreign country and may be accessible to that country's law enforcement and national security authorities.{/* Closed in b154 by the second reader (the fact check's rows CB20 and CB21; the question was for the EFVP office): the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada's Guidelines for processing personal data across borders (saved, _review/2026-11/batches/b154-3b-design/factcheck/web/opc-crossborder-guidelines.txt), quoting PIPEDA's Principle 4.1.3: "The organization shall use contractual or other means to provide a comparable level of protection while the information is being processed by a third party."; "PIPEDA does not distinguish between domestic and international transfers of data."; "Organizations need to make it plain to individuals that their information may be processed in a foreign country". ## Work with European sites under the GDPR

When a study has European sites, both the EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and Canadian privacy requirements apply. The EU recognizes PIPEDA as giving adequate protection, so transfers from the EU to organizations covered by PIPEDA are generally allowed. Quebec's laws have not separately received that recognition. How much this matters depends on the study's data flows: ask the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca), which handles the RI-MUHC's legal questions on research agreements, including data transfer agreements.

## Import the investigational drug

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span></div>

The Food and Drugs Act and its regulations govern importing drugs for a clinical trial into Canada. A person who imports a drug for a clinical trial must be authorized under Division 5 and, if the drug is imported, have a representative in Canada responsible for its sale. The sponsor or a person it designates is the importer, and individual investigators at Canadian sites may serve as importers.

For a shipment, Health Canada's guidance says:

- A No Objection Letter should be provided at the time of importation, to facilitate shipment and to show compliance with the regulations.
- A copy of the importer's details from Appendix 1 of the HC/SC 3011 form should be included with the shipment, along with the NOL.
- Any delegation of importation duties to third parties should be set out in a written agreement. Systems must be in place for monitoring, storage conditions, transport and disposition of the drug, and the sponsor remains responsible for its handling and storage.

The shipment may be sent only to a qualified investigator, for use in an authorized clinical trial, and diverting the drug into the commercial market is prohibited. The Canada Border Services Agency's Memorandum D19-9-1 governs the administration of health product imports at the border.

For a temperature-sensitive product, plan for the cold chain to hold through customs clearance, and allow for customs delays, especially for a first shipment to a new site or by a new route.

## Ship biological specimens abroad

Biological specimens shipped to another country follow Canada's requirements for transport, the Transportation of Dangerous Goods regulations for ground transport and the International Air Transport Association's (IATA) regulations for air transport, and the importing country's requirements. [Management of Biological Specimens](/kb/conduct/study-operations/biological-specimens) covers the training and certification these shipments need.

## Plan for the practical differences

Some points of an international study are worth settling with the sponsor early:

| Point | What to consider |
|---|---|
| Time zones | Monitoring visits, sponsor calls and safety reporting deadlines may be set in another time zone. Consider confirming which time zone governs the reporting clocks. |
| Language | At the MUHC, a consent form needs [an English and a French version](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form), unless the REB accepts a justification. An international sponsor may not have French translations ready, so allow time for translation. |
| Currency | Budgets with international sponsors are often in US dollars or euros. Consider addressing currency risk and the timing of conversion in the clinical trial agreement. |
| Insurance | Consider confirming that the sponsor's clinical trial insurance covers Canadian sites and is valid under Quebec law. |
| Regulatory reporting | The sponsor reports to Health Canada and to other countries' regulators. The site reports to the sponsor and the REB. In a trial of a device sold or imported for investigational testing, the qualified investigator also reports certain incidents to Health Canada and to the manufacturer or importer ([Reportable Events](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines)). Consider confirming that the sponsor's safety reporting covers every jurisdiction. |
