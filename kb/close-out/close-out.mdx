## Preparing for the close-out visit

Close-out is a sequence of steps, not a single event. Consider starting to prepare well before the last participant's last visit, so that close-out doesn't become months of reconciliation.

Before the sponsor or the monitor conducts the <Tooltip tip="The last monitoring visit, arranged by the sponsor or sponsor-investigator and conducted by a monitor at the site, to make sure that every required aspect of closing the study has been addressed before database lock." headline="Close-Out Visit (COV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cov">close-out visit (COV)</Tooltip>, the site must be ready on all of the following:
- The <Tooltip tip="The qualified or principal investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines, so that the conduct and integrity of the study and the safety of participants at the site can be verified." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file (ISF)</Tooltip> has been reviewed for completeness, and any remaining items resolved or documented.
- The last participant has completed the last scheduled visit, and the associated <Tooltip tip="A printed, optical or electronic document designed to record all the protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor on each trial participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">case report forms (CRFs)</Tooltip> are complete.
- All CRFs are complete and consistent with the source documents. Any corrections, additions or deletions are made, dated, explained where needed and initialled by the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, or a delegate.
- The principal investigator has reviewed, signed and dated the completed CRFs.
- All monitoring follow-up letters are on file, initialled and dated by the principal investigator.
- The data queries from previous monitoring visits are resolved.
- The study budget is reconciled, and any payments still owed to participants are made.
- Pharmacy, laboratory, diagnostic imaging and any other units involved in study procedures have been told of the upcoming close-out visit, so that they can prepare their own reconciliation steps.
- All study logs are complete, the Task Delegation Log included.

<Warning>
The <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> should now record the end dates of all team members, except those of the principal investigator and of the <Tooltip tip="A nurse, health professional or other qualified member of the research team who handles most of the administrative responsibilities and day-to-day activities of a clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">clinical research coordinator</Tooltip> who answers queries. The log should also carry the principal investigator's signature and date to undelegate the team members. Submit the Closure of Research Project form in Nagano only once all post-close-out activities have been reconciled and all team members have been undelegated on the log.
</Warning>

## The close-out visit

The COV is conducted by the sponsor, the sponsor-investigator or the monitor. During the visit, the monitor verifies, as required:
- All adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) are documented and reported correctly.
- External expedited reports, such as reports of suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs) or medical device incidents, are initialled and dated by the principal investigator and submitted to the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip>.
- CRFs are consistent with the source data.
- All corrections to source documents and CRFs are properly dated and initialled.
- Open questions from earlier monitoring, audits or inspections are resolved.
- The essential study documents are complete.
- All documents that require the principal investigator's documented oversight are signed or initialled and dated.

Discuss with the monitor any follow-up the study still needs, such as remaining action items, the participants' follow-up, data clarifications that may arise and record retention.

Final data queries are resolved during or immediately after the visit. Once all queries are resolved and finalized, the electronic CRF (eCRF) should be locked and signed by the principal investigator. The lock is the formal data freeze.

After the COV, the monitor sends a COV Notification and COV Report to your site. Review them, initial any required acknowledgements, file them in the ISF, and respond to any remaining action items. A copy of the COV report is kept in the site's ISF, and the original goes to the sponsor for the <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's or sponsor-investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines across all the study's sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">trial master file (TMF)</Tooltip>.

After the visit, ask the sponsor or sponsor-investigator when to expect any payments still owed and the final report. Ask also about its publication plans, its feedback on the site's performance, and any audits or regulatory inspections it plans or knows of.

In a sponsor-investigator study, the sponsor-investigator and the monitor [should refer to SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-013) for the template of the monitor's close-out visit report and of the close-out notification to sites.

## Supply reconciliation and archiving

After the COV, [all study-related supplies are returned or destroyed](/sops/cr-016#5-3), as arranged with the sponsor or sponsor-investigator. In a study with an <Tooltip tip="A pharmaceutical form of an active ingredient or placebo, a medical device or a natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product (IP)</Tooltip>, every unit must be fully accounted for in the accountability log, whether it was received, dispensed, returned, quarantined or destroyed.

Keep the accountability log with the essential study documents for the required archiving period. If the IP is to be destroyed, arrange its destruction with the MUHC Research Pharmacy, following the pharmacy's procedures, and send the destruction documentation to the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, with a copy for the ISF.

Randomization codes, unused CRFs, equipment and biological specimens are returned to the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, as the protocol or written instructions set. If the sponsor instructs it, unused CRFs may instead be destroyed on site, following local procedures for destroying confidential documents.

The ISF is then prepared for archiving, usually after the database is locked. Before you transfer the ISF to the archiving service, review it one final time for completeness, and confirm the archive location. The archive is typically Iron Mountain, which the RI-MUHC highly recommends for long-term retention. Make sure that the ISF documents contain no information that names a participant. Documents that do, such as an email about an SAE, should be redacted before archiving.

The principal investigator or a delegate should inform the archiving service of the required retention period:

| Study type | Retention period | Starts from |
|---|---|---|
| Health Canada-regulated studies (drug, device, natural health product) in which at least one participant signed a consent form, screen failures included | 15 years | Completion of the study |
| All other studies | 7 years, set by the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs). Sponsors or funders may require longer. | Date of publication of results |

Some sponsors ask for 25 years, the European Medicines Agency's requirement. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator must be informed of the archive location. Maintain an archiving log recording the archive location, box contents, barcodes, retention period and retrieval process. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs set the full archiving process](/sops/cr-015#5-3) and the structure of the ISF.

## Safety data reconciliation and database lock

Before the Nagano closure form is submitted, the study's safety data should be fully reconciled, all queries resolved and the eCRF locked and signed by the principal investigator. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs set this order](/sops/cr-016#5-2-5). Safety data reconciliation is separate from supply reconciliation and archiving.

### What safety reconciliation involves

At close-out, the following must be reviewed and confirmed before the database is locked:
- Every adverse event (AE) has a documented resolution or, if it is still ongoing, follow-up that complies with the protocol and the consent form.
- All SAEs have been reported to the sponsor, and any follow-up submissions the sponsor requires are complete.
- External SUSAR reports received from the sponsor are initialled and dated by the principal investigator, filed in the ISF and assessed for the REB's reporting requirements.
- The safety narrative in the final CRF entries is consistent with the source documents.

In a sponsor-investigator study, conduct a final reconciliation of the safety database before the database is locked. Confirm that all SAEs, including any events identified after the last participant's last visit, have been captured, assessed and reported in the required Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences (CIOMS) I format.

### Database lock

Database lock is the formal data freeze. After the lock, no data entry or correction is permitted without a documented data correction procedure. The lock follows this sequence:

1. All remaining data queries from the monitor are resolved.
2. The principal investigator conducts a final data review for completeness and consistency.
3. The eCRF is locked by the sponsor or the data management team.
4. The principal investigator signs the final eCRF, or a data certification as the study requires.

If your team manages the study's database and an error found after the lock means it has to be unlocked, [obtain the appropriate authorizations first](/sops/cr-107#5-3), and document the times and dates of the unlock and the relock.

The eCRF should be locked before the Nagano closure form is submitted. One of the closure conditions is that all data collection is complete, and an unlocked database does not meet it.

## Notifying the REB and Health Canada

The completion or early termination of a study [must be reported](/sops/cr-016#5-5-1) to the REB. The principal investigator [must report the change in study status](/sops/cr-026#5-6-2) to the REB in a timely fashion, ideally within 30 days of the COV. Submit the Closure of Research Project form (F10) in Nagano.

[The sponsor-investigator or the principal investigator should submit the form](/sops/cr-016#5-5-2) when all of these conditions are met:
- No participants remain involved at the site.
- Participants are no longer exposed to immediate research risks.
- All data collection is complete.
- All study supplies and investigational product have been returned or destroyed on site, if applicable.
- The close-out activities of the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, the investigator and the monitor, where there are any, are complete.

After the REB has been told of the closure, the site should send a copy of the correspondence with the REB to the sponsor or monitor, and inform the research team and the departments involved, such as pharmacy, laboratory and diagnostic imaging, of the closure. Once the sponsor has written the Clinical Study Report, it should also be submitted to the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca).

<Warning>
Once the Nagano study status is changed to "Closed", no further forms can be submitted. Documents the REB needs to acknowledge after closure, such as a Clinical Study Report, can only be submitted through the Nagano Discussion feature. Do not submit the closure form until all the conditions are met.
</Warning>

If the sponsor requests more data after the study is closed, [request the REB's approval through Nagano Discussion](/sops/cr-016#5-5-2). The REB sets the conditions of the request when it reviews it. [The REB requires](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) the consent of the patient or their legal representative, or the authorization of the MUHC's Director of Professional Services, for access to patients' records for that request.

### Complete an End of Participation in Research Form

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span></div>

For a study with an investigational product, [an End of Participation in Research Form must be completed](/sops/cr-016#5-5-4) for every participant and sent to Medical Records for scanning into the participant's electronic medical record. The original form is filed with the source documents.

### Tell Health Canada that the study has ended

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

Where a <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip> or an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)</Tooltip> was submitted to Health Canada, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator should also [inform Health Canada that the study has ended](/sops/cr-016#5-4-13).

For a device study for which Health Canada has issued an ITA, [the manufacturer or importer should submit a report of the study to Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/investigational-testing-authorizations-guidance/guidance-document.html#2.8.4) after the investigational testing closes, even if the testing stopped early. Where applicable, the report should be made available to the principal investigator and all investigators for review and comment. The sponsor shall keep records confirming that the report has been provided for review. The report should be provided to the REB.

If the sponsor or sponsor-investigator stops a drug or natural health product trial early, in its entirety or at a site, they must inform Health Canada within 15 days, with the reason and the impact on their proposed or ongoing trials of the product in Canada. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator must also inform all qualified investigators as soon as possible, with the reasons and, in writing, any potential risks to the health of participants or others.

When a sponsor stops a drug or natural health product trial, in its entirety or at a site, it must also stop the sale or importation of the product at each site concerned and take all reasonable measures to recover all unused quantities.

## If the study stops early

If the sponsor, the principal investigator or a health authority suspends or stops the study, [notify the REB and the organization promptly](/sops/cr-026#5-5-1), with a detailed explanation, and file the correspondence with the essential documents. If the sponsor suspends or stops the study, [the REB requires you to notify it immediately](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops), with the reasons.

If the REB has also suspended its approval, resume the study after the sponsor lifts its suspension only once the REB has reviewed the study again and lifted its own suspension.

If the REB suspends or stops the study, [inform the sponsor](/sops/cr-026#5-2-5) and give it a detailed written explanation. A suspension of recruitment should also be communicated to the sponsor and documented in the ISF.

Participants must be told when a clinical trial stops early. When a researcher, a sponsor or another body stops or unblinds all or part of a clinical trial, [the principal investigator has an ethical and a regulatory responsibility to inform the participants and the REB](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html#8) of the discontinuance or unblinding and the reasons for it.

Any risks to participants that may arise from the unexpected closing of a clinical trial must be communicated to the REB and the participants. Indicate the measures that will be taken to mitigate any such risk.

If a clinical trial stops early, [make sure its public registry entry is updated](/kb/close-out/publications#trial-registration).

When a study that is not a clinical trial stops early, consider telling participants promptly. Where an early stop affects participants' ongoing care or follow-up, particularly in a study with an investigational product, consider timing the arrangements for their care so that they are in place before the last contact with each participant ends. Participants may also need [care after a trial that stops early](#plan-care-for-participants-after-the-trial-ends).

Before the REB suspends or terminates its approval of a study, [it must consider the participants already enrolled](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops): how their care will continue, and whether, how and when they will be told of the suspension or termination. When the REB suspends or terminates its approval, no further activities can take place in the study other than the submission of an amendment or of reportable events.

## Multi-centric close-out sequence

When the MUHC is the lead site of a [multi-centric study](/kb/design/multi-centric#close-a-multi-centric-study-in-the-right-order), it should submit its Closure of Research Project form only once every participating site in Quebec's public health and social services network (<Tooltip tip="Quebec's public health and social services network, the réseau de la santé et des services sociaux, whose establishments include the MUHC." headline="RSSS" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#rsss">RSSS</Tooltip>) has submitted its own. Once the lead site submits, the other sites can no longer create new forms or submit documents in Nagano.

Where the MUHC is the lead site and the other sites are elsewhere in Quebec and in Canada, every other participating site must submit its closure report to its own REB before the MUHC submits its closure form.

When the MUHC is a participating site in a [multi-centric study](/kb/design/multi-centric#close-a-multi-centric-study-in-the-right-order), submit the Closure of Research Project form for the MUHC site on the lead site's form, in a timely manner, for the reviewing REB's acknowledgement.

A study with one Quebec site and sites in other provinces or countries is not a multi-centric study. In a study with only one Quebec site whose sponsor-investigator is at the MUHC, the other sites elsewhere in Canada must also submit their closure reports to their own REBs before the MUHC submits its closure form.

## Plan care for participants after the trial ends

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

Some participants still need care when their part in a trial ends. An adverse event may not have resolved, or a treatment may have helped them. Some of that care is specified in the protocol and explained in the consent form before the trial starts. Participants may need that care whether the trial ends as planned or [stops early](#if-the-study-stops-early).

### Ensure care for adverse events after participation

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span></div>

For a clinical trial of a drug or a biological product, the [ICH](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) states that the investigator and the institution should ensure that adequate medical care is provided to a participant for any adverse event related to the trial, including clinically significant laboratory values, during and following their participation.

The ICH also states that the investigator should follow the protocol and any other protocol-related documents when a participant in a trial of a drug or a biological product reaches the routine end of the trial, stops the investigational product, withdraws or is discontinued from the trial.

### Plan continued access to the investigational product

Before approving a study, [the REB considers](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) whether there will be adequate provisions, as appropriate, for continued access to the study product or an adequate replacement after the research is complete.

In a phase II drug trial, the [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html#2) requires researchers to:
- Give participants details on access to the new drug upon trial completion, as part of the consent process.
- Make reasonable efforts to secure continued access to the drug after the trial for the participants for whom it appears to be beneficial.

For a phase III drug trial, TCPS 2's rule is that researchers and the REB should address continuing access to the experimental therapy after the trial closes. If the treatment benefits participants and is safe, the proposal should state whether it will continue to be provided and under what conditions.

The World Medical Association (WMA) holds that the principles of its [*Declaration of Helsinki*](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/), a statement of ethical principles for medical research involving human participants, should be upheld by everyone involved in medical research. For a clinical trial, the WMA holds that:
- Sponsors and researchers must arrange post-trial provisions before the trial for all participants who still need an intervention identified as beneficial and reasonably safe in the trial.
- The provisions are to be provided by the sponsors and researchers themselves, by health care systems or by governments.
- A research ethics committee must approve any exception.
- The protocol must describe any post-trial provisions.
- Specific information about post-trial provisions must be disclosed to participants as part of informed consent.

### Say in the protocol and the consent form what happens after participation

The protocol and the consent form describe, as applicable, what each participant can expect when their participation ends. In a clinical trial of a drug or a biological product, the ICH states that [the protocol should specify the follow-up for participants who have discontinued the use of the investigational product](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html). The protocol should generally also describe the sequence and duration of all trial periods, including any follow-up.

In a clinical trial of a drug or a biological product, the ICH states that the consent discussion and the consent materials should explain, as applicable, the follow-up procedure for participants who stopped taking the investigational product, withdrew from the trial or were discontinued from it.

[Make sure the consent form has a paragraph](/sops/cr-501#5-1-4) telling the participant that clinical information useful to their continued care will be sent to their treating physician, as per standard of care. The consent form also needs [the participant's consent to inform their treating physician](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form) of their participation.

## Obligations that continue after close-out

Some obligations continue after the COV and the closure form.

The obligation to report safety events continues after the database lock and the closure form, through the follow-up period that the protocol and the <Tooltip tip="The written form that gives a participant the information essential to an informed decision about taking part in a study." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">consent form</Tooltip> define.

If the protocol sets a follow-up period after treatment, SAEs in that period must still be reported to the sponsor, and to the REB if they meet its reporting criteria, even after the closure form has been submitted.

After the study is formally closed, the principal investigator should still inform the sponsor, the REB and, where applicable, the regulatory authorities of any unexpected SAE or medical device incident reasonably thought to be associated with the investigational product.

For a sponsor-investigator drug trial, Health Canada asks for a [Development Safety Update Report (DSUR)](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#development-safety-update-report-dsur) only on request, for its safety monitoring of clinical trials.

The essential study documents in the ISF must remain preserved, organized and accessible for inspection for the full retention period. Even a study that closed years ago can be selected for a Health Canada inspection, and the ISF must be retrievable and inspection-ready throughout. The study team [should have a plan](/sops/cr-016#5-6-1) for how an auditor or inspector can access the study's data and essential documents after closure, including a clear contact point and retrieval process.

If the principal investigator receives notice of an audit or an inspection, they [must inform the sponsor](/sops/cr-016#5-6-4). For a regulatory inspection, they should also inform the REB and [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca).

When the retention period has ended, [do not destroy any files](/sops/cr-015#5-6-1) without consulting the principal investigator and the sponsor or sponsor-investigator. Keep their written permission in the archiving binder.

## Tell the team and participants the results

Once the sponsor or sponsor-investigator confirms that all participating sites are closed and all data analyzed, [the site should inform the research team and participants](/sops/cr-016#5-4-12) about study results and publications, once they are available.

The Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR) sets [requirements for registering the clinical trials it funds and making their results public](/kb/close-out/publications#cihr-funded-trials). Health Canada expects the sponsors of the trials its guidance covers to [submit summary results to a registry](/kb/close-out/publications#health-canada-direction), but the law does not require it.
