## Preparing for the close-out visit

Close-out is a sequence of steps, not a single event. Consider starting to prepare well before the last participant's last visit, so that close-out doesn't become months of reconciliation.

Before the sponsor or the monitor conducts the <Tooltip tip="Final monitoring visit to verify all study closure requirements have been addressed before database lock." headline="Close-Out Visit (COV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cov">close-out visit (COV)</Tooltip>, the site must be ready on all of the following:
- The <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file (ISF)</Tooltip> has been reviewed for completeness, and any remaining items resolved or documented.
- The last participant has completed the last scheduled visit, and the associated <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">case report forms (CRFs)</Tooltip> are complete.
- All CRFs are complete and consistent with the source documents. Any corrections, additions or deletions are made, dated, explained where needed and initialled by the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, or a delegate.
- The principal investigator has reviewed, signed and dated the completed CRFs.
- All monitoring follow-up letters are on file, initialled and dated by the principal investigator.
- The data queries from previous monitoring visits are resolved.
- The study budget is reconciled, and any payments still owed to participants are made.
- Pharmacy, laboratory, diagnostic imaging and any other units involved in study procedures have been told that close-out is approaching, so that they can prepare their own reconciliation steps.
- All study logs are complete, the Task Delegation Log included.

<Warning>
The <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> must be updated with end dates for all team members except the principal investigator and the <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">clinical research coordinator</Tooltip> who remain available for queries after closure. The principal investigator signs and dates the log to undelegate the team members. The log must be complete before the Closure of Research Project form can be submitted in Nagano.
</Warning>

## The close-out visit

The COV is conducted by the sponsor, the sponsor-investigator or the monitor. During the visit, the monitor verifies, as required:
- All adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse events (SAEs) are documented and reported correctly.
- External expedited reports, such as reports of suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (SUSARs) or medical device incidents, are initialled and dated by the principal investigator and submitted to the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip>.
- CRFs are consistent with the source data.
- All corrections to source documents and CRFs are properly dated and initialled.
- Open questions from earlier monitoring, audits or inspections are resolved.
- The essential study documents are complete.
- All documents that require the principal investigator's documented oversight are signed or initialled and dated.

Discuss with the monitor any follow-up the study still needs, such as remaining action items, the participants' follow-up, data clarifications that may arise and record retention.

Final data queries are resolved during or immediately after the visit. Once all queries are resolved and finalized, the electronic CRF (eCRF) should be locked and signed by the principal investigator. The lock is the formal data freeze.

After the COV, the monitor sends a COV Notification and COV Report to your site. Review them, initial any required acknowledgements, file them in the ISF, and respond to any remaining action items. A copy of the COV report is kept in the site's ISF, and the original goes to the sponsor for the <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">trial master file (TMF)</Tooltip>.

After the visit, ask the sponsor or sponsor-investigator when to expect any payments still owed and the final report. Ask also about its publication plans, its feedback on the site's performance, and any audits or regulatory inspections it plans or knows of.

In a sponsor-investigator study, the sponsor-investigator and the monitor [should refer to SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-013) for the template of the monitor's close-out visit report and of the close-out notification to sites.

## Supply reconciliation and archiving

After the COV, [all study-related supplies are returned or destroyed](/sops/cr-016#5-3), as arranged with the sponsor or sponsor-investigator. In a study with an <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product (IP)</Tooltip>, every unit must be fully accounted for in the accountability log, whether it was received, dispensed, returned, quarantined or destroyed.

Keep the accountability log with the essential study documents for the required archiving period. If the IP is to be destroyed, arrange its destruction with the MUHC Research Pharmacy, following the pharmacy's procedures, and send the destruction documentation to the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, with a copy for the ISF.

Randomization codes, unused CRFs, equipment and biological specimens are returned to the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, as the protocol or written instructions set. If the sponsor instructs it, unused CRFs may instead be destroyed on site, following local procedures for destroying confidential documents.

The ISF is then prepared for archiving, usually after the database is locked. Before you transfer the ISF to the archiving service, review it one final time for completeness, and confirm the archive location. The archive is typically Iron Mountain, which the RI-MUHC highly recommends for long-term retention. Make sure that the ISF documents contain no information that names a participant. Documents that do, such as an email about an SAE, should be redacted before archiving.

Inform the archiving service of the required retention period at the time of transfer:

| Study type | Retention period | Starts from |
|---|---|---|
| Health Canada-regulated studies (drug, device, natural health product) in which at least one participant signed a consent form, screen failures included | 15 years | Completion of the study |
| All other studies | 7 years, set by the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs). Sponsors or funders may require longer. | Date of publication of results |

Some sponsors ask for 25 years, the European Medicines Agency's requirement. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator must be informed of the archive location. Maintain an archiving log recording the archive location, box contents, barcodes, retention period and retrieval process. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs set the full archiving process](/sops/cr-015#5-3) and the structure of the ISF.

## Safety data reconciliation and database lock

Before the Nagano closure form is submitted, the study's safety data should be fully reconciled, all queries resolved and the eCRF locked and signed by the principal investigator. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs set this order](/sops/cr-016#5-2-5). Safety data reconciliation is separate from supply reconciliation and archiving.

### What safety reconciliation involves

At close-out, the following must be reviewed and confirmed before the database is locked:
- Every adverse event (AE) has a documented resolution or, if it is still ongoing, follow-up that complies with the protocol and the consent form.
- All SAEs have been reported to the sponsor, and any follow-up submissions the sponsor requires are complete.
- External SUSAR reports received from the sponsor are initialled and dated by the principal investigator, filed in the ISF and assessed for the REB's reporting requirements.
- The safety narrative in the final CRF entries is consistent with the source documents.

In a sponsor-investigator study, conduct a final reconciliation of the safety database before the database is locked. Confirm that all SAEs, including any events identified after the last participant's last visit, have been captured, assessed and reported in the required Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences (CIOMS) I format.

### Database lock

Database lock is the formal data freeze. After the lock, no data entry or correction is permitted without a documented data correction procedure. The lock follows this sequence:

1. All remaining data queries from the monitor are resolved.
2. The principal investigator conducts a final data review for completeness and consistency.
3. The eCRF is locked by the sponsor or the data management team.
4. The principal investigator signs the final eCRF, or a data certification as the study requires.

If your team manages the study's database and an error found after the lock means it has to be unlocked, [obtain the appropriate authorizations first](/sops/cr-107#5-3), and document the times and dates of the unlock and the relock.

The eCRF should be locked before the Nagano closure form is submitted. One of the closure conditions is that all data collection is complete, and an unlocked database does not meet it.

## Notifying the REB and Health Canada

The completion or early termination of a study [must be reported](/sops/cr-016#5-5-1) to the REB. The principal investigator [must report the change in study status](/sops/cr-026#5-6-2) to the REB in a timely fashion, ideally within 30 days of the COV. Submit the Closure of Research Project form (F10) in Nagano.

[The sponsor-investigator or the principal investigator should submit the form](/sops/cr-016#5-5-2) when all of these conditions are met:
- No participants remain involved at the site.
- Participants are no longer exposed to immediate research risks.
- All data collection is complete.
- All study supplies and investigational product have been returned or destroyed on site, if applicable.
- The close-out activities of the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, the investigator and the monitor, where there are any, are complete.

After the REB has been told of the closure, the site should send a copy of the correspondence with the REB to the sponsor or monitor, and inform the research team and the departments involved, such as pharmacy, laboratory and diagnostic imaging, of the closure. Once the sponsor has written the Clinical Study Report, it should also be submitted to the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca).

<Warning>
Once the Nagano study status is changed to "Closed", no further forms can be submitted. Documents the REB needs to acknowledge after closure, such as a Clinical Study Report, can only be submitted through the Nagano Discussion feature. Do not submit the closure form until all the conditions are met.
</Warning>

If the sponsor requests more data after the study is closed, [request the REB's approval through Nagano Discussion](/sops/cr-016#5-5-2). The REB sets the conditions of the request when it reviews it. [The REB requires](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) the consent of the patient or their legal representative, or the authorization of the MUHC's Director of Professional Services, for access to patients' records for that request.

### Complete an End of Participation in Research Form

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span></div>

For a study with an investigational product, [an End of Participation in Research Form must be completed](/sops/cr-016#5-5-4) for every participant and sent to Medical Records for scanning into the participant's electronic medical record. The original form is filed with the source documents.

### Tell Health Canada that the study has ended

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

Where a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip> or an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)</Tooltip> was submitted to Health Canada, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator should also [inform Health Canada that the study has ended](/sops/cr-016#5-4-13).

If the sponsor or sponsor-investigator stops a drug or natural health product trial early, in its entirety or at a site, they must inform Health Canada within 15 days, with the reason and the impact on their proposed or ongoing trials of the product in Canada. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator must also inform all qualified investigators as soon as possible, with the reasons and, in writing, any potential risks to the health of participants or others.

When a sponsor stops a drug or natural health product trial, in its entirety or at a site, it must also stop the sale or importation of the product at each site concerned and take all reasonable measures to recover all unused quantities.

## If the study stops early

If the sponsor, the principal investigator or a health authority suspends or stops the study, [notify the REB and the organization promptly](/sops/cr-026#5-5-1), with a detailed explanation, and file the correspondence with the essential documents. If the sponsor suspends or stops the study, [the REB requires you to notify it immediately](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops), with the reasons.

If the REB suspends or stops the study, [inform the sponsor](/sops/cr-026#5-2-5) and give it a detailed written explanation. A suspension of recruitment should also be communicated to the sponsor and documented in the ISF.

If a drug trial is terminated or suspended early for any reason, the qualified investigator and the institution [should promptly inform all participants and ensure their appropriate care and follow-up](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100/discontinuance-suspension-cancellation.html). In a drug or natural health product trial that the sponsor stops, at the site or in its entirety, the <Tooltip tip="The person responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a drug or natural health product trial at a clinical trial site, who is entitled to provide health care under the laws of the province where the site is located." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">qualified investigator</Tooltip> must inform the participants and the REB immediately, give them the reasons, and advise them in writing of any potential risks to the health of participants or others.

In other studies that stop early, consider telling participants promptly. Where the termination affects participants' ongoing care or follow-up, particularly in studies with investigational products, consider having arrangements for continuity of care in place before the last contact with each participant ends.

## Multi-centric close-out sequence

For [multi-centric studies](/kb/design/multi-centric) where the MUHC REB is the reviewing REB and the MUHC is the lead site, the closure sequence is strictly ordered.

Each participating site in Quebec's public health and social services network (<Tooltip tip="Quebec's public health and social services network (Réseau de la santé et des services sociaux), including the RI-MUHC." headline="RSSS" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#rsss">RSSS</Tooltip>) submits its own Closure of Research Project form, and the MUHC lead site should submit its closure form only once all of them have.

Where the MUHC is the lead site and the other sites are elsewhere in Quebec and in Canada, every other participating site must submit its closure report to its own REB before the MUHC submits its closure form. Where the MUHC is a participating site, it submits its own Closure of Research Project form, using the lead site's form, in a timely manner.

A study with only one Quebec site is not multi-centric, whether or not it has sites elsewhere in Canada. Submit its Closure of Research Project form in Nagano. If the sponsor-investigator is at the MUHC and other sites are elsewhere in Canada, those sites must submit their closure reports to their own REBs before the MUHC submits its closure form.

Coordinate closure across all participating sites, and confirm that each has submitted its closure form before you submit your own.

<Warning>
Once the lead site submits its closure form, the participating sites can no longer create new forms or submit documents in Nagano. A lead site that submits its closure form too early locks the remaining sites out.
</Warning>

## Obligations that continue after close-out

Some obligations continue after the COV and the closure form.

The obligation to report safety events continues after the database lock and the closure form, through the follow-up period that the protocol and the <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">consent form</Tooltip> define.

If the protocol sets a follow-up period after treatment, SAEs in that period must still be reported to the sponsor, and to the REB if they meet its reporting criteria, even after the closure form has been submitted.

After the study is formally closed, the principal investigator should still inform the sponsor, the REB and, where applicable, the regulatory authorities of any unexpected SAE or medical device incident reasonably thought to be associated with the investigational product.

For a sponsor-investigator drug trial, Health Canada asks for a [Development Safety Update Report (DSUR)](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#development-safety-update-report-dsur) only on request, for its safety monitoring of clinical trials.

The essential study documents in the ISF must remain preserved, organized and accessible for inspection for the full retention period. Even a study that closed years ago can be selected for a Health Canada inspection, and the ISF must be retrievable and inspection-ready throughout. The study team [should have a plan](/sops/cr-016#5-6-1) for how an auditor or inspector can access the study's data and essential documents after closure, including a clear contact point and retrieval process.

If the principal investigator receives notice of an audit or an inspection, they [must inform the sponsor](/sops/cr-016#5-6-4). For a regulatory inspection, they should also inform the REB and [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca).

When the retention period has ended, [do not destroy any files](/sops/cr-015#5-6-1) without consulting the principal investigator and the sponsor or sponsor-investigator. Keep their written permission in the archiving binder.

## Publications and authorship obligations

The federal research agencies require researchers to [disseminate a study's results publicly](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter4-chapitre4.html), in a timely manner and without undue restriction. Public dissemination is an ethical obligation.

For a clinical trial whose grant from the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR) was funded on or after January 1, 2022, [CIHR requires its summary results to be made public](/kb/close-out/publications#cihr-funded-trials). Health Canada expects sponsors of drug and natural health product trials in phases 1 to 3, and of Class II to IV device trials, [to submit summary results to a registry](/kb/close-out/publications#health-canada-direction), but the law does not require it.

Once the sponsor confirms that all sites are closed and all data analyzed, the site should inform the research team and participants about study results and publications.

[Publications, Authorship, and Results Disclosure](/kb/close-out/publications) covers authorship criteria, sponsors' publication rights and trial registration requirements.
