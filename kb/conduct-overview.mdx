Running a clinical study at the MUHC involves more than following the protocol and meeting regulatory deadlines. It means operating within a specific institutional environment — Quebec provincial law, MUHC policies, and a set of practical realities that <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> training alone does not cover. This section explains the layers of obligation that apply throughout an active study and how they fit together.

## The regulatory and institutional layers

Every requirement in clinical research at the MUHC comes from one or more layers of obligation. These layers are additive, not competing — meeting GCP requirements does not satisfy the provincial consent requirements, and meeting provincial requirements does not automatically satisfy MUHC institutional policies. Each layer applies independently.

| Layer | Governing frameworks | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| International | ICH GCP E6(R3), Declaration of Helsinki, ISO 14155 | The baseline for all clinical research worldwide. Participant protection, data integrity, monitoring, and reporting obligations. Covered by GCP and ISO training. |
| Federal (Canada) | Health Canada Food and Drug Regulations (Division 5), Natural Health Products Regulations, Medical Devices Regulations, PIPEDA, TCPS2 | <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">CTA</Tooltip>/<Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip> submission and authorization, <Tooltip tip="Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction — unexpected in nature, severity, or frequency; must be reported to Health Canada and the REB." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip> reporting timelines, clinical trial registration. |
| Provincial (Quebec) | Civil Code of Quebec, Act Respecting Health and Social Services Information (<Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act to modernize personal information protection law (in force Sept 2023). Requires EFVP before sharing personal data without consent." headline="Law 25 / Loi 25" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#law-25">Law 25</Tooltip>/<Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information (key provisions July 2024). Governs researcher access to health data through RSSS institutions." headline="Law 5 / Loi 5" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#law-5">Law 5</Tooltip>), MSSS research ethics framework | Consent rules under the Civil Code differ from common law provinces. Law 25 triggers the EFVP for studies accessing identifiable health data. MSSS mandatory paragraphs must appear in consent forms. |
| Institutional (MUHC) | MUHC research policies, <Tooltip tip="Individual formally authorized to issue the final written authorization for each research project at a Quebec RSSS institution." headline="Personne mandatée (PFM)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> authorization requirements, MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement, OPQ pharmacy regulations | Every team member must sign the MUHC Confidentiality Agreement before accessing participant records. Investigational products must be dispensed by an MUHC pharmacist. <Tooltip tip="Institutional authorization allowing a study to proceed at the RI-MUHC. Issued after ethics approval and feasibility review." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> from the Personne mandatée is required before any study procedures begin — <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approval alone is not sufficient. |

<Note>
In Quebec, the Civil Code governs who can consent to research and under what conditions. The treating physician cannot obtain a participant's consent. The age of consent for research is 18. Mandatory provincial paragraphs must appear in every REB-approved consent form — their absence is grounds for outright rejection at submission.
</Note>

## The arc of study conduct at the MUHC

Two tracks run in parallel throughout a study: a sequential lifecycle from activation to close-out, and a set of continuous obligations that apply at every stage.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Foundations (throughout)">
    Privacy and data integrity obligations apply throughout the entire study, not at a specific stage. The MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement must be signed before any team member accesses participant records. ALCOA+ data standards apply from the first source document entry. The retention clock starts at study closure — 15 years for Health Canada-regulated studies, 7 years for non-regulated ones.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Study activation (before first participant)">
    A study that has received REB approval and MUHC Authorization is authorized to run, but is not running yet. Before any participant can be approached, four conditions must all be confirmed in writing: REB approval, MUHC Authorization letter from the Personne mandatée, a completed <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip> with the full team trained and the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> signed, and the Sponsor's Go Letter (if applicable).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recruitment and screening">
    Recruitment materials — posters, phone scripts, social media content — require REB approval before use, and a separate MUHC Communications approval before anything is displayed at the MUHC. These are two distinct steps that happen sequentially. Pre-screening access to OACIS records requires an approved EFVP.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enrolment and consent">
    Consent in Quebec is governed by the Civil Code, not common law. The treating physician or nurse cannot obtain a participant's consent to their own research study. The mandatory MSSS paragraphs in every consent form reflect provincial statutory requirements, not REB preference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Study visits and procedures">
    In Quebec, the Pharmacy Act and the rules of the Ordre des pharmaciens du Québec require that a qualified pharmacist dispense all investigational products. The <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI cannot handle <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> directly — not for convenience, not for overnight or weekend situations, not without a pre-arranged pharmacy agreement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ongoing oversight (continuous)">
    Safety reporting, REB annual renewals, protocol amendments, monitoring visits, and Task Delegation Log maintenance run continuously in parallel throughout the active study. A lapse in REB renewal is not retroactively corrected — there will be no ethics coverage for the lapse period, which Health Canada inspectors treat as a major observation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Study close-out">
    Close-out is not the end of a study's regulatory life — it is the start of a 15-year accountability phase (7 years for non-HC-regulated studies). The <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> must remain organized and inspection-ready for the full retention period. Post-study <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reporting continues for the follow-up duration defined in the protocol.
  </Step>
</Steps>
