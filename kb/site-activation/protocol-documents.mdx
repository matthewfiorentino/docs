A study runs on a small set of documents: the protocol, the Investigator's Brochure, the consent form, the recruitment materials and the case report forms. The sponsor writes most of them. The site receives them, checks them, files them, and acts on each new version. A team that uses an outdated version, or puts an amendment into effect before it is approved, departs from what the Research Ethics Board (REB) approved.

## Read the protocol before the study starts

The protocol is the study's master plan. The protocol describes the scientific rationale, the research question, the study population, the procedures participants will undergo, the data that will be collected and the statistical methods used to analyze them. Everything the site does during the study should be traceable to a specific protocol requirement.

The sponsor writes the protocol, or the <Tooltip tip="The person responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a drug or natural health product trial at a clinical trial site, who is entitled to provide health care under the laws of the province where the site is located." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">qualified investigator</Tooltip> in a sponsor-investigator study. The <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> must approve the protocol before any participant is enrolled. Many drug, natural health product and Class II, III or IV medical device trials also need [Health Canada's authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization) first. [Do not ask anyone to consent](/sops/cr-006#5-3-8) before the REB's written approval, MUHC Authorization, the site initiation visit and, if the sponsor issues one, its go letter.{/* Sources: SOP-CR-102, Addendum 1.2.1; SOP-CR-006, section 5.3.8 ("Do not consent participants before receiving REB written approval, MUHC Authorization, holding an SIV and obtaining a go letter from the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, if applicable."); SOP-CR-007, section 5.1.1 (the trials for which "the Sponsor must first obtain Health Canada Approval": investigational new natural health products, investigational new drugs in Phases I to III or Phase IV with conditions, and Class II, III or IV new medical devices). Until Nov. 15, 2026: "must be approved by Health Canada (for drug and device studies) and the REB" (fact check facts-21, P1-3). */}

The date and version [should be clearly indicated on each page of the protocol and of the Investigator's Brochure](/sops/cr-102#1-2-4). The version and date of the approved protocol must appear on every consent form and <Tooltip tip="A printed, optical or electronic document designed to record all the protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor on each trial participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">case report form (CRF)</Tooltip> used in the study. Every signed consent form must reference the protocol version that was current when it was signed. A mismatch between the consent form's version and the protocol's version at the time of enrolment is an audit finding.

The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, and the research team [should review the whole protocol](/sops/cr-003#5-3), and the Investigator's Brochure where there is one, typically before the sponsor's Site Feasibility Visit and the site initiation visit. The team should ask the sponsor about anything in the study's logistics, medicine or procedures that isn't clear. The principal investigator's signature on the protocol confirms their commitment to conduct the study in compliance with the REB-approved protocol, good clinical practice and the regulations that apply.

When you have a question about whether something is required, permitted or prohibited in a study, consult the protocol. If the protocol is silent on a point, ask the sponsor rather than assume.

The good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation, [ICH E6(R3)](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6(R3)_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf), lists in its Appendix B the topics a protocol should generally include. For a drug trial, Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations requires the <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip> to Health Canada to contain a copy of the protocol.

## Review each new version of the IB

For a study that tests a drug, a natural health product or a medical device, the sponsor provides an <Tooltip tip="A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure (IB)</Tooltip>. For a device, an equivalent document or a report of prior investigations can take its place. For a Phase IV drug trial, a product monograph may be used in place of an IB.

The IB is a compilation of the clinical and non-clinical data on the <Tooltip tip="A pharmaceutical form of an active ingredient or placebo, a medical device or a natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip>, such as its chemistry, pharmacology, toxicology and any earlier experience in humans. The IB gives the team the scientific context to understand the risks it manages on participants' behalf.

The IB is not a public document, and the sponsor controls its distribution. Keep every version in the <Tooltip tip="The qualified or principal investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines, so that the conduct and integrity of the study and the safety of participants at the site can be verified." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file (ISF)</Tooltip>.

When the sponsor issues a new version:

- The principal investigator [initials and dates it](/kb/site-activation/isf#document-medical-oversight-by-the-principal-investigator) before it is filed, to document medical oversight.
- The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) count a new version of the IB as an amendment to the approved study, so [inform the REB of it before it is implemented](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#review-a-new-version-of-the-ib).
- Staff must be trained on it in a timely way, directly after the REB's approval.

New safety information in an IB can call for a protocol amendment, a [revision of the consent form](/sops/cr-006#5-4-1) or an expedited adverse event report under [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012), so a new IB should never be filed without being read. The principal investigator is responsible for making sure that relevant changes are assessed and acted on. Filing an updated IB without reviewing it for new safety signals is a compliance gap.

A sponsor-investigator who prepares an IB can use the verification checklists for the protocol and the IB in [SOP-CR-102](/sops/cr-102#1-2-3). Observational and retrospective studies usually have no IB, because they test no investigational product. In those studies, the protocol gives the scientific background.

## Prepare the consent form

The consent form structures the consent discussion, and the signed form is the evidence that the participant agreed, and what they agreed to, at the time. Start the consent form from the sponsor's master form, or from the [Centre for Applied Ethics' consent templates](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms) if the sponsor provides none. [Informed Consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form) has what the form must contain at the MUHC, including the registry details of a clinical trial.

Make sure the form reflects the protocol. If the sponsor's form leaves out something the protocol requires, the site should tell the sponsor before the form is submitted to the REB.

Identify each version of the form with its version number or date, or both, on every page, and make sure the file name of an electronic version reflects them too. Health Canada inspection findings have included inaccurate file names and versions that were not updated.

[Trial registration](/kb/close-out/publications#trial-registration) has who registers a clinical trial and what participants are told about it during the consent discussion. When an amendment changes the consent form, [indicate in the amendment your recommendation](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) on whether the new information should be given to current participants, past participants or both. [Study Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#re-consent-participants-on-an-amended-consent-form) has how the REB considers that recommendation when it decides whether participants are re-consented. [Keep consent current](/kb/recruitment/consent#keep-consent-current) has when the amended form may be used and when participants are re-consented.

## Have recruitment materials approved

Every recruitment material, such as a poster, a social media post or the text of an email, needs the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca)'s approval before it is used. Materials that patients or the public may see also need the approval of [MUHC Communications](mailto:communications@muhc.mcgill.ca), after the REB's. The REB reviews them for undue influence, coercive tone and the accuracy of their content.

[Recruitment, Screening and Enrolment](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#have-recruitment-materials-approved) has the order of the approvals, what an ad must contain, the languages and the rules for revised materials.

## Record study data in case report forms

Case report forms record the data the protocol specifies. Every data point the protocol requires has a field in the CRF. The relationship is intentional. The CRF does not define what is collected, the protocol does. Any discrepancy between the two is a protocol deviation.

[Data Integrity](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity) has the rules for recording and correcting data, on paper or electronic forms. [Missing the sponsor's timelines](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/edc-query-management#enter-data-promptly) for the CRFs generates queries and creates data integrity problems.

## Control versions and amendments

A common error is implementing a protocol amendment at the site before the REB has approved it. An amendment to the protocol, the consent form or the IB needs the REB's approval, and Health Canada's authorization where the trial needs it, before it is implemented at the site. Newly translated study documents, such as a translated consent form, also go to the REB before they are used.

[Staff must be trained on each amendment in a timely fashion](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management#add-a-new-team-member), directly after the REB approves it. [Study Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments) has how to submit an amendment, the sponsor's go letter, the two exceptions the REB allows, and how to implement and file the amendment.
