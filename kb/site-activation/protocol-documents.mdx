A study runs on a small set of documents: the protocol, the Investigator's Brochure, the consent form, the recruitment materials and the case report forms. The sponsor writes most of them. The site receives them, checks them, files them, and acts on each new version. A team that uses an outdated version, or puts an amendment into effect before it is approved, departs from what the Research Ethics Board (REB) approved.

## Read the protocol before the study starts

The protocol is the study's master plan. The protocol describes the scientific rationale, the research question, the study population, the procedures participants will undergo, the data that will be collected and the statistical methods used to analyze them. Everything the site does during the study should be traceable to a specific protocol requirement.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): is "Everything the site does during the study should be traceable back to a specific protocol requirement" a rule of the RI-MUHC's SOPs? If not, it becomes advice ("Consider …"). In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P1-1). */}

The sponsor writes the protocol, or the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">qualified investigator</Tooltip> in a sponsor-investigator study. The <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> must approve the protocol before any participant is enrolled. Many drug, natural health product and Class II, III or IV medical device trials also need [Health Canada's authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization) first. [Do not ask anyone to consent](/sops/cr-006#5-3-8) before the REB's written approval, MUHC Authorization, the site initiation visit and, if the sponsor issues one, its go letter.{/* Sources: SOP-CR-102, Addendum 1.2.1; SOP-CR-006, section 5.3.8 ("Do not consent participants before receiving REB written approval, MUHC Authorization, holding an SIV and obtaining a go letter from the Sponsor/Sponsor-Investigator, if applicable."); SOP-CR-007, section 5.1.1 (the trials for which "the Sponsor must first obtain Health Canada Approval": investigational new natural health products, investigational new drugs in Phases I to III or Phase IV with conditions, and Class II, III or IV new medical devices). The date and version [should appear on each page of the protocol](/sops/cr-102#1-2-4). The version and date of the approved protocol must appear on every consent form and <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">case report form (CRF)</Tooltip> used in the study.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): does any SOP require the protocol's version and date on every consent form and case report form? The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, and the research team [should review the whole protocol](/sops/cr-003#5-3), and the Investigator's Brochure where there is one, typically before the sponsor's Site Feasibility Visit and the site initiation visit. The team should ask the sponsor about anything in the study's logistics, medicine or procedures that isn't clear. The principal investigator's signature on the protocol confirms their commitment to conduct the study in compliance with the REB-approved protocol, good clinical practice and the regulations that apply.

When you have a question about whether something is required, permitted or prohibited in a study, consult the protocol. If the protocol is silent on a point, ask the sponsor rather than assume.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): is escalating to the sponsor when the protocol is silent the RI-MUHC's rule? If not, "Consider asking the sponsor". In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P1-8). */}

## Review each new version of the IB

For a study that tests a drug, a natural health product or a medical device, the sponsor provides an <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure (IB)</Tooltip>. For a device, an equivalent document or a report of prior investigations can take its place. For a Phase IV drug trial, a product monograph may be used in place of an IB.

The IB is a compilation of the clinical and non-clinical data on the <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip>, such as its chemistry, pharmacology, toxicology and any earlier experience in humans. The IB gives the team the scientific context to understand the risks it manages on participants' behalf.

The IB is not a public document, and the sponsor controls its distribution.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): the IB not public and its distribution controlled by the sponsor: ICH E6(R3), Appendix A.2.2, says only that the sponsor "may wish to include a statement instructing the investigator and other recipients to treat the IB as a confidential document". In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P1-10). */} Keep every version in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file (ISF)</Tooltip>.

When the sponsor issues a new version:

- The principal investigator [initials and dates it](/kb/site-activation/isf#document-medical-oversight-by-the-principal-investigator) before it is filed, to document medical oversight.
- The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) count a new version of the IB as an amendment to the approved study, so [inform the REB of it before it is implemented](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#review-a-new-version-of-the-ib).{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): SOP-CR-026_01, section 5.3.1, has the REB informed of a new IB before it is implemented, and SOP-CR-015_08, section 5.2.3, has amendments "REB approved/acknowledged"; the RI-MUHC addendum to SOP-CR-102, section 1.2.5, says "Any new version of IB or amendment to protocol must be submitted and receive REB approval." - Staff must be trained on it in a timely way, directly after the REB's approval.

New safety information in an IB can call for a protocol amendment, a [revision of the consent form](/sops/cr-006#5-4-1) or an expedited adverse event report under [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012), so a new IB should never be filed without being read. The principal investigator is responsible for making sure that relevant changes are assessed and acted on. Filing an updated IB without reviewing it for new safety signals is a compliance gap.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): which SOP sets the investigator's duty to review and assess a new IB, and which SOP-CR-012 report, if any, does an IB update trigger? Observational and retrospective studies usually have no IB, because they test no investigational product. In those studies, the protocol gives the scientific background.

## Prepare the consent form

The consent form structures the consent discussion, and the signed form is the evidence that the participant agreed, and what they agreed to, at the time. [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006) sets what the form must contain, and [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) how consent is obtained. [Informed Consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form) has the full list of what the form needs, including its English and French versions and its translation.

At the MUHC, preparing the form means more than adapting a sponsor's template:

- If the sponsor doesn't provide a master consent form, start from the [Centre for Applied Ethics' consent templates](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms). [Consent and Assent Templates](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates) helps you choose one.
- The templates carry, in red, the wording that Quebec's health ministry requires for clinical trials. If the sponsor provides a master form, insert that wording into the site's form before you submit it to the REB. Without it, the REB will reject the form.
- Verify the form with the Verification of an ICF Document checklist before you submit it.
- Make sure the form reflects the protocol. If the sponsor's form leaves out something the protocol requires, the site should tell the sponsor before the form is submitted to the REB.
- For a study of an investigational product, the form must be printed on MUHC letterhead with the MUHC Medical Records bar code FMU-9997, so that the form is filed in the Alert section of the participant's electronic medical record. The adult clinical trial template carries the bar code, and [the pediatric templates don't](/kb/recruitment/consent-templates#edit-the-template). Other studies don't need the bar code.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): SOP-CR-006_08, section 5.2.1, says the bar code is "already on the REB ICF templates"; kb/recruitment/consent-templates found it missing from the pediatric templates of Sept. 30, 2024. Should the SOP's text change, or the templates? */}
- Identify each version of the form with its version number or date, or both, on every page, and make sure the file name of an electronic version reflects them too. Health Canada inspection findings have included inaccurate file names and versions that were not updated.

[Inform the REB of newly translated study documents](/sops/cr-026#5-3-1), such as revised consent forms and participant materials, before you use them.

### Register a clinical trial and say so in the form

A clinical trial must be registered in a publicly accessible registry before the first participant is recruited. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator is responsible for registering it. In an investigator-initiated trial, the study team checks that the investigator has done so.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): does the study team check that an investigator-initiated trial is registered? ### Keep the consent form current

When the protocol changes in ways that affect participants' risks, procedures or rights, the consent form must be updated and approved again before the change takes effect. [The only exceptions](/sops/cr-006#5-7-1) are an emergency, where an immediate safety hazard to participants requires the change, and the exceptions to the informed consent process. In an emergency, document your conversation with the participant or their legally appointed representative.

[Do not begin using the amended consent form](/sops/cr-006#5-4-5) until you have the REB's written approval and, if the sponsor issues one, its go letter. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email.

[Print the REB submission and approval and the relevant emails](/sops/cr-006#5-4-6), and keep a record of the communications about the consent form amendment, with the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, the regulators and the REB. File them in the ISF.

When an amendment changes the consent form, the REB considers the researcher's recommendation in deciding whether participants must be re-consented. Once the REB has approved the amended form in writing, [re-consent each affected participant on it](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#re-consent-participants-on-an-amended-consent-form) at their earliest visit to the site. The sponsor, the REB or a study or department SOP may recommend or require something different.

Every signed consent form must reference the protocol version that was current when it was signed. A mismatch between the consent form's version and the protocol's version at the time of enrolment is an audit finding.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): a signed consent form must reference the protocol version current at signature, and a mismatch is an audit finding: no SOP item found (with P1-4). ## Have recruitment materials approved

Every material used to recruit participants, such as posters, flyers, social media content, phone scripts, press releases, MUHC screen content and videos, needs the REB's approval before it is used. Each material [should be submitted to the REB](/sops/cr-006#5-3) in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> as part of the initial study application.

Materials added or revised later go to the REB as amendments. Each material should carry a version number, in the document and in its electronic file name. The REB reviews them for undue influence, coercive tone and the accuracy of their content.{/* TO CONFIRM (the REB office): "and the accuracy of their content" is kept from the page as it stood; SOP-CR-009_08, section 5.2.1, says "The REB will review for undue influence or coercive tone of the content." (facts-21, P1-34). */}

After the REB approves them, any recruitment materials a patient may see, such as a poster, a screen, an email to MUHC patients or a social media post, also need the approval of [MUHC Communications](mailto:communications@muhc.mcgill.ca), wherever they appear. MUHC Communications' approval is sought outside Nagano, and only after the REB has approved the materials, never at the same time.

Recruitment materials and ads must be submitted to the REB in English and in French. ## Keep the essential documents in the ISF

The essential documents, taken together, let an auditor or an inspector evaluate how the trial was conducted and whether its data can be relied on. The essential documents are kept in the ISF throughout the study, from before it starts to after it ends. At any moment, someone should be able to open the ISF and verify that the study is being run with the right people, under the right approved documents, at a site with the right qualifications.

Monitors check at their visits that the principal investigator keeps the ISF as good clinical practice requires. [The Investigator Site File](/kb/site-activation/isf) has what goes in it and how to keep it, and [Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out/close-out) its final review and archiving.

## Control versions and amendments

Every page of the protocol should carry its version number and date, and every page of the consent form its version number or date, or both. When something goes wrong, such as an adverse event, an audit finding or a data query, investigators and inspectors need to know which version was in effect at the time.

When a significant change is needed, such as a new inclusion criterion, a revised dose or an updated safety section, the sponsor issues an amendment. An amendment to the protocol, the consent form or the IB needs the same approvals as the original document before it takes effect at the site: the REB's approval and, where it is required, Health Canada's authorization. [Do not implement any change](/sops/cr-026#5-3-1) until you have the REB's written approval and the sponsor's go letter or confirmation, if it issues one.

The REB allows two exceptions to its prior approval. A change needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants may be made at once, and must be reported to the REB immediately. An administrative change with no impact on participants may be submitted at the annual continuing review. [Study Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments) has the full process.

Train the staff on each amendment in a timely way, directly after the REB's approval. When a new version is approved, the previous version stays in the ISF, marked superseded. Auditors routinely examine earlier versions to verify that the approvals came in the right order.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): kept from the page as it stood; no source found for "routinely" (the fact check's P55). */}

<Warning>
A common error is implementing a protocol amendment at the site before the REB has approved it. Health Canada and the sponsor may have authorized the amendment, but it cannot be implemented until the REB has also approved it, unless it is needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants.
</Warning>

## Know what the site is responsible for

The site is responsible for receiving the study documents, reviewing them, filing them and acting on them correctly. In practice:

- Before the study opens, the principal investigator and the team review the whole protocol, and raise their questions with the sponsor ([Read the protocol before the study starts](#read-the-protocol-before-the-study-starts)).
- The ISF is kept current. The clinical research coordinator usually keeps it, and the principal investigator remains ultimately responsible for it. [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015) sets what it contains and how it is structured.
- When an amendment arrives, the site documents its receipt, submits it to the REB and holds it as pending until it has the REB's written approval and the sponsor's go letter, if there is one. The exception is a change needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants. [SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026) covers the REB's ongoing review.
