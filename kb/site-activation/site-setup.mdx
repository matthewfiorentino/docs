Approval by the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> does not open a study to participants.

Between site selection and the first participant, the study needs its approvals, a site initiation visit and, in many studies, the sponsor's written permission to start.

Some of the steps run at the same time. The F11 form in Nagano starts the MUHC's institutional feasibility review at the same time as the REB's review, where one applies. A clinical trial agreement can be signed once the REB has approved the study, even if other feasibility reviews are still under way. The order of Health Canada's letter and the REB's approval can differ.

Other steps wait for the ones before them. MUHC Authorization comes only after the reviews are complete and favourable, every agreement is fully executed and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it. The site initiation visit follows MUHC Authorization. The sponsor's go letter, where the sponsor issues one, follows the site initiation visit.

The setup work around the approvals and the visit, such as the agreements, the site file and the equipment, runs at the same time. A delay in any of it can delay the start.

## Follow the steps to activation in order

A study is usually activated at the MUHC in this order:

1. The sponsor selects the site after its [feasibility assessment](/kb/planning/feasibility), which can include a Site Feasibility Visit.
2. The study is submitted in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>, and the study's agreements go to the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).
3. Health Canada authorizes the study, where it needs Health Canada's authorization. For a natural health product trial, the REB approves the study before Health Canada authorizes it.
4. The REB carries out its scientific and ethics review, and the MUHC carries out its [institutional feasibility review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#institutional-feasibility-review).
5. The <Tooltip tip="The person whom an establishment of Quebec's health network formally mandates to authorize the research conducted under its auspices." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> issues <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>.
6. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator holds the <Tooltip tip="A monitoring visit conducted by a monitor as a final evaluation of the site, and to train the site team on the study protocol and its associated documents and processes." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">site initiation visit (SIV)</Tooltip>.
7. The sponsor sends its go letter, where it issues one, and enrolment can begin.

The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, is responsible for having every contract and agreement reviewed and fully executed by the Research Agreements Office, through My Contracts and Nagano, before MUHC Authorization. The budget is negotiated alongside the agreement, and the principal investigator approves it ([Study Budgets](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts), including [the negotiation with an industry sponsor](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#negotiate-the-budget-with-an-industry-sponsor)). What an agreement needs, and when it can be signed, is on [Research Agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements).

Both the REB's review and the institutional feasibility review must be complete and favourable before the Personne mandatée issues MUHC Authorization. [Study Submission and Review](/kb/submission/submission-overview) explains both reviews. The MUHC Authorization letter [should be filed](/sops/cr-007#5-4-11) in the <Tooltip tip="The qualified or principal investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines, so that the conduct and integrity of the study and the safety of participants at the site can be verified." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file (ISF)</Tooltip>.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

### Get Health Canada authorization where the study needs it

## Prepare for the site initiation visit

The SIV is the sponsor's last check that the site is ready to start the study, and the moment the sponsor trains the team on the study. The SIV is held after Health Canada's authorization, where the study needs it, the REB's approval and MUHC Authorization. The SIV can be held on site or virtually.

Once all the approvals are in, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator sends a letter confirming the date, time and place of the SIV. The confirmation letter and the approvals themselves should be filed in the ISF. [Keep the printed REB approval letter in the ISF](/sops/cr-007#5-4-11) until a document management system that complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR Part 11) is in place.

The sponsor then ships the study materials. Make sure the site has adequate, secure space for them, temperature-controlled where needed.

Before the SIV, the team should review the protocol and the other study materials and prepare a list of questions. The site should make sure that the SIV is held in a confidential area.

Have these other documents ready in the ISF before the SIV:

- The executed clinical trial agreement.
- The signed protocol and the signed <Tooltip tip="A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure (IB)</Tooltip>, where the study has one.
- The REB's approval of the protocol and of every document participants will see.
- The CVs and licences of every team member who will be delegated tasks.

<Warning>
For every drug study, industry-sponsored or investigator-initiated, that uses the MUHC Pharmacy Services, the MUHC Pharmacy must be informed and asked to attend the SIV, or a separate SIV for pharmacy staff. The pharmacist must be trained and delegated on the Task Delegation Log before any investigational product is dispensed.
</Warning>

## Take part in the site initiation visit

The sponsor, the sponsor-investigator or the monitor runs the SIV. The principal investigator and every team member who will be delegated protocol tasks should attend. If someone can't attend, the reason should be documented, the person should be trained afterwards, and that training documented and its confirmation added to the SIV documentation.

The SIV can cover, among other things:

- The protocol, its objectives and procedures, and the training to come on each amendment.
- The inclusion and exclusion criteria, and the screening and enrolment procedures.
- Knowledge and management of the investigational product, biological products, medical devices or radiopharmaceuticals, where they apply.
- The research team's good clinical practice, legal and regulatory obligations.
- Virtual visits and work done off site.
- Adverse events, serious adverse events, adverse reactions, serious adverse reactions and medical device incidents.
- Monitoring and inspections.
- Case report forms and data management.
- The essential documents in the ISF.
- The consent form procedures.
- Biological samples.
- Any other point specific to the protocol.

The team should ask the sponsor about anything in the study's logistics, medicine or procedures that isn't clear. Time permitting, [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) call it best practice](/sops/cr-005#5-2-5) to design the visit-specific source document forms before the SIV, so that any ambiguity in the protocol can be cleared up with the monitor.

Record who took part in the SIV, for example on a signed Training Log, and keep the record in the ISF. For a virtual SIV, the list of attendees downloaded from the meeting platform can replace the wet-ink signatures. The monitor must also sign the study's Monitoring Log.

Protocol training [must take place before a team member is delegated tasks](/sops/cr-003#5-3) on the <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>. The team members trained at the SIV should then be delegated their tasks on the log. The principal investigator's signature on the log confirms that each person is qualified and trained for the tasks delegated.

Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.

After the SIV, the sponsor, the sponsor-investigator or the monitor writes the SIV report, confirming that the site has been trained on and understands every aspect of the study and the good clinical practice requirements. The completed, signed report is sent to the site to review, act on and file in the ISF, with the SIV training slides and the training log.

## Wait for the go letter before you enrol

Once the sponsor is satisfied that the site is ready, it activates the study by sending the site a go letter. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email.

[Do not begin the study](/sops/cr-007#5-4-8) until you have the REB's written approval, MUHC Authorization and the sponsor's go letter, if it issues one. Participant enrolment [should begin only after the go letter](/sops/cr-005#5-3-4) has arrived. The go letter should be printed and filed in the ISF.

In a study where the principal investigator is also the sponsor, a separate go letter may not be required.

[Do not ask anyone to consent](/sops/cr-006#5-3-8) before all four of these conditions are met:

| Condition | What it confirms |
|---|---|
| The REB's written approval | The REB has completed its scientific and ethics review. It has found the study ethical and the consent form adequate, and participants' rights protected. |
| MUHC Authorization | The Personne mandatée has confirmed that the institutional feasibility review is complete and favourable: Quality Assurance, the agreements, the Director of Medical and Professional Services, and nursing, the laboratory and the pharmacy where they apply. Without MUHC Authorization, the MUHC has not authorized the research to take place at the MUHC, whatever the REB has approved. |
| The SIV, with the Task Delegation Log signed | The team has been trained, and the site is ready to start. |
| The sponsor's go letter, if it issues one | The sponsor has confirmed that the site is ready, and has authorized enrolment to begin at the site. When the sponsor issues one, it must be received before anyone is asked to consent, and it should be filed in the ISF. |

## Plan for what can delay the start

Most delays before the first participant come from steps that take longer than expected:

- The negotiation of the agreements and the budget. Consider starting the budget early, and flag complex indemnification or intellectual property clauses to the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).
- The REB's review. Consider checking the REB's meeting calendar in Nagano and timing the submission around holiday closures and busy periods.
- Health Canada's review, which cannot be rushed. If you are the sponsor-investigator, consider applying early.
- The pharmacy and laboratory setup. Investigational product storage, laboratory accreditation and normal ranges must be confirmed before the SIV. Consider starting them early, rather than after MUHC Authorization.
- The availability of the team. If a key team member, such as the principal investigator, the <Tooltip tip="A nurse, health professional or other qualified member of the research team who handles most of the administrative responsibilities and day-to-day activities of a clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">clinical research coordinator</Tooltip> or the research nurse, can't attend the SIV, the whole timeline shifts.

### Prepare for a qualification visit by the sponsor

Some sponsors hold a site qualification visit (SQV) after the feasibility assessment, before they commit to a clinical trial agreement. The sponsor's team, typically its clinical research associate and a study manager, visits the site. The team checks that the facilities, the staff and the processes described during feasibility match what is in place.

At a qualification visit, sponsors typically look at:

| Area | What the sponsor looks at |
|---|---|
| Facilities | Clinic space, the pharmacy, the laboratory, storage capacity for the investigational product, and patient flow. |
| Staff | The availability of the principal investigator and the key staff, their experience in the therapeutic area, and whether their good clinical practice training is current. |
| Regulatory readiness | The REB's current workload and timelines, and the systems for documenting delegation and training. |
| Competing studies | Enrolment in similar protocols, overlap in the pool of participants, and the team's capacity. |
| Infrastructure | Equipment, IT systems and data management capabilities. |

A well-prepared visit shows the sponsor that the site can handle the study. To prepare for it, consider:

- Confirming that the good clinical practice training of every team member who will be involved is current.
- Having up-to-date CVs ready for the principal investigator and the key staff.

## Calibrate and maintain the study equipment

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

Measurements on participants and the storage of investigational products are only as reliable as the equipment behind them. [Document which equipment the study will use](/sops/cr-025#5-1), including equipment used for inpatients, outpatients and at home. Depending on the study, it can include:

- Thermometers and temperature recorders for storing investigational products and biological specimens.
- Incubators and centrifuges.
- Blood pressure cuffs, weigh scales and height scales.
- Chemotherapy infusion pumps, electrocardiogram (ECG) machines and spirometers.
- Diagnostic imaging equipment.

The principal investigator should identify the equipment that is critical to the study and its specifications. Calibration is generally recommended at least once a year, unless the equipment's operating manual or SOPs say otherwise. On a log, the principal investigator should record the dates of calibration, the device's details, who calibrated it and the person responsible for the instrument. The principal investigator should make every maintenance, safety and calibration certificate available on request.

If another party maintains the equipment, such as a pharmaceutical partner for an instrument specific to the study, [make sure that this responsibility is defined in writing](/sops/cr-025#5-2), for example in the clinical trial agreement or a contract.

In a trial you sponsor yourself, [the monitor should review the equipment used for the study at the site initiation visit](/sops/cr-013#appendix-3), where applicable. The review covers the creation of a study inventory and the filing of the equipment's maintenance and calibration certificates.

[Retain the equipment maintenance records](/sops/cr-025#5-4) for 25 years for a study under a Clinical Trial Application (CTA) to Health Canada, and for as long as required for other studies. Make sure that they can be retrieved for an inspection throughout that period. For a study regulated by Health Canada, the principal investigator should also keep the service, maintenance, cleaning and calibration records of critical equipment, and its manual, for 25 years.

Consider keeping a study-specific equipment inventory with its calibration certificates, rather than relying on the RI-MUHC's general inventory. The SOP's Study Equipment Inventory and Routine Maintenance Documentation form can serve.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-004#5-1-3) have the sponsor or sponsor-investigator make sure that all electronic software the study uses, its electronic data capture system included, [has been validated](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#know-who-validates-electronic-systems) and complies with [21 CFR Part 11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11).
