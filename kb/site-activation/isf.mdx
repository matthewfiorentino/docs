The <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file (ISF)</Tooltip> holds all the essential documents of a study at its site. During the study, the team works from these documents: the approved protocol, the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>, the training records and the consent forms.

Afterwards, the same documents are the record that sponsor auditors, the RI-MUHC's Quality Assurance and regulatory inspectors use to check how the study was conducted, sometimes years later. A document filed late is hard to reconstruct, so the ISF is kept current from the start.

## Set up the ISF

The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, is responsible for setting up the ISF and keeping it current. The principal investigator or a delegate [should create the ISF](/sops/cr-015#5-1) after the sponsor or sponsor-investigator first makes contact, or as soon as a protocol outline is available.

The work can be delegated to trained team members, such as the clinical research coordinator, and the principal investigator remains ultimately responsible for it. ISO 14155 is the good clinical practice standard for devices of the International Organization for Standardization (ISO). In a medical device study, the [RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-015#3-0-responsibilities) read it as delegating the responsibility along with the task.

[Before delegating the ISF's tasks](/sops/cr-015#5-1-6), the principal investigator must hold RI-MUHC [Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status) and the appropriate level of training, and create a Training Log and a Task Delegation Log. Identify the team members responsible for collecting, filing, updating and storing the study's documents. Make sure each of them has documented training and is delegated these tasks on the Task Delegation Log, with the principal investigator's sign-off.

The principal investigator must ensure participants' confidentiality at all times, and must make sure that only the team members authorized on the log can access the documents.

Make sure that the agreement between the sponsor or sponsor-investigator and the investigator, and any agreement between the investigator and an academic research organization, such as the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM), [describe how the essential documents are managed](/sops/cr-015#5-1-1): how long they are archived, their return or destruction, and any delegation to a third party.

Documents go into the ISF before the study starts, during the study and after it ends. At any point, the ISF must stay in a condition that would pass an inspection.

## Organize the binders

The ISF is organized in numbered tabs, with a table of contents, so that any document can be found quickly.

Industry sponsors may provide a table of contents or pre-labelled binders. If the sponsor provides no structure, use the ISF filing and archiving structure in [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-015#5-1), based on the good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH). No tab should be left blank. If a document is stored elsewhere, for example as a PDF, a note to file under its tab should say where.

<Note>
For a medical device study, Annex E of ISO 14155 should be followed for the ISF's structure and its essential documents, in place of the ICH guideline. The principles of keeping an ISF apply to both, but the list of documents differs.
</Note>

## Know which documents go in the ISF

[ICH E6(R3), Appendix C](https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6(R3)_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf), lists the essential records in one table, and marks those that should generally be in place before the trial starts. For a drug trial, the ISF [should include all the applicable essential documents](/sops/cr-015#5-1) of the ICH guideline. The lists below follow the RI-MUHC's practice, including MUHC documents the ICH list doesn't have. Not every document applies to every study.

Documents marked *wet sig* carry original handwritten signatures or initials, and must be kept in their original paper form. Documents marked *versioned* carry a version number and a date.

### Before the study starts

The protocol and the approvals:
- The protocol and all its amendments, with the principal investigator's signature pages *(wet sig, versioned)*.
- The <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure (IB)</Tooltip>, the product monograph or the device manual, with its signature page *(versioned)*.
- The REB-approved consent forms, in every language version *(versioned)*.
- The approved recruitment materials, and the other approved documents participants will see, such as questionnaires and diaries.
- Translation certificates, where there are any.
- The <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>'s initial approval letter, listing every approved document and its version.
- The <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> letter from the <Tooltip tip="The person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research: the Director of Innovation, Performance and Quality, who issues MUHC Authorization for each study." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip>.
- Health Canada's No Objection Letter (NOL), Notice of Authorization (NOA) or Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA), where the study needs one.
- The approval of the privacy impact assessment (EFVP), on form F17 or F18 in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>, where the study needs one.
- The REB's current membership list.

The team and its delegation:
- The principal investigator's CV, initialled and dated every two years, and licence, where applicable *(versioned)*.
- The current CVs of the sub-investigators and the other team members, and the licences of those who hold one.
- The Task Delegation Log, signed by the principal investigator before anyone performs a delegated task *(wet sig)*.
- The training records: good clinical practice, the SOP Reader and Assessment certificates from TalentLMS, Health Canada Division 5 or ISO 14155 training where the study's level requires it, and the protocol training.
- The principal investigator's Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status.
- The Qualified Investigator's Undertaking (QIU), for a drug or natural health product trial.
- For a device study, the qualified investigator's written undertaking, which the manufacturer or importer keeps in its records.
- The MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement of each team member with access to OACIS *(wet sig)*.
- The signature and initials log of the authorized staff.

The agreements:
- The signed clinical trial agreement *(wet sig)*.
- The confidentiality or non-disclosure agreement *(wet sig)*.
- The conflict of interest and financial disclosures.

The site's activation:
- The sponsor's go letter, where the sponsor issues one.
- The pharmacy agreement and the plan for the accountability of the <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip>.
- The laboratory certifications and normal reference ranges.
- The equipment's calibration certificates.
- The <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">site initiation visit (SIV)</Tooltip> report, its Training Log and its training slides.
- The Monitoring Log, which the monitor and the clinical research coordinator sign at the SIV.

### During the study

Amendments and renewals:
- Protocol amendments, signed by the principal investigator before they are implemented *(wet sig, versioned)*.
- The revised consent forms approved by the REB, with the version in use marked current *(versioned)*.
- IB amendments, initialled and dated by the principal investigator on receipt *(wet sig)*.
- The REB's approvals of continuing review and of amendments.
- The sponsor's safety communications, initialled and dated on receipt.

Monitoring and oversight:
- The monitoring visit logs and monitoring letters.
- The site's responses to monitoring letters *(wet sig)*.
- Audit reports and responses, where there are any.

The participants' records:
- The signed and dated consent forms, one for each participant *(wet sig)*.
- The Participant Identification Log, which is never given to an external sponsor and is not archived with the essential documents.
- The source documents, such as visit notes, laboratory results, adverse event logs and diaries.
- The randomization and enrolment logs.
- The screen failure log.

The investigational product:
- The accountability and dispensing log, kept by the pharmacist.
- The records of shipment, receipt and returns.
- The temperature and storage monitoring logs.

Safety reporting:
- The reports of <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">serious adverse events (SAEs)</Tooltip>, initialled and dated by the principal investigator *(wet sig)*.
- The external <Tooltip tip="A serious adverse reaction to a drug or natural health product whose nature, severity or frequency the Investigator's Brochure or product label does not anticipate; the sponsor reports it to Health Canada, and the site reports it to the REB when it meets the REB's criteria." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip> reports received from the sponsor, reviewed, initialled and dated.

The team's changes:
- Updates to the Task Delegation Log, with the date of each addition and removal *(wet sig)*.
- The CVs and training records of new team members.

Communications:
- Correspondence with the sponsor, the monitor, the REB and Health Canada, printed and filed.
- Telephone and video conference logs.
- Notes to file for missing, late or out-of-sequence entries.

Superseded documents:
- Every earlier version of the protocol and the consent form, marked superseded, with initials and date *(versioned)*.
- Superseded SOPs, source form templates and working instructions.

## The ISF and the Trial Master File

The ISF is the site's collection of essential documents. The <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">Trial Master File (TMF)</Tooltip> is the sponsor's collection, with the essential documents of every site, from every country in the study. The site's ISF should mirror the sponsor's TMF, except for a few documents.

The monitor checks at the site visits that the principal investigator keeps the ISF as good clinical practice requires. A document filed at the site that the sponsor hasn't received, or a sponsor's document the site hasn't filed, is a monitoring finding. A study's final close-out can only be done once the monitor has reviewed both files and confirmed that every document is where it should be.

### If you are the sponsor-investigator

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

A sponsor-investigator sets up, maintains and updates the TMF, with the essential documents of each participating site.

In a single-site study, only one structure is needed: the ISF and the TMF are one and the same. Consider still keeping them as distinct collections within the same binders, in case the study is later audited or expanded to other sites. If a sponsor-investigator of a multicentre study is also the principal investigator at the site, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs call it best practice](/sops/cr-015#5-1) to have both a TMF and an ISF.

In a multicentre study, the sponsor-investigator should collect and file the essential documents of every site, from every country.

The TMF has three levels: the trial, each country and each site. The SOPs' TMF index lists which documents go at each level and study milestone. The sponsor-investigator is responsible for making sure that IT has validated the software used for an electronic TMF, and that it complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR Part 11).

## Keep essential documents on paper

Keeping essential documents on paper is the RI-MUHC's own rule.

[Primary source essential documents must be in paper form](/sops/cr-015#5-1-12) until the RI-MUHC has a validated document management system, with a password, an audit trail and a backup, that complies with [21 CFR Part 11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11), the United States' regulation on electronic records and electronic signatures. Until then, [the main ISF should ideally be paper based, in binders](/sops/cr-015#5-1-9), together with the participants' source document files. All current study documents should be filed on paper in binders.

A shared drive may hold electronic essential documents only as a backup. [No participant information should be saved on a shared drive](/sops/cr-015#5-1-13), unless for temporary data transfer. ## Keep the ISF current during the study

The ISF is updated as the study goes, not caught up at monitoring visits or rebuilt at close-out. Add each document as it is generated or received, once the principal investigator has initialled and dated the documents that need it. A monitor or an inspector can visit at any time, and the ISF they review must reflect the study as it stands.

During the study, the ISF gathers documents in several categories:

- Each monitoring visit produces a monitoring letter and the site's written response. File both promptly, initialled and dated by the principal investigator.
- Each REB submission produces an approval or an acknowledgement letter. File it, with the versions of the documents it lists.
- Each amendment to the protocol, the consent form or the IB arrives from the sponsor with a signature page. The principal investigator signs and dates it, and it is filed. Make sure the REB has approved or acknowledged the amendment before it is implemented. [Study Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#wait-for-every-approval-before-you-implement-the-amendment) gives the exceptions.

When a team member joins, their CV, their training records and their signed entry on the Task Delegation Log must be in the ISF before they perform any study task. When a team member leaves, update the log with an end date, signed off by the principal investigator. A log entry with no start or end date is an inspection finding ([Team Management](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management)).

The participants' files are kept apart from the regulatory binders, and are part of the ISF. Each participant has a source document file, with the consent forms, the adverse event assessment logs, visit notes, laboratory results and any other data produced during their participation. The participants' files are the source from which data are transcribed into the <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">case report form (CRF)</Tooltip>, and against which the monitor verifies the source data.

Every document in the ISF must carry a version number and a date, including the documents the site creates or adapts for the study, such as the Task Delegation Log, source form templates and working instructions. Documents without version control are an inspection finding.

## Document medical oversight by the principal investigator

The RI-MUHC's SOPs make the principal investigator responsible for documenting medical oversight throughout the study. The principal investigator does it by initialling and dating each essential document that bears on participant safety or data integrity, in a timely way, before it is filed in the ISF. The documents to initial and date include:

- All safety reports, local and external, such as SAE reports and the SUSAR reports received from the sponsor.
- All amendments to study documents, such as IB amendments.
- The sponsor's applicable newsletters and safety communications.
- Clarifications of protocol procedures.
- Any other document on participant safety or data integrity.

The principal investigator's initials and date show that the document was read.

If the principal investigator confirms oversight by email, for example to confirm the review of an IB amendment, the email should list the documents read with their file names, to show which versions were read. The email should be printed and filed in the ISF. An email that only says "acknowledged", without naming the documents, does not document oversight.

If a task is delegated to a specialist, such as an ophthalmologist or a radiologist, the specialist should have signed the MD specialist agreement, filed with the Task Delegation Log. The specialist should initial and date the documents about the participant's assessment in their specialty, and the principal investigator should then initial and date them too, before they are filed in the participants' files.

## Keep superseded documents

Each time an essential document is revised, such as the protocol, the consent form, the IB, a source form template or an SOP, the previous version is superseded. Keep it in the ISF, clearly marked, apart from the current version. Superseded documents are never discarded.

To mark a superseded paper document, draw a line through its first page, write "superseded", and add your initials and the date. If superseded documents stay in the same binder as the current version, they must be placed behind it, with a clear tab between them. If they move to another binder or are stored electronically, a note to file in the current binder says where. Obsolete electronic files should be stored in a folder marked obsolete.

Superseded documents keep the audit trail whole. An inspector reviewing a safety event in month 6 of a study needs to know which version of the protocol was in effect then. A site that can produce only the current version cannot show that it followed the approved documents at the time of the event.

## Keep a communications section

The ISF should have a communications section for each study, kept by the clinical research coordinator. The section records, in date order, the written and verbal communications that matter to the study: correspondence with the sponsor, the monitor, the REB, the pharmacy and Health Canada, telephone and video conference logs, and any communication that records a decision, a finding or an instruction that affected the study.

Emails may be filed electronically during the study. All pertinent emails about the study should also be printed and filed in the ISF, in date order.

Pertinent emails include monitoring visit confirmations and their follow-up, the sponsor's communications on protocol changes or safety information, REB correspondence, and any written exchange that records a decision about the study. An ISF with only forms and approvals, and no record of the communications that shaped the study, is a significant gap at an audit.

Consider writing each telephone and video conference log at the time of the call: who was on it, the date, the topics and any decisions or actions. Label a log written afterwards as retrospective.

A separate communication log can be kept for contacts with participants, such as visit arrangements, adverse events reported by telephone and re-consent discussions. The participants' communication log is part of the participant's file, not the regulatory binder.

## Keep the records after the study

The retention periods, and how the ISF is archived at close-out, are on [Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving). Never destroy study files without consulting the principal investigator and the sponsor or sponsor-investigator.

[A record of the archived study documents and their location must be readily available at all times](/sops/cr-015#5-5-2). [Data Integrity](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#follow-alcoa+) gives the time regulatory inspectors allow for producing the records.

A study whose ISF was kept current needs little work at close-out. Documents reconstructed after the fact are a risk to the integrity of the record.
