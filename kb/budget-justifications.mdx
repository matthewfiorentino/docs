---

The [Research Budget Tool](/kb/budget-tool) calculates your costs. Most funding agencies also require a written justification for each budget line — a brief explanation of what was estimated, how, and why.

This page covers what to write. For the underlying rates and fee schedules, see [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/budgets-contracts).

---

## What reviewers check

CIHR Project Grant reviewers and most foundation funders verify the same things:

- Salary matches the institution's published scale for the stated role and level
- FTE allocation is credible for the workload described
- Multi-year cost increases follow a stated policy
- Participant reimbursements are distinguished from stipends or honoraria
- Overhead is correctly applied (or excluded, with a reason)
- Contingency has a stated complexity rationale

---

## Personnel

For CIHR applications, all staff costs go under **Salaries and Stipends**. Each person needs four things covered: what they will do, their FTE allocation, their salary basis, and the benefits rate.

The tool's **Staff & Salaries** section shows the annual salary, FTE%, and calculated hourly rate. Use those numbers directly.

**Example:**
> The Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC) will dedicate 100% FTE (1,820 hours/year) to study operations: participant screening and enrolment, informed consent, visit coordination, data entry, query resolution, and REB correspondence. Salary reflects the RI-MUHC scale midpoint for a Level 6 Coordinator (\$44,408/year), plus 19% employer benefits (\$8,438), for a Year 1 total of \$52,846. A 5% annual cost-of-living adjustment is applied in Years 2 and 3, consistent with collective agreement projections.

**PI salary:** Not typically included in IIT or foundation budgets — the PI's contribution is considered part of their academic role. For industry-sponsored studies where a PI salary line is required, use the same structure above.

**Benefits rate:** The tool applies 19%. Validate the current rate with RI-MUHC People & Culture before submitting — Quebec employer obligations (QPP, QPIP, EI, FSF, RREGOP) may run higher.

## CIM services

Reference the current CIM price schedule and specify which rate tier applies. The tool selects the rate automatically: Foundation grants use the **non-industry rate** (PI-initiated, not pharma-funded); IIT/CIHR grants also use the **non-industry rate**; industry-sponsored studies use the **industry rate** (pharma-funded). Confirm pricing with CIM before submission — the schedule may be updated.

**Example (non-industry / IIT or Foundation):**
> Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) will be performed at the RI-MUHC McConnell Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM) at the non-industry rate of \$212 per test. With 2 tests per participant across 45 participants (90 tests total), the CIM subtotal for this service is \$19,080.

**Example (industry-sponsored):**
> Cardiopulmonary exercise testing (CPET) will be performed at CIM at the industry rate of \$354 per test. With 2 tests per participant across 45 participants (90 tests total), the CIM subtotal for this service is \$31,860.

**Example:**

## Participant costs

Reviewers pay close attention to the distinction between reimbursements and stipends. Get this right in the justification.

**Reimbursements** (transportation, parking) offset out-of-pocket costs and are not income:

> Participants will receive a \$15 parking reimbursement and a \$10 transit reimbursement per visit to offset out-of-pocket costs associated with study participation. These amounts reflect actual costs and are not compensation. With 6 visits per participant across 45 participants, total reimbursement costs are \$6,750.

**Stipends or honoraria** recognize participant time and require a separate ethics justification. McGill Research policy caps participant payments at \$500 per participant per calendar year; amounts above this threshold may trigger CRA T4A reporting obligations. The tool will flag the per-participant annual total if it exceeds this amount — if that happens, confirm with your grants officer whether a T4A process is in place before submitting the budget.

> Participants will receive a \$50 honorarium per completed visit in recognition of the time burden of study participation. The amount and its justification were reviewed and approved by the [REB name] as part of the ethics submission. With 6 visits per participant across 45 participants, total honorarium costs are \$13,500.

## Patient Partners & Engagement

CIHR iKT grants require an explicit justification for patient partner costs. Non-iKT grants may still include these if partners are involved in study conduct. Distinguish patient partners (community members with lived experience contributing to the research) from enrolled study participants.

**Example:**
> Two patient partners will contribute to protocol review, participant-facing materials, and knowledge translation activities over the 3-year study. Partners will receive honoraria of \$25/hour for an estimated 20 hours/year, totalling \$1,500/year (\$4,500 over 3 years). Travel reimbursement is budgeted at \$50/meeting × 4 meetings/year = \$400/year. Total patient partner costs: \$5,700.

## Knowledge Translation

For CIHR grants, open-access publication fees, conference registration and travel for dissemination, and KT materials go under **Knowledge Translation** — not Other. Enter these in the Knowledge Translation section of Study Costs so they map correctly in the CIHR Budget View.

**Example:**
> Open-access publication fees are budgeted at \$2,500 per article for an estimated 3 publications (\$7,500 total). The PI and CRC will present findings at one national conference in Year 3: \$2,000 airfare + \$800 accommodation + \$400 registration × 2 attendees = \$6,400. Total Knowledge Translation: \$13,900.

---

## Software and data management

REDCap is the most common item here. Justify the annual cost and number of years. If your institution covers REDCap centrally, note that and omit it.

**Example:**
> REDCap database licence and setup: \$1,200 setup fee + \$600/year × 3 years = \$3,000. REDCap is used for electronic data capture, query management, and audit trail. Hosted by RI-MUHC IT under the institutional licence.

Translation services and recruitment advertising are also entered under Other Costs and should each have a brief justification noting the language(s) or media channel and the basis for the estimate.

## Travel

**CIHR does not have a separate Travel category.** Where you classify travel determines which budget line it falls under — and where you *enter* it in the tool determines how it maps:

- **Dissemination travel** — conferences, stakeholder meetings, knowledge translation events → enter in the **Knowledge Translation** section of Study Costs → maps to CIHR **Knowledge Translation**
- **Operational travel** — monitoring visits, site initiation visits, investigator meetings → enter in the **Travel** section of Study Costs → maps to CIHR **Other**

If you enter operational travel in the wrong section it will be misclassified in the CIHR Budget View.

**Example (operational):**
> The PI will attend the sponsor's annual investigator meeting in Year 1. Estimated at \$1,200 economy airfare plus \$250 accommodation: \$1,450 per trip. Classified under Other (operational travel).

**Example (dissemination):**
> The PI and CRC will present study findings at [conference] in Year 3. Estimated at \$2,000 airfare, \$800 accommodation, and \$400 registration per attendee: \$6,400 total. Classified under Knowledge Translation.

## Contingency

State the complexity tier and the specific reasons your study falls in it. The tool's **Review** section calculates the contingency amount — use the rationale below to write the justification.

| Tier | Recommended rate | Range | Typical study types |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 9% | 8–10% | Observational, retrospective, low-visit-count studies with a stable protocol |
| Moderate | 11% | 10–12% | Prospective interventional, Phase II–III, multi-visit studies |
| High | 13.5% | 12–15% | Phase I, first-in-human, complex interventional, or studies with elevated amendment risk |

**Example:**
> A 13.5% contingency reserve is included, reflecting the high complexity of this Phase I dose-escalation study. Unanticipated costs may arise from protocol amendments, dose committee decisions requiring additional assessments, and unscheduled participant safety visits.

## CIHR budget category mapping

The tool's **CIHR Budget View** button (in the Review section) maps your costs to CIHR's four required categories. Use those totals when completing the budget tables in ResearchNet.

| CIHR category | What goes here |
|---|---|
| Salaries and Stipends | All personnel — research staff, students, postdocs |
| Consumables | CIM services, laboratory tests, pharmacy, materials and supplies |
| Knowledge Translation | Dissemination travel, conference fees, open-access publication costs, patient partner costs |
| Other | Participant costs, operational travel, contingency, other direct costs |

**Formatting requirements:** All amounts in Canadian dollars. Grand total must be a multiple of \$5,000 (round up). Each category requires a justification narrative.

## REB fees

The tool's **Study Profile** section calculates REB fees automatically from the MSSS fee schedule and shows the breakdown. Use those figures directly in the budget narrative.

Whether to include REB fees in the funded total depends on the funder — some count them as direct costs, others require them listed separately or covered institutionally. Use the **Include REB fees in total?** toggle in Study Profile and note your choice in the justification.

**Example (fees included):**
> REB fees are calculated per the MSSS fee schedule (April 2025) and included as a direct cost: \$634 scientific review + \$6,348 ethical review + \$1,904 site authorization + \$1,268 annual ethics monitoring (2 renewals × \$634) + \$1,268 annual authorization monitoring (2 renewals × \$634) = \$11,422. These fees are non-refundable regardless of REB outcome.

> **Note:** The MSSS schedule includes two separate annual renewal fees: fee #3 (ethics monitoring, capped at 10 sites) and fee #6 (authorization monitoring, per participating institution). Both apply at each renewal. For multicentre studies, fee #3 is capped at 10 institutions but fee #6 applies to all sites — use the **Study Profile** REB calculator in the tool, which handles both automatically.

For the full MSSS fee schedule and overhead rates, see [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/budgets-contracts).
