As a <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crc">Clinical Research Coordinator</Tooltip>, you are the operational backbone of site-level trial management. Unlike the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">Qualified Investigator</Tooltip>, the CRC role has no regulatory definition in Canadian law — there is no national licence, no regulated title, and no statutory scope of practice. What defines your role is what has been delegated to you on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> and what your own professional credentials permit you to do independently.

<Note>
**Training comes first.** Every team member must complete institutional training before appearing on the Task Delegation Log. See [Training Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements) to confirm your level.
</Note>

## Institutional setup

When you are hired, you receive the RI-MUHC Research Staff Handbook by email. It covers your institutional orientation: Portal access, ID card, payroll and Logibec, benefits, mandatory training, and how the RI-MUHC is organized. The items below are the key setup tasks that apply to you as a CRC. Complete them in your first days.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set up your Portal account">
    Your login credentials arrive by email. The Portal is where you access applications, submit IT requests, complete training, book rooms, and manage your profile.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get your ID card">
    Your supervisor requests this through the Portal. Your card gives you access to RI-MUHC sites and specific rooms. At the Glen site, tap your card at a main entrance before interior doors will work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up Logibec eEspresso">
    The payroll system — approve your timesheet every pay period. When recording training time, use the code **TRAIN** for [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) courses and **JOBTR** for on-the-job training. These codes ensure your training hours count toward Quebec Law 90 reporting. Submit training certificates to **training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete mandatory institutional training">
    Five courses required for all members of The Institute in TalentLMS: Let's Be Allies (Foundations in EDI), Cybersecurity Basics at The Institute, Prevention of Violence and Psychological Harassment in the Workplace and Research Space, Creating a Culture of Care, and MUHC Emergency Measures. These are institutional requirements, separate from your clinical research training below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the Research Staff Handbook">
    Covers benefits (sick/personal days, group insurance, pension plan, vacation), the Employee Assistance Program (EAP — free, confidential support available 24/7), Code of Conduct, and how the RI-MUHC is structured. Keep it as a reference.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Key contacts to know from day one: The Research Facilitator helps you navigate clinical research processes and procedures at the RI-MUHC. For compliance questions, contact <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> at **QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca**. For training questions, contact **training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca**. For IT issues, submit an IT Rescue ticket through the Portal.

<Note>
  **New to clinical research?** The [Clinical Research 101](/kb/clinical-research-101) and [Study Lifecycle](/kb/study-lifecycle) articles will give you a quick orientation to how clinical trials work, who is involved, and how research is regulated in Canada and Quebec. For a map of what happens once a study is active, see [Study Conduct Overview](/kb/conduct-overview).
</Note>

### Your first weeks

While you work through your institutional and clinical research training, use your first weeks to learn the local environment you'll be working in. Ask your supervisor or <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> to help you with the following.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Meet the clinical departments you'll work with">
    Introductions to pharmacy, laboratory, radiology, nursing, and any service departments involved in your studies. Know who your contacts are and how to reach them before your first study visit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Shadow an experienced CRC">
    If possible, observe a colleague during a study visit, a monitoring visit, or a consent session. Seeing the workflow in practice before you do it yourself is one of the most effective ways to learn.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Learn the physical space">
    Know where your study visits happen, where the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> binders are kept, and where you'll be working day-to-day. If your studies run at multiple sites (Glen, MGH), visit each one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Join Community Connect">
    Sessions hosted by the Research Facilitator covering guidance and context on clinical research at the RI-MUHC. See [Community Connect](/kb/community-connect) for upcoming sessions. A good way to connect with peers, ask questions, and stay informed about institutional changes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  **Working at CIM?** If your studies run through the Centre for Innovative Medicine, you have additional CIM-specific SOPs, Work Instructions, and facility access requirements. See the [CIM Staff Guide](/cim/researcher-guide) and speak with your supervisor about CIM-specific training.
</Note>

## Clinical research training

Before you can be delegated tasks on a study, you need SOP training, regulatory certification (if your level requires it), and a signed Task Delegation Log. These requirements apply to everyone — regardless of role.

Training requirements depend on your study type. Drug trials (Level I), device studies (Level II), and NHP studies (Level III) follow the SOP-CR series (30–31 SOPs) with a 2-year renewal. Prospective observational (Level IV) and retrospective studies (Level V) follow the SOP-LR series with a 5-year renewal. [External certifications](/training/external-certifications) (<Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, reading, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>, Division 5, ISO 14155) are only required for certain levels as noted below. For the full matrix, see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete GCP certification (Levels I and II only)">
    ICH Good Clinical Practice training through any recognized provider — the RI-MUHC has a CITI licence (free for staff), but other providers are accepted. Certificates valid for 3 years from other providers are recognized for 2 years at the RI-MUHC. For Level II (medical device studies), completing ICH-GCP satisfies the requirement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete Health Canada Division 5 training (Level I only)">
    Required if your study involves drugs under a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application</Tooltip> — biologics, gene/cell therapies, vaccines, radiopharmaceuticals, or NHPs. Available through CITI or other recognized providers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete ISO 14155 training (Level II only)">
    Required if your study involves medical devices under an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">Investigational Testing Authorization</Tooltip>. ISO 14155 is the GCP equivalent for device studies.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the SOP Reader">
    Read and acknowledge every SOP assigned to your training level in TalentLMS. You can read SOPs on this site at any time, but your initial reading must be completed and tracked through the Reader in [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) to count toward your compliance record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the Competency Assessment">
    Scenario-based assessment certifying your understanding of the SOPs. Two paths: SOP-CR (Levels I–III, renews every 2 years) and SOP-LR (Levels IV–V, renews every 5 years).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Be added to the Task Delegation Log">
    Once your training is complete, the PI adds you to the TDL specifying which tasks you are authorized to perform. You cannot perform any study-related task until this is signed by both you and the PI. See [Building Your Research Team](/kb/building-your-team).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete protocol-specific training">
    The sponsor or PI provides training on the specific study protocol before you begin any study tasks. This is separate from your institutional SOP training. Document all training on the Training Log.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  **Keeping current:** When a specific SOP is updated, only that unit resets in the Reader — you re-read that SOP only, not the full set. Your Competency Assessment renews on a 2-year (SOP-CR) or 5-year (SOP-LR) cycle. GCP certification (Levels I–II) renews every 2 years. If your training lapses, you must be removed from the Task Delegation Log until it's current. If you are on leave for more than 6 months, your training must be renewed before returning to study tasks ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002)).
</Info>

### The Task Delegation Log and scope limitations

The Task Delegation Log defines what you are authorized to do on each study. Your PI's countersignature confirms your qualification for those tasks — but it does not transfer their accountability to you, and it does not transfer yours to them. For managing team changes during an active study — adding and removing members, leaves, and QI/PI changes — see [Team Management](/kb/team-management).

<Warning>
  **Scope limitation for non-nurse CRCs:** Quebec's *Code des professions* reserves specific clinical acts to licensed nurses. Non-nurse CRCs cannot perform blood draws, administer medication, perform patient assessments, or carry out any act reserved to nurses — regardless of what appears on the Task Delegation Log or the study protocol. If your study requires these tasks, a licensed nurse or CRC-Nurse must be delegated those activities. See the [Research Nurse Orientation](/kb/roles/nurse-orientation) for details.
</Warning>

Understanding how your PI's accountability is structured — what only they can sign, what they must oversee, and what they can delegate — helps you work effectively within the delegation framework. See the [Investigator Orientation](/kb/roles/pi-orientation) for the full picture.

### Documentation and the ISF

Setting up and maintaining the ISF (Levels I–III) or study file (Levels IV–V), essential documents, and study-level records. **All levels.**

The ISF is the site's regulatory binder — it contains every essential document required by ICH GCP. You set it up before the first participant is enrolled and maintain it throughout the study. It must be inspection-ready at all times: current protocol, REB approvals, delegation log, training records, CVs, correspondence, and financial disclosures.

- Set up and organize the ISF according to ICH GCP requirements
- File essential documents before, during, and after the study
- Maintain the delegation log, training records, and CV collection
- Track protocol versions and ensure the correct version is in use
- Prepare documentation for archiving at close-out

### Informed consent

Explaining the study, obtaining voluntary agreement, and documenting consent correctly under Quebec law. **Levels I–IV.**

Consent is both a document and a process. You explain the study's purpose, procedures, risks, and alternatives. The participant must have time to consider, ask questions, and decide freely. In Quebec, consent is governed by the Civil Code (Articles 20, 21, 24, 25), and specific rules apply to minors and incapable persons. You document the process in source records and ensure the signed <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> is filed in the ISF.

- Explain the study to potential participants and answer their questions
- Obtain and document written informed consent before any study procedures
- Re-consent participants when protocol amendments change the risk–benefit profile
- Apply consent requirements for minors (Art. 21 CCQ) and incapable adults
- Maintain the ICF version log and ensure only the current approved version is used

### Recruitment and screening

Identifying potential participants, applying eligibility criteria, and enrolling those who qualify. **Levels I–IV.**

You screen potential participants against inclusion/exclusion criteria, coordinate with clinical teams to identify candidates, track screening and enrolment numbers, and ensure all recruitment materials have REB approval. Screening may involve reviewing medical records (OACIS), speaking with patients directly, and coordinating with referring physicians.

- Pre-screen candidates using medical records and referral lists
- Apply inclusion and exclusion criteria to determine eligibility
- Track and report recruitment and retention metrics
- Ensure all recruitment materials (ads, flyers, letters) are REB-approved
- Schedule and coordinate screening visits

### Data collection and integrity

Collecting, entering, and verifying clinical data following ALCOA+ principles. **All levels.**

Every data point you record must be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, and accurate (ALCOA+). You enter data from source documents into CRFs or EDC systems, resolve data queries from the sponsor or CRO, and maintain source documentation that supports every <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> entry. Data corrections must follow Good Documentation Practice — single line through the error, date, initials, and reason.

- Create and maintain source documents for each participant
- Enter data into paper CRFs or electronic data capture (EDC) systems
- Resolve data queries from the sponsor or monitoring team
- Ensure CRF entries match source documents (source data verification)
- Follow Good Documentation Practice for corrections

### Safety reporting

Recognizing, documenting, and reporting adverse events within required timelines. **Levels I–III.**

You are often the first person to learn about an <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip>. Your job is to recognize it, document it accurately, assess whether it meets the threshold for serious (<Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip>) or unexpected (<Tooltip tip="Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction — unexpected in nature, severity, or frequency; must be reported to Health Canada and the REB." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip>), and report it to the PI for medical assessment. SAEs must be reported to the sponsor within 24 hours. You also log AEs in the CRF, track concomitant medications, and ensure the REB receives reportable event notifications within required timelines.

- Recognize and document adverse events at every study visit
- Assess whether an AE meets SAE criteria and notify the PI immediately
- Complete SAE report forms and submit to the sponsor within 24 hours
- Log AEs in the CRF and update concomitant medication records
- File SUSAR notifications from the sponsor in the ISF

### Protocol deviations

Identifying, documenting, classifying, and reporting departures from the approved protocol, GCP, or institutional SOPs. **All levels.**

Deviations happen. The question is whether you identify them early, document them correctly, and report them within required timelines. Common CRC-related deviations include missed visit windows, consent timing issues, incomplete screen failure documentation, and delayed monitoring letter responses.

- Identify deviations during routine work and escalate to your PI immediately
- Document what happened, when, why, and what corrective action was taken
- Implement CAPAs to prevent recurrence

### Monitoring and inspections

Preparing for and supporting sponsor monitoring visits, audits, and regulatory inspections. **Levels I–III.**

Monitors (CRAs) visit the site to verify data against source documents, check regulatory compliance, and confirm participant safety. You prepare documentation for the visit, provide access to source records, respond to findings, and implement corrective actions. The same documentation standards apply to internal audits and [Health Canada inspections](/kb/hc-inspection-guide) — but inspections are unannounced.

- Prepare and organize source documents, CRFs, and the ISF before visits
- Provide access to participant records for source data verification
- Review monitoring visit reports and complete follow-up actions
- Implement CAPAs from findings
- Maintain inspection-ready documentation at all times

### Investigational products

Receiving, storing, dispensing, and accounting for study drugs and devices. **Levels I–III.**

If your study involves an investigational drug or device, you maintain accountability records for receipt, storage, dispensing, and return or destruction. Storage conditions (temperature, light, humidity) must be monitored and documented. At the MUHC, pharmacy handles dispensing for most drug studies, but you coordinate with them and maintain site-level accountability logs.

- Receive and inventory shipments of investigational products
- Monitor and document storage conditions
- Maintain drug/device accountability logs
- Coordinate with pharmacy for dispensing
- Arrange return or destruction of unused products at close-out

### Biological specimens

Collecting, processing, labelling, shipping, and maintaining inventory of research samples. **Levels I–III.**

You collect, process, label, and ship biological samples according to the protocol and lab manual. Specimens must be handled following IATA TDG regulations for dangerous goods transport. You maintain specimen inventory logs, coordinate with labs (internal and external), and ensure proper chain-of-custody documentation.

- Collect and process samples according to the lab manual
- Label specimens with correct participant and visit identifiers
- Package and ship samples following IATA TDG requirements
- Maintain specimen inventory and chain-of-custody logs
- Coordinate with the central lab and site laboratory

### Budgets, contracts, and study finances

Participant reimbursements, expense tracking, invoicing, and understanding the study budget. **All levels.**

Even as a junior CRC, you interact with study finances: processing participant reimbursements, tracking expenses against the study budget, preparing invoices for sponsor milestones, and ensuring contracts are signed before work begins. As you gain experience, you may contribute to budget development, negotiate with sponsors on per-patient costs, and manage financial reporting.

- Process participant reimbursements and maintain payment records
- Track study expenses and submit invoices for sponsor milestones
- Ensure contracts and agreements are signed before study activities begin
- Understand the study budget structure and per-patient cost breakdown
- Coordinate with Research Grants and finance teams on cost centre management

### Study close-out

Final data reconciliation, document archiving, IP return, REB closure notification, and retention requirements. **Levels I–IV.**

Close-out is a structured process, not just the end of enrolment. You resolve outstanding data queries, complete final CRF pages, return or destroy unused investigational products, archive the ISF and all essential documents, notify the REB of study completion, and ensure participant records meet retention requirements. For Division 5 studies, the retention period is 15 years.

- Resolve all outstanding data queries and complete final CRF entries
- Coordinate drug/device return or destruction with pharmacy
- Prepare the ISF and essential documents for archiving
- Submit the Study Completion Report to the REB via Nagano
- Prepare for and support the close-out monitoring visit
- Confirm document retention requirements are met (15 years for Division 5 studies). See [Research Integrity](/kb/governance/research-integrity) for archiving and retention obligations.

### Systems and software

The platforms you'll use daily — submissions, data capture, clinical records, and training. **All levels.**

CRCs work across several systems depending on the study. Some are institutional (Nagano, OACIS, REDCap, TalentLMS); others are sponsor-provided EDC systems (Medidata Rave, Oracle InForm, Veeva). Protocol-specific training on sponsor systems is provided during study startup.
