## Understanding the role

Level V studies — retrospective chart reviews and studies using previously anonymized biobanked samples — centre on data extraction and privacy rather than participant contact. Your work is database management, accurate chart abstraction, de-identification, and REB maintenance.

This is a different operational profile from all other levels. There is typically no recruitment, no study visits, no <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip>, no monitoring visit, and no <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reporting. The core competencies are: accurate data abstraction from medical records, rigorous de-identification, REDCap or database management, privacy compliance under Quebec law, and <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> maintenance.

Your training level is **SOP-LR** (9 SOPs, 5-year renewal cycle). No <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> certification is required. Your authorization comes through the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>.

<Warning>
**Consent waiver is not automatic.** Most retrospective chart review studies operate under a waiver of consent granted by the REB. However, under <Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act to modernize personal information protection law (in force Sept 2023). Requires EFVP before sharing personal data without consent." headline="Law 25 / Loi 25" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#law-25">Loi 25</Tooltip>, whether a waiver is granted now depends on the outcome of the <Tooltip tip="Required under Quebec Law 25 before communicating personal information without consent for research. Non-compliance: up to $25M or 4% of worldwide turnover." headline="ÉFVP — Privacy Impact Assessment" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#efvp">ÉFVP</Tooltip> (privacy impact assessment). The legal default is always to obtain consent. Access to identifiable data without consent requires approval from the ÉFVP committee. A waiver is granted when the committee determines that obtaining consent is not feasible — for example, a chart review of 500 patients from 2000 to 2010, where many patients may have died, been lost to follow-up, or have outdated contact information. In contrast, if you are reviewing records for a small number of recent patients, the committee may determine it is reasonable to contact those patients and obtain their consent. Confirm the consent requirement with your <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> and the REB before beginning data extraction.
</Warning>

If the ÉFVP committee determines that consent is feasible, you will need to obtain informed consent from participants before accessing their records. In that case, the consent and recruitment sections of the [Level IV CRC Guide](/kb/roles/crc-guide-iv) apply to the consent component of your study.

## Data extraction

Data extraction from medical records is the core of your work. Accuracy and consistency are critical — errors at this stage propagate through the entire analysis.

- **Follow the extraction plan exactly.** Extract only the variables specified in the protocol. If you encounter relevant data that is not in the plan, do not extract it without PI approval and, if necessary, a protocol amendment.
- **Use participant IDs, not names.** Never enter patient names or health card numbers into the research database. Maintain the identification key (linking participant IDs to chart numbers) separately and securely.
- **Document your extraction process.** Record which records you reviewed, when, and any issues encountered (missing data, ambiguous entries, conflicting information in the chart).
- **Handle missing data consistently.** Decide in advance (with your PI) how missing data will be coded. Do not leave fields blank without documentation of why the data is missing.
- **Verify a sample.** For large extractions, have a second person verify a random sample of extracted records against the source charts. Document the verification process and the error rate.
- **Corrections follow GDP.** If you discover an error in your extraction, correct it in the database using the audit trail (in REDCap, use the Data History feature). Do not overwrite without documentation.

## Privacy and data protection

See also: [Privacy and Confidentiality](/kb/privacy) | [Data Security](/kb/data-security)

Privacy is the highest-risk area for retrospective studies. You are accessing identifiable health information without participant consent. The legal and institutional frameworks that permit this are specific and must be followed precisely.

- An **ÉFVP** (privacy impact assessment under Loi 25) is likely required for any retrospective study that accesses identifiable PHI. Confirm with your PI and QA before starting.
- Your **OACIS access must be authorized and auditable.** Access logs may be reviewed — access only the records and data elements specified in your protocol.
- **De-identification is not optional.** The research database must contain participant IDs only. The key linking IDs to chart numbers must be stored separately, with access restricted to authorized team members.
- If data is shared outside the MUHC — to a collaborating institution, a statistician, or a cloud analysis platform — confirm that appropriate **data transfer agreements** are in place and that the data is de-identified before transfer unless the agreement specifically authorizes identifiable data.
- **Cross-border transfers** (data leaving Quebec or Canada) have additional requirements under Loi 25. Confirm with the Privacy Office or your PI before any transfer.
- At study completion, confirm the **disposition of identifiable data**: is the identification key destroyed, retained for a specified period, or transferred? Document this in the study file.

## Ongoing management

- **Annual REB renewal** — submit via Nagano before approval expires. Include updated progress, any changes to the data extraction scope, and current personnel.
- **Protocol amendments** — if the data extraction plan changes (new variables, expanded patient population, new data sources), submit an amendment via Nagano before implementing the change.
- **Staff changes** — update the delegation log when team members join or leave. Incoming team members must have current SOP training before accessing study data.
- **SOP updates** — complete updated Reader units in [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) when they reset.
- **Competency Assessment** — renews on a 5-year cycle (SOP-LR).
- **Leave of absence:** if you are on leave for more than 6 months, your training must be renewed before returning to study tasks. The delegation log must reflect that you are undelegated during leave ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002)).

## Protocol deviations

Deviations can happen on retrospective studies — extracting data outside the approved protocol scope, accessing records for patients not in the approved population, or failing to submit an amendment before changing the extraction plan. The process is the same as for any study: identify, document, report, correct.

- **Identify** deviations as they occur and notify your PI immediately.
- **Document** what happened, when, why, and what corrective action was taken.
- **Report** major deviations to the REB via Nagano. Minor deviations are documented in the study file.
- **Correct** the underlying issue — for example, if you extracted data for a patient outside the approved cohort, document it and confirm with your PI whether the data must be excluded from analysis.

## Close-out

- Confirm all data extraction is complete and the database is final.
- Submit the **Study Completion Report** to the REB via Nagano.
- Record end dates on the delegation log for all team members.
- Determine the **disposition of the identification key** — destroy it, retain it for a specified period, or transfer responsibility. Document this decision and execute it.
- Archive the study file. Retention requirements follow institutional policy and Quebec law — the 15-year Division 5 requirement does not apply to Level V studies. Confirm with your PI and QA.
