## Understanding the role

Level IV studies are prospective observational, educational, or non-invasive intervention studies. You are still working with human participants, still obtaining consent, and still subject to REB oversight and SOP compliance — but the operational profile is different from interventional trials.

There is typically no commercial sponsor, no investigational product, no CRA monitoring your site, and no SAE reporting to a sponsor. Your PI is usually both the investigator and the person responsible for the study's conduct. Data collection may involve surveys, interviews, non-invasive measurements, chart abstraction alongside clinical care, or observation of routine procedures. You also serve as a **participant advocate** — even in observational studies, you are the person closest to the participant's experience.

Your training level is **SOP-LR** (18 SOPs, 5-year renewal cycle). No GCP certification is required. Your authorization to work on the study comes through the Task Delegation Log, same as all levels.

### Your training requirements

| Level | Study type | SOP series | GCP | Other certs | Renewal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IV | Prospective observational, educational, non-invasive interventions | SOP-LR (18 SOPs) | — | — | 5 years |

No external certifications (GCP, Division 5, ISO) are required. For the full training matrix, see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/kb/training).

### On the study team

- **QI/PI:** carries ultimate legal and regulatory accountability for the study. You execute; they oversee. Your PI's countersignature on the delegation log confirms your qualification for your tasks.
- **Sub-Investigator (Sub-I):** assists the PI and can serve as PI backup if they hold RI-MUHC Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status (Researcher Status is valid for Level IV since no medical oversight is required).
- **Research Assistant:** may support data collection, survey administration, participant scheduling, or data entry under CRC or PI supervision.
- **Research Nurse:** if your study involves clinical procedures (blood draws, vital signs), a research nurse handles tasks reserved under Quebec's _Code des professions_.

### Institutional contacts

- **QA department:** contact QA when you are uncertain about compliance — before a deviation occurs, not after. QA verifies that training, Research Privileges or Researcher Status, and delegation documentation are current at every Nagano submission.
- **Research Facilitator:** your entry point for navigating RI-MUHC processes and the Nagano submission workflow.
- **Research Agreements Office:** reviews contracts and agreements where applicable.

### MUHC service departments

These departments may conduct feasibility reviews depending on your study. Answer the F11 form accurately — underreporting delays MUHC Authorization.

- **Nursing services:** if your study requires MUHC nursing resources, a nursing feasibility review is triggered.
- **Laboratory (OPTILAB-MUHC):** if your study requires hospital laboratory services (e.g. blood collection, sample processing), a laboratory feasibility review is required.
- **Director of Professional Services (DPS):** approves use of MUHC clinical resources (imaging, medical records, other services) required by the study.
- **Privacy and information security:** if your study involves identifiable personal health information or uses software platforms for data collection, the ÉFVP (privacy impact assessment) may be required.

<Note>
**If you also work on interventional studies:** Your training level is set by your highest-risk active study. If you hold both a Level IV observational and a Level I drug trial, your training requirement is Level I. See the [CRC Guide — Interventional Studies](/kb/roles/crc-guide) for that content.
</Note>

## Part 1: Study lifecycle

A chronological walkthrough from study preparation through close-out for prospective observational studies.

## Study preparation

Before your first participant contact, the following must be in place.

<Warning>
**MUHC Authorization before any study procedures.** The Personne mandatée must issue the MUHC Authorization letter. File it before any recruitment, screening, or consent activity.
</Warning>

- **REB approval and MUHC Authorization** — submit via Nagano (F11-H or appropriate form type). The Authorization letter must be filed before any participant contact.
- **Protocol review** — read the full protocol and understand the data collection procedures, visit schedule (if any), and participant population.
- **Delegation log** — signed by your PI with your specific tasks listed. File your CV and TalentLMS Competency Assessment certificate.
- **Data collection tools** — set up REDCap databases, case report forms, survey instruments, or interview guides. Confirm they match the protocol and have been tested.
- **Study file** — while a full ISF structure may not be required for all observational studies, maintain a study file containing: the protocol, REB approval letter, MUHC Authorization, delegation log, consent forms, and any correspondence. Your PI and QA can advise on what's required for your specific study.

## Consent and enrolment

See also: Consent in Quebec | SOP-CR-008

Level IV studies involve participant contact, so consent applies. The same Quebec-specific rules govern the process.

- **Use the current REB-approved ICF.** Version control is your responsibility.
- Under **Bill 96**, consent forms at public institutions must be available in French. Provide the French version to French-speaking participants.
- Give the participant adequate time to read, ask questions, and decide. Document the process in the source record.
- Confirm **who conducts the consent discussion** on your delegation log. For studies involving clinical decisions or medical risk, the PI may need to be present.
- **Incapacitated adults (Art. 21 CCQ)** — the same hard legal requirements apply as for interventional studies. Consent from the person with legal authority AND MSSS-designated REB authorization are required.
- Some observational studies may qualify for a **waiver or alteration of consent** — for example, observation of routine care where the research adds no intervention. This must be approved by the REB; you cannot make this determination independently.

For eligibility assessment, documentation, screening logs, and enrolment logs, see Recruitment and Screening in the Domain Reference below.

## Active conduct

See also: Data Integrity | SOP-CR-014

Active conduct for observational studies centres on data collection, documentation integrity, and participant management — without the drug accountability, monitoring visits, and safety reporting infrastructure of interventional trials.

### Data collection and integrity

- Follow the protocol's data collection procedures exactly. Even without a sponsor CRA checking your work, the data must be accurate, complete, and verifiable.
- Complete data entry promptly. For REDCap studies, resolve any automated validation errors as they arise.
- Corrections follow Good Documentation Practice: single line through the error, correct value, initial and date. For electronic systems, use the audit trail.
- Your PI must review data periodically — document this review.

### Safety and adverse events

Level IV studies typically do not involve interventions that cause adverse events. However:

- If a participant reports a health concern during a study visit or interaction, document it and notify your PI.
- If the study protocol requires safety reporting (some observational studies do), follow the protocol's reporting procedures.
- Report any **unanticipated problems involving risk to participants** to the REB, regardless of whether the study has a formal AE reporting requirement.

### Ongoing management

- **Staff changes:** update the delegation log when team members join or leave.
- **SOP updates:** complete updated SOP Reader units in TalentLMS when they reset.
- **Protocol amendments:** submit via Nagano, document team training, update the study file with the new approved version.
- **Leave of absence:** if you are on leave for more than 6 months, your training must be renewed before returning to study tasks. The delegation log must reflect that you are undelegated during leave (SOP-CR-002).

## Annual renewal

- Submit the annual renewal via Nagano before REB approval expires. A lapse means you must stop all participant contact until renewed.
- The renewal includes a progress report, updated participant counts, and a current personnel list.
- File the new REB approval letter in the study file and notify the PI.
- Your Competency Assessment renews on a **5-year cycle** (SOP-LR). Watch the expiry date alongside the REB renewal.

## Close-out

- Confirm all data collection is complete and all data queries or validation errors are resolved.
- Submit the **Study Completion Report** to the REB via Nagano.
- Record end dates for all team members on the delegation log.
- Archive the study file. Retention requirements depend on the funding source, institutional policy, and Quebec law — confirm with your PI and QA. The 15-year Division 5 requirement does not apply to Level IV studies.

## Recruitment and screening

Recruitment for observational studies often relies on clinic referrals, patient registries, or chart review to identify eligible participants. The same documentation rules apply: maintain a screening log, document eligibility criteria assessment, and file screen failures with reasons.

- Pre-screen using OACIS or clinic records. Confirm your access is authorized for this purpose.
- All recruitment materials must be REB-approved before use.
- Eligibility criteria assessment — some criteria may require PI judgment. Verify your delegation.
- Record every screened participant on the Screening Log, including screen failures.

    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - How do you build referral relationships with clinical teams for an observational study?
    - What are the most common reasons for screen failure on our current studies?

## Privacy and data protection

Privacy is especially important for observational studies because you are often working with identifiable health information from clinical records. Quebec's Loi 25 applies fully.

- If your study accesses identifiable personal health information, an **ÉFVP** (privacy impact assessment) may be required before data collection begins.
- Your OACIS access must be authorized and limited to what is necessary for the study.
- Do not store identifiable data on personal devices or unapproved systems.
- Use participant IDs, not names, in all study documents. Maintain the identification key separately and securely.
- If data is shared outside the MUHC, confirm that appropriate data agreements are in place. Cross-border transfers have additional requirements under Loi 25.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Privacy and data protection">
    **Review:** Read the data management plan for one of your studies. Identify what PHI is collected and where it is stored.

    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - Has an ÉFVP been completed for this study?
    - What systems am I authorized to access, and where is that documented?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Protocol deviations

Deviations happen on observational studies too — missed data collection windows, consent issues, eligibility documentation gaps. The process is the same: identify, document, report, correct.

- **Identify** deviations during routine work and escalate to your PI immediately.
- **Document** what happened, when, why, and what corrective action was taken.
- **Report** major deviations to the REB. Minor deviations are documented and filed.
- **Correct** the underlying issue to prevent recurrence.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Protocol deviations">
    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - What are the most common deviations on our observational studies?
    - When does a deviation need to be reported to the REB vs. just documented internally?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Data management

For most Level IV studies, data management centres on REDCap or a similar institutional platform rather than a sponsor-provided EDC.

- Complete REDCap training before entering data. Training is available through CORD.
- Enter data promptly after each collection event. REDCap tracks entry timestamps.
- Resolve validation errors and missing data as they arise — do not let them accumulate.
- Your PI must sign off on data before any analysis or publication. Confirm the process for your study.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Data management">
    **Review:** If your study uses REDCap, explore the data quality module and the logging feature. Understand how the audit trail works.

    **Discuss with your supervisor:**
    - Who built the REDCap database and who do I contact if I find an issue?
    - What's our process for data review and sign-off before analysis?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
