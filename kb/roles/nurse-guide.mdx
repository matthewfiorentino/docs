This guide covers the clinical procedures that require your nursing licence on interventional studies (Levels I–III) — the layer that sits on top of the <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crc">CRC's</Tooltip> coordination work. For licensed nurses on a study's main <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>, whether you also coordinate the study or perform clinical tasks only.

## How to use this guide

This guide covers the nursing-specific operational layer for interventional studies: pre-visit preparation for IP visits, IP administration, specimen collection, clinical monitoring during visits, AE assessment within nursing scope, and between-visit contact.

**Scope.** Levels I–III cover drug, medical device, and natural health product studies. The dominant pattern at the RI-MUHC — and the focus of this guide — is IP administration visits (Level I drug studies, Level III NHP studies). For Level II medical device studies, the visit workflow, clinical monitoring, and AE capture sections still apply; IP-specific content is replaced by device-specific procedures governed by ISO 14155 and the study protocol. Refer to [SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024) for device-specific requirements.

<Info>
  **If you also coordinate the study (<Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip>-Nurse):** the [CRC Guide — Interventional](/kb/roles/crc-guide) covers the full operational picture — study setup, <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip>, consent, screening, documentation, monitoring visits, close-out, team coordination. Use it as your primary reference and this page for the clinical layer on top.

<Note>

</Note>

For the prerequisites — OIIQ licence, CPR, reserved acts scope, oncology competency, and institutional training — see the [Nurse Training Pathway](/kb/roles/nurse-pathway). The content here applies regardless of setting: outpatient research clinics, ward-based studies, [DCT/at-home visits](/kb/decentralized-trials), or the [CIM](/cim/services). Where specific CIM Work Instructions formalize a practice covered here, it is flagged inline.

## Before the visit

The CRC Guide's pre-visit checklist applies to you. Three nurse-specific additions:

- **Pre-visit safety check for IP visits** — for any visit involving an investigational product, the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip> confirms IP side effects, verifies emergency equipment is ready, and confirms qualified staff are available. This is a general expectation regardless of setting. At CIM, it is formalized as WI-CIM-AD001 Appendix 1 (Safety Checklist Prior to Study Visit), completed before each such visit. Other settings follow the equivalent planning practice without the specific WI template. If you are aware of any gap, flag it to the QI before the visit begins.
- **Review the <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip>** — know the IP's known side effects and safety signals. You should be able to anticipate what to watch for during and after administration.
- **Confirm pharmacy has dispensed IP** — the prepared dose is correct, labelled with the participant's study number, and not expired. Never administer IP that has not come through Pharmacy's dispensing workflow, unless the study has an approved nurse dispensing waiver on file (see [Nurse Training Pathway](/kb/roles/nurse-pathway)).

## Investigational product administration

In Quebec, IP administration is governed by both Health Canada Division 5 and provincial pharmacy legislation. The MUHC Research Pharmacy holds the central role in IP management — receipt, storage, dispensing, and accountability. You administer what Pharmacy has dispensed.

- **Pharmacy dispenses; you administer.** Under the standard Quebec rule, IP is dispensed by the Research Pharmacy. Administer only from pharmacy-dispensed supply — never from unpackaged stock, expired supply, or supply not traceable to a dispensing record.
- **Follow the protocol** for route, dose, rate, and infusion procedure. Deviations (e.g. infusion interrupted, dose adjusted without authorization) must be documented and reported as [protocol deviations](/kb/reportable-events).
- **Complete IP accountability documentation** — dose administered, time, lot number if required. Pharmacy and the CRA audit this at [monitoring visits](/kb/monitoring-audits).
- **If the study has a nurse dispensing waiver** — confirm the pharmacy-issued waiver is in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> before dispensing directly. The waiver is study-specific; do not assume it carries across studies. See the [Nurse Training Pathway](/kb/roles/nurse-pathway) for the waiver process.
- **Report IP-related reactions immediately** — both clinical management and documentation. Escalate AEs and SAEs through the [safety reporting pathway](/kb/reportable-events).

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — IP administration">
    **Observe:** Accompany the research pharmacist during an IP dispensing session. Note how they verify the dose, check labelling, and document the dispensing record.

    **Discuss with your QI or supervisor:**
    - Walk me through the emergency unblinding procedure for this study — who has the codes, and what triggers unblinding?
    - What happens if I discover a temperature excursion or labelling discrepancy at the time of administration?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Specimen collection

The CRC Guide covers specimen logistics — Lab Manual, labelling, processing, shipping. Your specific contribution is the collection itself: blood draws, urine, swabs, and other samples that require your licence.

<Warning>
  **Blood draws are reserved under the *Code des professions*** — to nurses and phlebotomists only. If non-nurse team members (a non-nurse CRC, research assistant, trainee) appear on the TDL for specimen collection, that is a delegation error to flag before the next collection visit.
</Warning>

- **Order of draw matters** — some assays are sensitive to contamination from prior tubes. Follow the Lab Manual's specified order.
- **Processing window** — for specimens requiring time-sensitive processing (e.g. PBMC isolation, serum separation), your collection time starts the clock. Document the time of collection accurately. A deviation from the processing window is reportable.
- **Coordinate hand-off** — once collected, specimens go to Processing per the Lab Manual. Your role ends at transfer unless you are also delegated for processing tasks.

## Clinical monitoring during visits

You are the team member with the clinical licence to perform in-visit monitoring and assessment. This is your most distinct contribution beyond CRC coordination.

- **Vital signs and ECG** — per the protocol's specified timing. For IP infusions, protocols typically require frequent vitals during and after administration. Deviations from the timing grid are documented.
- **Observe and assess during and after IP administration** — especially for first doses, infusions, or known risk IPs. Watch for infusion reactions, hypersensitivity signs, changes in participant-reported symptoms.
- **Clinical assessment within your scope** — symptoms, participant-reported concerns, physical signs that fall within nursing assessment. Medical diagnosis remains with the QI.
- **Escalate in real time** — if participant condition changes during a visit (vital sign abnormalities, reaction signs, reported distress), notify the QI immediately, not at the end of the visit.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Clinical monitoring">
    **Observe:** If possible, shadow an experienced research nurse during an infusion visit. Note the timing of vital signs relative to IP administration, what they watch for during the observation period, and how they document the assessment.

    **Discuss with your QI or supervisor:**
    - What are the known safety signals for this IP, and at what point during the infusion are they most likely to appear?
    - What is the escalation threshold — at what vital sign change or symptom do I stop the infusion and call the QI?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Safety and AE assessment

The CRC Guide's AE section applies to you. Your clinical assessment capability adds one distinct layer: you can assess clinical significance and severity within nursing scope, which informs but does not replace the QI's medical assessment.

- **Ask, observe, document** — at every visit, ask about new or changed symptoms. Observe during procedures. Document what participants report, using their own words where possible.
- **Clinical assessment within scope** — you can assess severity (mild, moderate, severe) and clinical significance based on your nursing training. Document what you observed and your assessment. The QI's medical assessment determines causality, expectedness, and final AE classification — your documentation is the foundation for that determination, not a substitute for it.
- **For SAEs, notify the QI immediately** — not at the next scheduled handoff. <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reporting timelines are tight: sponsor notification typically within 24 hours.
- **Follow-up to resolution** — open AEs require follow-up. Your next-visit assessment of a previously reported AE is part of the clinical trail the QI relies on.

## Between visits

Research nurses are often the most consistent point of contact for participants between scheduled visits — receiving calls, responding to questions, fielding concerns outside business hours.

- **Participant calls** — if a participant calls with a new symptom or concern, document the call (date, time, content, your assessment, action taken). Assess clinical urgency within your scope and escalate to the QI as needed.
- **After-hours coverage** — confirm with the QI and CRC what after-hours coverage applies for your study. Some studies require dedicated after-hours nurse contact; others route calls to on-call clinical services or to the QI directly. The protocol and Site Initiation materials should specify the arrangement.
- **Document everything** — between-visit contact is source data. The log you keep feeds the QI's ongoing safety review and the next monitoring visit.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Learning activity — Between visits">
    **Review:** Check the after-hours coverage protocol for your study. Confirm who participants should call outside business hours and what your role is in that coverage.

    **Discuss with your QI or supervisor:**
    - What documentation standard do you expect for between-visit participant calls — is a note in the source record sufficient, or do I also update the CRF?
    - If a participant calls with a symptom that could be an AE, what is the escalation pathway before the next scheduled visit?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## At close-out

Your clinical tasks end when the last participant completes the last scheduled visit. Confirm with the CRC that all nursing-related source documentation is complete and filed. If you administered IP, verify your accountability records are reconciled with pharmacy. Your end date is recorded on the Task Delegation Log by the QI.
