This page is for licensed nurses who appear on a study's main <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> (TDL) at the RI-MUHC — whether you also coordinate the study or perform clinical tasks only. Your OIIQ licence and the RI-MUHC institutional training framework operate in parallel; neither substitutes for the other.

## Who this is for

Two patterns are common for research nurses at the RI-MUHC:

Common across outpatient research clinics, ward-based inpatient protocols, DCT/at-home visits, and the CIM.

## Dual accountability

You are accountable on two tracks at once. Institutional rules (RI-MUHC SOPs, ICH <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>, the delegation log) govern your study-specific tasks. Your OIIQ licence governs the clinical acts you are legally permitted to perform.

- **Institutional layer** — you complete RI-MUHC institutional training, appear on the Task Delegation Log with specific tasks delegated, and operate within the protocol and the SOPs. The <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip> countersigns your delegation entry.
- **Professional licence layer** — your OIIQ licence defines what clinical acts you can legally perform. Your scope of practice imposes obligations that exist independently of what the QI delegates.

<Warning>
  **When the two layers conflict, your professional licence wins.** If a study protocol or a delegation entry asks you to perform an act outside your scope of practice, you must refuse. Document the issue with your QI and the lead CRC. Contact <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> at **QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca** if the situation is unclear. **You are personally responsible for refusing tasks outside your scope, regardless of what the TDL says.**
</Warning>

## Your OIIQ licence

Your Ordre des infirmières et infirmiers du Québec licence must be valid throughout your placement.

- **File a current copy of your licence in your employment record.** Your manager or People & Culture maintains this. For external nurses on CIM studies, the licence is filed in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>.
- **Flag the renewal date.** Add a reminder at least 30 days before expiry. You cannot perform reserved acts if your licence lapses — even for a day.
- **Maintain your OIIQ continuing education obligations.** OIIQ requires continuing education under the *Règlement sur la formation continue obligatoire des infirmières et infirmiers*. Check current requirements on the OIIQ website. Some CANTRAIN modules may be recognized; confirm directly with OIIQ.

## Reserved acts and scope

Quebec's *Code des professions* reserves specific clinical acts to licensed nurses. These cannot be delegated to non-nurse CRCs, research assistants, or trainees — regardless of what a protocol or the Task Delegation Log says. Your licence enables these acts; your delegation entry on the TDL authorizes them for a specific study.

**Acts you can perform:**

- Administer medication (oral, IM, SC, IV) under medical prescription, including investigational products
- Perform blood draws and other specimen collection procedures
- Perform IV infusions and manage infusion procedures
- Perform ECGs, vital signs, and physical assessments within nursing scope
- Assess participant condition within nursing scope — symptoms, response to IP, adverse events

**Acts that remain outside nursing scope:**

- Prescribing — you administer prescribed medication but cannot prescribe
- Medical diagnosis and medical assessment — remain reserved to physicians
- Dispensing investigational product — ordinarily reserved to pharmacy (see nurse dispensing exception below)

### Nurse dispensing exception

In specific circumstances, a qualified nurse may be approved to dispense IP directly to research participants without pharmacy intermediation. This is an exception to the standard Quebec *Pharmacy Act* rule and requires explicit approval.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Request at study submission in Nagano">
    The QI requests the nurse dispensing exception as part of the initial submission. It cannot be added later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="MUHC Research Pharmacy review">
    Pharmacy verifies compliance with Quebec's *Pharmacy Act*. If approved, Pharmacy issues a waiver.
  </Step>
  <Step title="File the waiver in the ISF">
    The pharmacy-issued waiver must be kept in the essential study documents for the duration of the study.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  Do not assume the nurse dispensing exception applies to your study. Approval must be obtained through Nagano at the time of submission. If a protocol anticipates nurse-administered IP, plan for this review early — the waiver is required before the study can begin.
</Warning>

## CPR and emergency preparedness

Current CPR certification (BLS / CPR-HCP) is required before working in any clinical research area, regardless of setting. Keep your certification current — renewal is your responsibility.

Emergency preparedness beyond CPR is site-specific:

- **Site fire and evacuation procedures** — confirm the route, assembly point, and alarm procedures for your workspace before your first shift. Glen, Montreal General, and satellite sites each have their own procedures.
- **Emergency escalation pathway** — know which number to dial, who responds, and whether a code team is available. This varies significantly between outpatient clinics, ward-based work, and specialized facilities.
- **Research-specific emergency planning** — for IP visits with known safety signals (first-in-human doses, infusion reactions, anaphylaxis risk), the emergency plan must be established before the visit begins. The QI is responsible for confirming this in advance.

Courses are available through TalentLMS (self-enrollment) and in-person sessions.

## Specialized competencies

Certain study types require specialized competencies beyond general research nursing. These cannot be waived and apply wherever you perform these tasks at the RI-MUHC.

### Oncology and Phase I — chemotherapy and biotherapy

Nurses administering study chemotherapy or biotherapy must complete a **competency-based educational program specific to chemotherapy and biotherapy administration** before performing these tasks. This is a distinct requirement beyond GCP and standard nursing qualifications.

<Warning>
  Do not administer study chemotherapy or biotherapy without completing the competency program. Regardless of prior oncology experience elsewhere, the RI-MUHC program is required before you can be delegated these tasks at the Institute.
</Warning>

- **Chemotherapy and biotherapy competency program** — competency-based, confirmed through assessment, not just attendance
- **Safe handling of hazardous drugs** — PPE, spill procedures, closed-system transfer devices, and waste handling

## The Task Delegation Log and study-specific training

You must be on the study's main Task Delegation Log before you can perform any delegated clinical task.

- **TDL entry with specific tasks** — the TDL lists each delegated task against your name, with start and end dates. Generic entries ("clinical tasks") are not sufficient — tasks must be specific enough for an auditor to verify the delegation matches what you actually did.
- **Protocol training** — delivered at the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#siv">Site Initiation Visit</Tooltip> (SIV) or equivalent. Covers the study schema, eligibility criteria, visit schedule, IP administration, <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ae">AE</Tooltip> reporting, and protocol-specific assessments. Document your training in the ISF.
- **IP-specific training where applicable** — some IPs require additional training before administration. The sponsor or Research Pharmacy typically provides this.
- **Updated training when the protocol changes** — protocol amendments that affect your tasks require re-training and a revised TDL entry.

## Nursing-specific obligations throughout a study

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Before each visit (IP studies)">
    Know the IP's safety profile and confirm emergency readiness with the QI before any IP visit. Verify with pharmacy that the prepared dose is correct and not expired. See the [Nurse Guide — Before the Visit](/kb/roles/nurse-guide#before-the-visit) for the full pre-visit checklist.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="IP administration">
    Administer only from pharmacy-dispensed supply. Follow the protocol for route, dose, rate, and infusion procedure. Complete IP accountability documentation after every administration. See the [Nurse Guide — IP Administration](/kb/roles/nurse-guide#investigational-product-administration) for the full procedure.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Specimen collection">
    Blood draws are reserved acts under the *Code des professions* — if non-nurse team members appear on the TDL for specimen collection, flag the delegation error. See the [Nurse Guide — Specimen Collection](/kb/roles/nurse-guide#specimen-collection) for collection and hand-off procedures.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical monitoring during visits">
    Perform vital signs, ECG, and clinical assessments per the protocol's timing. Escalate in real time if participant condition changes — notify the QI immediately, not at the end of the visit. See the [Nurse Guide — Clinical Monitoring](/kb/roles/nurse-guide#clinical-monitoring-during-visits) for the full monitoring workflow.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="AE assessment and safety reporting">
    Assess severity and clinical significance within nursing scope. Document your observations — they are the foundation for the QI's medical assessment. For SAEs, notify the QI immediately. See the [Nurse Guide — Safety and AE Assessment](/kb/roles/nurse-guide#safety-and-ae-assessment) for scope boundaries and escalation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Between visits">
    You are often the most consistent point of contact for participants between scheduled visits. Document every call as source data. See the [Nurse Guide — Between Visits](/kb/roles/nurse-guide#between-visits) for documentation standards and after-hours coverage.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Working with your team

Research nursing is collaborative. A few counterparts you work with regardless of track.

- **The QI** — makes medical decisions on the study (causality, expectedness, diagnosis) and countersigns your TDL entry. You document clinical facts and assessments within nursing scope; the QI makes the medical determinations.
- **The CRC or lead CRC-Nurse** — if you're a protocol-specific nurse, the CRC handles coordination — regulatory submissions, consent, scheduling, data capture, monitoring visits. Coordinate on visit flow and escalate AEs through them. If you're a CRC-Nurse, you are this person.
- **MUHC Research Pharmacy** — central role in IP management: receipt, storage, dispensing, accountability, and blinding. Primary contact for dispensing waivers, cold-chain studies, and radiopharmaceuticals. Reach out early for studies with complex IP handling.
- **Sponsor's CRA (monitor)** — external, verifies compliance. Not your supervisor. Be available during monitoring visits where your clinical procedures or IP administration are being reviewed.
- **QA and Research Facilitator** — contact QA (**QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca**) for audit preparation, deviation reporting, and scope-of-practice questions. Your Research Facilitator helps with study logistics and cross-team coordination.

## If you work at CIM

The Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM) at the Glen (blocks B4 and C4) and Montreal General adds site-specific Work Instructions on top of the generic requirements on this page.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Emergency preparedness at CIM">
    In addition to BLS/CPR-HCP certification, CIM staff complete: **Code Blue** (cardiac and respiratory emergencies), **Code Red and Code Green** (fire and evacuation — site-specific at Glen and MGH), and **UBEC verification** (Unit Based Emergency Cart — confirms emergency cart is stocked and accessible).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="IP visits — WI-CIM-AD001">
    The CIM formalizes the pre-visit safety check as **WI-CIM-AD001 Appendix 1** (Safety Checklist Prior to Study Visit). Complete it before each IP administration visit.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="CIM Infusion Centre">
    The CIM Infusion Centre, developed with the Cedars Cancer Centre, provides dedicated space for research infusion procedures. Nurses working in this setting follow infusion-specific procedures and safety requirements. Bookings and inquiries: **cim.infusion@muhc.mcgill.ca**.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Study Briefings and general nursing tasks — WI-CIM-GN005">
    CIM-coordinated studies sometimes need additional nurses for general nursing tasks (blood draws, vitals, ECG) on specific shifts. That work uses a lighter-weight framework governed by **WI-CIM-GN005**, with its own General Nursing Task Delegation Log (Appendix 3). This is separate from the main study TDL. Protocol-specific tasks (IP administration, study-specific assessments) must still be on the **main study TDL**, countersigned by the QI.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="External nurses on CIM studies — WI-CIM-GN005 Appendix 4">
    If you are a nurse from outside the CIM assigned to general nursing on a CIM-coordinated study, additional requirements apply under Appendix 4 of WI-CIM-GN005: condensed GCP and protocol training record before any delegated task, your CV and current OIIQ licence filed in the ISF, Code Blue training for the site, and supervision by a CIM nurse for overnight or out-of-hours interventions.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
