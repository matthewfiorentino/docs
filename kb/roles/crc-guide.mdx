## Understanding the role

### The CRC role at the RI-MUHC

The CRC is the operational backbone of site-level trial management. Unlike the Qualified Investigator, the CRC role has no regulatory definition in Canadian law — there is no national licence, no regulated title, and no statutory scope of practice. What defines your role is what has been delegated to you on the Task Delegation Log and what your own professional credentials permit you to do independently.

At the RI-MUHC, CRCs work across a wide range of clinical research settings. Some hold ACRP (CCRC®) or SOCRA (CCRP®) certification; others come from nursing, allied health, or administrative backgrounds. The scope assigned on the delegation log must reflect your actual qualifications — your PI is responsible for confirming this before countersigning.

For the tasks on your log, you carry personal accountability under GCP. The QI/PI retains ultimate legal responsibility for the study, but that does not shield you from accountability for work you signed off on. Document everything, follow your SOPs, and if you are uncertain, escalate before acting. You also serve as a **participant advocate** — the person closest to the participant's experience who can flag concerns about safety, burden, or wellbeing to the PI.

### Study types and what changes for your work

### Your training requirements

This guide covers Levels I–III. Your specific requirements depend on your study type:

| Level | Study type | SOP series | GCP | Other certs | Renewal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | Drug (biologics, vaccines, gene/cell therapies) | SOP-CR (31 SOPs) | Required | HC Division 5 | 2 years |
| II | Medical device and surgery | SOP-CR (30 SOPs) | Required | ISO 14155 | 2 years |
| III | Natural health products | SOP-CR (30 SOPs) | — | — | 2 years |

If you work on studies at different levels, your training is set by the highest. For the full training matrix, see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/kb/training).

### On the study team

- **QI/PI:** carries ultimate legal and regulatory accountability for the study. You execute; they oversee. Your PI's countersignature on the delegation log confirms your qualification for your tasks, but it does not transfer their accountability to you — and it does not transfer yours to them.
- **Sub-Investigator (Sub-I):** assists the PI and can serve as PI backup if they hold RI-MUHC Human Research Privileges. For interventional studies with investigational products (Levels I–III), Researcher Status is not sufficient — the Sub-I backup must be a CPDP member with Privileges.
- **Research Nurse / CRC-Nurse:** performs clinical procedures (blood draws, vital signs, medication administration) that require nursing credentials. Tasks reserved under Quebec's _Code des professions_ cannot be delegated to non-nurse CRCs regardless of what appears on the TDL.
- **Research Assistant:** may support data entry, specimen processing, filing, or participant scheduling. Typically works under CRC or PI supervision with a narrower scope on the delegation log.
- **Data Manager / Biostatistician:** on larger studies or investigator-initiated trials, may handle database design, query management, or analysis.

### External to the study team

**Sponsor's CRA (monitor):** an external representative of the Sponsor who verifies your data and GCP compliance. They are not your supervisor and cannot instruct you to deviate from the approved protocol or your SOPs. Monitoring letters must be responded to, with all queries resolved and filed in the ISF. Your PI must initial every monitoring letter.

### Institutional contacts

- **QA department:** the RI-MUHC's quality assurance team. Contact QA when you are uncertain about whether a task is compliant — before a deviation occurs, not after.
- **Research Facilitator:** your entry point for navigating RI-MUHC processes, the Nagano submission workflow, and study-specific questions.
- **Research Agreements Office:** reviews contracts and agreements to protect the rights and interests of the RI-MUHC, investigator, and research team. No study can begin without a signed agreement where one is required.
- **Training and Learning Solutions Advisor:** for questions about TalentLMS, the SOP Reader, the Competency Assessment, or training records. **training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca**

### MUHC service departments

These departments conduct their own feasibility reviews during the Nagano submission process. Answer the F11 form accurately — underreporting which services are needed delays MUHC Authorization when the gap is discovered during feasibility review.

- **MUHC Pharmacy:** investigational product storage, dispensing, accountability, and return. Pharmacy issues a pharmacy agreement that must be signed before the study begins and maintains its own Task Delegation Log, which must be filed in your study's essential documents at close-out.
- **Nursing services:** if your study requires MUHC nursing resources, a nursing feasibility review is triggered.
- **Laboratory (OPTILAB-MUHC):** if your study requires hospital laboratory services, a laboratory feasibility review and agreement are required.
- **Director of Professional Services (DPS):** reviews resource utilization — approves use of MUHC resources such as beds, operating rooms, imaging, and medical records.
- **Privacy and information security:** if your study involves identifiable personal health information, software, or platforms, the ÉFVP (privacy impact assessment under Loi 25) is triggered via the F17/F18 forms in Nagano.

### Your role in Nagano submissions

CRCs frequently build Nagano submissions on behalf of the PI. You create the project, complete the F11 form, and upload supporting documents — but the submission is not complete until the PI completes the final commitment and signature step in Nagano. You cannot substitute for that signature.

For a full walkthrough of the Nagano submission process, see the Nagano User Guide.

## Part 1: Study lifecycle

A chronological walkthrough from pre-activation through close-out.

## Pre-activation

Everything that must be in place before the first participant is approached. Pre-activation work is the foundation every subsequent monitoring visit, audit, and inspection will evaluate.

<Warning>
**MUHC Authorization letter before any study procedures.** REB approval alone does not authorize participant contact. The Personne mandatée must issue the MUHC Authorization letter confirming all three reviews — scientific, ethical, and institutional feasibility — are positive. The Authorization letter must be filed in the ISF before any recruitment, screening, or consent activity begins.
</Warning>

### ISF setup

- Set up the Investigator Site File (ISF) structure per SOP-CR-015 before the Site Initiation Visit — not after. The SIV is a review of an ISF already in progress, not the starting point.
- File the MUHC Authorization letter in the ISF as soon as it is received.
- File all regulatory documents before SIV: current protocol (signed), current ICF (approved), Investigator's Brochure or product monograph, REB approval letter, and — for drug studies — the Health Canada No Objection Letter (NOL) and Qualified Investigator Undertaking (QIU) form signed by your PI.
- For medical device studies: file the Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA) from Health Canada.
- For Division 5 drug studies: the QI/PI must sign the QIU form. MUHC investigators should **not** sign the US FDA Form 1572 — if a US sponsor requests it, use the RI-MUHC ICH GCP attestation form (Appendix 4 of SOP-CR-002) instead.
- Confirm the executed clinical trial agreement is filed. Contracts are managed via My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal. No agreement, no Authorization.
- If the Sponsor requires a confidentiality agreement or NDA, ensure it is signed before any study materials or data are shared (SOP-CR-019).

### Team, training, and delegation

- The Task Delegation Log must be complete before any study task. Every team member performing protocol activities must be named, have signed the log, and have the PI's countersignature — before they perform any task (SOP-CR-002).
- File the current CV and all training certificates for every team member on the delegation log. Certificates must not be within 90 days of expiry at submission.
- Confirm the Sub-Investigator PI backup is named on the log and holds RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status. Fellows and rotating residents cannot serve as PI backup.
- If pharmacy is involved, obtain the Pharmacy's own Task Delegation Log and file it in the ISF.

### Site Initiation Visit

- The SIV is led by the Sponsor's CRA and covers protocol training, investigational product procedures, EDC system access, source document templates, and a review of the ISF for completeness.
- Document all SIV training on the Training Log. File the attendance sheet and training slides in the ISF. The Training Log is a separate document from the delegation log — it records all training events.
- Register the clinical trial on ClinicalTrials.gov before the first participant is enrolled. This is a Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator obligation, but confirm it has been done before you begin recruitment (SOP-CR-007).

## Consent and enrolment

See also: Consent in Quebec | SOP-CR-008 | SOP-CR-006

The highest-risk phase for protocol deviations. The rules here are specific, some are Quebec-specific, and violations are among the most common findings at inspections.

### Before you approach anyone

- No screening, recruitment contact, or consent activities before the MUHC Authorization letter is in the ISF. Not "pending" — received and filed.
- All recruitment materials must be REB-approved before use. If materials are to be displayed on MUHC walls or screens, MUHC Communications approval is required after REB approval.
- Always use the current REB-approved version of the ICF. Version control is your responsibility.

### The consent process

- Under Quebec's Charte de la langue française (Bill 96), consent forms at public institutions must be **available in French**. Provide the French version to French-speaking participants. Never use a translated or modified ICF that has not been REB-approved.
- Give the participant adequate time to read, ask questions, and consider participation. Consent must be free and informed — document the process in the source record, not just the signature.
- Confirm who conducts the consent discussion. This is typically a delegated task — verify it is listed on your Task Delegation Log before leading a consent process.
- Non-nurse CRCs cannot perform reserved acts. Eligibility blood draws, medication administration, patient assessment, or any act reserved to nurses under the OIIQ must be performed by licensed personnel regardless of what the delegation log says.
- **Incapacitated adults in Quebec — Civil Code Art. 21.** Research involving an incapacitated adult requires consent from the person with legal authority AND authorization from a Quebec MSSS-designated REB. If the incapacitated person understands and objects, they cannot be enrolled regardless of substitute consent. This is a hard legal requirement under the Civil Code of Québec.

### Eligibility and enrolment documentation

- Eligibility must be confirmed against every inclusion and exclusion criterion. Document each criterion in the source record.
- Record every screened participant on the Screening Log — including screen failures and reasons for ineligibility. This log is reviewed at every monitoring visit.
- Record every enrolled participant on the Enrolment Log with their participant ID. Never use participant names in study documents — use the assigned ID and maintain the Subject Identification Log separately in a secure location.
- File a copy of the signed ICF in the ISF and provide a copy to the participant. Document in the source record that a copy was given.

## Active conduct

The longest and most operationally complex phase. Three areas dominate: source data and CRF integrity, safety reporting, and monitoring visit management.

### Source data and CRF integrity

- Source documents are the original records of study procedures — hospital charts, lab reports, nursing notes, participant diaries. CRF entries must match source documents. Discrepancies are a finding at every monitoring visit.
- Complete CRFs promptly after each visit. Late entries require a note explaining the delay. Never back-fill or reconstruct data without documenting the circumstances.
- Corrections to source documents and CRFs: draw a single line through the error, add the correct value, sign or initial, and date. Never use correction fluid. For eCRF corrections, follow the system's audit trail process.
- All QI/PI-delegated review of data — lab results, safety reports, medical assessments — must be initialled and dated by the QI/PI to document oversight.

### Safety reporting

<Warning>
**AE and SAE reporting timelines are fixed and non-negotiable.** Missed timelines are among the most cited findings at Health Canada inspections. When in doubt, report and then clarify — do not wait for certainty before notifying your PI.
</Warning>

Your role in safety reporting is to identify, document, and escalate promptly — the QI/PI performs the medical assessment and determines reportability.

  <Step title="Notify the QI/PI immediately" />
  <Step title="Complete the SAE report form" />
  <Step title="Support the QI/PI in meeting Sponsor and REB reporting timelines" />

External SUSARs from the Sponsor must be filed in the ISF and initialled and dated by the QI/PI to document review. See also SOP-CR-012.

Participant safety issues, informed consent problems, or undue risk should be reported to the MUHC REB immediately — even if no formal protocol deviation has occurred. Do not wait for the next reporting cycle.

### Monitoring visits

- **Before the visit:** resolve all outstanding data queries from the previous monitoring letter. Ensure source documents, CRFs, and the ISF are current and accessible. Notify relevant departments (pharmacy, lab) of the visit date.
- **During the visit:** be available to answer questions and provide access to records. The monitor will verify source data against CRFs, review the ISF, check delegation and training logs, and review IP records if applicable.
- **After the visit:** sign and date the Monitoring Log. File the monitoring follow-up letter in the ISF as soon as it arrives. The QI/PI must initial and date every monitoring letter — chase this promptly, it must be complete before the next monitoring visit.
- Respond to all queries and CAPA requests in the monitoring letter within the stated timelines.

### Ongoing team and document management

- **Staff changes:** when a team member joins or leaves, update the delegation log immediately, notify the Sponsor, and update the study contact list.
- **SOP updates:** when an SOP is revised, your TalentLMS Reader unit for that SOP resets. Complete the updated reading before your next study task that the SOP governs.
- **Protocol amendments:** when an amendment is REB-approved, document team training on the Training Log before implementing the change. Update the ISF with the new approved version.
- **Training certificate expiry:** if your GCP or Competency Assessment certificate lapses while a study is active, you must be removed from the delegation log until renewal is complete.
- **Leave of absence:** if you are on leave for more than 6 months, your training must be renewed before returning to study tasks. The delegation log must reflect that you are undelegated during leave, and a trained backup must be named (SOP-CR-002).

## Annual renewal

See also: SOP-CR-007 | SOP-CR-026

REB approval is valid for up to one year. A lapse in approval halts the study. Track the expiry date from day one and treat renewal as a recurring calendar event.

- Submit the annual renewal via Nagano before REB approval expires. The MUHC Authorization is automatically revalidated with each REB renewal — but only if the renewal is timely.
- The annual renewal submission includes a progress report, updated participant counts, any safety information, and a current list of study personnel.
- File the new REB approval letter in the ISF as soon as it is received.
- Watch your own training certificate expiry alongside the REB renewal date. QA will flag expired or near-expiry credentials and the submission will not pass feasibility review.

## Close-out

See also: Study Close-Out | SOP-CR-016

Close-out is CRC-heavy work. The Sponsor's monitor conducts a Close-Out Visit to verify the ISF is complete and data are clean before the site is formally closed.

### Before the close-out visit

- Confirm the last participant has completed the last scheduled visit and all associated CRFs are complete and accurate.
- Review the ISF for completeness against the SOP-CR-015 essential document checklist. Resolve all outstanding data queries and CRF corrections before the monitor arrives.
- Ensure all monitoring letters are filed and QI/PI-initialled. For every visit on the Monitoring Log, there must be a corresponding initialled follow-up letter in the ISF.
- Notify all relevant departments of the upcoming close-out visit date: pharmacy, lab, and any clinical services involved.

### Delegation log and team close-out

- Record end dates for all team members on the Task Delegation Log as they conclude study activities. The QI/PI must sign and date the log to confirm undelegation.
- The CRC and QI/PI remain on the log to respond to post-close-out queries. All other team members' end dates should be recorded before or at the close-out visit.

### After the close-out visit

- File the Close-Out Visit Report and the Close-Out Notification letter from the monitor in the ISF.
- Submit the Study Completion Report to the REB via Nagano (SOP-CR-026).
- Update the ClinicalTrials.gov registration to reflect study completion.

### Archiving

- **15-year retention** for Health Canada regulated studies (drug, device, NHP). This is the minimum under Division 5 (C.05.012). Do not destroy or transfer any study documents without written authorization from both the QI/PI and the Sponsor.
- Obtain the Essential Document Destruction Request form signed by the QI/PI and Sponsor before any destruction at the end of the retention period (SOP-CR-015).

<Warning>
**Inspections after close-out.** Health Canada may inspect a site after study completion — sometimes years later. The ISF must remain complete, retrievable, and intact for the full retention period.
</Warning>

## Part 2: Domain reference

Operational guidance organized by competency domain. These sections cover areas of CRC work that span the full study lifecycle. Each includes practical learning activities you can do with your supervisor or an experienced colleague.

## Recruitment and screening

### Identifying potential participants

Before you can screen anyone, you need a pool of candidates. How you build that pool depends on the study type and your site's patient population.

**Chart review and pre-screening.** For most interventional studies, you start by reviewing medical records to identify patients who might meet key eligibility criteria. At the MUHC, this means OACIS — the clinical information system. Confirm your OACIS permissions with your supervisor before you begin. Pre-screening against OACIS is not the same as formal screening — pre-screening identifies candidates to approach, while formal screening happens after consent.

**Referral from clinical teams.** Many participants come through referrals from attending physicians, residents, nurses, or allied health professionals. Building these relationships is essential. A one-page summary of key eligibility criteria shared with the clinical team works better than a detailed protocol summary.

**Recruitment materials.** Posters, advertisements, social media text, patient letters, and any other materials used to identify or attract potential participants must be REB-approved before use. If materials will be displayed on MUHC walls or screens, MUHC Communications Department approval is required after REB approval.

**Recruitment tracking.** Maintain a screening log from the start. Record every candidate you pre-screen or approach, whether they were eligible, whether they consented, and the reason for any screen failure.

### Applying eligibility criteria

Every inclusion and exclusion criterion must be assessed and documented for every participant.

- Some criteria require clinical judgment — these must be assessed by the QI/PI or a delegated Sub-I, not by the CRC independently, unless your delegation log specifically authorizes it.
- Some criteria depend on timing — track windows carefully and document the date each criterion was confirmed.
- Some criteria reference local standards — confirm the reference ranges with your site's laboratory before screening begins.

Document each criterion in the source record, not just a summary checkbox. If a participant is borderline on a criterion, document the clinical reasoning and ensure the QI/PI has signed off.

### Screen failures

- Record the screen failure on the Screening Log with the specific reason.
- Complete any required screen-failure CRF pages.
- If the participant underwent any study procedures before the screen failure was identified, document those procedures and ensure any safety follow-up is completed.
- Do not re-screen a participant unless the protocol explicitly permits re-screening.

## Investigational products

### Receipt and inventory

When an IP shipment arrives at the site:

- Verify the shipment against the packing list: correct product, correct quantity, correct lot numbers, no damage.
- Check temperature monitors if applicable. An out-of-range reading must be reported to the Sponsor before the product is used.
- Record the shipment in the Drug Accountability Log: date received, quantity, lot/batch number, expiry date, storage location.
- Transfer to the appropriate storage location immediately.

### Storage and monitoring

- Store investigational products according to the protocol and Investigator's Brochure — typically a dedicated research fridge or cabinet with continuous temperature monitoring.
- Check and document storage conditions daily or per your site's SOP.
- Expired or damaged products must be quarantined and the Sponsor notified. Do not destroy or return without written Sponsor instructions.
- Products must be stored separately from commercial medications and labelled as "For Clinical Trial Use Only" or equivalent.

### Dispensing and accountability

- For drug studies, pharmacy typically dispenses the IP. Your role is to coordinate: confirm the correct visit, confirm the participant is eligible for dosing, and document that dispensing occurred.
- Record every dispensing event: date, participant ID, quantity, lot/batch number, and who dispensed.
- Track participant compliance (returned containers, pill counts, diary entries) at each visit.

### Return and destruction

At close-out, reconcile the Drug Accountability Log against actual inventory. Every unit received must be accounted for: dispensed, returned, destroyed, or remaining. Never destroy IP without written Sponsor authorization.

## Biological specimens

### Collection and processing

- Follow the Lab Manual provided by the Sponsor or central laboratory. It specifies tube types, draw order, fill volumes, processing steps, and storage conditions.
- Process specimens within the window specified in the Lab Manual. Deviations from processing windows are reportable.
- If you are not a licensed nurse or phlebotomist, you cannot perform blood draws — confirm your delegation log and professional credentials before collection visits.

### Labelling

- Label every specimen container with the protocol-specified identifiers: participant ID, visit number, date and time of collection, sample type. Never use participant names.
- Use Sponsor-provided labels if available. Verify the label against the participant's source record before the specimen leaves the room.

### Shipping

Package specimens for transport according to IATA TDG regulations. Most biological specimens are classified as UN3373, Category B — requiring triple packaging and proper labelling. Use a Sponsor-approved carrier and follow the Sponsor's shipping instructions. IATA TDG training may be required depending on your site and the type of specimens.

### Specimen inventory

Maintain a Specimen Inventory Log for every study: specimens collected, processed, shipped, stored on site, and — at close-out — final disposition.

## Budgets, contracts, and study finances

### Participant reimbursements

- Participants may receive compensation for travel, parking, or time. The amount and method are specified in the protocol and REB-approved ICF.
- Maintain a Participant Reimbursement Log documenting every payment: date, participant ID, amount, and reason.

### Expense tracking and invoicing

- Study budgets are managed through cost centres on the RI-MUHC Portal (MyAccounts).
- For industry-sponsored studies, the Sponsor pays based on milestones. Your role may include tracking when milestones are met and flagging them for invoicing.
- For investigator-initiated studies, track spending against the approved budget and alert your PI if expenditures are trending above projections.

### Contracts

- No study activity can begin before the clinical trial agreement is signed and filed. Contracts are managed via My Contracts on the Portal.
- If amendments to the study scope, budget, or timeline are negotiated, ensure the contract amendment is executed before implementing changes.
- Data Transfer Agreements (DTAs), Material Transfer Agreements (MTAs), and confidentiality agreements may also be required.

## Privacy and data protection

**Loi 25** (Quebec Law 25) governs the protection of personal information. If your study collects, accesses, or uses identifiable personal health information (PHI), the institution may be required to complete a privacy impact assessment (ÉFVP) before data collection begins.

### Day-to-day implications

- **Access to medical records.** Your access to OACIS and other clinical systems must be authorized and limited to what is necessary for the study.
- **Data storage.** Participant data must be stored on secure, institutionally approved systems. Do not store identifiable data on personal devices, USB drives, or unapproved cloud services.
- **Data sharing.** If identifiable or coded data is shared outside the MUHC, confirm that the appropriate data agreements and privacy assessments are in place. Cross-border transfers have additional requirements under Loi 25.
- **De-identification.** Use participant IDs, not names, in all study documents. The Subject Identification Log must be stored separately in a secure location with restricted access.

## Protocol deviations

### What counts as a deviation

A protocol deviation is any departure from the approved protocol, ICH GCP, institutional SOPs, or the conditions of REB approval. This includes missed or out-of-window visits, eligibility criteria not fully met or documented, consent issues, incorrect dosing, CRF data entered incorrectly or late, missed safety reporting timelines, and study procedures performed by someone not on the delegation log or whose training has lapsed.

Not all deviations are the same severity. The standard classification is: minor (no impact on participant safety or data integrity), major (could affect safety or data integrity), and critical (did affect safety or data integrity).

### Your role

- **Identify.** Deviations are often discovered during routine work. The earlier you catch a deviation, the better.
- **Document.** Record what happened, when, why, and what corrective action was taken. Document in the source record and on any deviation tracking form the Sponsor requires.
- **Report.** Notify your PI immediately. For major and critical deviations, the Sponsor, QA, and the REB must be notified within required timelines.
- **Correct.** Implement corrective and preventive actions (CAPAs). If the deviation was caused by a systemic issue, work with your PI and QA to fix the process, not just the individual instance.

### Common CRC-related deviations

- Visit windows missed because scheduling was not tracked systematically
- Consent not obtained before study procedures — usually a timing issue, not intentional
- Screen failure documentation incomplete — missing the reason or the QI/PI sign-off
- IP accountability log not updated at every dispensing event
- Monitoring letter responses not completed within the stated timelines

## Data management beyond CRFs

### EDC systems

Most industry-sponsored studies use a Sponsor-provided EDC system (e.g., Medidata Rave, Oracle InForm, Veeva Vault CDMS). For investigator-initiated studies, REDCap is the most common platform at the RI-MUHC.

- Complete EDC training before entering data. Most Sponsors provide system-specific training; REDCap training is available through CORD.
- Enter data promptly after each visit. Most EDC systems track data entry timeliness.

### Query resolution

Queries are automated or manual flags raised when data entries are incomplete, inconsistent, or out of range.

- Respond to queries promptly. Open query counts are reviewed at every monitoring visit.
- When resolving a query, check the source document first. If the CRF entry was wrong, correct it following GDP.
- Never change source documents to match CRFs. The source is the original record; the CRF must match the source.

### Database lock

At the end of the study, the database is locked — no further changes without formal unlocking. Before lock: all CRFs must be complete and clean, all queries resolved and closed, and the QI/PI must sign the data review confirmation. Missing the database lock deadline can delay the entire study.
