What you need to know before visiting an RI-MUHC research site as an observer — what you may and may not do, confidentiality obligations, and requirements for international visitors.

## What observers can and cannot do

An observer visits a research site to watch and learn. Observers are not members of the research team, are not listed on the Task Delegation Log, and cannot perform any study tasks.

**You can:**

- Observe research activities, team workflows, and site operations
- Ask questions of the research team (outside of participant interactions)
- Attend team meetings and training sessions

**You cannot:**

- Perform any protocol-related task — no data entry, no filing, no specimen handling, no document preparation
- Interact with research participants in any capacity
- Access any study documents, patient records, or institutional systems
- Be listed on the Task Delegation Log

<Note>
  If you want to contribute to study activities rather than observe, you need to be onboarded as a [volunteer](/kb/roles/onboarding-volunteers) or [research trainee](/kb/roles/onboarding-trainees) instead — different processes with different eligibility requirements.
</Note>

## International observers

<Warning>
  **An official RI-MUHC invitation letter is required before entering Canada.** This letter must be obtained from your designated HR advisor at the RI-MUHC. It cannot be requested retroactively — if you arrive without it, you will not be permitted to observe. Contact the hosting PI's team as early as possible; the process takes time.
</Warning>

The hosting PI is responsible for initiating the invitation letter request through RI-MUHC HR. The letter confirms the purpose and duration of the visit, the hosting department, and the PI's supervision. International observers should also confirm their visa or entry requirements with Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) before making travel arrangements.

## Before your visit

Whether you are a Canadian or international observer, the following must be completed before you begin observing.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign the Confidentiality and Security Agreement">
    You must sign this before entering any area where patient or participant information may be visible. The hosting PI or CRC will provide the form.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Obtain the RI-MUHC invitation letter (international observers only)">
    Must be in hand before entry to Canada. The hosting PI initiates the request through RI-MUHC HR.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Site orientation">
    The hosting PI or CRC should brief you on site-specific rules, where you may and may not go, and what to do if you observe something that concerns you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

See the RI-MUHC Portal for the full observer onboarding checklist.

## Confidentiality

Even as an observer, you will be in environments where confidential information is visible — participant names on schedules, medical information on screens, conversations between team members. You are bound by the Confidentiality and Security Agreement you signed.

**You must not:**

- Discuss anything you see or hear about patients, participants, or study data with anyone outside the hosting research team
- Photograph, record, or copy any documents, screens, or materials containing patient or participant information
- Share details about specific studies, participants, or institutional processes on social media, in publications, or with colleagues at other institutions

These obligations continue after your observation period ends. A confidentiality breach by an observer has the same institutional consequences as a breach by a team member.

## During your visit

Your hosting PI or their delegate (typically the CRC) is responsible for your supervision while on site. If you observe something that concerns you — a potential safety issue, a deviation from protocol, or a confidentiality concern — mention it to the hosting PI or CRC privately, not in front of participants.

If a participant asks who you are, the hosting PI or CRC should introduce you as an observer and confirm with the participant that your presence is acceptable. If a participant objects to your presence, you must leave the interaction immediately.
