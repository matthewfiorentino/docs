As QI or PI, you carry ultimate legal and regulatory accountability for your study at the RI-MUHC site. Tasks can be delegated — accountability cannot. This guide maps what is yours personally, what your team handles, and your obligations at each stage.

<Note>
**A note on terminology.** Health Canada's Division 5 uses _Qualified Investigator_ (QI) — a physician or dentist entitled to practice under provincial law and a member in good standing of their professional association. ICH GCP uses _Principal Investigator_ (PI). At the RI-MUHC, QI is the precise term for Division 5 drug trials; PI is used more broadly. There must be no more than one QI per study per site.
</Note>

## Prerequisites

Two prerequisites must be in place before you can submit a study in Nagano. QA verifies both at submission. If either is missing or within 90 days of expiry, the feasibility check fails and the Personne mandatée cannot issue MUHC Authorization.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Research Privileges or Researcher Status">
    - **Human Research Privileges** are for CPDP members (physicians and dentists) leading studies that require medical oversight — drug trials, protocols involving acts reserved to physicians under Quebec's _Loi médicale_. Apply via TalentLMS. Valid for 3 years.
    - **Human Researcher Status** is for non-CPDP researchers (PhDs, nurses, physiotherapists, psychologists, and others) leading studies that do not require medical oversight — observational studies, surveys, retrospective analyses. Apply via TalentLMS. Valid for 3 years.
    - **Pharmacist exception:** although pharmacists are CPDP members, they are not eligible for Human Research Privileges under the MSSS and MUHC frameworks. Pharmacists acting as PI or Sub-I backup must obtain Human Researcher Status instead.
    - **MNI exception:** MNI PIs conducting studies at the MNI obtain McGill Research Privileges. MNI PIs conducting studies at other MUHC sites (RVH, MGH, MCH) must obtain RI-MUHC Privileges.
    - The 90-day rule applies: at submission, your Privileges or Status must be active and not within 90 days of expiry. Lapsed credentials will prevent MUHC Authorization and may halt active studies mid-course.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Institutional training">
    - Complete the SOP Reader and Competency Assessment in TalentLMS at the level required for your study type. Use the Study Type and Training Classifier if you are unsure. Your certificate must not be within 90 days of expiry at submission.
    - GCP (ICH E6(R3)) is required for Levels I and II. Health Canada Division 5 is required for Level I drug trials. GCP certificates are accepted from any provider on the TransCelerate GCP Mutual Recognition Program list. The RI-MUHC holds an institutional licence with CITI, giving affiliated staff free access — but other recognized providers are equally valid.
    - Your training level is set by your highest-risk active study across all protocols. If you are running a Level I drug trial and a Level IV observational study, you must hold Level I certification.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What only you sign

Delegation is appropriate for most operational tasks. The items below require your personal signature and cannot be delegated.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Qualified Investigator Undertaking (QIU)">
    Division 5 drug trials only. You sign before the study begins, confirming you will conduct the trial per Health Canada regulations and GCP. One QI per study per site. **Do not sign FDA Form 1572** — it requires you to follow US law. Use the RI-MUHC ICH GCP Attestation form (SOP-CR-002 Appendix 4) if a US Sponsor requires the equivalent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Task Delegation Log — countersignature">
    You countersign every team member's entry, confirming they are qualified and trained for their assigned tasks. Required before any team member performs a single delegated task, and again whenever tasks or personnel change during the study.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Medical oversight of AEs, SAEs, and safety documents">
    You review and initial all adverse event documentation, SAE reports, SUSAR reports, lab results, and any document requiring medical assessment. This is ongoing throughout the study. If you delegate AE causality assessment to a qualified Sub-I, that delegation must be on the log before it occurs.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Monitoring visit follow-up letters">
    Every monitoring letter from the Sponsor's CRA must be initialled and dated by you before filing in the ISF. A missing initial on a monitoring letter is one of the most consistent findings at Health Canada inspections.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="PI Commitment and Signature in Nagano">
    Your CRC can prepare the entire F11 submission — but the submission is not complete until you complete the final commitment and signature step in Nagano. The same applies to annual renewals and amendments.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Team-prepared — your sign-off required

- **Annual REB renewal.** Your CRC typically prepares the Nagano renewal submission — progress report, updated participant counts, personnel list, safety summary. You review and sign off in Nagano. Submit before approval lapses; MUHC Authorization auto-renews with each timely REB renewal.
- **Nagano amendments (F1 form).** Protocol changes, team member changes, consent form updates, and other amendments are prepared by your CRC and submitted via Nagano. You sign. Amendments must be submitted and approved before implementing any change — not after.
- **SAE and deviation reports.** Your CRC documents the event and prepares the report. You review, assess causality and seriousness, add your medical assessment, and sign. The report is only complete with your assessment.

## Study submission

All human research studies at the RI-MUHC are submitted through Nagano. The F11 submission triggers the triple review — scientific, ethics (REB), and institutional feasibility — that leads to MUHC Authorization from the Personne mandatée.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Before you open Nagano">
    Confirm your Privileges or Researcher Status and institutional training are both current and not within 90 days of expiry. Have a finalized protocol, the current Investigator's Brochure or product monograph, draft ICF(s) in English and French, and any planned recruitment materials. For drug studies, a Clinical Trial Application (CTA) must be submitted to Health Canada and a No Objection Letter (NOL) obtained before MUHC Authorization is issued — start both processes as early as possible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contracts and agreements">
    Clinical trial agreements, confidentiality agreements, and inter-institutional agreements must be submitted via My Contracts on the Portal and fully executed by the Research Agreements Office before MUHC Authorization is issued. Start this process early — contract negotiation can take weeks.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Health Canada authorizations">
    For drug studies: a CTA and NOL are required. For medical device studies: an Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA) is required. For Class III and IV devices, the approved REB application must also be submitted to Health Canada before enrolment begins — this is a separate obligation under the Medical Devices Regulations.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
**The MUHC Authorization letter is the green light — not REB approval alone.** REB approval, Health Canada authorization, and institutional feasibility review must all be positive before the Personne mandatée issues MUHC Authorization. Do not begin study procedures — including screening and consent — until the Authorization letter is in your ISF.
</Warning>

### Multi-site studies

- **If the MUHC is the lead site:** your Nagano submission covers all participating Quebec RSSS institutions. You are the REB Lead. Each participating site still completes its own institutional feasibility review and obtains its own MUHC Authorization from its Personne mandatée.
- **If the MUHC is a participating site (MEO):** the reviewing REB is the lead institution's REB. Your Nagano file is a participating-site submission. You still require RI-MUHC feasibility review and MUHC Authorization. Contact the Research Facilitator before starting a MEO submission.
- For industry-sponsored multi-site trials, CATALIS FAST TRACK may compress the authorization timeline to eight weeks or less. The RI-MUHC participates in the FAST TRACK network.

### Sponsor-Investigator studies

If you are both Sponsor and QI, you hold dual obligations: filing the CTA with Health Canada, arranging independent monitoring, managing the investigational product supply chain including GMP compliance, and fulfilling all QI site responsibilities. Health Canada does not distinguish between commercial and non-commercial sponsors. You must also register the clinical trial on ClinicalTrials.gov before the first participant is enrolled (SOP-CR-007).

## Building and maintaining your team

### Setting up the delegation log

- The Task Delegation Log must be set up before any study tasks begin — including your own. Name every team member, their role, their specific delegated tasks, and their start date. You countersign each entry; team members sign to accept delegation.
- Every person on the log must hold current institutional training at the correct level before being added. You are confirming this with your countersignature.
- Appoint a Sub-Investigator as your backup who also holds RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status equivalent to yours. Fellows and rotating residents cannot serve as PI backup.
- If you use CIM services, a CIM service agreement must be in place before CIM staff are added to the delegation log. Pharmacy maintains its own Task Delegation Log — obtain it and file it in the ISF before close-out.

### Training requirements for your team

Your entire team trains at the same level as the study requires. File every team member's current CV and all training certificates in the ISF before the SIV. CVs must be signed and dated within the past 2 years. When a team member's training certificate lapses mid-study, they must come off the delegation log until renewed. You are responsible for monitoring your team's certificate expiry, not just your own.

### Staff changes during the study

- **When a team member joins:** confirm their training is current, add them to the delegation log with your countersignature, notify the Sponsor, and update the study contact list.
- **When a team member leaves:** record their end date on the delegation log with your countersignature confirming undelegation. Notify the Sponsor. Submit a Nagano amendment (F1) for any change to listed investigators.
- **If you leave the study:** both you and the incoming QI/PI must sign the Task Delegation Log. The Sponsor and REB must be notified promptly. A Nagano amendment is required. The outgoing QI/PI must delegate all QI/PI tasks to the incoming QI/PI before departing.

## Ongoing obligations during the study

### What direct supervision means in practice

Delegating tasks does not mean stepping away from the study. Direct supervision means you are available and reachable during study activities, you review all data and safety documents in a timely manner, and you document that review with your initials. You do not need to be physically present for every procedure — but if a team member performs a delegated task requiring medical oversight, you must be contactable.

The most common cause of study halts at active sites is a PI becoming unavailable without an adequately qualified Sub-I backup named on the delegation log. If you are going on sabbatical, parental leave, or an extended absence, appoint a Sub-I backup with Privileges or Researcher Status before you leave and update the delegation log and Nagano accordingly.

SOP-CR-002 recommends writing a department-specific working instruction describing how QI/PI oversight is achieved for your team: how safety documents are distributed, how quickly you commit to reviewing and initialling them, and who contacts you if an urgent issue arises outside normal hours.

### Medical oversight

You retain medical oversight of all study activities at the site even when tasks are delegated. For every AE and SAE, your CRC documents and reports to you — you assess causality, determine seriousness, and document your assessment. Your initials are required on every SAE report before it is filed.

SAE reporting timelines are fixed — see SOP-CR-012 for the full schedule. SUSAR reports distributed by the Sponsor must be reviewed, initialled, and dated by you to document your awareness. In Quebec, investigational product management is delegated to a trained pharmacist, but you retain ultimate responsibility for ensuring the IP is used per the approved protocol (SOP-CR-010).

### Informed consent obligations

You are responsible for ensuring informed consent is obtained from every participant before any study procedure. Document the consent process in the source record: who conducted the discussion, when, that the participant had adequate time to consider, and that questions were answered. The ICF signature alone is not sufficient documentation.

- **Re-consent obligations:** when a protocol amendment affects participant risk, burden, or procedures, you must ensure enrolled participants are re-consented using the newly approved ICF before the change is implemented.
- **Incapacitated adults in Quebec — Civil Code Art. 21:** research involving an incapacitated adult requires substitute consent from the person with legal authority AND authorization from an MSSS-designated REB. If the incapacitated person understands and objects, they cannot be enrolled regardless of substitute consent (SOP-CR-008).
- Under Quebec's _Charte de la langue française_ (Bill 96), consent forms at public institutions must be available in French. Always use the current REB-approved ICF version.

### Protocol deviations

All deviations must be documented. Major or critical deviations must be reported to QA and the REB. If your team identifies a potential deviation, they should notify you before acting — not after. Concerns about participant safety, informed consent, or undue risk should be reported to the MUHC REB immediately, even without a formal protocol deviation (MUHC REB SOP 405 and SOP-CR-026).

### Conflict of interest

Conflict of interest declarations are required at initial submission and at every annual renewal. Financial relationships with the Sponsor — consulting fees, equity, intellectual property royalties, research funding — must be disclosed. If you hold an academic appointment at McGill University, you must also comply with McGill's COI policy — this is separate from the RI-MUHC institutional COI declaration. If a COI arises during a study, disclose it promptly to the REB via a Nagano amendment.

### Annual renewal

Track the REB approval expiry date from day one. Submit the renewal via Nagano before approval lapses — a lapse halts the study and requires a new submission cycle. MUHC Authorization auto-renews with each timely REB renewal; a lapsed REB approval voids it. The renewal submission includes a progress report, updated participant counts, any new safety information, and a current list of study personnel (SOP-CR-007).

<Note>
**Proposed regulatory change — investigator eligibility.** Health Canada published a proposal in the Canada Gazette (December 2025) that would broaden QI eligibility beyond physicians and dentists to other licensed healthcare practitioners. This remains in consultation as of April 2026. Monitor developments if this affects your team structure.
</Note>

## Close-out, inspections, and archiving

### At close-out

Review and sign off on the completed ISF before the Sponsor's Close-Out Visit. Ensure all monitoring letters are initialled, all CRF corrections are complete, and the delegation log has end dates and your signature for all team members coming off the study. You and your CRC remain on the delegation log after close-out to respond to post-close-out queries. Submit the Study Completion Report to the REB via Nagano and update the ClinicalTrials.gov registration (SOP-CR-026).

### Health Canada inspections

Health Canada inspects approximately 2% of Canadian clinical trial sites per year. Inspections can be announced (5 days to 4 weeks notice) or unannounced, and typically last 5 or more business days. Observations are classified as Risk 1 (critical), Risk 2 (major), or Risk 3 (minor) under GUI-0043.

As PI, you are present for the opening and closing meetings with the inspector. You provide access to the site, the ISF, and study personnel. QA leads the coordination — notify QA as soon as an inspection notice is received and do not move or reorganize any study documents before QA reviews the situation. The best preparation for an inspection is a well-maintained ISF and a team that follows SOPs consistently throughout the study (SOP-CR-017).

### Archiving

**15-year retention** applies to Health Canada regulated studies (drug, device, NHP) under Division 5 (C.05.012). The ISF must remain complete, secure, and retrievable for the full retention period. Do not destroy any study documents without written authorization from you and the Sponsor. If you change institutions before the retention period ends, notify the Sponsor and ensure storage transfer arrangements are made. You retain personal accountability for the integrity of the archived file (SOP-CR-015).
