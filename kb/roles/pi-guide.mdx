As <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip> or <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip>, you carry ultimate legal and regulatory accountability for your study at the RI-MUHC site. Tasks can be delegated — accountability cannot. This guide maps what is yours personally, what your team handles, and your obligations at each stage.

<Note>
**A note on terminology.** Health Canada's Division 5 uses _Qualified Investigator_ (QI) — a physician or dentist entitled to practice under provincial law and a member in good standing of their professional association. ICH <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> uses _Principal Investigator_ (PI). At the RI-MUHC, QI is the precise term for Division 5 drug trials; PI is used more broadly. There must be no more than one QI per study per site.
</Note>

<Info>
  This guide covers day-to-day operations for interventional studies. For your personal accountability map — prerequisites, what only you sign, and training levels — see the [Investigator Pathway](/kb/roles/pi-pathway).
</Info>

## Prerequisites

Two prerequisites must be in place before you can submit a study in Nagano. QA verifies both at submission. If either is missing or within 90 days of expiry, the feasibility check fails and the <Tooltip tip="Individual formally authorized to issue the final written authorization for each research project at a Quebec RSSS institution." headline="Personne mandatée (PFM)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> cannot issue MUHC Authorization.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Research Privileges or Researcher Status">
    - **Human Research Privileges** are for CPDP members (physicians and dentists) leading studies that require medical oversight — drug trials, protocols involving acts reserved to physicians under Quebec's _Loi médicale_. Apply via [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms). Valid for 3 years.
    - **Human Researcher Status** is for non-CPDP researchers (PhDs, nurses, physiotherapists, psychologists, and others) leading studies that do not require medical oversight — observational studies, surveys, retrospective analyses. Apply via [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms). Valid for 3 years.
    - **Pharmacist exception:** although pharmacists are CPDP members, they are not eligible for Human Research Privileges under the MSSS and MUHC frameworks. Pharmacists acting as PI or Sub-I backup must obtain Human Researcher Status instead.
    - **MNI exception:** MNI PIs conducting studies at the MNI obtain McGill Research Privileges. MNI PIs conducting studies at other MUHC sites (RVH, MGH, MCH) must obtain RI-MUHC Privileges.
    - The 90-day rule applies: at submission, your Privileges or Status must be active and not within 90 days of expiry. Lapsed credentials will prevent MUHC Authorization and may halt active studies mid-course.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Institutional training">
    - Complete the SOP Reader and Competency Assessment in [TalentLMS](/apps/talentlms) at the level required for your study type. Use the [Study Type and Training Classifier](/training/study-classifier) if you are unsure. Your certificate must not be within 90 days of expiry at submission.
    - GCP (ICH E6(R3)) is required for Levels I and II. Health Canada Division 5 is required for Level I drug trials. GCP certificates are accepted from any provider on the TransCelerate GCP Mutual Recognition Program list. The RI-MUHC holds an institutional licence with CITI, giving affiliated staff free access — but other recognized providers are equally valid. See [External Certifications](/training/external-certifications) for the full list of accepted providers.
    - Your training level is set by your highest-risk active study across all protocols. If you are running a Level I drug trial and a Level IV observational study, you must hold Level I certification.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  **Study design support.** The [Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)](/kb/bcu) provides sample size calculations, randomization plans, and Statistical Analysis Plans. [CORD](/kb/cord) builds REDCap databases and reviews CRFs. Engage both before finalizing your protocol — statistical and data design decisions are costly to revise after enrollment begins.
</Note>

<Info>
  **Regulatory context.** Your interventional study is governed by four overlapping layers of authority: international (ICH GCP), federal (Health Canada Division 5, TCPS2), provincial (Quebec Civil Code, Loi 25, MSSS framework), and institutional (RI-MUHC SOPs, MUHC Authorization). Standard GCP training covers the first two — Quebec-specific and institutional requirements are learned through the SOP Reader, the Competency Assessment, and this site. See [Regulatory Framework](/kb/governance/regulatory-framework) and [Institutional Structure](/kb/governance/institutional-structure).
</Info>

## What only you sign

Delegation is appropriate for most operational tasks. The items below require your personal signature and cannot be delegated.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Qualified Investigator Undertaking (QIU)">
    Division 5 drug trials only. You sign before the study begins, confirming you will conduct the trial per Health Canada regulations and GCP. One QI per study per site. **Do not sign FDA Form 1572** — it requires you to follow US law. Use the RI-MUHC ICH GCP Attestation form ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002) Appendix 4) if a US Sponsor requires the equivalent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Task Delegation Log — countersignature">
    You countersign every team member's entry, confirming they are qualified and trained for their assigned tasks. Required before any team member performs a single delegated task, and again whenever tasks or personnel change during the study.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Medical oversight of AEs, SAEs, and safety documents">
    You review and initial all <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip> documentation, <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reports, <Tooltip tip="Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction — unexpected in nature, severity, or frequency; must be reported to Health Canada and the REB." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip> reports, lab results, and any document requiring medical assessment. This is ongoing throughout the study. If you delegate AE causality assessment to a qualified Sub-I, that delegation must be on the log before it occurs.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Monitoring visit follow-up letters">
    Every monitoring letter from the Sponsor's CRA must be initialled and dated by you before filing in the ISF. A missing initial on a monitoring letter is one of the most common findings at Health Canada inspections.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="PI Commitment and Signature in Nagano">
    Your CRC can prepare the entire F11 submission — but the submission is not complete until you complete the final commitment and signature step in Nagano. The same applies to annual renewals and amendments.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Team-prepared — your sign-off required

- **Annual <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> renewal.** Your CRC typically prepares the Nagano renewal submission — progress report, updated participant counts, personnel list, safety summary. You review and sign off in Nagano. Submit before approval lapses; MUHC Authorization auto-renews with each timely REB renewal.
- **Nagano amendments (F1 form).** Protocol changes, team member changes, consent form updates, and other amendments are prepared by your CRC and submitted via Nagano. You sign. Amendments must be submitted and approved before implementing any change — not after.
- **SAE and deviation reports.** Your CRC documents the event and prepares the report. You review, assess causality and seriousness, add your medical assessment, and sign. The report is only complete with your assessment.

## Study submission

All human research studies at the RI-MUHC are submitted through Nagano. The F11 submission triggers the triple review — scientific, ethics (REB), and institutional feasibility — that leads to MUHC Authorization from the Personne mandatée.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Before you open Nagano">
    Confirm your Privileges or Researcher Status and institutional training are both current and not within 90 days of expiry. Have a finalized protocol, the current <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip> or product monograph, draft <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip>(s) in English and French, and any planned recruitment materials. For drug studies, a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application</Tooltip> (CTA) must be submitted to Health Canada and a <Tooltip tip="Letter from Health Canada confirming a CTA is satisfactory and authorizing the sponsor to proceed." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#nol">No Objection Letter</Tooltip> (NOL) obtained before MUHC Authorization is issued — start both processes as early as possible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contracts, agreements, and budget">
    All contracts must be submitted via My Contracts on the Portal and fully executed by the Research Agreements Office before MUHC Authorization is issued. Start early — contract negotiation can take weeks.

    **Industry-sponsored studies:** The Sponsor proposes a Clinical Trial Agreement (CTA) covering conduct obligations, financial terms, publication rights, data ownership, and indemnification. The Research Agreements Office reviews and negotiates on behalf of the institution. Your responsibility is to confirm the budget covers all institutional costs — pharmacy, nursing, laboratory, overhead (30% for industry), and archiving (15 years). Verify the Sponsor will provide a Certificate of Insurance naming the MUHC. The Sponsor also pays REB billing fees — confirm their budget accounts for these across the full study lifecycle.

    **Investigator-initiated studies:** You build the budget. Use the [RI-MUHC Budget Tool](/kb/budgets-contracts) to account for staff salaries, CORD data management, BCU biostatistics, pharmacy, monitoring (independent monitor required for regulated studies), participant reimbursement, overhead (0% for peer-reviewed grants, 15% for foundation-funded), and archiving costs. Foundation-funded studies are now subject to REB billing fees under the expanded MUHC definition of "private enterprise." Budget for contingency (8–15% depending on complexity). Submit inter-institutional agreements if multi-site.

    See [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/budgets-contracts) for overhead rates, the REB fee schedule, and the full agreement type list.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Health Canada authorizations">
    Health Canada does not issue a formal "approval" for clinical trials — it issues a letter confirming no objection, or an authorization to proceed. The requirement and the HC response differ by study type:

    - **Drug, biologic, vaccine, radiopharmaceutical, or gene/cell therapy:** Submit a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip> to the Therapeutic Products Directorate (TPD) or Biologics and Genetic Therapies Directorate (BGTD). HC issues a <Tooltip tip="Letter from Health Canada confirming a CTA is satisfactory and authorizing the sponsor to proceed." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#nol">No Objection Letter (NOL)</Tooltip>. Review takes 30 calendar days. The NOL must be in hand before MUHC Authorization is issued.
    - **Natural health product (NHP):** Submit an NHP Clinical Trial Application to the Natural and Non-prescription Health Products Directorate (NNHPD). HC issues a Notice of Authorization (NOA). The NOA must be in hand before MUHC Authorization is issued.
    - **Medical device (Class II, III, or IV) or IVDD:** Obtain an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)</Tooltip> from the Medical Devices Bureau (MDB). For Class III and IV devices, the approved REB application must also be submitted to HC before enrolment begins — this is a separate obligation under the Medical Devices Regulations.

    Submit your HC application in parallel with your Nagano submission — do not wait for one before starting the other. See [Health Canada Submissions](/kb/regulations) for the full submission track table and SOP references.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
**The MUHC Authorization letter is the green light — not REB approval alone.** REB approval, Health Canada authorization, and institutional feasibility review must all be positive before the Personne mandatée issues MUHC Authorization. Do not begin study procedures — including screening and consent — until the Authorization letter is in your ISF.
</Warning>

### Multi-site studies

- **If the MUHC is the lead site:** your Nagano submission covers all participating Quebec <Tooltip tip="Quebec's public health and social services network (Réseau de la santé et des services sociaux), including the RI-MUHC." headline="RSSS" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#rsss">RSSS</Tooltip> institutions. You are the REB Lead. Each participating site still completes its own institutional feasibility review and obtains its own MUHC Authorization from its Personne mandatée.
- **If the MUHC is a participating site (MEO):** the reviewing REB is the lead institution's REB. Your Nagano file is a participating-site submission. You still require RI-MUHC feasibility review and MUHC Authorization. Contact the Research Facilitator before starting a MEO submission.
- For industry-sponsored multi-site trials, CATALIS FAST TRACK may compress the authorization timeline to eight weeks or less. The RI-MUHC participates in the FAST TRACK network.

### Sponsor-Investigator studies

If you are both Sponsor and QI, you hold dual obligations: filing the CTA with Health Canada, arranging independent monitoring, managing the investigational product supply chain including GMP compliance, and fulfilling all QI site responsibilities. Health Canada does not distinguish between commercial and non-commercial sponsors. You must also register the clinical trial on ClinicalTrials.gov before the first participant is enrolled ([SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007)).

You must fund independent monitoring (you cannot monitor your own study), IP supply and management, the Trial Master File, SUSAR reporting to Health Canada, and — for multi-site studies — oversight of all participating sites. The insurance framework is also more complex than for industry-sponsored studies; discuss with the Research Agreements Office early in planning, not at submission. See [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/budgets-contracts) and [Sponsor-Investigator Studies](/kb/sponsor-investigator).

## Building and maintaining your team

### Setting up the delegation log

- The Task Delegation Log must be set up before any study tasks begin — including your own. Name every team member, their role, their specific delegated tasks, and their start date. You countersign each entry; team members sign to accept delegation.
- Every person on the log must hold current institutional training at the correct level before being added. You are confirming this with your countersignature.
- Appoint a Sub-Investigator as your backup who also holds RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status equivalent to yours. Fellows and rotating residents cannot serve as PI backup.
- If you use CIM services, a CIM service agreement must be in place before CIM staff are added to the delegation log. Pharmacy maintains its own Task Delegation Log — obtain it and file it in the ISF before close-out.

<Tip>
  **Running a Phase I, oncology, or IV/infusion study?** The [Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)](/cim/planning) provides clinical research infrastructure — nursing, coordination, monitoring, regulatory support, and technical platforms. CIM offers both turnkey and à la carte service models. Engage early: CIM availability is limited and a service agreement must be in place before CIM staff are added to your delegation log.
</Tip>

### Training requirements for your team

Your entire team trains at the same level as the study requires. File every team member's current CV and all training certificates in the ISF before the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip>. CVs must be signed and dated within the past 2 years. When a team member's training certificate lapses mid-study, they must come off the delegation log until renewed. You are responsible for monitoring your team's certificate expiry, not just your own.

### Staff changes during the study

- **When a team member joins:** confirm their training is current, add them to the delegation log with your countersignature, notify the Sponsor, and update the study contact list.
- **When a team member leaves:** record their end date on the delegation log with your countersignature confirming undelegation. Notify the Sponsor. Submit a Nagano amendment (F1) for any change to listed investigators.
- **If you leave the study:** both you and the incoming QI/PI must sign the Task Delegation Log. The Sponsor and REB must be notified promptly. A Nagano amendment is required. The outgoing QI/PI must delegate all QI/PI tasks to the incoming QI/PI before departing.

For hiring research staff, workforce planning, and employment agreements, see [Hiring and HR Support](/kb/hiring-hr-support). For inclusive team composition, recruitment strategies, and GBA+ consultation (required by CIHR), see [EDI in Research](/kb/edi).

### Sub-Investigator responsibilities

A Sub-Investigator (Sub-I) is a physician or qualified professional who assists the QI/PI and can serve as PI backup when you are unavailable. The Sub-I is not a regulatory title in Canadian law — it is defined by what you delegate on the Task Delegation Log. Understanding the boundaries matters, because monitors and inspectors will check whether the Sub-I who performed a task was actually qualified and delegated to do it.

**What a Sub-I can do** (when delegated and documented on the TDL):

- Eligibility assessments and enrollment decisions
- Obtaining informed consent (if appropriately trained per [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008))
- Clinical assessments and medical evaluations during study visits
- AE/SAE causality and severity assessments
- IP prescription (drugs, biologics) — requires medical licensure
- Protocol deviation assessment and documentation
- Serving as PI backup during absences (if they hold the required credentials — see below)

**What a Sub-I cannot do:**

- Sign as QI/PI on regulatory documents (CTA submissions, REB applications, Nagano forms) — these require your signature specifically
- Act as PI backup without holding RI-MUHC [Human Research Privileges](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status) (or Researcher Status for non-interventional studies). Researcher Status alone is not sufficient for interventional studies with investigational products (Levels I–III).
- Override your medical judgment on eligibility, causality, or clinical significance determinations

**Credential requirements for Sub-I backup:**

| Study type | Minimum credential |
|---|---|
| Interventional with IP (Levels I–III) | RI-MUHC Human Research Privileges (CPDP member) |
| Observational / non-interventional (Level IV–V) | RI-MUHC Researcher Status |

<Warning>
**Fellows and rotating residents cannot serve as PI backup.** Fellows and longitudinal residents may be named as Sub-Investigators on the TDL with a qualified title (e.g., "Sub-I (fellow)"), but all their medical assessments must be co-signed by you or a qualifying Sub-I with Privileges. Rotating residents cannot be named as Sub-Investigators at all. See [Trainee Onboarding](/kb/roles/onboarding-trainees) for the full scope limitations. ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002))
</Warning>

**Pharmacist exception:** although pharmacists are CPDP members, they are not eligible for Human Research Privileges under MSSS and MUHC frameworks. Pharmacists acting as Sub-I backup must obtain Human Researcher Status instead.

<Note>
**Grant applications vs. study documents:** for grant applications, fellows and residents may be listed as Sub-Investigators on the protocol or grant proposal. However, they should generally not be named as Sub-Investigators on the Task Delegation Log. On the TDL they should be listed as student, fellow, resident, or research assistant.
</Note>

## Ongoing obligations during the study

### What direct supervision means in practice

Delegating tasks does not mean stepping away from the study. Direct supervision means you are available and reachable during study activities, you review all data and safety documents in a timely manner, and you document that review with your initials. You do not need to be physically present for every procedure — but if a team member performs a delegated task requiring medical oversight, you must be contactable.

Before the Site Initiation Visit (SIV), confirm with your CRC that: the ISF is assembled and complete, the delegation log lists all team members with current training, study supplies and IP storage are arranged through pharmacy, and consent forms match the REB-approved version. Your presence at the SIV is expected — the Sponsor's CRA will confirm these items with you directly.

During active conduct, ensure your team has a documented escalation path for urgent safety issues: who they contact, by what channel, and the expected response time. If you are unavailable, your Sub-Investigator backup must be reachable and the team must know to redirect.

The most common cause of study halts at active sites is a PI becoming unavailable without an adequately qualified Sub-I backup named on the delegation log. If you are going on sabbatical, parental leave, or an extended absence, appoint a Sub-I backup with Privileges or Researcher Status before you leave and update the delegation log and Nagano accordingly.

[SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002) recommends writing a department-specific working instruction describing how QI/PI oversight is achieved for your team: how safety documents are distributed, how quickly you commit to reviewing and initialling them, and who contacts you if an urgent issue arises outside normal hours.

### Medical oversight

You retain medical oversight of all study activities at the site even when tasks are delegated. For every AE and SAE, your CRC documents and reports to you — you assess causality, determine seriousness, and document your assessment. Your initials are required on every SAE report before it is filed.

When a team member identifies a potential adverse event, you assess causality (relationship to the investigational product), severity, seriousness, and expectedness. This assessment cannot be delegated. For SAEs, your initial assessment must be documented within 24 hours. If you disagree with the Sponsor's or CRA's causality assessment, document your rationale — your clinical judgment as QI takes precedence at the site level. Post-study SAEs remain your responsibility for the follow-up period defined in the protocol.

SAE reporting timelines are fixed — see [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012) for the full schedule. SUSAR reports distributed by the Sponsor must be reviewed, initialled, and dated by you to document your awareness. In Quebec, investigational product management is delegated to a trained pharmacist, but you retain ultimate responsibility for ensuring the IP is used per the approved protocol ([SOP-CR-010](/sops/cr-010)).

### Informed consent obligations

See also: [Consent in Quebec](/kb/consent) | [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008) | [SOP-CR-006](/sops/cr-006)

You are responsible for ensuring informed consent is obtained from every participant before any study procedure. Document the consent process in the source record: who conducted the discussion, when, that the participant had adequate time to consider, that questions were answered, and that they signed voluntarily. The ICF signature alone is not sufficient documentation.

- **Re-consent obligations:** when a protocol amendment affects participant risk, burden, or procedures, you must ensure enrolled participants are re-consented using the newly approved ICF before the change is implemented. Document the re-consent discussion separately.
- **Withdrawal:** if a participant withdraws consent mid-study, document the withdrawal, confirm whether already-collected data may be retained (per the original ICF), and follow the protocol's withdrawal procedures.
- **Incapacitated adults in Quebec — Civil Code Art. 21:** research involving an incapacitated adult requires substitute consent from the person with legal authority AND authorization from an MSSS-designated REB. If the incapacitated person understands and objects, they cannot be enrolled regardless of substitute consent ([SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008)).
- Under Quebec's _Charte de la langue française_ (Bill 96), consent forms at public institutions must be available in French. Always use the current REB-approved ICF version.

No study procedure — including screening blood draws, eligibility questionnaires, or medical record review for screening — may be performed before signed informed consent is obtained and documented. If the protocol requires pre-screening activities before formal consent, confirm the permissible scope with the REB.

### Protocol deviations

All deviations must be documented. Major or critical deviations must be reported to QA and the REB. If your team identifies a potential deviation, they should notify you before acting — not after. Concerns about participant safety, informed consent, or undue risk should be reported to the MUHC REB immediately, even without a formal protocol deviation ([MUHC REB SOP 405](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) and [SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026)).

### Conflict of interest

COI disclosure operates through **two independent tracks** — both are required. The **McGill institutional track** requires annual disclosure of all ongoing interests to your supervisor or dean. The **REB study-level track** requires disclosure at initial Nagano submission and whenever a new conflict arises — financial relationships with the Sponsor (consulting fees, equity, IP royalties, research funding), any personal stake in the outcome, and any relationship that a reasonable observer could perceive as a conflict. The COI is also disclosed to participants in the ICF.

If a COI arises during a study, disclose it promptly via Nagano amendment — do not wait for annual renewal. See [Conflict of Interest](/kb/governance/conflict-of-interest).

### Research integrity

You are responsible for honest, rigorous, and transparent conduct by your entire team. Research misconduct — fabrication, falsification, or plagiarism — is defined consistently across TCPS2, the Tri-Agency Framework, and McGill policy. If you become aware of suspected misconduct, you have an obligation to report it through McGill's structures. Not every error is misconduct; the test is whether there was an intentional departure from accepted standards or reckless disregard for them. See [Research Integrity](/kb/governance/research-integrity).

### Annual renewal

Track the REB approval expiry date from day one. Submit the renewal via Nagano before approval lapses — a lapse halts the study and requires a new submission cycle. MUHC Authorization auto-renews with each timely REB renewal; a lapsed REB approval voids it. The renewal submission includes a progress report, updated participant counts, any new safety information, and a current list of study personnel ([SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007)).

<Note>
**Proposed regulatory change — investigator eligibility.** Health Canada published a proposal in the Canada Gazette (December 2025) that would broaden QI eligibility beyond physicians and dentists to other licensed healthcare practitioners. This remains in consultation as of April 2026. Monitor developments if this affects your team structure.
</Note>

## Close-out, inspections, and archiving

### At close-out

See also: [Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out) | [SOP-CR-016](/sops/cr-016)

Review and sign off on the completed ISF before the Sponsor's <Tooltip tip="Final monitoring visit to verify all study closure requirements have been addressed before database lock." headline="Close-Out Visit (COV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cov">Close-Out Visit</Tooltip>. Ensure all monitoring letters are initialled, all <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> corrections are complete, and the delegation log has end dates and your signature for all team members coming off the study. You and your CRC remain on the delegation log after close-out to respond to post-close-out queries. Submit the Study Completion Report to the REB via Nagano and update the ClinicalTrials.gov registration ([SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026)).

### Health Canada inspections

Health Canada inspects approximately 2% of Canadian clinical trial sites per year. Inspections can be announced (5 days to 4 weeks notice) or unannounced, and typically last 5 or more business days. Observations are classified as Risk 1 (critical), Risk 2 (major), or Risk 3 (minor) under GUI-0043.

As PI, you are present for the opening and closing meetings with the inspector. You provide access to the site, the ISF, and study personnel. QA leads the coordination — notify QA as soon as an inspection notice is received and do not move or reorganize any study documents before QA reviews the situation ([SOP-CR-017](/sops/cr-017)).

See also: [Monitoring, Audits, and Inspections](/kb/monitoring-audits) | [Hosting a Health Canada Inspection](/kb/hc-inspection-guide) | [The Investigator Site File](/kb/isf)

### Archiving

**15-year retention** applies to Health Canada regulated studies (drug, device, NHP) under Division 5 (C.05.012). The ISF must remain complete, secure, and retrievable for the full retention period. Do not destroy any study documents without written authorization from you and the Sponsor. If you change institutions before the retention period ends, notify the Sponsor and ensure storage transfer arrangements are made. You retain personal accountability for the integrity of the archived file ([SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015)).

For downloadable ISF templates, delegation logs, and archiving checklists, see [SOP Appendices and Templates](/sops/appendices).
