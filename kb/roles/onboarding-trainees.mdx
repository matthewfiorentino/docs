What you need to know as a graduate student, postdoctoral fellow, clinical fellow, or resident joining a clinical research team at the RI-MUHC — training requirements, what you can be delegated, and rules that apply specifically to trainees.

## Who is a research trainee

A research trainee is a student working under the supervision of a <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> at the RI-MUHC, enrolled in a Canadian university program. This includes undergraduate students, MSc and PhD candidates, clinical (MD) fellows, and postdoctoral fellows. Postdoctoral fellows must be registered with McGill University.

<Warning>
  **Trainee or volunteer?** The distinction matters for what you can do. If you are enrolled full-time in a Canadian university program and the work relates to your thesis or academic research, you are a trainee. If you are not enrolled, or the work is unrelated to your own research, you must be onboarded as a volunteer instead — with a more restricted task scope. See [Volunteering on a Research Team](/kb/roles/onboarding-volunteers).
</Warning>

## What you can be delegated

Trainees have a broader task scope than volunteers. With appropriate training and delegation, you can participate in most study activities.

**You can:**

- Be listed on research protocols
- Approach and interact with research participants (with training and delegation)
- Access identifiable patient health records (with appropriate authorization)
- Perform delegated study tasks — data collection, consent (if trained and delegated), specimen handling, documentation, and other tasks as specified on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">TDL</Tooltip>

**You cannot:**

- Perform acts reserved to licensed professionals — blood draws, medication administration, and other acts reserved under Quebec's *Code des professions* require the appropriate licence regardless of your delegation
- Lead a study independently — trainees are not eligible for Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status
- Serve as <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI backup — this applies specifically to fellows and residents (see below)

Your specific tasks are defined on the Task Delegation Log, countersigned by your PI. You may only perform tasks that are explicitly listed for you on the log.

## Getting started

Complete all steps before performing any study tasks.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete institutional SOP training">
    Complete the SOP Reader and Competency Assessment in TalentLMS at the level required for the study type. There is no exemption for students. Training must be documented on a valid certificate before you are added to the TDL. See [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete external certifications (if required by study type)">
    <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> (Levels I and II), Health Canada Division 5 (Level I), ISO 14155 (Level II). The RI-MUHC has a CITI licence — free for investigators, staff, and trainees.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sign the Confidentiality and Security Agreement">
    Required before accessing any RI-MUHC systems or identifiable data ([SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019), Appendix 1). See the confidentiality section below.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Be added to the Task Delegation Log">
    Your PI adds you with your specific delegated tasks and countersigns to confirm your qualification. You sign to accept. No study tasks before this is complete. See [SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete mandatory institutional training">
    Five courses are required for all members of The Institute in TalentLMS: Let's Be Allies (Foundations in EDI), Cybersecurity Basics at The Institute, Prevention of Violence and Psychological Harassment in the Workplace and Research Space, Creating a Culture of Care, and MUHC Emergency Measures. These are institutional requirements, separate from your clinical research SOP training.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete protocol-specific training">
    Training on the specific study protocol, procedures, and systems before you begin any tasks. For industry-sponsored trials, this is typically delivered at the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#siv">Site Initiation Visit</Tooltip>. This is separate from institutional SOP training. Document all training on the Training Log.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Training requirements

Your training level is set by the study type, not by your role. A trainee on a Level I drug trial needs Level I SOP Reader, Competency Assessment, GCP, and Division 5 — the same as a <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip> on the same study. Your PI confirms this with their countersignature on the delegation log.

Training certificates are valid for 2 years (Levels I–III) or 5 years (Levels IV–V). If your certificate expires during your placement, you must stop performing delegated tasks until it is renewed. TalentLMS sends reminders before expiry.

For the full training matrix, see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements).

## Fellows and residents

Clinical fellows and residents can participate on research teams but occupy a restricted position.

- Fellows and longitudinal residents can be named as Sub-Investigators on the Task Delegation Log, with the title "Sub-I (fellow)" or "Sub-I (longitudinal resident)"
- Cannot serve as QI/PI backup — fellows and residents are not eligible for Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status
- Cannot perform unsupervised QI/PI tasks — all medical assessments must be co-signed by the QI/PI or a qualifying Sub-Investigator with Privileges
- Rotating residents cannot be named as Sub-Investigators at all

Departments that delegate tasks to fellows or longitudinal residents must have a department-specific SOP detailing which tasks are delegated and how supervision is documented.

<Note>
  **Grant applications vs. study documents:** for grant applications, fellows and residents may be listed as Sub-Investigators on the protocol or grant proposal. However, they should generally not be named as Sub-Investigators on the Task Delegation Log or other essential study documentation. On the TDL they may be listed as student, fellow, resident, or research assistant. ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002))
</Note>

## Confidentiality and privacy

You must sign the MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement ([SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019), Appendix 1) before accessing any RI-MUHC systems or identifiable participant data. This is an institutional requirement separate from any study-specific confidentiality agreement.

The rules are the same as for any team member: never discuss participant information outside the research team, do not photograph or copy documents containing patient information, and handle identifiable data only within the scope authorized by the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>. These obligations continue after your placement ends.

See [Privacy and Confidentiality in Research](/kb/conduct/privacy) for the full regulatory framework.

## Employment status and stipend

Research trainees are not employees of the RI-MUHC. You receive a stipend or bursary from your PI's research grant, administered as a scholarship — not processed through RI-MUHC payroll. This stipend supports your academic progression and thesis work and is treated accordingly for tax purposes (T4A / Relevé 1).

The exception is postdoctoral fellows, for whom federal income tax is deducted directly from the stipend.

Your employment status does not change your compliance obligations. The same SOP training, TDL, and confidentiality requirements apply to trainees as to employees.

## Support and supervision

As a research trainee, you are supported by two institutions: your university and The Institute.

### Your supervisor (PI)

Your PI is your primary point of contact for research-related questions, study tasks, and day-to-day supervision. If challenges arise — unclear expectations, lack of communication, concerns about progress — speak with your Graduate Program Advisor or Director first. For confidential advice, McGill's Ombudsperson for Students is available to all McGill-registered trainees.

### Who handles what

| Concern | Your university | The Institute |
|---|---|---|
| Academic program, thesis, coursework | Graduate program staff, GPS | — |
| Issues with supervision or your PI | GPS, Ombudsperson | — |
| Scholarships, registration, student life | GPS or Enrolment Services | — |
| Stipend, eEspresso, payroll questions | — | HR Service Centre — **ri.hr.centre@muhc.mcgill.ca** |
| Research space, safety, workplace conduct | — | Your supervisor, Program staff |
| Harassment or inappropriate behaviour | Office for Mediation and Reporting, Ombudsperson | — |
| Career and professional development | CaPS, Skillsets | DCAT, Tremplin |
| Clinical research compliance questions | — | Your PI, <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip>, Research Facilitator |

Non-McGill trainees: your home university remains responsible for academic registration, program progress, and student services.

## Resources for trainees

### Research Trainee Handbook

Your primary reference for day-to-day essentials: Portal access, ID cards, stipend payments and eEspresso, IT support, web apps, campus logistics, lab safety, and institutional training courses. Ask your supervisor or program staff if you have not received a copy.

### Desjardins Centre for Advanced Training (DCAT)

DCAT is the only program of its kind in Quebec housed within a health research institute. It provides professional and career development opportunities for life science researchers at every level — from undergraduate students to postdoctoral fellows.

- **Career seminars** — Demystifying Your Career Options, Job and Internship Hunting, Breaking Down Barriers for Career Growth, Identifying and Leveraging Transferable Skills
- **Career exploration** — Future in Focus (Precision Medicine Career Insight and Discovery Program), Career Spark Networking Lunch and Career Day
- **Career advising and mentorship** — one-on-one advising and mentorship activities connecting you with professionals across science, policy, healthcare, and innovation

DCAT is a partner in the Tremplin Consortium of McGill-affiliated health centres, funded by the Fonds de recherche du Québec (FRQ). Contact **dcat@muhc.mcgill.ca** to join the trainee newsletter.

### CANTRAIN

Canada's national clinical trials training platform, free for RI-MUHC investigators, staff, and trainees. Stream 1 is the Graduate and Postdoctoral Trainee Career Development Program (offered through the DCAT/Tremplin partnership), covering clinical trial methodology, regulatory frameworks, and career pathways in clinical research. The Common Core Foundation (14 modules) provides a shared baseline across all streams. CANTRAIN courses count toward SOCRA and ACRP continuing education hours.

## During your placement

- You work under the supervision of your PI. If you are uncertain about whether a task is within your scope or how to handle a situation, ask before proceeding.
- If your tasks change during the placement, the TDL must be updated and countersigned by your PI before you perform the new tasks.
- If you witness a potential safety issue, protocol deviation, or confidentiality concern, report it to your PI or the CRC immediately.
- Keep your training current. If your certificate expires, you must stop performing delegated tasks until renewal is complete.
- When your placement ends, your PI records your end date on the Task Delegation Log.
