## Understanding the role

Level V studies use existing data or previously collected specimens. In most cases there is no participant contact, no consent process, no <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip>, no monitoring, and no <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reporting. Your primary accountability areas are: ensuring the ÉFVP and consent waiver are properly obtained, authorizing and controlling data access, and maintaining REB compliance. The same [four-layer regulatory framework](/kb/governance/regulatory-framework) applies — including Quebec privacy law (Loi 25, ÉFVP) and [institutional requirements](/kb/governance/institutional-structure) (MUHC Authorization, RI-MUHC SOPs) that are not covered by standard GCP training.

## The consent waiver decision

<Note>
See also: [Privacy and Confidentiality in Research](/kb/conduct/privacy) | [Informed Consent](/kb/conduct/consent)
</Note>

Most retrospective studies operate under a consent waiver, but under <Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act to modernize personal information protection law (in force Sept 2023). Requires EFVP before sharing personal data without consent." headline="Law 25 / Loi 25" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#law-25">Loi 25</Tooltip>, whether a waiver is granted depends on the ÉFVP (privacy impact assessment).

<Warning>
**The legal default is always to obtain consent.** Access to identifiable data without consent requires approval from the ÉFVP committee. A waiver is granted when the committee determines that obtaining consent is not feasible — for example, a chart review of 500 patients from 2000 to 2010, where many patients may have died, been lost to follow-up, or have outdated contact information.

In contrast, if you are reviewing records for a small number of recent patients — say, five patients from the last year — the committee may determine it is reasonable to contact those patients and obtain consent, even though the research is retrospective.
</Warning>

The ÉFVP committee evaluates whether obtaining consent is infeasible — not merely inconvenient. Factors include: whether participants can be located, whether contacting them would reveal their health status to third parties, and whether the research could practicably be conducted with consent. A large cohort size alone does not establish infeasibility; the committee assesses the specific circumstances.

**Timeline:** Allow 4–6 weeks for the ÉFVP review. The outcome is communicated to the REB, which makes the final consent waiver decision. If the ÉFVP determines consent is feasible, you must obtain consent from each participant — consider whether this changes the study design or feasibility before proceeding.

If consent is required, you and your team follow the same consent process described in the [Level IV PI Guide](/kb/roles/pi-guide-iv) and the corresponding consent SOPs, even though the study remains Level V in every other respect. Confirm the consent requirement before your team begins any data extraction.

## Prerequisites

- **Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status** — Researcher Status is valid for Level V. Apply via TalentLMS. Valid for 3 years. The 90-day rule applies at submission.
- **SOP Reader and Competency Assessment** — Level V path (SOP-LR, 9 SOPs, 5-year renewal). Complete both in TalentLMS.

## Study submission

<Note>
See also: [Preparing Your Submission](/kb/submission/preparing) | [Submitting via Nagano](/kb/submission/submitting-via-nagano)
</Note>

- Submit via Nagano. No Health Canada submission is required.
- The F17/F18 forms in Nagano trigger the ÉFVP if identifiable data is involved. Complete these accurately.
- The REB approval should explicitly state whether a consent waiver has been granted and specify the authorized data sources and patient population.
- **Budget and agreements:** Most Level V studies are grant-funded or unfunded. If applying for funding, use the [RI-MUHC Budget Tool](/kb/planning/data-services) to account for staff time for data extraction, CORD database builds, Data Warehouse access, and BCU statistical support. Overhead is 0% for peer-reviewed grants, 15% for foundation-funded studies. Foundation-funded studies are also subject to REB billing fees. If data will be shared outside the MUHC — including with collaborators at other institutions or jurisdictions — a Data Transfer Agreement is required before any data leaves the site. Submit contracts via My Contracts on the Portal. See [Contracts, Budgets, and Agreements](/kb/planning/budgets-contracts).
- <Tooltip tip="Institutional authorization allowing a study to proceed at the RI-MUHC. Issued after ethics approval and feasibility review." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> from the <Tooltip tip="Individual formally authorized to issue the final written authorization for each research project at a Quebec RSSS institution." headline="Personne mandatée (PFM)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> is required before your team accesses any patient records for research purposes.

## Data access and privacy

<Note>
See also: [Privacy and Confidentiality in Research](/kb/conduct/privacy) | [Research Data Security](/kb/conduct/data-security)
</Note>

Privacy is the highest-risk area for retrospective studies. You are responsible for ensuring your team accesses only the data authorized by the REB and handles it in compliance with Quebec law.

- **OACIS access** must be authorized specifically for the study. For data beyond OACIS (lab results at scale, pharmacy dispensing, administrative data), the MUHC Data Warehouse may be required.
- **De-identification is not optional.** The research database must contain participant IDs only. The identification key must be stored separately with restricted access.
- **Data transfer agreements** are required if data is shared outside the MUHC. Cross-border transfers (data leaving Quebec or Canada) have additional requirements under Loi 25.
- At study completion, determine the **disposition of identifiable data** — destroy the identification key, retain it for a specified period, or transfer responsibility. Document this decision.

**De-identification standards:** Remove or code all direct identifiers (name, health insurance number, date of birth, address, phone number). Indirect identifiers (age, sex, diagnosis date, postal code) may remain if they cannot reasonably re-identify an individual in context. When in doubt, consult the CORD team. The identification key linking participant IDs to identifiers must be stored separately from the research database — encrypted, access-restricted, and with a documented disposition plan (destroy after analysis, or archive under specific conditions).

**Data extraction:** Define the extraction plan before beginning — which variables, from which systems, for which date range. Have a second team member verify a sample (e.g., 10%) of extracted records against source for accuracy. Document the verification in the study file. If the extraction scope changes mid-study, submit an amendment before implementing the change.

<Info>
CORD can build and test REDCap databases for your study. Contact them early — database architecture decisions are costly to change after extraction begins. See [CORD](/kb/planning/cord).
</Info>

The [MUHC Data Warehouse](/kb/planning/data-warehouse) centralizes clinical and administrative data from over 18 hospital systems. For cohort identification and feasibility assessment, the Data Warehouse is often the starting point. For statistical methodology, sample size considerations, and analysis planning, consult the [Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU)](/kb/planning/bcu).

## Ongoing obligations

- **<Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>:** set up before any data access begins. See [Building Your Research Team](/kb/planning/building-your-team). Your team members must have current SOP training before being added. Countersign each entry.
- **Annual REB renewal:** submit via Nagano before approval expires. Include updated progress and any changes to data extraction scope. See [Tracking and Responding to Submissions](/kb/submission/tracking-responding).
- **Amendments:** if the data extraction plan changes — new variables, expanded population, new data sources — submit an amendment before implementing the change.
- **Deviations:** document all deviations, including unauthorized data access or extraction outside the approved scope. Report major deviations to QA and the REB. See [Reportable Events](/kb/conduct/reportable-events).
- **Conflict of interest:** COI disclosure operates through two independent tracks — McGill institutional (annual disclosure to your supervisor) and REB study-level (at Nagano submission and whenever a new conflict arises). Both are required. If a COI arises mid-study, disclose promptly via Nagano amendment. See [Conflict of Interest](/kb/governance/conflict-of-interest).
- **Research integrity:** The same research integrity obligations apply to retrospective studies. Data fabrication, falsification, or selective omission of records in a chart review is research misconduct. If you become aware of suspected misconduct, report it through McGill's structures. See [Research Integrity](/kb/governance/research-integrity).

## Close-out

- Confirm all data extraction is complete and the database is final. See [Study Close-Out](/kb/conduct/close-out).
- Submit the Study Completion Report to the REB via Nagano.
- Record end dates on the delegation log for all team members.
- Execute the data disposition plan — destroy or archive the identification key as documented.
- Archive the study file. The 15-year Division 5 retention requirement does not apply. Confirm retention requirements with QA based on funding source and institutional policy.
