As a Clinical Research Coordinator, you are the operational backbone of site-level trial management. Unlike the Qualified Investigator, the CRC role has no regulatory definition in Canadian law — there is no national licence, no regulated title, and no statutory scope of practice. What defines your role is what has been delegated to you on the Task Delegation Log and what your own professional credentials permit you to do independently.

## Institutional setup

When you are hired, you receive the RI-MUHC Research Staff Handbook by email. Complete the following steps in your first days.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set up your Portal account">
    Your login credentials arrive by email. The Portal is where you access applications, submit IT requests, complete training, book rooms, and manage your profile.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get your ID card">
    Your supervisor requests this through the Portal. Your card gives you access to RI-MUHC sites and specific rooms. At the Glen site, tap your card at a main entrance before interior doors will work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up Logibec eEspresso">
    Approve your timesheet every pay period. When recording training time, use the code **TRAIN** for TalentLMS courses and **JOBTR** for on-the-job training. Submit training certificates to **training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete mandatory institutional training">
    Five courses are required for all members of The Institute in TalentLMS: Let's Be Allies (Foundations in EDI), Cybersecurity Basics at The Institute, Prevention of Violence and Psychological Harassment in the Workplace and Research Space, Creating a Culture of Care, and MUHC Emergency Measures. These are institutional requirements, separate from your clinical research training.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the Research Staff Handbook">
    Covers benefits (sick/personal days, group insurance, pension plan, vacation), the Employee Assistance Program (EAP — free, confidential support available 24/7), Code of Conduct, and how the RI-MUHC is structured.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Clinical research training

Before you can be delegated tasks on a study, you need SOP training, regulatory certification (if your level requires it), and a signed Task Delegation Log. These requirements apply to everyone — regardless of role.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete GCP certification (Levels I and II only)">
    ICH Good Clinical Practice training through any recognized provider — the RI-MUHC has a CITI licence (free for staff). Certificates valid for 3 years from other providers are recognized for 2 years at the RI-MUHC. For Level II (medical device studies), completing ICH-GCP satisfies the requirement.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete Health Canada Division 5 training (Level I only)">
    Required if your study involves drugs under a Clinical Trial Application — biologics, gene/cell therapies, vaccines, radiopharmaceuticals, or NHPs. Available through CITI or other recognized providers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete ISO 14155 training (Level II only)">
    Required if your study involves medical devices under an Investigational Testing Authorization.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the SOP Reader">
    Read and acknowledge every SOP assigned to your training level in TalentLMS. Your initial reading must be completed and tracked through the Reader to count toward your compliance record.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the Competency Assessment">
    Scenario-based assessment certifying your understanding of the SOPs. Two paths: SOP-CR (Levels I–III, renews every 2 years) and SOP-LR (Levels IV–V, renews every 5 years).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Be added to the Task Delegation Log">
    Once your training is complete, the PI adds you to the TDL specifying which tasks you are authorized to perform. You cannot perform any study-related task until this is signed by both you and the PI.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete protocol-specific training">
    The sponsor or PI provides training on the specific study protocol before you begin any study tasks. This is separate from your institutional SOP training. Document all training on the Training Log.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  **Training requirements depend on your study type.** Drug trials (Level I), device studies (Level II), and NHP studies (Level III) follow the SOP-CR series with a 2-year renewal. Prospective observational (Level IV) and retrospective studies (Level V) follow the SOP-LR series with a 5-year renewal. For the full matrix, see [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements).
</Info>

### The Task Delegation Log and scope limitations

The Task Delegation Log defines what you are authorized to do on each study. Your PI's countersignature confirms your qualification for those tasks — but it does not transfer their accountability to you, and it does not transfer yours to them.

<Warning>
  **Scope limitation for non-nurse CRCs:** Quebec's *Code des professions* reserves specific clinical acts to licensed nurses. Non-nurse CRCs cannot perform blood draws, administer medication, perform patient assessments, or carry out any act reserved to nurses — regardless of what appears on the Task Delegation Log or the study protocol. If your study requires these tasks, a licensed nurse or CRC-Nurse must be delegated those activities. See the [Nurse Training Pathway](/kb/roles/nurse-pathway) for details.
</Warning>

## Competency domains

### Documentation and the ISF

The ISF is the site's regulatory binder. You set it up before the first participant is enrolled and maintain it throughout the study. It must be inspection-ready at all times: current protocol, REB approvals, delegation log, training records, CVs, correspondence, and financial disclosures.

- Set up and organize the ISF according to ICH GCP requirements
- File essential documents before, during, and after the study
- Maintain the delegation log, training records, and CV collection
- Track protocol versions and ensure the correct version is in use
- Prepare documentation for archiving at close-out

### Informed consent

Consent is both a document and a process. You explain the study's purpose, procedures, risks, and alternatives. In Quebec, consent is governed by the Civil Code (Articles 20, 21, 24, 25), and specific rules apply to minors and incapable persons.

- Obtain and document written informed consent before any study procedures
- Re-consent participants when protocol amendments change the risk–benefit profile
- Apply consent requirements for minors (Art. 21 CCQ) and incapable adults
- Maintain the ICF version log and ensure only the current approved version is used

### Recruitment and screening

You screen potential participants against inclusion/exclusion criteria, coordinate with clinical teams to identify candidates, track screening and enrolment numbers, and ensure all recruitment materials have REB approval.

- Pre-screen candidates using medical records and referral lists
- Apply inclusion and exclusion criteria to determine eligibility
- Track and report recruitment and retention metrics
- Ensure all recruitment materials are REB-approved before use

### Data collection and integrity

Every data point you record must be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, and accurate (ALCOA+). Data corrections must follow Good Documentation Practice — single line through the error, date, initials, and reason.

- Create and maintain source documents for each participant
- Enter data into paper CRFs or electronic data capture (EDC) systems
- Resolve data queries from the sponsor or monitoring team
- Ensure CRF entries match source documents (source data verification)

### Safety reporting (Levels I–III)

You are often the first person to learn about an adverse event. Your job is to recognize it, document it accurately, assess whether it meets the threshold for serious (SAE) or unexpected (SUSAR), and report it to the PI for medical assessment. SAEs must be reported to the sponsor within 24 hours.

- Recognize and document adverse events at every study visit
- Notify the PI immediately when an AE may meet SAE criteria
- Complete SAE report forms and submit to the sponsor within 24 hours
- File SUSAR notifications from the sponsor in the ISF

### Regulatory and REB submissions

You prepare and submit REB applications, amendments, annual renewals, reportable events, and study closures through Nagano. You track submission status, respond to REB queries, and ensure all approvals are current and filed.

<Note>
  Your CRC can build the entire F11 submission in Nagano on behalf of the PI — but the submission is not complete until the PI completes the final commitment and signature step. You cannot substitute for that signature.
</Note>

- Prepare initial REB submissions and respond to reviewer queries
- Submit protocol amendments, annual renewals, and reportable events
- Track approval status and expiry dates
- Coordinate with the sponsor on Health Canada filings (CTAs, ITAs)

### Operational guidance by study stage

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Pre-activation">
    The MUHC Authorization letter must be in the ISF before any recruitment, screening, or consent activity begins. REB approval alone is not sufficient.

    - Set up the ISF structure per SOP-CR-015 before the Site Initiation Visit
    - File all regulatory documents: protocol, ICF, IB, REB approval, HC authorization (NOL or ITA), and QIU
    - Confirm the executed clinical trial agreement is filed
    - Complete the delegation log before any study task — including your own
    - File current CVs and training certificates for all team members
    - Document SIV training on the Training Log
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Consent and enrolment">
    The consent phase carries the highest risk of protocol deviations. The rules are specific and some are Quebec-specific.

    - Use only the current REB-approved ICF version — version control is your responsibility
    - Under Bill 96, provide the French version to French-speaking participants
    - Document the consent process in the source record — the ICF signature alone is not sufficient
    - Record every screened participant on the Screening Log, including screen failures and reasons
    - Record every enrolled participant on the Enrolment Log using participant IDs, not names
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Active conduct">
    Source data integrity, safety reporting, and monitoring visit management dominate the active phase.

    - Complete CRFs promptly after each visit; late entries require an explanatory note
    - Corrections: single line through the error, correct value, initial, date
    - When an AE occurs: document in source, notify PI immediately, complete SAE form within 24 hours
    - Before monitoring visits: resolve all outstanding queries from the previous letter
    - After monitoring visits: file the follow-up letter and chase PI initials promptly
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Annual renewal">
    REB approval lapses if renewal is not submitted on time. A lapse halts the study and is a major Health Canada inspection observation.

    - Track the REB approval expiry date from day one
    - Prepare the annual renewal submission in Nagano before approval expires
    - Include: progress report, updated participant counts, safety information, current personnel list
    - Confirm that QA feasibility (Privileges, training currency) will pass before PI sign-off
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Close-out">
    Close-out is CRC-heavy work. The Sponsor's monitor conducts a Close-Out Visit to verify ISF completeness and data integrity.

    - Resolve all outstanding data queries and complete final CRF entries
    - Ensure all monitoring letters are filed and PI-initialled
    - Record end dates for all team members on the delegation log
    - Submit the Study Completion Report to the REB via Nagano
    - For Division 5 studies: **15-year retention requirement** applies. Do not destroy documents without written authorization from the PI and Sponsor.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
