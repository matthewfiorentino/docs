This page covers onboarding requirements for three categories of people who contribute to research at the RI-MUHC without holding a staff position: research trainees, volunteers, and site observers. Each category has distinct eligibility rules, a different permitted task scope, and specific forms to complete before starting work.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Research Trainees">
    ## Who is a research trainee

    A research trainee is a student working under the supervision of a <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> at the RI-MUHC, enrolled in a Canadian university program. This includes undergraduate students, MSc and PhD candidates, clinical (MD) fellows, and postdoctoral fellows. Postdoctoral fellows must be registered with McGill University.

    <Note>
      **Trainee or volunteer?** If you are enrolled full-time in a Canadian university program and the work relates to your thesis or academic research, you are a trainee. If you are not enrolled, or the work is unrelated to your own research, you must be onboarded as a volunteer instead — with a more restricted task scope.
    </Note>

    ## What you can be delegated

    Trainees have a broader task scope than volunteers. With appropriate training and delegation, you can participate in most study activities:

    - Be listed on research protocols
    - Approach and interact with research participants (with training and delegation)
    - Access identifiable patient health records (with appropriate authorization)
    - Perform delegated study tasks — data collection, consent (if trained and delegated), specimen handling, documentation, and other tasks as specified on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">TDL</Tooltip>

    **Restrictions that apply regardless of delegation:**

    - Acts reserved to licensed professionals (blood draws, medication administration, and other acts reserved under Quebec's *Code des professions*) require the appropriate licence regardless of what is on the TDL
    - Trainees are not eligible for Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status and cannot lead a study independently
    - Trainees cannot serve as <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI backup

    Your specific tasks are defined on the Task Delegation Log, countersigned by your PI. You may only perform tasks that are explicitly listed for you.

    ## Getting started

    Complete all steps before performing any study tasks.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Complete institutional SOP training">
        Complete the SOP Reader and Competency Assessment in TalentLMS at the level required for the study type. There is no exemption for students. Training must be documented on a valid certificate before you are added to the TDL. See [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Complete external certifications if required">
        <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> (Levels I and II), Health Canada Division 5 (Level I), ISO 14155 (Level II). The RI-MUHC has a CITI licence — free for investigators, staff, and trainees.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Sign the Confidentiality and Security Agreement">
        Sign before accessing any RI-MUHC systems or identifiable data. This is the MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement ([SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019), Appendix 1), separate from any study-specific confidentiality agreement.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Be added to the Task Delegation Log">
        Your PI adds you with your specific delegated tasks and countersigns to confirm your qualification. You sign to accept. No study tasks before this is complete.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Complete mandatory institutional training">
        Five courses required for all members of The Institute in TalentLMS: Let's Be Allies (Foundations in EDI), Cybersecurity Basics at The Institute, Prevention of Violence and Psychological Harassment, Creating a Culture of Care, and MUHC Emergency Measures.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Complete protocol-specific training">
        Training on the specific study protocol, procedures, and systems before you begin any tasks. For industry-sponsored trials, this is typically delivered at the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#siv">Site Initiation Visit</Tooltip>. Document all training on the Training Log.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    ## Fellows and residents

    Clinical fellows and residents can participate on research teams but occupy a restricted position.

    - **Fellows and longitudinal residents** can be named as Sub-Investigators on the TDL, with the title "Sub-I (fellow)" or "Sub-I (longitudinal resident)"
    - **Cannot serve as QI/PI backup** — fellows and residents are not eligible for Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status
    - **Cannot perform unsupervised QI/PI tasks** — all medical assessments must be co-signed by the QI/PI or a qualifying Sub-Investigator with Privileges
    - **Rotating residents cannot be named as Sub-Investigators at all**

    Departments that delegate tasks to fellows or longitudinal residents must have a department-specific SOP detailing which tasks are delegated and how supervision is documented.

    <Note>
      **Grant applications vs. study documents:** fellows and residents may be listed as Sub-Investigators on grant proposals. However, they should generally not be named as Sub-Investigators on the Task Delegation Log or other essential study documentation. On the TDL they may be listed as student, fellow, resident, or research assistant.
    </Note>

    ## Employment status and stipend

    Research trainees are **not employees** of the RI-MUHC. You receive a stipend or bursary from your PI's research grant, administered as a scholarship — not processed through RI-MUHC payroll. This stipend is treated accordingly for tax purposes (T4A / Relevé 1). The exception is postdoctoral fellows, for whom federal income tax is deducted directly from the stipend.

    Your employment status does not change your compliance obligations. The same SOP training, TDL, and confidentiality requirements apply to trainees as to employees.

    ## During your placement

    - Work under the supervision of your PI. If you are uncertain about whether a task is within your scope, ask before proceeding.
    - If your tasks change during the placement, the TDL must be updated and countersigned by your PI before you perform the new tasks.
    - If you witness a potential safety issue, protocol deviation, or confidentiality concern, report it to your PI or the <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip> immediately.
    - Keep your training current — if your certificate expires, you must stop performing delegated tasks until renewal is complete.
    - When your placement ends, your PI records your end date on the Task Delegation Log.

  <Tab title="Research Volunteers">
    ## What you can and cannot do

    Volunteers contribute valuable support to research teams, but there are strict institutional rules about permitted tasks. These rules exist to protect participants and to protect you.

    **Permitted tasks:**

    - Administrative tasks — filing, photocopying, organizing study binders, scheduling, data entry from de-identified sources
    - Non-participant-facing study support — preparing materials, entering coded data, supporting logistics
    - Tasks delegated on the Task Delegation Log — within the limits above, and only after your PI has countersigned your entry

    **Not permitted regardless of delegation:**

    - Contact, recruit, or perform procedures on research participants
    - Access identifiable patient health records (OACIS, medical charts, any system containing patient names, MRNs, or dates of birth)
    - Be listed on research protocols
    - Perform any act reserved to a licensed professional (blood draws, medication administration, medical assessments)

    <Note>
      **Professional order members:** if you are a member of a Quebec professional order (e.g. OIIQ, CMQ) and can provide your membership number, you may be eligible for an expanded task scope including access to identifiable patient health records, approaching and interacting with research participants, and being listed on research protocols. <Tooltip tip="Required under Quebec Law 25 before communicating personal information without consent for research. Non-compliance: up to $25M or 4% of worldwide turnover." headline="ÉFVP — Privacy Impact Assessment" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#efvp">ÉFVP</Tooltip> authorization through <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> is a separate requirement that applies regardless of professional order membership. Confirm with your supervising PI and the Research Facilitator before taking on expanded tasks.
    </Note>

    ## Eligibility

    To volunteer at the RI-MUHC, you must:

    - Be eligible to work in Canada (Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or valid work or study permit)
    - Hold at minimum a high school leaving certificate

    If you are a student enrolled in a Canadian university program and the work relates to your thesis or academic research, you may qualify as a **research trainee** instead — trainees have a broader task scope. See the Research Trainees tab on this page.

    ## Getting started

    Volunteer onboarding is handled through RI-MUHC HR. Complete all steps before beginning any work.

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Supervisor's Authorization form">
        Signed by the PI who will supervise your placement. This form confirms the PI takes responsibility for your activities and training.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Sign the Confidentiality and Security Agreement">
        Sign before accessing any RI-MUHC systems or being present in areas where patient information may be visible. This is the MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement ([SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019), Appendix 1).
      </Step>
      <Step title="Provide proof of eligibility to work in Canada">
        Documentation of Canadian citizenship, permanent residency, or valid work/study permit.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Be added to the Task Delegation Log">
        Your PI adds you to the study's TDL with your specific delegated tasks (within the permitted scope) and countersigns to confirm your qualification. You sign to accept. No study tasks before this is complete.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    ## Training requirements

    If you are added to the Task Delegation Log, you must complete institutional SOP training at the level required for the study type — the same requirement as any other team member. There is no exemption for volunteers. Training must be documented on a valid TalentLMS certificate before you begin any delegated tasks.

    ## Confidentiality

    Your confidentiality obligations are the same as those of any team member — a breach has the same consequences regardless of your employment status.

    - Never discuss participant information outside the research team
    - Do not photograph, screenshot, or copy any documents containing patient or participant information
    - If you handle coded study data, the coding key is never accessible to you — you work with study numbers only
    - These obligations continue after your volunteer placement ends

    ## During your volunteer placement

    - Work under the direct supervision of the PI or their delegate (typically the CRC). Ask before performing any task you are uncertain about.
    - If your tasks change during the placement, the TDL must be updated and countersigned by the PI before you perform the new tasks.
    - If you witness a potential safety issue, a deviation from the protocol, or a confidentiality concern, report it to your supervising PI or the CRC immediately.
    - When your placement ends, your PI records your end date on the Task Delegation Log.
  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Site Observers">
    ## What observers can and cannot do

    An observer visits a research site to watch and learn. Observers are **not** members of the research team, are not listed on the Task Delegation Log, and cannot perform any study tasks.

    **Permitted:**

    - Observe research activities, team workflows, and site operations
    - Ask questions of the research team (outside of participant interactions)
    - Attend team meetings and training sessions

    **Not permitted:**

    - Perform any protocol-related task — no data entry, no filing, no specimen handling, no document preparation
    - Interact with research participants in any capacity
    - Access any study documents, patient records, or institutional systems
    - Be listed on the Task Delegation Log

    <Note>
      If you want to contribute to study activities rather than observe, you need to be onboarded as a volunteer or research trainee instead — both have different eligibility requirements and a different process.
    </Note>

    ## International observers

    <Warning>
      **An official RI-MUHC invitation letter is required before entering Canada.** This letter must be obtained from your designated HR advisor at the RI-MUHC before travel. It cannot be requested retroactively — if you arrive without it, you will not be permitted to observe. Contact the hosting PI's team as early as possible; the process takes time.
    </Warning>

    The hosting PI is responsible for initiating the invitation letter request through RI-MUHC HR. The letter confirms the purpose and duration of the visit, the hosting department, and the PI's supervision. International observers should also confirm visa or entry requirements with Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) before making travel arrangements.

    ## Before your visit

    Whether you are a Canadian or international observer, the following must be completed before you begin observing:

    <Steps>
      <Step title="Sign the Confidentiality and Security Agreement">
        Sign before entering any area where patient or participant information may be visible. The hosting PI or CRC will provide the form.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Obtain the RI-MUHC invitation letter (international observers only)">
        Must be in hand before entry to Canada. The hosting PI initiates the request through RI-MUHC HR.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Complete site orientation">
        The hosting PI or CRC should brief you on site-specific rules, where you may and may not go, and what to do if you observe something that concerns you.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    ## Confidentiality

    Even as an observer, you will be in environments where confidential information is visible — participant names on schedules, medical information on screens, conversations between team members. You are bound by the Confidentiality and Security Agreement you signed.

    - Do not discuss anything you see or hear about patients, participants, or study data with anyone outside the hosting research team
    - Do not photograph, record, or copy any documents, screens, or materials containing patient or participant information
    - Do not share details about specific studies, participants, or institutional processes on social media, in publications, or with colleagues at other institutions

    These obligations continue after your observation period ends. A confidentiality breach by an observer has the same institutional consequences as a breach by a team member.

    ## During your visit

    Your hosting PI or their delegate (typically the CRC) is responsible for your supervision while on site. If you observe something that concerns you — a potential safety issue, a deviation from protocol, or a confidentiality concern — mention it to the hosting PI or CRC privately, not in front of participants.

    If a participant asks who you are, the hosting PI or CRC should introduce you as an observer and confirm with the participant that your presence is acceptable. If a participant objects to your presence, you must leave the interaction immediately.
