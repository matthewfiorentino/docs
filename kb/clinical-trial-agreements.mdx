A Clinical Trial Agreement (CTA) is the legal contract between the sponsor and the institution governing the conduct of a clinical trial. At the RI-MUHC, the Research Agreements Office negotiates and executes CTAs on behalf of investigators. The financial aspects of trial agreements — budget negotiation, overhead, payment terms — are covered in [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/budgets-contracts) and [Negotiating an Industry-Sponsored Budget](/kb/budget-negotiation). This page covers the legal provisions that investigators should understand.

## Why investigators should read the CTA

The QI/PI is personally responsible for the conduct of the trial at the site. While the Research Agreements Office negotiates the legal terms and Finance handles the budget, the investigator should understand what the agreement commits the institution and the research team to — particularly regarding publication rights, data ownership, liability, and termination.

## Key clauses

### Indemnification

The indemnification clause defines who bears financial responsibility if something goes wrong — participant injury, regulatory penalties, or third-party claims. In a standard CTA:

- **The sponsor indemnifies the institution and investigator** against claims arising from the investigational product itself, the protocol design, or the sponsor's actions — provided the site followed the protocol and applicable laws.
- **The institution (and by extension the investigator) remains responsible** for claims arising from the site's own negligence, protocol deviations, or failure to follow the agreed procedures.

What to look for: ensure the indemnification is not one-sided. The sponsor should not be indemnified for problems the sponsor caused. The institution should not accept liability for the product's inherent risks.

### Publication rights

Publication clauses govern when and how the investigator can publish study results. Standard terms typically include:

- **Sponsor review period** — the sponsor is entitled to review manuscripts before submission, usually 30–90 days. This is normal and protects legitimate interests (confidential information, patent filings).
- **Delay for patent protection** — the sponsor may request a short delay (typically 30–60 additional days) to file patent applications.
- **No suppression** — the investigator should retain the right to publish regardless of whether results are favourable. A clause that gives the sponsor the right to prevent publication indefinitely is a red flag.
- **Multi-centre publication requirements** — in multi-site trials, the primary publication is often coordinated centrally by the sponsor. Site-specific publications may require sponsor approval.

<Note>
If the CTA restricts your ability to publish results, raise this with the Research Agreements Office. McGill and the RI-MUHC have institutional positions on publication rights that the Agreements Office will defend during negotiation.
</Note>

### Data ownership and access

The CTA defines who owns the data generated during the trial and who can access it after the study closes.

- **Sponsor-owned data** — in most industry-sponsored studies, the sponsor owns the study data. The site retains access to its own source documents but may not have access to the full study dataset.
- **Investigator access** — confirm that the CTA grants the investigator access to the site's own data for the duration required by the protocol and regulatory retention periods.
- **Secondary use** — if you anticipate wanting to use the data for secondary analyses or publications, this must be addressed in the CTA before the study begins.

### Intellectual property

IP clauses address ownership of inventions or discoveries arising from the study. Standard terms typically assign background IP (what each party brought to the study) to its owner, and foreground IP (what was generated during the study) to the sponsor. Investigators should confirm:

- Pre-existing IP belonging to the investigator or McGill is protected
- The assignment of foreground IP is reasonable given the parties' contributions
- Any licensing rights retained by the institution are clearly defined

### Termination

The termination clause specifies the conditions under which either party can end the agreement early.

- **Sponsor termination** — sponsors typically reserve the right to terminate the study for any reason with written notice. The CTA should require the sponsor to cover costs incurred up to termination and provide for an orderly wind-down (participant transition, data transfer).
- **Site termination** — the site should have the right to withdraw if participant safety is at risk, if the sponsor fails to meet its obligations (e.g., non-payment), or for other cause.
- **Participant obligations** — the CTA should address how enrolled participants will be managed after early termination (continued access to IP, follow-up, transfer to standard care).

What to look for: ensure the sponsor cannot terminate without covering accrued costs. A CTA that allows termination without financial obligation to the site for work already performed is problematic.

### Insurance

The sponsor is required to carry clinical trial insurance covering participant injury. The CTA should specify:

- The type and amount of insurance coverage
- That the insurance covers the duration of the study plus an appropriate tail period
- That the institution and investigator are named as additional insureds (or at minimum not excluded from coverage)

## The model CTA (mCTA)

The model Clinical Trial Agreement (mCTA) is a pan-Canadian instrument developed through a collaboration between CIHR, Innovative Medicines Canada, and HealthCareCAN, and maintained by the Canadian Clinical Trials Coordinating Centre (CCTCC). <Tooltip tip="A Quebec provincial organization that works to optimize clinical research through standardized tools, training, and support for research centres." headline="CATALIS" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#catalis">CATALIS</Tooltip> promotes the mCTA for use across Quebec research institutions. The mCTA provides balanced standard language for the clauses described above. When a sponsor proposes a custom CTA, the Research Agreements Office compares it against the mCTA to identify deviations from standard institutional positions.

Using the mCTA as a starting point can accelerate negotiations because both parties begin from recognized standard language rather than negotiating every clause from scratch.

## Confidential Disclosure Agreements

Before a sponsor shares the protocol or confidential study information, a Confidential Disclosure Agreement (CDA) or Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) is typically required. CDAs are usually straightforward, but review the duration of confidentiality obligations and confirm the CDA does not restrict your ability to discuss the research with institutional colleagues who need to be involved (e.g., pharmacy, REB submission preparation).

## The Research Agreements Office

The Research Agreements Office at the RI-MUHC:

- Reviews and negotiates all CTAs, CDAs, and other research agreements
- Ensures agreements comply with institutional policies and Quebec law
- Coordinates with the investigator, Finance, and the sponsor to resolve outstanding issues
- Maintains executed agreements on file

Investigators should not sign research agreements directly. All agreements must go through the Research Agreements Office to ensure institutional review and proper execution.

### Quebec civil law considerations

Quebec operates under the Civil Code rather than Common Law. This affects contract interpretation — for instance, the concept of "good faith" in contractual relationships is codified in Quebec law and applies to CTAs. The Research Agreements Office is familiar with these distinctions and ensures agreements are enforceable under Quebec law.

## When a participant is injured

A participant injury in a clinical trial triggers multiple obligations simultaneously. Understanding the process — and the distinction between institutional coverage and sponsor insurance — prevents gaps that could leave a participant without appropriate care or the institution without appropriate protection.

### Immediate obligations

When a participant reports or experiences an injury, the first obligation is clinical: ensure the participant receives appropriate medical care. Document the event as an adverse event in the source documents and assess whether it meets <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> criteria. Notify the Sponsor within 24 hours per the SAE reporting requirements. The investigator's medical and regulatory obligations are described in [Reportable Events and Protocol Deviations](/kb/reportable-events).

### Quebec Civil Code — civil liability framework

Under Quebec's Civil Code, the institution and the investigator have a duty not to cause harm through fault (Civil Code of Quebec, Art. 1457). In the research context:

- If a participant suffers harm as a result of the investigator's **fault** — a protocol deviation, negligence in conduct, or failure to follow the approved procedure — the institution and/or investigator may be liable.
- If the harm arises from the **inherent risks of the investigational product or protocol** — risks that were disclosed in the informed consent process and are within the bounds of what the Sponsor agreed to cover — the Sponsor's indemnification and insurance obligation applies.

The <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#icf">informed consent form</Tooltip> is the legal record of what risks were disclosed. A well-documented consent process is both an ethical requirement and a legal protection for the investigator.

### Sponsor insurance — what it covers

The CTA requires the Sponsor to carry clinical trial insurance. This typically covers:

- Harm arising from the **investigational product** itself — toxicity, manufacturing defects, or unforeseen adverse reactions
- Harm arising from **protocol-mandated procedures** — procedures the participant would not have undergone outside the study
- Harm arising from the **sponsor's own fault** — failures in the protocol design or the Sponsor's obligations

Sponsor insurance does **not** cover harm arising from the site's own negligence, protocol deviations, or failure to follow the agreed procedures. The site remains responsible for the consequences of its own conduct.

### MUHC institutional coverage

The RI-MUHC carries institutional liability coverage for clinical activities at the institution, including research activities conducted under approved protocols. This coverage does not replace Sponsor insurance — both apply simultaneously and independently when a covered event occurs.

If a participant is injured and it is unclear whether the Sponsor's insurance or the institutional coverage applies, contact the Research Agreements Office immediately. Do not attempt to resolve coverage questions without institutional guidance — early communications can affect coverage outcomes.

### No-fault compensation

Most Canadian clinical trial CTAs do not include no-fault compensation clauses — compensation without requiring proof of fault by either party. ICH E6(R3) and TCPS2 require that participants not bear the financial cost of research-related injuries, but the mechanism for meeting this obligation varies by sponsor. Review the indemnification and insurance clauses carefully with the Research Agreements Office. Confirm that:

- The Sponsor's insurance covers the full duration of the study plus a tail period adequate for late-onset injuries
- There is no requirement for participants to waive their legal rights as a condition of participation (such waivers are unenforceable under Quebec law)
- The CTA does not shift the Sponsor's inherent product risk onto the site through overly broad indemnification language
