Before you can submit a study, you need a team. Who is on that team, what role they hold, and what tasks they can be delegated are not flexible choices — they are defined by RI-MUHC SOPs, Quebec law, and federal regulations. This page maps out the team structure and the rules that govern it.

## The QI/PI accountability principle

The <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">Qualified Investigator</Tooltip> or <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">Principal Investigator</Tooltip> is ultimately responsible for the conduct of the study at the site — regardless of how tasks are distributed across the team. Delegation reduces workload; it does not transfer accountability.

This principle shapes everything about how a research team is built and documented. Every task performed by every team member must be delegated in writing on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> before it is performed. The QI/PI's signature on each delegation entry is their documented assurance that the person is trained and qualified for those specific tasks. See Team Management During a Study for the full TDL requirements once the study is running.

<Note>
A research team is not assembled informally. Every person who performs a protocol task — including trainees and volunteers — must be on the TDL with tasks explicitly listed, training documented, and the QI/PI signature in place before they begin.
</Note>

## Core team roles

Most research teams at the RI-MUHC follow a standard structure. The roles below map to the task categories in the Task Delegation Log ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002), Appendix 1).

| Role | Who fills it | Key task scope |
|------|-------------|---------------|
| Qualified Investigator (QI) / Principal Investigator (PI) | MD or dentist with RI-MUHC Human Research Privileges, or non-MD with Researcher Status (for studies not requiring medical oversight). Only one QI per study per site. | Overall study accountability. Prescribes investigational medication (MDs only). Signs the QIU for Health Canada-regulated studies. Cannot fully delegate medical oversight. |
| Sub-Investigator | Team member delegated specific QI/PI tasks. The backup Sub-I must hold the same Privileges or Researcher Status as the QI/PI. | Performs delegated protocol tasks including medical assessments (if MD). Acts as QI/PI backup during leave — backup Sub-I only. Fellows and longitudinal residents may be Sub-Is with restrictions (see below). |
| <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">Clinical Research Coordinator</Tooltip> (CRC) / Research Nurse | RI-MUHC or MUHC employee. Must complete institutional training at the appropriate level for the study type. | Day-to-day study coordination: participant scheduling, consent process support, data entry, source documentation, regulatory files. Cannot perform reserved medical acts unless a licensed professional. |
| Research Assistant / Coordinator | RI-MUHC or MUHC employee, or research trainee (see below). Institutional training required. | Administrative and data-related tasks as delegated. Scope depends on qualifications. Cannot approach, recruit, or perform procedures on participants unless eligible under the rules below. |
| Department or rotating team (Pharmacy, Lab, Nursing, Imaging) | The department Manager or Supervisor is named on the TDL, not individual staff. | Manager signs [SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002) Appendix 2 confirming training and staff qualification. Department maintains its own staff CVs and licences. MUHC Pharmacy maintains a separate Pharmacy Task Delegation Log. |

## Who needs Privileges or Researcher Status

Not everyone on the team needs RI-MUHC credentials — only those who lead or co-lead the study. Specifically, the QI/PI, the Sponsor-Investigator, and any Sub-Investigator designated as QI/PI backup must hold either Human Research Privileges (for CPDP members) or Human Researcher Status (for non-CPDP researchers) before the study can be submitted.

All other team members — CRCs, research assistants, trainees — do not need Privileges or Researcher Status. They need institutional training at the appropriate level for the study type, documented on a valid TalentLMS certificate. For the full credentials framework and the pharmacist exception, see Institutional Structure — Investigator credentials.

## Non-employee team members

Research teams at the RI-MUHC often include people who are not employees: graduate students, postdoctoral fellows, undergraduate trainees, volunteers, and international observers. Their presence on a team is welcome and common — but their status determines what tasks they can be delegated, and the rules are strict around participant contact.

<Warning>
Only MUHC and RI-MUHC employees, research trainees, and residents are authorized to be listed on research protocols, approach a participant, or access identifiable patient health records (OACIS and similar MUHC clinical systems). Volunteers may not perform these tasks unless they are a member of a professional order (e.g. OIIQ) and can provide their membership number. Professional order membership establishes eligibility for this access — EFVP authorization through <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> is a separate requirement that applies regardless of who is accessing records.
</Warning>

## Volunteers

A volunteer is an individual contributing their skills in a research or office setting without employment status. Volunteers are not paid through RI-MUHC payroll and have no employment contract.

### Eligibility

To volunteer at the RI-MUHC, the individual must be eligible to work in Canada (Canadian citizen, permanent resident, or valid work/study permit holder) and hold at minimum a high school leaving certificate.

### Task scope

Volunteers **can:**
- Perform administrative and data tasks as delegated on the TDL
- Support non-participant-facing study activities
- Access identifiable patient health records and approach participants *only if* they are a member of a professional order and provide their membership number

Volunteers **cannot:**
- Be listed on research protocols (unless a professional order member)
- Contact, recruit, or perform procedures on research participants
- Access identifiable patient health records (unless a professional order member)

Volunteer onboarding is handled through RI-MUHC HR. A Supervisor's Authorization form signed by the PI, a Confidentiality and Security Agreement, and proof of eligibility to work in Canada are required. See the RI-MUHC Portal for the full onboarding checklist.

## Research trainees

A research trainee is a student working under the supervision of a PI at The Institute, enrolled in a Canadian university program. Trainees may be at the undergraduate, MSc, PhD, clinical (MD) fellow, or postdoctoral fellow level. Postdoctoral fellows must be registered with McGill University.

Trainees are **not employees**. They receive a stipend or bursary from the PI's research grant, administered as a scholarship — not processed through RI-MUHC payroll. This stipend supports their academic progression and thesis work, and is treated accordingly for tax purposes (T4A / Relevé 1). The exception is postdoctoral fellows, for whom federal income tax is deducted directly.

### Research trainees vs. volunteers

The key distinction for study participation: a research trainee enrolled full-time in a Canadian university program is treated differently from a volunteer. Trainees **can** be listed on research protocols and can be delegated a broader range of tasks, provided they have the appropriate institutional training. A student who is not enrolled in a Canadian university program, or who is doing work unrelated to their own thesis or research, must be onboarded as a volunteer instead — with the more restricted task scope that applies to volunteers.

### Fellows and residents on a research team

Fellows and residents occupy a specific and restricted position. The rules are covered in detail on Team Management During a Study, but the essential points for team setup are:

- Residents on rotation **cannot** be named as Sub-Investigators
- Fellows and longitudinal residents **can** be Sub-Investigators, but cannot serve as QI/PI backup and cannot perform unsupervised QI/PI tasks
- Neither fellows nor residents are eligible for Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status — they cannot lead a study independently
- Medical assessments by fellows or longitudinal residents require co-signature by the QI/PI or a qualifying Sub-Investigator with RI-MUHC Privileges

### Training requirements for trainees

Trainees performing delegated protocol tasks must complete the SOP Reader and Assessment at the appropriate level for the study type, exactly as any other team member. There is no exemption for students. Training must be documented on a valid TalentLMS certificate before the trainee is added to the TDL.

## Observers

An observer visits the research site to observe activities without performing any study tasks. Observers are not listed on the TDL and cannot perform any protocol-related tasks.

For observers coming from a foreign country, an **official invitation letter from the RI-MUHC** is required. This letter must be obtained from your designated HR advisor *before* the observer enters Canada — the letter cannot be requested retroactively. Contact your HR advisor as early as possible; the process takes time.

<Warning>
The invitation letter must be in hand before the observer's entry into Canada. An observer who arrives without it will not be permitted to observe at the RI-MUHC. Plan accordingly and allow sufficient lead time.
</Warning>

As with volunteers, observers must sign a Confidentiality and Security Agreement before beginning. See [Observing at an RI-MUHC Research Site](/roles/observer) for observer requirements and restrictions.
