A study team is put together before the study is submitted, and the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, stays responsible for everything the team does. Who may join the team, and which tasks each person may be given, depends on their role, their credentials and their training. The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) set most of these rules, and some come from Health Canada and Quebec law.

## Delegate every task on the Task Delegation Log

The principal investigator keeps ultimate responsibility for the conduct of the study at the site, however the tasks are shared out. [The principal investigator keeps oversight of every person or party given a task](/sops/cr-002#5-3-5), and remains responsible for supervising them.

Every task is delegated in writing on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>. [The log is completed for each team member before they conduct any study task](/sops/cr-002#5-6-1), and it should be kept up to date throughout the study. Each person's training for a task must be documented before that person and the principal investigator sign the log.

The principal investigator's signature on the log is their assurance that the person given the task is qualified and trained to do it. Each team member signs and dates the log to accept the tasks delegated to them.

<Warning>
Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.
</Warning>

Changes to the team once the study is running, such as new members, people who leave, leaves of absence and a change of principal investigator, are covered in [Team Management During a Study](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management).

## Fill the core roles

Most study teams have the roles below. The tasks each role may be given are the task categories of [the Task Delegation Log](/sops/cr-002#appendix-1).

| Role | Who can fill it | What the role does |
|---|---|---|
| Principal investigator | A physician or dentist with Human Research Privileges, or, for a study that does not require medical oversight, a researcher with Researcher Status. There is no more than one principal investigator at each study site. | Leads the study at the site and is responsible for its overall conduct. Medical care and medical decisions in the study are under the principal investigator's supervision. In a drug or natural health product trial, the principal investigator is the qualified investigator and signs the [Qualified Investigator Undertaking](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management). |
| Sub-investigator | A team member delegated some of the principal investigator's tasks. The sub-investigator named as the principal investigator's backup must hold the same Privileges or Researcher Status, qualifications and training as the principal investigator. | Carries out the tasks delegated on the log, including medical decisions delegated to a qualified and trained sub-investigator. The backup sub-investigator temporarily replaces the principal investigator if needed. Fellows and longitudinal residents may be sub-investigators, with [restrictions](#fellows-and-residents-on-a-research-team). |
| <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">Clinical research coordinator</Tooltip> or research nurse | An employee of the RI-MUHC, of the MUHC or of a principal investigator. Holds the study's level of institutional training. | Coordinates the study day to day with the principal investigator: the Research Ethics Board (REB) submission, source document forms, information for participants, appointments and monitoring visits, study procedures the principal investigator authorizes, and case report forms. Liaises with hospital departments and the REB. Supports the consent process and keeps the study's regulatory files. Does not perform acts reserved to a licensed professional, unless licensed. |
| Research assistant | An employee of the RI-MUHC, of the MUHC or of a principal investigator, or a research trainee. Holds the study's level of institutional training. | Administrative and data tasks, as delegated. What a research assistant may do depends on their qualifications. A research assistant may contact, recruit or perform procedures on participants only if [allowed to work with participants](#add-people-who-are-not-employees). |
| A department or a team on rotation, such as the pharmacy, a laboratory, nursing or imaging | The department's manager or supervisor is added to the log, not each member of their staff. | The manager signs [the Task Delegation to a Manager or Supervisor of a Team on Rotation form](/sops/cr-002#5-7-1), keeps the staff's CVs and licences in the department's files, and passes the training on to the staff before they do the task. The MUHC Pharmacy keeps its own Pharmacy Task Delegation Log. |

In a study with an investigational product, only the principal investigator and sub-investigators who are physicians can prescribe medication.

## Who needs Privileges or Researcher Status

Only the people who lead a study, or stand in for its leader, need a credential as well as training. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-500#2-0-scope) require these people to hold Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status before the study is submitted:

- The principal investigator.
- A sub-investigator delegated the principal investigator's backup tasks.
- A sponsor-investigator.

Other team members, such as clinical research coordinators, research assistants and trainees, do not need Privileges or Researcher Status. These team members need the institutional training for the study's level. [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status) says which credential each investigator needs, including the rule for pharmacists, and how to apply.

## Add people who are not employees

Study teams often include people who are not employees: graduate students, postdoctoral fellows, undergraduate students, volunteers and visitors who come to observe. What each of them may do depends on their status.

<Warning>
Volunteers and other people who are not affiliated with the RI-MUHC or the MUHC may not contact or recruit research participants, or perform procedures on them, at the RI-MUHC or the MUHC, because they are not covered by professional or academic liability insurance.
</Warning>

[Who cannot work with participants](/training/compliance-requirements#who-cannot-work-with-participants) lists everyone the rule covers, including students without valid academic insurance.

Volunteers are not paid through RI-MUHC payroll and have no employment contract. | A visitor who watches research activities without doing any study task. |
| On the Task Delegation Log | Yes, once trained. | Yes, for tasks that do not involve participants, once trained. | No. |
| May contact, recruit or perform procedures on participants | Yes, if they have valid academic insurance, with the training and the delegation for the task. | No. | No. |
| May be listed on research protocols, or access identifiable health records | Yes. | No. | No. |
| Where the details are | [Trainee](/roles/trainee) | [Volunteer](/roles/volunteer) | [Observer](/roles/observer) |

## Fellows and residents on a research team

Fellows and residents may work on a study, but they may not conduct research independently. None of them can be a sponsor-investigator or a principal investigator, or a sub-investigator delegated the principal investigator's responsibilities, and none can apply for Privileges or Researcher Status. [A medical assessment by a fellow or a resident must be co-signed](/sops/cr-002#5-3-3) by the principal investigator or a qualified sub-investigator with Privileges or Researcher Status.

Residents may be listed on research protocols, approach participants and access identifiable patient health records, such as those in OACIS.

Residents on rotation cannot be named sub-investigators.

Fellows and longitudinal residents can be named sub-investigators, but they cannot replace the principal investigator. The Task Delegation Log must show exactly which duties they may carry out.

[On the Task Delegation Log](/sops/cr-002#5-6-2), fellows and residents should generally not be named sub-investigators. The log can name them, and medical students, as a student, a fellow, a resident or a research assistant. For a grant application, fellows and residents can be listed as sub-investigators on the protocol or the grant proposal.

Some departments make an exception and give fellows and longitudinal residents the title "Sub-I (fellow)" or "Sub-I (longitudinal resident)". In that case:

- These fellows and residents must not be delegated the principal investigator's tasks without supervision, and they cannot be the principal investigator's backup.
- The principal investigator must ensure that any of the principal investigator's tasks these fellows and residents carry out is under the principal investigator's direct, documented supervision, with every medical assessment co-signed by the principal investigator or a sub-investigator with Privileges. The log should clearly show this supervision.
- The department should write its own SOP that says which tasks it delegates to fellows or longitudinal residents and how it supervises them.
