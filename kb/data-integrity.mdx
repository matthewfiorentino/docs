A clinical trial record may be inspected up to 15 years after a study closes — long after the people who ran it have moved on. The record has to stand alone and tell the story of what happened without anyone present to explain it. That is why documentation requirements exist.

## Why the requirements exist

Every documentation requirement in <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> — initialling corrections, prohibiting Wite-Out, requiring contemporaneous entries — exists for one reason: the study record must be able to stand alone. A Health Canada inspector reviewing a trial five years after closure, or a court examining an adverse outcome fifteen years later, will rely entirely on what was written down. No one will be present to explain what was meant or fill in gaps.

This is the 15-year inspection frame. It is not an abstraction. Under Health Canada regulations, clinical trial records must be retained for 15 years after study completion for HC-regulated studies. Sponsor records must be kept for 25 years. The record does not belong to the research team — it belongs to the public interest in safe, well-documented research.

<Note>
**This is different from data security.** Data integrity is about the trustworthiness and auditability of the research record — whether it was documented correctly in the first place. Data security is about protecting the data you hold from unauthorized access or breach. Both matter, but they address different risks. See [Research Data Security](/kb/data-security) for the storage and sharing framework.
</Note>

## ALCOA+ in practice

ALCOA+ is the foundational framework for good documentation practice in clinical research, defined in ICH GCP E6(R3). The original five principles form the acronym ALCOA; four additional principles were added to address electronic records and modern data management. At the RI-MUHC, ALCOA+ applies across all study records — hospital records in OACIS, data entered in REDCap, paper source documents, and submission records in <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>.

**ALCOA — the original five principles**

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="A — Attributable" icon="user-pen">
    It must be clear who collected or entered the data, and when. Every entry signed and dated by the person who made it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="L — Legible" icon="eye">
    The record must be readable now and 15 years from now. Permanent ink. No overwriting that obscures the original.
  </Card>
  <Card title="C — Contemporaneous" icon="clock">
    Recorded at the time the activity occurred — not reconstructed from memory later. Backdating is a serious GCP violation.
  </Card>
  <Card title="O — Original" icon="file">
    The first place data was recorded is the source. Transcriptions must be traceable back to the original source document.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A — Accurate" icon="check">
    Reflects what actually happened. Errors corrected with a single line through the original, initialled and dated, with the reason noted.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**+ Four additional principles (ICH GCP E6(R3))**

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="C — Complete" icon="list-check">
    All data required by the protocol is present. No blank fields without explanation. Missing data is documented as such.
  </Card>
  <Card title="C — Consistent" icon="arrows-rotate">
    Data is internally consistent across documents. Dates, values, and sequences match across source documents and <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> entries.
  </Card>
  <Card title="E — Enduring" icon="database">
    Recorded on durable media that will remain readable for the required retention period. Pencil, sticky notes, and erasable ink are not permitted.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A — Available" icon="magnifying-glass">
    Can be retrieved for audit or inspection when required. Records must be stored where they can be located and accessed on request.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Documentation do's and don'ts

The following table covers the most common documentation errors seen during Health Canada inspections and <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> audits at the RI-MUHC.

| Do | Don't |
|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| ✓ Correct errors with a single line, leaving the original legible; initial, date, and note the reason | ✗ Use Wite-Out, correction fluid, or tape over any entry |
| ✓ Sign and date source data at the time of collection | ✗ Backdate entries or record events from memory without noting the retrospective nature |
| ✓ Complete all signature and date fields at the time of the activity | ✗ Leave signature or date fields blank to be completed later |
| ✓ Use approved, current versions of all documents at the point of use | ✗ Use superseded versions of SOPs, forms, or templates |
| ✓ Annotate and explain transcription error corrections | ✗ Make undocumented corrections of any kind — including minor typos |
| ✓ Use a Note-to-File to explain missing, late, or out-of-sequence entries | ✗ Reconstruct entries without indicating they were recorded retrospectively |
| ✓ Record in permanent ink; store documents to prevent deterioration | ✗ Use pencil, sticky notes, or erasable ink on any source document |
| ✓ Keep the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">delegation log</Tooltip> current and reflecting actual task assignments | ✗ Allow team members to perform tasks not listed on their delegation log entry |

<Warning>
**The 12 most common Health Canada inspection findings** include incorrect or missing version numbers on study documents, incomplete delegation logs, and undocumented corrections. Version numbering in particular is flagged as a common finding: incorrect versioning of the protocol, <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip>, or study documents is a pattern Health Canada inspectors specifically look for. See Monitoring, Audits and Inspections for the full findings list and how to prepare.
</Warning>

## Source documents at the RI-MUHC

A source document is the original record where data was first captured. At the RI-MUHC, source documents include hospital records in OACIS, ICFs, inclusion/exclusion criteria checklists, randomization records, laboratory notes and reports, pharmacy dispensing and storage records, concomitant medication records, participant diaries, communication logs, and data from automated instruments.

**OACIS is the primary source for most clinical data.** Anything documented in OACIS is, by definition, a source document. Data transcribed from OACIS into a CRF or REDCap database must be traceable back to the OACIS record. Monitors will verify this traceability during Source Data Verification (SDV).

For most low-to-moderate complexity studies at the RI-MUHC, REDCap is the eCRF platform. REDCap is validated and compliant with 21 CFR Part 11. For studies requiring more than approximately 1,000 variables, discuss platform requirements with the DW team or sponsor. Any electronic data capture system used in an HC-regulated study must be validated before use — contact IT via the RI-MUHC Portal before the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#siv">Site Initiation Visit</Tooltip>.

<Note>
**Source documents must stay in their original state.** A source document cannot be corrected after the fact by overwriting. If a correction is necessary, the original entry must remain legible. The correction is made alongside — never on top of — the original, with initials, date, and reason.
</Note>

## Certified copies

A certified copy is a legal substitute for an original source document. Monitors, auditors, and Health Canada inspectors may request certified copies for review. A certified copy is only valid if it meets three conditions: it is a complete, accurate reproduction of the original; it was made directly from the original by the person certifying it; and it is marked with the certifier's initials, date, and the statement **"True and Exact Copy."**

The person who makes the copy must be the person who certifies it. This cannot be delegated — certification is a personal attestation that you made this copy from the original and verify its accuracy. QA assists with certified copy control procedures during inspections.

## Document retention

Records must be retained for the full applicable period before any destruction. Destruction requires sponsor authorization for sponsor-held records, and must follow institutional procedures. Early destruction of study records is a serious regulatory violation.

| Study type | Retention period | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------|
| HC-regulated studies (drug, device, NHP) — any study subject to Health Canada regulations under Part C Division 5, <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip>, or NHP <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">CTA</Tooltip> | 15 years after study completion | Sponsor-held records: 25 years. Destruction requires sponsor authorization. |
| Non-regulated studies — prospective observational, retrospective, secondary data analysis | 7 years after study completion | Institutional policy minimum. Individual funding agencies may require longer. |
| Essential documents (ISF/<Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip>) | Per above, by study type | The <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">Investigator Site File</Tooltip> must be complete and current throughout the study. See [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015) for ISF/TMF content requirements. |

## eSource

When the first record of a data point is captured electronically — in OACIS, REDCap, a validated eCRF, or another system — that electronic record is the source document. The same ALCOA+ principles apply: the entry must be attributable, contemporaneous, and unmodifiable without an audit trail.

At the RI-MUHC, the most common eSource scenarios are:

- **OACIS** — hospital records entered by clinicians are source by default.
- **REDCap** — when used as the primary data collection tool (not just for transcription from paper), REDCap entries are source. The system must be validated for electronic records before use — at Canadian sites, this means compliance with Health Canada GUI-0100 and GUI-0050 (Annex 11); for trials also filed under a US IND, 21 CFR Part 11 applies concurrently.
- **Sponsor eCRF platforms** — for studies where the sponsor's EDC system is the designated source, this must be documented in the Data Management Plan before data collection begins.

The distinction that matters is between eSource (data entered directly into the system) and transcription (data copied from a paper or other source). For transcription, the paper record remains the source and the electronic entry must be verifiable against it. For eSource, the electronic record stands alone — which means the system's audit trail, access controls, and backup procedures must be documented and inspection-ready.

<Note>
If a study designates an electronic system as source, confirm with IT that the system meets validation requirements before the SIV. Late discovery that a system lacks a compliant audit trail can compromise data collected up to that point. ICH E6(R3) uses the term **"source records"** to encompass both paper and electronic source data — the ALCOA+ principles apply regardless of media.
</Note>

## Preparing for Source Data Verification

Source Data Verification (SDV) is the monitor's comparison of CRF entries against source documents. How well-organized your records are directly affects how long monitoring visits take and how many queries result.

Steps that reduce SDV friction:

- **Pre-organize by participant and visit.** Before a monitoring visit, confirm that source documents for each participant are complete and accessible — not scattered across multiple binders or systems. For OACIS-sourced data, have the relevant records identified and printable.
- **Flag transcription points.** Where data moves from source to CRF, make the path traceable. If a lab value in the CRF came from an OACIS report, the CRF annotation or worksheet should reference the OACIS record date and source.
- **Resolve known discrepancies before the visit.** If you've identified errors or missing entries, correct them before the monitor arrives rather than generating queries. Use proper correction procedures — single line through, initials, date, reason.
- **Keep the Delegation Log current.** Monitors verify that the person who collected and signed data was delegated to do so at the time. Backdated delegation log entries are a common finding.
- **Have consent documentation accessible.** Monitors verify that the correct ICF version was signed before any study procedures. Organize consent forms so version numbers and dates are quickly verifiable.

Common SDV findings to avoid:

| Finding | What happened | How to prevent it |
|---|---|---|
| CRF entry does not match source | Transcription error or source updated after CRF entry | Verify CRF entries against source at the time of entry, not in batches |
| Source document missing or incomplete | Worksheet not completed during the visit, or filed in the wrong location | Complete source documents contemporaneously; file immediately |
| Consent version mismatch | Participant signed a superseded ICF version | Track ICF version dates and cross-check before each visit |
| Undocumented correction | Error corrected without single-line, initials, date, and reason | Train all team members on GDP correction procedures per [SOP-CR-014](/sops/cr-014) |
