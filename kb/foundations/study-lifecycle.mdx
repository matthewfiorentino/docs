Most clinical studies at the RI-MUHC go through the same stages, from a Phase III drug trial to a prospective observational study. The rules, the timelines and the people involved differ from one kind of study to another, but the order of the stages stays the same. Knowing that order makes each standard operating procedure (SOP) and each detailed page easier to place.

Retrospective studies, such as chart reviews and analyses of databases, have fewer stages: feasibility, submission and ethics review, conduct, closing the study in Nagano, and archiving, without site activation, sponsor monitoring or the close-out steps specific to clinical trials. [Retrospective and Secondary-Data Research](/paths/retrospective) gives their steps in order.

## Follow the eight stages of a study

### 1. Check that the study can be done here

The study idea exists. The question at this stage is whether the site, the team, its resources and its patient population can carry it out. Feasibility is the earliest point at which serious problems can be caught, before money and time are committed.

The <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip>, who leads the study at the MUHC, checks whether enough patients exist, whether the team has the expertise and whether the site can support what the protocol asks. [Feasibility](/kb/planning/feasibility) explains the steps.

Consider also whether the [Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)](/cim/planning) should take part, for studies that need specialized infrastructure. Research at the CIM ranges from first-in-human (Phase I) trials to Phase III trials, industry-sponsored or investigator-initiated. The CIM can run an industry-sponsored study from start to finish, or provide individual services for a study initiated by its principal investigator.

### 2. Design and plan the study

The protocol is the technical core of the study. It sets the research question, the design, the eligibility criteria, the procedures, the data management plan and the statistical analysis. The investigator writes it for an investigator-initiated study, and the sponsor provides it for an industry-sponsored trial. A biostatistician should be involved before data collection begins, not after.

Alongside the protocol, [the team is assembled](/kb/team/building-your-team), its roles are defined, [the budget is prepared](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) and [funding is secured](/kb/funding/funding-applications). For industry-sponsored studies, [contract negotiations](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements) start at the same time. The consent form is drafted at this stage, not as a last task before submission.

### 3. Submit the study and have it reviewed

Before any participant is approached, a study at the MUHC needs three favourable reviews: a scientific review, an ethics review by the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> and an institutional feasibility review. The three reviews run in parallel. The <Tooltip tip="The person whom an establishment of Quebec's health network formally mandates to authorize the research conducted under its auspices." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> issues <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, which lets the study begin, only once all three are favourable.

A study at more than one public institution of Quebec's health and social services network is [reviewed by one REB for every site, and each institution still reviews the study's feasibility and authorizes it at its own site](/kb/design/multi-centric).

Every submission goes through <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>, a platform deployed across more than 25 institutions of Quebec's health and social services network. [Study Submission and Review](/kb/submission/submission-overview) explains each review.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

### 4. Activate the site

Authorization is received, but the study is not yet ready to run. Site activation covers the work between authorization and the first participant: the sponsor's monitor holds the <Tooltip tip="A monitoring visit conducted by a monitor as a final evaluation of the site, and to train the site team on the study protocol and its associated documents and processes." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">site initiation visit</Tooltip>, the <Tooltip tip="The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> is completed, the <Tooltip tip="The qualified or principal investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines, so that the conduct and integrity of the study and the safety of participants at the site can be verified." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file</Tooltip> is kept current, and the logistics are confirmed, such as the data capture systems, the pharmacy and access to electronic data capture (EDC). [Site Activation and Study Setup](/kb/site-activation/site-setup) explains the steps.

No recruitment activity may begin before all activation requirements are met. Consent may be sought only after the REB's written approval, MUHC Authorization, the site initiation visit and, if the sponsor issues one, its go letter. The Task Delegation Log must be in place, and the sponsor's monitor must verify that the site is ready, before the first participant is approached.

The principal investigator or a delegate [should create the investigator site file](/kb/site-activation/isf#set-up-the-isf) after the sponsor or sponsor-investigator first makes contact, or as soon as a protocol outline is available, and keep it current.

<Warning>
Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.
</Warning>

### 5. Recruit, screen and enrol participants

Recruitment follows set rules. All recruitment materials must be approved by the REB before any outreach begins. Materials a patient may see also need the approval of [MUHC Communications](mailto:communications@muhc.mcgill.ca), after the REB's. Potential participants are identified through approved channels, screened against the protocol's inclusion and exclusion criteria and, if eligible, invited to take part through a formal consent process. [Recruitment and Enrolment](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment) explains the steps.

<Warning>
All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes before all the required signatures are on the form is a deviation.
</Warning>

[Consent continues through the study](/kb/foundations/clinical-research-101#keep-consent-going-through-the-study). If important new information that may be relevant to their consent becomes available, [it must be communicated to participants in a timely manner](/kb/recruitment/consent#keep-consent-current), and [the REB decides whether they must be re-consented](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#re-consent-participants-on-an-amended-consent-form). The REB also requires researchers to inform former participants of any new information that may be relevant to their long-term health.

Once consent is obtained and eligibility confirmed, the participant is enrolled and assigned a participant identification number.

### 6. Run the study according to the protocol

Visits take place, data are collected and entered, and adverse events are assessed and reported. Protocol deviations are documented, and the Task Delegation Log is kept current as the team changes. The sponsor's monitor checks the site regularly, on site or remotely, for the accuracy of the data and compliance with the protocol.

The REB must be kept informed of amendments, safety signals and reportable events.

Amendments to the protocol or the consent form must receive the REB's approval before they are implemented, unless they are needed to eliminate an immediate hazard. An amendment is not in effect until [all the required approvals are in hand](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#wait-for-every-approval-before-you-implement-the-amendment). Acting on an unapproved amendment is a protocol deviation.

While the study runs, each of these pages holds the rules for one part of the work:

- [Data Integrity](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity): record, correct and keep study data so that the record can be trusted, following the <Tooltip tip="The principles of good documentation practice, under which a record is attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate, and also complete, consistent, enduring and available." headline="ALCOA+" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#alcoa">ALCOA+</Tooltip> principles.
- [Storing and Sharing Human Data](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security): learn how the study's data are classified, then store, send and archive each kind securely.
- [EDC Systems and Query Management](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/edc-query-management): enter data in the sponsor's electronic data capture (EDC) system, answer its queries and reconcile serious adverse events.
- [The Investigator Site File](/kb/site-activation/isf): keep the study's essential documents current until they are archived.
- [Investigational Products in Quebec](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products): manage the investigational product with the MUHC Research Pharmacy, from its receipt to its return or destruction.
- [Management of Biological Specimens](/kb/conduct/study-operations/biological-specimens): collect, process, store and ship participants' biological specimens.
- [Conducting a Decentralized Trial](/kb/conduct/study-operations/decentralized-trials): run the parts of a trial that happen away from the site, such as remote monitoring, electronic consent and products sent to participants.
- [Team Management During a Study](/kb/conduct/study-operations/team-management): record each change to the team on the Task Delegation Log, including a leave of absence and a change of principal investigator.
- [Informed Consent in Quebec](/kb/recruitment/consent): obtain participants' consent again when the study changes.
- [Reportable Events and Protocol Deviations](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events): find which safety events, concerns and protocol deviations to report to the sponsor, the REB and Health Canada, and by when.
- [Deviation Management and CAPA](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management): classify a deviation, find its root cause and work through the corrective and preventive action (CAPA) process.
- [Study Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments): get a change to the study approved before you implement it.
- [Study Submission and Review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid): keep the REB's approval valid with its continuing review.
- [Monitoring, Audits, and Inspections](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits): prepare for the sponsor's monitoring visits, including monitors' access to [OACIS](/apps/oacis), the MUHC's electronic medical record system, and for audits and Health Canada's inspections.
- [Hosting a Health Canada Inspection](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/hc-inspection-guide): prepare the team, the documents and the facility for an inspection, and respond to its findings.

### 7. Close out the study

A study doesn't end with the last participant's visit. Close-out is a structured process: all data are entered and verified, the close-out monitoring visit is held, the REB receives a final report, the sponsor is told, and the site submits a close-out notification confirming completion. Where an application was filed with Health Canada, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator should tell Health Canada that the study has ended. Financial close-out runs at the same time. [Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out/close-out) explains the steps.

Any remaining <Tooltip tip="A pharmaceutical form of an active ingredient or placebo, a medical device or a natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip> must be returned to the sponsor or destroyed according to documented procedures.

The sponsor-investigator who registered the study is responsible for keeping its registry record up to date for the duration of the study, including its completion. Sponsors of drug and natural health product trials in phases 1 to 3, and of Class II to IV medical device trials, [should keep their trial information up to date with the registry](/kb/close-out/publications). The site should inform participants about study results and publications, once they are available.

### 8. Archive the records

All study documents, including the investigator site file, consent forms, source documents, <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant who has been given an investigational product, whether or not it is related to that product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip> records, monitoring reports and correspondence, must be archived securely. [How long they are kept](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving) depends on the study.

Archiving is more than storing files. Documents must be organized, indexed and available to authorized people for an inspection or an audit, which may come years after a study closes. The archiving responsibility belongs to the site, and it does not pass to the sponsor at close-out.

## See how early decisions shape later stages

The stages follow one another, and early decisions limit what is possible later. The costliest mistakes happen when a team treats the stages as separate boxes to tick.

- **From feasibility to submission.** The answers the investigator gives at feasibility become the data the institutional review evaluates at submission. A protocol submitted without a realistic feasibility assessment often raises questions during review that delay authorization by weeks.
- **From design to close-out.** Decisions made at the design stage, such as the data management plan, the statistical analysis and the source document templates, decide how easy close-out will be years later. A data management plan that isn't ready for an audit from the start becomes an inspection problem at the end.
- **From activation to conduct.** The quality of site activation, such as how thoroughly the site initiation visit covers the protocol, how complete the investigator site file is and how well the Task Delegation Log reflects real responsibilities, affects how many deviations occur during the study.
- **From conduct to archiving.** Health Canada aims to inspect about 2 per cent of clinical trials, and an inspection can come years after a study closes. Documents that are missing, unsigned or contradicted by other records at close-out can't be rebuilt afterwards.
