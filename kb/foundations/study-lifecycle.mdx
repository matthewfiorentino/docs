Most clinical studies at the RI-MUHC go through the same stages, from a Phase III drug trial to a prospective observational study. The rules, the timelines and the people involved differ from one kind of study to another, but the order of the stages stays the same. Knowing that order makes each standard operating procedure (SOP) and each detailed page easier to place.

Retrospective studies, such as chart reviews and analyses of databases, have fewer stages: feasibility, submission and ethics review, conduct and archiving, without site activation, sponsor monitoring or the close-out steps specific to clinical trials. [Retrospective and Secondary-Data Research](/paths/retrospective) gives their steps in order.

## Follow the eight stages of a study

### 1. Check that the study can be done here

The study idea exists. The question at this stage is whether the site, the team, its resources and its patient population can carry it out. Feasibility is the earliest point at which serious problems can be caught, before money and time are committed.

The <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip>, who leads the study at the MUHC, checks whether enough patients exist, whether the team has the expertise and whether the site can support what the protocol asks. Consider also whether the [Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)](/cim/services) should take part, for studies that need specialized infrastructure. [Feasibility](/kb/planning/feasibility) explains the steps.

### 2. Design and plan the study

The protocol is the technical core of the study. It sets the research question, the design, the eligibility criteria, the procedures, the data management plan and the statistical analysis. The investigator writes it for an investigator-initiated study, and the sponsor provides it for an industry-sponsored trial. A biostatistician should be involved before data collection begins, not after.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA and the Biostatistics Consulting Unit): SOP-CR-102 has a sponsor-investigator "Consult with methodologists, statisticians, etc., as necessary" when writing a protocol; is there a rule for every study before data collection? In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P5-2). */}

Alongside the protocol, the team is assembled, its roles are defined, the budget is prepared and funding is secured. For industry-sponsored studies, contract negotiations start at the same time. The consent form is drafted at this stage, not as a last task before submission.

### 3. Submit the study and have it reviewed

Before any participant is approached, a study at the MUHC needs three favourable reviews: a scientific review, an ethics review by the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> and an institutional feasibility review. The three reviews run in parallel. The <Tooltip tip="The person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research: the Director of Innovation, Performance and Quality, who issues MUHC Authorization for each study." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> issues <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, which lets the study begin, only once all three are favourable. Every submission goes through <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>. [Study Submission and Review](/kb/submission/submission-overview) explains each review.

For a new study, [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) confirms during the institutional feasibility review that the principal investigator holds Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status, as applicable. QA also confirms the level of institutional training the protocol requires.

For a trial of a new drug or natural health product, or of a new Class II to IV medical device, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs have the sponsor obtain Health Canada's approval first](/sops/cr-007#5-1-1).

For a drug trial, Health Canada first screens the Clinical Trial Application (CTA) for completeness. If the application is complete, Health Canada's 30-day default review counts from the date it received the application. The study cannot be activated until it has Health Canada's authorization as well as the three institutional approvals.

### 4. Activate the site

Authorization is received, but the study is not yet ready to run. Site activation covers the work between authorization and the first participant: the sponsor's monitor holds the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">site initiation visit</Tooltip>, the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> is completed, the investigator site file is set up, and the logistics are confirmed, such as the data capture systems, the pharmacy and access to electronic data capture (EDC).

No recruitment activity may begin before all activation requirements are met. Consent may be sought only after the REB's written approval, MUHC Authorization, the site initiation visit and, if the sponsor issues one, its go letter. The investigator site file and the Task Delegation Log must be in place, and the sponsor's monitor must verify that the site is ready, before the first participant is approached.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA; facts-21, P5-6): "the sponsor's monitor must verify site readiness" is not the SOPs' wording; SOP-CR-007, section 5.1.4, calls an SIV after approval "Best practice". ### 5. Recruit, screen and enrol participants

Recruitment follows set rules. All recruitment materials must be approved by the REB before any outreach begins. Materials a patient may see also need the approval of [MUHC Communications](mailto:communications@muhc.mcgill.ca), after the REB's. Potential participants are identified through approved channels, screened against the protocol's inclusion and exclusion criteria and, if eligible, invited to take part through a formal consent process. [Recruitment and Enrolment](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment) explains the steps.

Consent is a process, not only a signature. Under the federal research agencies' [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter3-chapitre3.html), it must be documented, ongoing and voluntary throughout the study.

<Warning>
All required signatures must be on the current REB-approved consent form before any screening procedure takes place. A procedure done even minutes before all the required signatures are on the form is a deviation.
</Warning>

Consent does not last unchanged. If important new information that may be relevant to their consent becomes available, it must be communicated to participants in a timely manner, and the REB decides whether they must be re-consented. In a clinical trial, when new information is relevant to participants' welfare, researchers must promptly inform every participant it applies to, former participants included. Once consent is obtained and eligibility confirmed, the participant is enrolled and assigned a participant identification number.

### 6. Run the study according to the protocol

Visits take place, data are collected and entered, and adverse events are assessed and reported. Protocol deviations are documented, and the Task Delegation Log is kept current as the team changes. The sponsor's monitor checks the site regularly, on site or remotely, for the accuracy of the data and compliance with the protocol.

The REB must be kept informed of amendments, safety signals and reportable events. Continuing review is required at least once a year, or more often if the REB directs.

Amendments to the protocol or the consent form must receive the REB's approval before they are implemented, unless they are needed to eliminate an immediate hazard. A change made without the REB's prior review to eliminate an apparent immediate hazard to a participant must be reported to the REB immediately. Administrative amendments with no effect on participants may be submitted at the annual continuing review.

### 7. Close out the study

A study doesn't end with the last participant's visit. Close-out is a structured process: all data are entered and verified, the close-out monitoring visit is held, the REB receives a final report, the sponsor is told, and the site submits a close-out notification confirming completion. Where an application was filed with Health Canada, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator should tell Health Canada that the study has ended. Financial close-out runs at the same time. [Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out/close-out) explains the steps.

Any remaining <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip> must be returned to the sponsor or destroyed according to documented procedures.

The sponsor-investigator who registered the study is responsible for keeping its registry record up to date for the duration of the study, including its completion. Sponsors of drug and natural health product trials in phases 1 to 3, and of Class II to IV medical device trials, [should keep their trial information up to date with the registry](/kb/close-out/publications). The site should inform participants about study results and publications, once they are available.

### 8. Archive the records

All study documents, including the investigator site file, consent forms, source documents, <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip> records, monitoring reports and correspondence, must be archived securely. [How long they are kept](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving) depends on the study.

Archiving is more than storing files. Documents must be organized, indexed and available to authorized people for an inspection or an audit, which may come years after a study closes. The archiving responsibility belongs to the site, and it does not pass to the sponsor at close-out.

## Know what is specific to the MUHC and Quebec

The eight stages apply wherever clinical research is conducted. Some features of Quebec and the RI-MUHC differ from how research runs elsewhere in Canada.

### Three reviews and the Personne mandatée

No study at the MUHC may begin without the written authorization of the Personne mandatée. At the MUHC, the Personne mandatée is the person to whom the President and Chief Executive Officer has delegated, in writing, the authorization of research projects. The authorization can be issued only after all three reviews, scientific, ethics and institutional, are favourable. REB approval alone is not enough to begin.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

## See how early decisions shape later stages

The stages follow one another, and early decisions limit what is possible later. The costliest mistakes happen when a team treats the stages as separate boxes to tick.

- **From feasibility to submission.** The answers the investigator gives at feasibility become the data the institutional review evaluates at submission. A protocol submitted without a realistic feasibility assessment often raises questions during review that delay authorization by weeks.{/* TO CONFIRM (MF): "by weeks" is a typical duration, which MF's rule keeps off the Hub. Recommendation in the master doc: drop it. In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P5-19). */}
- **From design to close-out.** Decisions made at the design stage, such as the data management plan, the statistical analysis and the source document templates, decide how easy close-out will be years later. A data management plan that isn't ready for an audit from the start becomes an inspection problem at the end.
- **From activation to conduct.** The quality of site activation, such as how thoroughly the site initiation visit covers the protocol, how complete the investigator site file is and how well the Task Delegation Log reflects real responsibilities, affects how many deviations occur during the study.
- **From conduct to archiving.** Health Canada aims to inspect about 2 per cent of clinical trials, and an inspection can come years after a study closes. Documents that are missing, unsigned or contradicted by other records at close-out can't be rebuilt afterwards.

## Know when an amendment needs approval

An amendment re-enters review. The REB must approve it before it is implemented, except to eliminate an immediate hazard. A change made without the REB's prior review to eliminate an apparent immediate hazard to a participant must be reported to the REB immediately. Significant changes may need a new institutional review.

An amendment is not in effect until all the required approvals are in hand. Acting on an unapproved amendment is a protocol deviation.

### Amendments to a drug or natural health product trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

For a drug or natural health product trial, some amendments need [Health Canada's authorization first](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments): those that affect the selection, monitoring or dismissal of participants, the evaluation of the product's efficacy or safety, or the risk to participants, those that extend the trial, and changes to the chemistry and manufacturing information that may affect the product's safety or quality.

If the trial endangers the health of a participant or another person, the sponsor may make such an amendment immediately, and must send Health Canada its information within 15 days after the amendment.
