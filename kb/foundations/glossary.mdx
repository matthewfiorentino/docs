## A

<AccordionGroup>
  <a id="adr"></a>
  <Accordion title="Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)">
    A noxious and unintended response to a drug or natural health product, where a causal relationship between the product and the adverse event is at least a reasonable possibility.

    Before a product is approved, or for a new use of it, every noxious and unintended response to it at any dose should be considered an adverse drug reaction. For a marketed product, an adverse drug reaction is a noxious and unintended response that occurs at doses normally used for the prophylaxis, diagnosis or therapy of disease, or for the modification of physiological function.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="ae"></a>
  <Accordion title="Adverse Event (AE)">
    Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant who has been given an investigational product, whether or not it is related to that product.

    An adverse event can be any unfavourable and unintended sign, including an abnormal laboratory finding, or any symptom or disease that occurs while the investigational product is in use. The product can be a drug, a natural health product or a medical device. [How to assess and report one](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="ALCOA+">
    The principles of good documentation practice, under which a record is attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate, and also complete, consistent, enduring and available.

    The RI-MUHC's SOPs apply these principles to study documentation. The good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) says that source records should be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate and complete.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Annotation (CRF/eCRF)">
    A representation of the variables and tables in a paper case report form or in data entry screens.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Applicable Regulatory Requirements">
    Any laws and regulations that address the conduct of clinical trials of investigational products.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Approached Participant">
    A patient or other person who has been identified for a study, with whom the informed consent process has begun, who has been given a consent form and who has been invited to take part.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Approval (REB)">
    The decision of a research ethics board that a trial has been reviewed and may be conducted at the site, within the constraints set by the REB, the institution, good clinical practice and the applicable regulatory requirements.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Audit">
    A systematic and independent examination of trial-related activities and documents to determine whether those activities were conducted, and the data recorded, analyzed and accurately reported, according to the REB-approved protocol, the applicable SOPs, good clinical practice and the applicable regulatory requirements.

    The sponsor's SOPs and the site's SOPs and work instructions both apply.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Audit Certificate">
    A declaration by the auditor confirming that an audit has taken place.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Audit Report">
    A written evaluation by the sponsor's auditor of the results of an audit.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Audit Trail">
    Documentation that allows the course of events in a study to be reconstructed.

    The audit trail should show each initial entry and each change to data fields or records, by whom and when and, where applicable, why. In a computerized system, it should be secure, computer-generated and time-stamped.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## B

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Bioanalysis">
    The measurement of a drug and its major metabolites in a biological sample, such as cell culture media, plasma, serum, urine or another human biological matrix.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Biologic">
    A drug prepared from a biological starting or source material, such as one derived from a microorganism, a virus, an animal, a human or a plant.

    Examples include vaccines, blood and its derivatives, certain hormones and enzymes, recombinant DNA products, gene therapies and transgenics. Biologics are one of the two large categories of drugs. The other is pharmaceuticals, which are synthetic drugs made from chemicals.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Biological Product">
    A product derived from living organisms and used to prevent, treat or diagnose disease in humans or animals, or for development, experiment or investigation purposes.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Biological Sampling">
    The collecting, processing and analyzing of biological samples.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Blinding/Masking">
    A procedure in which one or more parties to a trial are kept unaware of the treatment assignments.

    Single-blinding usually means that the participants are unaware. Double-blinding usually means that the participants, the investigators, the monitor and, in some cases, the data analysts are unaware. [Blinding levels in trial design](/kb/foundations/trial-design).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## C

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Calibration">
    A set of operations that establish, under specified conditions, the relationship between the values indicated by a measuring instrument and the corresponding values realized by standards.

    The result of a calibration may be recorded in a calibration certificate or a calibration report.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="3ctn"></a>
  <Accordion title="Canadian Cancer Clinical Trials Network (3CTN)">
    Canada's national network supporting the development of cancer clinical trial sites across academic research centres.

    The network provides site infrastructure, training and frameworks, including the 3CTN EDI Framework and Toolkit (2024). It connects the RI-MUHC with national cancer trial infrastructure.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="cord"></a>
  <Accordion title="Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)">
    A service unit of the RI-MUHC's Centre for Outcomes Research and Evaluation (CORE) that helps research teams collect and manage their study data, from the data plan in a protocol or grant application to the end of the project.

    [CORD](mailto:cord@rimuhc.ca)'s services include reviewing data plans and data collection tools, building and hosting REDCap projects, and managing study data ([what CORD offers and how to request it](/kb/design-support/cord)).
  </Accordion>
  <a id="crf"></a>
  <Accordion title="Case Report Form (CRF)">
    A printed, optical or electronic document designed to record all the protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor on each trial participant.

    An electronic version is an electronic case report form (eCRF).
  </Accordion>
  <a id="catalis"></a>
  <Accordion title="CATALIS Québec">
    An independent not-for-profit organization, mandated and funded by the Government of Quebec, that coordinates provincial efforts to improve the processes, infrastructure and services that support clinical research in Quebec.

    CATALIS Québec powers Clinical Trials Quebec. Its FAST TRACK service is for industry-sponsored clinical trials at more than one site in Quebec. The service runs the ethics and feasibility reviews in parallel ([multi-site studies in Quebec](/kb/design/multi-centric)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Causality/Attribution Assessment">
    The investigator's judgment, in a study with an investigational product, of whether there is a reasonable possibility that an adverse event is caused by that product.

    The attribution may be related, not related or unknown. Adverse events judged to have at least a possible relationship with the investigational product are called adverse reactions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)">
    The RI-MUHC's McConnell Centre for Innovative Medicine, which provides clinical research services, facilities and technical platforms at the Glen site and the Montreal General Hospital.

    For clinical trials, including industry-sponsored trials, it provides study coordination, pharmacy and regulatory affairs support, and participant care facilities. Most industry-sponsored studies at the RI-MUHC run through the CIM. A sponsored trial can also run at the MUHC without the CIM running it ([CIM services](/cim/services)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Certified Copy">
    A paper or electronic copy of an original record, verified by a dated signature or generated through a validated process, that is an exact copy with all the same attributes and information as the original.

    The person who makes the copy should mark it True and Exact Copy, and initial and date it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Certified Translator or Interpreter">
    A person who knows a language as a native speaker or through extensive training or experience, especially in the area needed, such as medical terminology, and who holds a certification or designation granted by the provincial regulatory body for the profession.

    A qualified translator or interpreter has the same knowledge without the professional title or designation. An interpreter converts spoken material from one language to another, and a translator converts written material.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Civil Code of Québec">
    Quebec's Civil Code, which sets the conditions for research that could interfere with the integrity of a person, including who may consent for a minor or for an adult who cannot consent, and what participants may receive.

    Its articles 20 to 25 set those conditions. Article 21 covers minors and adults who cannot consent. Article 25 limits what a participant may receive in such research to an indemnity for the loss and inconvenience suffered ([consent in Quebec](/kb/recruitment/consent)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clean Database">
    A database from which errors have been eliminated.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Development Plan (CDP)">
    A high-level document prepared by the sponsor that sets out the strategy for developing an investigational product from early research through to regulatory approval, including the planned sequence of clinical trials.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Equipoise">
    A genuine uncertainty, on the part of the relevant expert community, about which interventions are most effective for a given condition.

    This uncertainty is what makes research necessary to compare the interventions. It links a clinician's duty of care with the need for research to show that the interventions offered are safe and effective. [TCPS 2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html) says clinical equipoise should be considered as a starting point for the design and review of clinical trials. In studies of interventions intended to benefit participants, it must exist about which interventions are most effective.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Hold">
    An order issued by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to a sponsor to delay a proposed clinical investigation or to suspend an ongoing one.

    When a proposed study is on clinical hold, participants may not be given the investigational product. When an ongoing study is on clinical hold, no new participants may be recruited and placed on the investigational product. Participants already in the study should be taken off the investigational product unless the FDA specifically permits otherwise in the interest of patient safety.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Investigation Plan (CIP)">
    The name that ISO 14155, the good clinical practice standard for medical device studies, gives to the protocol and its amendments.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Research Associate (CRA)">
    A person employed by the sponsor, or by a contract research organization acting for the sponsor, who is responsible for determining that a trial is being conducted according to the protocol at the site.

    A CRA is also called a monitor. A monitor's duties may include helping to plan and start a trial, assessing its conduct and assisting in data analysis. Monitors work with the site's clinical research coordinator to review all the data and documents of the trial.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="crc"></a>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)">
    A nurse, health professional or other qualified member of the research team who handles most of the administrative responsibilities and day-to-day activities of a clinical trial.

    The CRC acts as the link between the site and the sponsor, makes sure all data and records are reviewed before a monitor's visit, and performs designated participant assessments. Other names include trial coordinator, study coordinator, research coordinator, clinical coordinator, research nurse and protocol nurse.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Study Report (CSR)">
    A written description of a study of any therapeutic, prophylactic or diagnostic agent in human participants, in which the clinical and statistical descriptions, presentations and analyses are fully integrated into a single report.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Trial">
    Any research project that prospectively assigns human participants to intervention groups, or to concurrent comparison or control groups, to study the cause-and-effect relationship between a medical intervention and a health outcome.

    The interventions include drugs, surgical procedures, devices, behavioural treatments and changes in the process of care. Clinical trials are one of the two types of clinical study. The other type is observational studies ([how a study gets its data](/paths#how-a-study-gets-its-data)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Trial Agreement">
    A written, signed and dated agreement between two or more parties to a study that sets out how tasks and obligations are delegated and distributed and, where appropriate, the financial arrangements.

    The protocol may serve as its basis. Other names include contract and clinical study agreement (CSA). The RI-MUHC's SOPs prefer the name clinical study agreement, to avoid confusion with the Clinical Trial Application (CTA). The [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) reviews it ([clinical trial agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements)).
  </Accordion>
  <a id="cta"></a>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)">
    An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations.

    A drug trial needs one unless it uses a marketed drug within the conditions of its notice of compliance or its drug identification number ([when Health Canada must authorize a study](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study)). A change to a trial running under a CTA goes to Health Canada as a Clinical Trial Application Amendment (CTA-A).
  </Accordion>
  <a id="cov"></a>
  <Accordion title="Close-Out Visit (COV)">
    The last monitoring visit, arranged by the sponsor or sponsor-investigator and conducted by a monitor at the site, to make sure that every required aspect of closing the study has been addressed before database lock.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Coded Data (De-identified)">
    Participant data that have been assigned a code, with the direct identifiers removed from the dataset and held separately.

    With a single code, the key linking the code to the direct identifiers is available only to authorized members of the research team. With double or multiple codes, a third party, such as the data holder, holds the key, which is not available to the researchers.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE)">
    A standardized system, published by the US National Cancer Institute, for grading the severity of adverse events.

    Its grades 1 to 5 run from mild to death. Many oncology and other clinical trial protocols use it as their adverse event grading scale.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="consort-c"></a>
  <Accordion title="CONSORT-Children and Adolescents (CONSORT-C)">
    A 2026 extension of the CONSORT reporting guideline for reports of randomized trials with participants aged 0 to 19 years.

    The CONSORT-C checklist has 13 new reporting items. Examples include:

    - The results of each primary and secondary outcome for each prespecified age group.
    - Whether the dose or formulation of the intervention was appropriate for the trial population, and any adjustments made based on age, weight or body surface area.
    - Whether the participating children and adolescents were given information about the trial, and whether assent was given or consent obtained, if appropriate for their age.
    - Whether the trial's interventions or procedures caused fear, pain or distress, or were invasive, and the measures taken to mitigate them.

    Its companion is SPIRIT-C 2026.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Continuing Review">
    The periodic ethics review of an ongoing study by the REB after its initial approval.

    The RI-MUHC's SOPs require it at least once a year, before the previous approval expires. It checks that the balance of risks and benefits stays acceptable as new information emerges ([keeping approval valid](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Contract Research Organization (CRO)">
    A person or an organization, commercial, academic or other, contracted by a sponsor to perform one or more of the sponsor's study-related duties and functions.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="capa"></a>
  <Accordion title="Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)">
    A series of actions taken to resolve a failure to comply with the study's requirements, good clinical practice or the applicable regulatory requirements, and to prevent it from happening again.

    The steps are to identify the issue, to correct it and to prevent it from recurring.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## D

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Data Management Plan (DMP)">
    A document that describes how a study's data will be collected, processed, cleaned and archived.

    The plan defines the roles, systems, timelines and quality control procedures.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="dsmb"></a>
  <Accordion title="Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)">
    An independent committee that the sponsor may establish to assess, at intervals, the progress of a clinical study, its safety data and its critical efficacy endpoints, and to recommend to the sponsor whether to continue, modify or stop the study.

    Other names include independent data-monitoring committee (IDMC), data and safety monitoring board, data monitoring committee and monitoring committee.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Database Lock">
    The action taken to prevent further changes to a clinical trial database.

    A database is locked after review and query resolution, once it has been determined that the database is ready for analysis.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="dsur"></a>
  <Accordion title="Development Safety Update Report (DSUR)">
    An annual integrated safety report on an investigational product still in clinical development, in the format of ICH guideline E2F.

    Since Dec. 4, 2015, Health Canada asks sponsors for a DSUR only on request. A DSUR covers all the safety data collected during the reporting period, an updated assessment of benefits and risks, and any regulatory action taken. For marketed products, the corresponding report is the periodic safety update report (PSUR).
  </Accordion>
  <a id="dht"></a>
  <Accordion title="Digital Health Technology (DHT)">
    Software or hardware used to collect clinical trial data, such as wearable sensors, electronic patient-reported outcome platforms and other connected devices.

    In a trial with decentralized elements, sponsors and qualified investigators are responsible for ensuring that the computerized systems and digital health technology used are fit for purpose, reliable and appropriate for the trial design. A digital tool that performs a medical purpose, such as diagnosing or monitoring a disease, may be regulated as a medical device ([designing a decentralized trial](/kb/design/dct-design)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Direct Access">
    Permission to examine, analyze, verify and reproduce any records and reports that are important to the evaluation of a clinical study.

    Domestic and foreign regulatory authorities and the sponsor's monitors and auditors have direct access. Any party with direct access should take all reasonable precautions, within the applicable regulatory requirements, to keep participants' identities and the sponsor's proprietary information confidential.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="dtp"></a>
  <Accordion title="Direct-to-Participant (DTP)">
    A decentralized clinical trial model in which the investigational product is shipped directly to a participant's home rather than dispensed at the site.

    It requires chain-of-custody documentation, confirmation that the participant received the product and a pharmacist's professional oversight under Quebec pharmacy law, wherever the product is delivered.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Documentation">
    All records, in any form, that describe or record the methods, conduct or results of a study, the factors affecting it and the actions taken.

    The forms include written, electronic, magnetic and optical records, scans, X-rays and electrocardiograms.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Drug Accountability">
    The process of being responsible for the investigational product at the site by following safe pharmaceutical, medical device or natural health product practices.

    The investigator is responsible for investigational product accountability at the site, including the records of its receipt, storage, dispensing, return and destruction.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Drug Identification Number (DIN)">
    A number that Health Canada assigns to a drug product before it is marketed in Canada.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## E

<AccordionGroup>
  <a id="efvp"></a>
  <Accordion title="EFVP (privacy impact assessment)">
    The privacy impact assessment, or évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée, that Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information requires with a researcher's request for access to health and social services information without consent, including access before consent for pre-screening.

    At the MUHC, it is completed in French, on form F17, a short version for pre-screening, or form F18 for other access without consent. It is submitted in Nagano, and the principal investigator signs it.

    It may also be required when participants' information will leave Quebec, or when the study acquires, develops or alters technology to handle that information ([when an EFVP is needed](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed)). The MUHC's [Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) reviews it. Consider starting it early in study planning.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Electronic Case Report Form (eCRF)">
    An electronic version of the case report form, used to capture participant data and transmit them to the sponsor.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Electronic Consent (eConsent)">
    Informed consent in which the participant signs an electronic informed consent form (eICF), sometimes after a consent discussion held at a distance.

    Electronic consent follows every part of the consent process that a paper form follows, and adds conditions on the software, the signatures, the REB's review and the storage of the signed forms ([obtain consent remotely or electronically](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-remotely-or-electronically)). Other names include e-consent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Electronic Data Capture (EDC)">
    A computerized system for collecting clinical trial data electronically rather than on paper.
  </Accordion>

## H – I

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Health Canada (HC)">
    The federal department that regulates drugs, medical devices and natural health products in Canada, including their use in clinical trials.

    It authorizes clinical trials of drugs under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, and clinical trials of natural health products and the sale of Class II, III or IV medical devices for investigational testing under their own regulations ([when Health Canada must authorize a study](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Human Research">
    At the RI-MUHC, any research involving human participants, or involving biological material, human embryos, fetuses, fetal tissue, reproductive materials or stem cells from living or deceased people.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="privileges"></a>
  <Accordion title="Human Research Privileges">
    The MUHC's general institutional permission for a physician or dentist who is a member of the MUHC's Council of Physicians, Dentists, Pharmacists and Midwives to conduct human research at the MUHC and the RI-MUHC.

    Privileges cover research that requires medical oversight and research that does not. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-500#4-0-definitions) require them of the qualified investigator or principal investigator of a Health Canada-regulated study with an investigational product.

    Researchers who are not members of the council hold [Researcher Status](#researcher-status) instead. Although pharmacists are members of the council, the MUHC requires pharmacists acting as principal investigator to obtain Researcher Status instead of Privileges. In a study that does not require medical oversight, the RI-MUHC's SOPs also have a pharmacist acting as the principal investigator's backup obtain Researcher Status ([which credential you need](/training/compliance-requirements#know-which-credential-you-need)). Other names include Privileges, research privileges and RI-MUHC Institutional Privileges.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="ICH E6(R3)">
    The current version of the good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), which Health Canada implemented on April 1, 2026.

    Its central idea is quality by design: identifying the factors critical to quality before the study begins, and building controls proportionate to them. The guideline also covers risk-based monitoring, the governance of electronic data and decentralized trial elements. Health Canada implemented its Annex 2 on Nov. 4, 2026.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Impartial Witness">
    A person who is independent of the study and cannot be unfairly influenced by the people involved in it, who attends the informed consent process when the participant or their legally appointed representative cannot read, and who reads the consent form and any other written information given to the participant.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Inclusion Criteria">
    The criteria that prospective participants must meet to be eligible to take part in a study.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="irlet"></a>
  <Accordion title="Indigenous Research Level of Engagement Tool (IRLET)">
    One of a family of tools developed by the Saskatchewan Centre for Patient-Oriented Research (SCPOR) to help research teams define and document the intended level of engagement with Indigenous communities at each stage of a research project.

    IRLET is the version for Indigenous communities. PORLET is the version for patient-oriented research, and PaIRLET addresses partner engagement. The tools distinguish consultation, collaboration and community leadership.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Informed Consent">
    A process by which a participant voluntarily confirms their willingness to take part in a study, after being informed of every aspect of the study that is relevant to their decision.

    The informed consent process must be documented for each participant, using the Informed Consent Process Checklist for example. Consent is not a one-time event: it continues throughout the participant's involvement ([consent in Quebec](/kb/recruitment/consent)).
  </Accordion>
  <a id="icf"></a>
  <Accordion title="Informed Consent Form (ICF)">
    The written form that gives a participant the information essential to an informed decision about taking part in a study.

    The participant, or their [legally appointed representative](#lar), signs it to show their intent to give informed consent. The person obtaining consent must also sign it. It is also called a consent form.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Inspection">
    An official review by a regulatory authority of the documents, facilities, records and other resources that it considers related to a clinical trial, at the site, at the sponsor's or a contract research organization's facilities, or elsewhere.

  </Accordion>
  <a id="ip"></a>
  <Accordion title="Investigational Product (IP)">
    A pharmaceutical form of an active ingredient or placebo, a medical device or a natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial.

    It includes a product with a marketing authorization when it is used or assembled differently from the approved form, used for an unapproved indication or used to gain further information about an approved use. It is also called the study drug, the study medical device or the study natural health product.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="ita"></a>
  <Accordion title="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)">
    Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing.

    Whether a device study needs one depends on the device's class and licence and on the study ([when a study needs an ITA](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization)). It is the device counterpart of a Clinical Trial Application.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="isf"></a>
  <Accordion title="Investigator Site File (ISF)">

    It should mirror the sponsor's or sponsor-investigator's trial master file, which holds the essential documents of every site.   </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Investigator-Initiated Study">
    A research study developed, initiated and conducted by an investigator, whose obligations then include those of a sponsor as well as those of an investigator.

    In a clinical trial, that investigator is a [sponsor-investigator](#sponsor-investigator).
  </Accordion>
  <a id="ib"></a>
  <Accordion title="Investigator's Brochure (IB)">
    A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants.

    It informs the site's investigators of the product's known safety and efficacy profile.
  </Accordion>

## P – Q

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Participant">
    A person, a patient or a healthy volunteer, who takes part in a clinical study, either as a recipient of the investigational product or as a control.

    Other names include study participant, research participant and study patient.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Patient-Reported Outcomes (PRO)">
    Measures of a patient's health status reported directly by the patient, without interpretation by a clinician.

    PRO instruments must be validated and follow the same data management standards as other case report form data.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="perc"></a>
  <Accordion title="Pediatric Emergency Research Canada (PERC)">
    A national network for pediatric emergency medicine research, which offers a network of sites, established multi-site protocols and methodological support for emergency department studies.

    The Montreal Children's Hospital is a PERC site.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="psur"></a>
  <Accordion title="Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR)">
    A periodic safety report on a marketed drug that summarizes its safety experience worldwide.

    For investigational products still in development, the corresponding report is the development safety update report (DSUR). Since March 1, 2013, Health Canada accepts either a PSUR or a periodic benefit-risk evaluation report (PBRER) to meet its requirements for annual summary reports.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Personne mandatée">
    The person whom an establishment of Quebec's health network formally mandates to authorize the research conducted under its auspices.

    At the MUHC, the President and Chief Executive Officer has delegated this authority to the Director of Innovation, Performance and Quality, who issues MUHC Authorization for each study. No study may begin without that authorization. The Personne mandatée is also the MUHC's person responsible for the responsible conduct of research. The full French name is personne formellement mandatée (PFM). Questions about MUHC Authorization go to the [Personne mandatée's office](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Pharmacokinetics and Pharmacodynamics (PK and PD)">
    Pharmacokinetics is how the body affects a drug, through its absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion, and pharmacodynamics is how the drug affects the body, through the relationship between the drug's concentration and its effect.

    Both are studied often in Phase I and II trials.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="pi"></a>
  <Accordion title="Principal Investigator (PI)">
    The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there.

    At the MUHC, anyone who acts as principal investigator must first hold Human Research Privileges, Researcher Status or a written institutional authorization ([credentials](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status)). In a drug, natural health product or medical device trial, Health Canada's rules call the person responsible for the trial at a site the [qualified investigator](#qi). The RI-MUHC's SOPs often write QI/PI. Principal investigator is primarily a US term.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Protocol">
    A document that describes the objectives, design, methodology, statistical considerations and organization of a trial.

    It usually also gives the trial's background and rationale, which may instead be in documents the protocol references. In the good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation, protocol means the protocol and its amendments. ISO 14155, for device studies, calls it the clinical investigation plan (CIP).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Protocol Amendment">
    A written description of a change to a protocol, or of a formal clarification of it.

    An amendment needs the REB's approval and, where applicable, Health Canada's before it is implemented ([study amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments)). A change needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants may be made before the REB's approval, and must be reported to the REB immediately. In a sponsor-investigator study, the sponsor-investigator should also be notified as soon as possible afterwards.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Protocol Deviation">
    An incident of non-compliance with the protocol that may or may not have a significant effect on participants' rights, safety or welfare, or on the integrity of the data.

    It is also called a protocol violation ([deviations and their severity](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events)).
  </Accordion>
  <a id="qi"></a>
  <Accordion title="Qualified Investigator (QI)">
    The person responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a drug or natural health product trial at a clinical trial site, who is entitled to provide health care under the laws of the province where the site is located.

    In a drug or natural health product trial, [Health Canada requires the qualified investigator to be a physician](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.05.001.html) and a member in good standing of a professional medical association. For a drug or natural health product to be used for dental purposes only, the qualified investigator may be a physician or a dentist, and a member in good standing of a professional medical or dental association.

    Through an [interim policy](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#know-what-health-canada-is-changing), Health Canada also accepts a person who is not a physician or dentist as the qualified investigator of a drug trial, if their education, training, experience and scope of practice suit the trial. Medical decisions about the trial outside that person's licensed scope of practice must then be made by a qualified physician or dentist on the trial team, licensed in the site's province or territory and responsible for medical oversight and participant care.

    At the MUHC, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs require Human Research Privileges](/sops/cr-500#4-0-definitions) of the qualified investigator or principal investigator of a Health Canada-regulated study with an investigational product: a drug, a medical device or a natural health product. [Privileges](/training/compliance-requirements#know-which-credential-you-need) are for physicians and dentists who are members of the MUHC's Council of Physicians, Dentists, Pharmacists and Midwives. The MUHC's requirement also applies to a qualified investigator whom Health Canada accepts under its interim policy.

    In a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor must ensure that each clinical trial site has no more than one qualified investigator.

    For a medical device, the Medical Devices Regulations define the qualified investigator as a member in good standing of a professional association of persons entitled to provide health care in the province, whom the ethics committee of the health care facility where the testing takes place designates to conduct the testing.

  <Accordion title="Qualified Investigator's Undertaking (QIU)">
    An undertaking each qualified investigator signs and dates before taking on their responsibilities in a drug or natural health product trial, stating that they will conduct the trial in accordance with good clinical practices and, if the sponsor discontinues the trial in its entirety or at a clinical trial site, will immediately inform the participants and the REB, give them the reasons and advise them in writing of any potential risks to the health of participants or others.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Randomization">
    The process of assigning trial participants to treatment or control groups using an element of chance, to reduce bias.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="REB Exemption Letter">
    A letter from the MUHC REB confirming that a project is QA/QI rather than research and therefore does not need REB review.

    A team that believes its project is QA/QI may need one, for example to provide at the time of publication. To request one, complete the Centre for Applied Ethics' [Research vs QA/QI Screening Tool](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/templates-consent-forms) (Word) and email it to [reb@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) with the subject Request for REB exemption letter.   </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Re-consent">
    The process of obtaining a participant's informed consent again, on an REB-approved amended consent form, when new information may affect their willingness to continue.

    Initial consent covers participation as the participant understood it at the time. It does not cover participation under conditions that did not yet exist ([keep consent current](/kb/recruitment/consent#keep-consent-current)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Recruitment">
    The processes and activities used to identify patients or other participants for clinical trials, from a base population through to their enrolment in the study.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="reb"></a>
  <Accordion title="Research Ethics Board (REB)">
    A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being.

    Its French name is comité d'éthique à la recherche (CER). The international name is independent ethics committee (IEC), and in the United States, institutional review board (IRB). The MUHC's REB is independent and reports directly to the MUHC's board of directors. Write to it at [reb@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) ([how a study is reviewed](/kb/submission/submission-overview#how-a-study-is-reviewed)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Researcher Status">
    The MUHC's permission for a researcher who is not a member of the MUHC's Council of Physicians, Dentists, Pharmacists and Midwives to conduct research that does not require medical oversight.

    Holders include people with a doctorate, nurses, physiotherapists, psychologists and radiation therapy technologists. Although pharmacists are members of the council, the MUHC requires pharmacists acting as principal investigator to obtain Researcher Status instead of Privileges. In a study that does not require medical oversight, the RI-MUHC's SOPs also have a pharmacist acting as the principal investigator's backup obtain Researcher Status. Research that requires medical oversight needs [Human Research Privileges](#privileges) ([which credential you need](/training/compliance-requirements#know-which-credential-you-need)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Retrospective Study">
    Research that reviews records from the past, such as medical records, administrative databases or biobank samples, or that obtains information about past events through interviews or surveys.

    It does not follow participants forward in time. A study of records, or of samples already held in a biobank, is at training Level V ([find your study's training level](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Risk-Based Monitoring (RBM)">
    A monitoring approach in which monitoring activities are tailored to the participant protection and data integrity risks of a particular study, rather than applied uniformly.

    ICH E6(R2) formalized it, and E6(R3) expands it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="RSSS">
    Quebec's public health and social services network, the réseau de la santé et des services sociaux, whose establishments include the MUHC.

    It includes the integrated health and social services centres (CISSS and CIUSSS) and the university hospital centres.
  </Accordion>

## S

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Santé Québec">
    The Quebec government body of which the MUHC has been an establishment since Dec. 1, 2024.

    Its research access centre, the Centre d'accès pour la recherche (CAR), is the provincial access point for health data for researchers who are not attached to Quebec's health and social services network.

  <a id="sae"></a>
  <Accordion title="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)">
    An adverse event that meets any one of these seriousness criteria: hospitalization or prolonged hospitalization, a congenital malformation, persistent or significant disability or incapacity, a life-threatening event or death, or a medically important event as defined in the REB-approved protocol.

    The good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation says that an important medical event that may not be immediately life-threatening or result in death or hospitalization, but that may jeopardize the participant or require intervention to prevent a serious outcome, should generally be considered serious. Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)">
    A monitoring visit conducted by a monitor as a final evaluation of the site, and to train the site team on the study protocol and its associated documents and processes.

    It takes place after REB approval and MUHC Authorization.

    An administrator, investigator or research team member qualified by experience, skills and training to give final approval of the RI-MUHC's SOPs.

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="SOP-LR">
    The RI-MUHC's lower-risk series of SOPs, for study Levels IV and V: prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada and involving no surgery, and retrospective studies, including those of samples already held in a biobank.

    Its SOP Reader and Assessment is valid for 5 years, after which the course and assessment must be completed again. Its companion is the higher-risk series, SOP-CR.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Source Data">
    All the information in original records, and in certified copies of original records, of clinical findings, observations or other activities in a clinical trial that is needed to reconstruct and evaluate the trial.

    Source data are contained in source documents.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Source Documents">
    The original documents, data and records of a study, such as hospital records, clinical and office charts, laboratory notes, participants' diaries, pharmacy dispensing records and data recorded by automated instruments.

    They also include copies or transcriptions certified as accurate after verification, X-rays, participant files, and records kept at the pharmacy, the laboratories and the medico-technical departments involved in the trial.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="spirit-c"></a>
  <Accordion title="SPIRIT-Children and Adolescents (SPIRIT-C)">
    A 2026 extension of the SPIRIT guideline for the protocols of randomized trials with participants aged 0 to 19 years.

    The SPIRIT-C checklist has 17 new reporting items. Examples include:

    - A justification for including several age groups, or children and adolescents at different developmental stages, and how the trial addresses possible differences in treatment effects related to age or development.
    - Whether the dose or formulation of the intervention is appropriate for the trial population, and any adjustments made based on age, weight or body surface area.
    - The validity, reliability, feasibility and responsiveness of the outcome measurement instruments for the prespecified age groups.
    - All efforts to reduce the risk that taking part in the trial poses to the child or adolescent.
    - Whether developmentally appropriate materials with understandable information on the trial process will be provided to participants when obtaining informed consent or assent, and where the materials can be found or whether they are available on request.
    - Plans for assessing outcomes and harms beyond the formal study completion date.

    Its companion is CONSORT-C 2026. The SPIRIT | CONSORT-C initiative developed both guidelines, with input from researchers at MICYRN member institutions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sponsor">
    An individual, company, institution or organization that takes responsibility for the initiation, management and arrangement of the financing of a clinical trial.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sponsor-Investigator">
    An individual who both initiates and conducts, alone or with others, a clinical trial, and under whose immediate direction the investigational product is administered to, dispensed to or used by a participant.

    The term covers only individuals, never a corporation or an agency. A sponsor-investigator's obligations include those of a sponsor and those of an investigator ([when you are the sponsor-investigator](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Standard Operating Procedure (SOP)">
    Detailed, written instructions to achieve uniformity in the performance of a specific function.

    At the RI-MUHC, 33 clinical research SOPs govern how studies are conducted, organized in the CR series, the sponsor-investigator series (the 100s) and the 500 series ([the SOPs](/sops/overview)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Study Levels (I to V)">
    The RI-MUHC's five levels of study risk, from Level I for the highest-risk studies to Level V for the lowest-risk ones, which set the training a study's team needs.

    Level I covers drug studies, including biologics, gene and cell therapies, blood products, vaccines and radiopharmaceuticals. Level II covers medical device and surgery studies, and Level III natural health product studies.

    Level IV covers prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada and involving no surgery, such as educational and non-invasive interventions (psychotherapy, physiotherapy, diet, exercise, massage). Level V covers retrospective studies, including those of samples already held in a biobank. The highest-risk study a person works on sets their training level ([find your study's training level](/paths#find-the-training-level-your-study-needs)).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sub-Investigator">
    A qualified member of the study team, designated and supervised by the qualified or principal investigator at a site, who performs critical investigator tasks as described on the Task Delegation Log or makes important trial-related decisions.

    At least one qualified sub-investigator should be delegated on each study as the backup to replace the qualified or principal investigator in their absence. That backup should have the same qualifications and training as the qualified or principal investigator, including RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="SUSAR">
    A serious adverse reaction to a drug or natural health product that is not identified, in its nature, severity or frequency, in the risk information in the investigator's brochure or on the product's label.

    SUSAR stands for suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction. The sponsor reports it to Health Canada within set timelines. The qualified or principal investigator reports it to the REB when it meets the REB's reporting criteria ([reportable events](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events)).
  </Accordion>

## T – W

<AccordionGroup>
  <a id="tdl"></a>
  <Accordion title="Task Delegation Log (TDL)">
    The investigator's list of the appropriately qualified people to whom the investigator has delegated significant study-related tasks.

    The investigator must maintain the log. It is completed for each site team member before that person does any study-related task, and it should be kept up to date throughout the study. Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.

    The good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation says that the investigator should ensure that a record is kept of the people and parties to whom the investigator has delegated trial-related activities. It is filed in the investigator site file. Other names in the RI-MUHC's SOPs include delegation log and study delegation log.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="TCPS 2 Article 2.5 (QA/QI exemption)">
    The article of TCPS 2 under which quality assurance and quality improvement studies used exclusively for assessment, management or improvement purposes are not research and fall outside REB review.

    [Article 2.5](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html) covers studies that involve the assessment, management or improvement of a service or program using established methods, that do not aim to generate generalizable knowledge, and that place no additional burden on participants beyond standard care. Even when a project is exempt, the principles of respect for persons, concern for welfare and justice still apply. The [Is It Research?](/is-it-research) screening tool helps you decide whether a project qualifies.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Therapeutic Misconception">
    The tendency of research participants to experience a research intervention as personal treatment rather than as part of a scientific investigation.

    TCPS 2 names it as a risk to informed consent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Therapeutic Products Directorate (TPD)">
    The directorate of Health Canada that the RI-MUHC's SOPs name as responsible for evaluating and monitoring the safety, effectiveness and quality of pharmaceutical drugs and other therapeutic products for human use, including medical devices.

    Health Canada's [Pharmaceutical Drugs Directorate](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/corporate/about-health-canada/branches-agencies/health-products-food-branch/therapeutic-products-directorate.html), formerly the Therapeutic Products Directorate, is the regulator of prescription pharmaceutical drugs for human use. Health Canada has a separate Medical Devices Directorate for medical devices.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Transport of Dangerous Goods (TDG)">
    Canada's federal regulations on the classification, packaging, labelling and transport of dangerous goods, including biological specimens and infectious substances.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="tmf"></a>
  <Accordion title="Trial Master File (TMF)">
    The sponsor's or sponsor-investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines across all the study's sites.

    It contains the essential documents of every site, and each site's investigator site file should mirror it.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="tcps2"></a>
  <Accordion title="Tri-Council Policy Statement (TCPS 2)">
    The policy of Canada's three federal research agencies on the ethical conduct of research involving humans, in its 2022 edition.

    As a condition of funding, the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR), the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC) and the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada (SSHRC) require researchers and their institutions to apply it. Institutions must ensure that the research conducted under their auspices complies with it. Its three core principles are respect for persons, concern for welfare and justice.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Triple Evaluation">
    The three evaluations a research project needs before an establishment of Quebec's health and social services network may authorize it: the scientific evaluation, the ethics evaluation by the REB where the applicable standards require one, and the institutional feasibility evaluation, with the EFVP where one is needed.

    All the required evaluations must be complete and favourable before the authorization is issued. At the MUHC, a research project with human participants needs all three. The Hub calls them the [scientific review, ethics review and institutional feasibility review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#how-a-study-is-reviewed).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Unanticipated Adverse Device Effect (UADE)">
    Any serious adverse effect on health or safety caused by a device that was not previously identified in nature, severity or degree of incidence in the investigational plan, or any other unanticipated serious problem associated with a device.

    UADE is a US term.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Vanessa's Law">
    The federal law, the Protecting Canadians from Unsafe Drugs Act (2014), under which hospitals report serious adverse drug reactions and medical device incidents to Health Canada within 30 days after they are first documented in the hospital.

    The hospital reports whether or not the patient is admitted. A reaction is excluded if it relates only to a drug authorized for a clinical trial under Division 5, a drug sold through the Special Access Program or a vaccine given under a provincial routine immunization program. An incident is excluded if it involves only a device under an investigational testing or special access authorization. Those already have their own reporting routes. Natural health products are outside the hospital's reporting.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Vulnerable Participants">
    People whose willingness to volunteer for a clinical trial may be unduly influenced by the expectation, justified or not, of benefits from taking part, or of retaliation from senior members of a hierarchy if they refuse.

    Examples include medical, pharmacy, dental and nursing students, subordinate hospital and laboratory staff, employees of the pharmaceutical industry, members of the armed forces and people kept in detention. Others include patients with incurable diseases, people in nursing homes, unemployed or impoverished people, patients in emergency situations, ethnic minority groups, homeless people, nomads, refugees, minors and people incapable of giving consent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Wet Signature">
    A handwritten signature applied in ink by the signatory in person, as opposed to an electronic or digital signature.

    Many essential documents in clinical research require one.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="who-trds"></a>
  <Accordion title="WHO Trial Registration Data Set (WHO TRDS)">
    The World Health Organization's (WHO) minimum set of 24 items of trial information that must appear in a registry for a clinical trial to be considered fully registered.

    [Its current version](https://www.who.int/tools/clinical-trials-registry-platform/network/who-data-set) is 1.3.1. The International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) recommends that medical journal editors require a trial's registration in a public registry, at or before the time of the first participant's consent for enrolment, as a condition of considering it for publication. The ICMJE accepts registration in a primary registry of WHO's International Clinical Trials Registry Platform that includes the 24-item data set, or in ClinicalTrials.gov, which provides data to the platform.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="wi"></a>
  <Accordion title="Work Instructions (WI)">
    Documents that describe only a sequence of technical steps and instructions for a process, without the responsibilities, timelines, communication or resources that an SOP details.

    A document that details responsibilities, timelines, communication or resources [should be an SOP](/sops/cr-001#5-2-1), and an SOP can also contain technical steps.

    Department-specific or protocol-specific work instructions fall outside [the RI-MUHC's process for its SOPs](/sops/cr-001#2-0-scope), because the department, the qualified or principal investigator or the sponsor-investigator approves them. For a department's internal work instruction, the approval should involve the review and signature of its author and at least one reviewer, then the approval of the department's leader. A work instruction should refer to an RI-MUHC SOP that details the responsibilities, timelines, communication or resources, and the RI-MUHC's SOPs include [a template for work instructions](/sops/cr-001#appendix-2).

    The Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM), for example, has its own work instructions, with codes that start with WI-CIM. The RI-MUHC's SOPs also call them working instructions.
  </Accordion>
