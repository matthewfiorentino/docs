You can raise a concern about how research is conducted at the MUHC, and you must be protected against reprisal when you do.

Responsible conduct of research, as Canada's three federal research agencies define it, is the behaviour expected of anyone who conducts or supports research, from the formulation of the research question to the dissemination of results and the management of research funds. If you aren't sure whether your project is research or quality improvement, [Is It Research?](/is-it-research) helps you decide.

The MUHC and the RI-MUHC must ensure that the activities of their research community meet “the highest standards of scientific, ethical and responsible conduct.” The MUHC has its own process for the review and follow-up of a concern or allegation of a breach, set out in its [*Regulatory Framework for Clinical Research at Santé Québec – McGill University Health Centre: Organization and Good Practices*](https://rimuhc.ca/conduct-research/policies#framework){/* TO CONFIRM at the rimuhc.ca launch: the Framework's page section, rimuhc.ca/conduct-research/policies#framework (the rimuhc chat, Nov. 13, 2026; the PDF's address will change in WordPress, so the page is linked, not the PDF). */}.

## What happens after a concern is reported

The Personne mandatée first assesses the risk of each report, from an administrative or procedural irregularity to active harm to a participant's safety, and may, where applicable, impose protective measures without delay to ensure participants' safety.

The Allegations Intake Committee, composed of the Personne mandatée as chair, the RI-MUHC's Director of Clinical Research and the MUHC's Director of Medical and Professional Services (DMPS), then assesses how credible and serious the report is. It decides on the response, which can include provisionally suspending research privileges pending a formal assessment, and convenes a review committee to oversee the formal investigation when a full investigation is warranted.{/* TO CONFIRM (the Framework's owner): section 6 calls the investigating committee the "Conduct Review Committee" and Appendix B the "Clinical Research Review Committee"; the page names neither. Appendix B also sets response times (preliminary assessment within 5 business days; McGill told within 15 business days; investigation generally within 90 calendar days), left out of reader text under MF's rule on durations (STYLE-HUB E): for MF. */}

If the Allegations Intake Committee finds that the concern could be a breach of the responsible conduct of research, the Personne mandatée must inform McGill University's Research Integrity Office, and the two must then decide which institution is best placed to conduct the formal investigation.

When the MUHC conducts the formal investigation, the review committee must appoint an investigation subcommittee, which must interview the principal investigator concerned (the researcher who leads the study at the MUHC), the person who reported the concern (where applicable and with that person's consent) and all witnesses, and write an investigation report.

If the concern is about you as a study's principal investigator, you must be granted all the guarantees of procedural fairness, including being told of any allegation in writing, the opportunity to present your responses and the right to be accompanied or represented by legal counsel. When a formal investigation is required, the review committee must tell you in writing what the allegations are, how the investigation will run and what your rights are.

You must receive a copy of the investigation report before it is finalized, with the opportunity to submit written observations, and confidentiality must be maintained unless the law provides otherwise.

At the end of the investigation, the review committee must issue a written decision. Its corrective measures must be proportionate, fair and supported by evidence, and can include:

- Dismissal with no further action.
- Mandatory training or corrective supervision.
- A formal corrective action plan, with timelines and follow-up.
- Suspension of research privileges until corrective measures are in place, or their permanent revocation.
- Mandatory reporting to McGill's Research Integrity Office, a professional order, a funding agency or Health Canada, when required.
- Disciplinary measures in accordance with the employment or accreditation policies of the MUHC and the RI-MUHC.

You can appeal the review committee's decision within 30 calendar days of its written transmission, to an appeal authority that the MUHC's management designates. An appeal is admissible when it rests on a breach of procedural fairness likely to have significantly influenced the decision, a decisive error of fact or law that affected the decision, or new and relevant evidence that wasn't available at the time of the investigation.

The person who reported the concern must be told that the report was received, whether the Allegations Intake Committee escalated it and what the final decision is, subject to confidentiality rules.

If McGill conducts the investigation, its [*Regulations Concerning the Investigation of Research Misconduct*](https://www.mcgill.ca/secretariat/repository-university-policies-and-regulations) apply to it. If the allegation concerns you, you can appeal to McGill's Provost by written notice within 10 working days of receiving the final report of the Committee on Research Misconduct and the Vice-President (Research and Innovation).

## How you are protected when you report

In the MUHC's process, the identity of the person who reports must be kept confidential unless the law provides otherwise or that person chooses to disclose it, and that person must be protected against any reprisal or negative professional consequence. A reprisal is in itself grounds for separate disciplinary proceedings. An allegation made knowingly and in bad faith may be subject to disciplinary measures in accordance with the applicable institutional policies.

The MUHC's process for events and allegations must also take systemic power dynamics into account and include measures to reduce their influence on how it is conducted and on its decisions. No one may be discriminated against in the process on the basis of gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, ethnic origin, religion, disability or any other ground.

McGill entitles anyone who makes a good-faith allegation of research misconduct to protection from reprisal; it defines a good-faith allegation as one that isn't malicious or frivolous, made by someone who has reasonable grounds to believe that research misconduct may have occurred.

The federal research agencies require the institutions they fund to commit, in their policies, to protecting from reprisals, as far as possible and consistently with the relevant legislation, anyone who makes an allegation in good faith or gives information about one. The agencies also require those policies to commit the institution to considering an anonymous allegation that comes with enough information to assess it without asking the person who made it for more.

Based on: the *Regulatory Framework*, Appendix B, sections 3.03 and 4.01; McGill University's [*Regulations Concerning the Investigation of Research Misconduct*](https://www.mcgill.ca/secretariat/repository-university-policies-and-regulations), sections 1.11 and 4.3; the [*Tri-Agency Framework: Responsible Conduct of Research*](https://rcr.ethics.gc.ca/eng/framework-cadre-2021.html) (2021), article 4.3.3.

## What counts as a breach of the responsible conduct of research

At the MUHC, breaches of the responsible conduct of research include, without being limited to, any contravention of a funding agency's policy on the conduct of research, for example:

- Fabrication, falsification or plagiarism.
- Mismanagement of research funds.
- Undue appropriation of intellectual property rights.
- Any other conduct that is a significant departure from the ethical and professional standards generally recognized in the relevant scientific community for the design, conduct, evaluation or dissemination of research, or for the treatment of human beings involved in research activities.

The agencies define fabrication as “making up data, source material, methodologies or findings, including graphs and images”; falsification as “manipulating, changing, or omitting data, source material, methodologies or findings, including graphs and images, without appropriate acknowledgement, such that the research record is not accurately represented”; and plagiarism as “presenting and using another's published or unpublished work, including theories, concepts, data, source material, methodologies or findings, including graphs and images, as one's own, without appropriate referencing and, if required, without permission.”

For research misconduct, the MUHC's process for events and allegations uses McGill University's definition, which also names failing to correctly disclose and manage a conflict of interest; [Conflict of Interest](/kb/governance/conflict-of-interest) explains how to declare one. McGill excludes from research misconduct differences of interpretation or judgment about data or results that are reasonable in the circumstances in which they are made or reached.

If you apply for or hold funding from CIHR, NSERC or SSHRC, you must strive to follow the best research practices honestly, accountably, openly and fairly, and are responsible, at a minimum, for rigour, record keeping, accurate referencing, authorship, acknowledgement and conflict of interest management.

For the agencies, a breach is any failure to comply with an agency policy at any stage of a project, from the funding application to the dissemination of results. Besides fabrication, falsification and plagiarism, the agencies give other examples of breaches in their [*Tri-Agency Framework: Responsible Conduct of Research*](https://rcr.ethics.gc.ca/eng/framework-cadre-2021.html), including:

- Destroying your own or another person's research data or records in contravention of the applicable funding agreement, institutional policy, laws, regulations or professional or disciplinary standards, or to avoid the detection of wrongdoing.{/* The 2021 text is ambiguous as printed in both official languages (a stray "or"); the page uses the conditional reading, as the 2016 text did (F1 row 78). */}
- Redundant publication or self-plagiarism.
- Invalid authorship and inadequate acknowledgement of contributors.
- Misrepresentation in an application to the agencies or a related document.
- Mismanagement of grant or award funds.
- Failing to meet the agencies' requirements, or the relevant policies, laws or regulations, for certain types of research, or to obtain the appropriate approvals, permits or certifications before conducting it.
- Breaching the agencies' review processes.

Whether a breach was intentional or an honest error isn't relevant to the agencies in deciding whether there was a breach; intent is a consideration in deciding how severe the recourse is. The agencies' examples of what may be a serious breach include recruiting human participants into a study with significant risks or harms without REB approval, or not following approved protocols.

You must report to the REB that approved your study any protocol deviation at your site that meets the REB's reporting criteria; [Reportable Events and Protocol Deviations](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events) explains how.

The RI-MUHC also refers to McGill University's [*Regulation on the Conduct of Research*](https://www.mcgill.ca/secretariat/repository-university-policies-and-regulations), to the federal agencies' framework and to the [*Policy for the Responsible Conduct of Research*](https://frq.gouv.qc.ca/conduite-responsable-en-recherche/) of the Fonds de recherche du Québec (FRQ) (2022, French only).{/* The FRQ's English page (frq.gouv.qc.ca/en/responsible-conduct-of-research/, read Nov. 13, 2026) still describes the earlier policy, adopted in 2014 and in force since Sept. 1, 2015; the French page says the update came into force on Nov. 1, 2022. ## Meet your commitments as a principal investigator

Holding [research privileges or researcher status](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status) comes with obligations. Your commitments include:

- Complying with the standards of ethics and responsible conduct of research generally applicable to your type of research.
- Declaring, in accordance with the RI-MUHC's policies, any investigation or sanction you may be subject to in connection with your research.
- Consenting to the disclosure of information that identifies you to the competent authorities if an allegation of a breach of the responsible conduct of research is substantiated.

Failing to meet the obligations that come with research privileges or researcher status may lead to their suspension or withdrawal.

Based on: the *Regulatory Framework*, section 3.1.01A.

## Find training on research ethics and responsible conduct

Research privileges and researcher status also commit you to having the required knowledge of research, including the standards of ethics and responsible conduct of research, as demonstrated by the training the RI-MUHC provides; [Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) sets out the training each study level requires.

If you apply for or hold funding from CIHR, NSERC or SSHRC, the agencies make you responsible for becoming familiar with the principles of responsible conduct of research and applying them. If you oversee others, you should appropriately supervise and train your trainees and research staff in them.
