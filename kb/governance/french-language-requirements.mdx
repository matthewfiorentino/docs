Quebec's [Charter of the French Language (CQLR c. C-11)](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/c-11) and the 2022 amendments introduced by [Bill 96 (Loi 14, 2022, c. 14)](https://www.publicationsduquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fileadmin/Fichiers_client/lois_et_reglements/LoisAnnuelles/en/2022/2022C14A.PDF) establish French as the language of work, commerce, and institutional communication in Quebec. For clinical research at the RI-MUHC, this has concrete implications for informed consent forms, clinical trial agreements, and participant-facing materials — particularly for industry-sponsored studies that originate English-language documents.

This page covers what the Charter requires in a research context, what Bill 96 changed, when bilingual documents are needed, and what happens when a protocol amendment requires re-translation.

## What Bill 96 changed and when

Bill 96 was assented on June 1, 2022, but its obligations came into force in phases:

| Date | Obligation |
|---|---|
| **June 1, 2022** | Bill 96 receives assent. Core amendments to the Charter take effect. Strengthened language-of-work protections and expanded OQLF enforcement authority. |
| **June 1, 2023** | Contracts of adhesion must be provided in French first. If a party requests an English version, that is permitted — but the French version must be provided before or at the same time. |
| **June 1, 2025** | Predominance rules for public signage; francization obligations extended to private businesses with 25 or more employees; trademark and labelling rules tighten. |

<Note>
  The June 2023 obligation is **"French version provided first"** for contracts of adhesion — not a requirement for certified translations. A sponsor providing an English-only CTA or ICF does not automatically comply by having a translation made after the fact; the French version must be available at the point of signature.
</Note>

## How this applies to clinical research documents

### Informed Consent Forms (ICFs)

ICFs are participant-facing documents. At the RI-MUHC, French-language participants have a right to receive the ICF in French. In practice this means:

- **All studies enrolling Quebec participants should have a French ICF.** For industry-sponsored studies, the sponsor is responsible for providing the French version; the site should confirm this during feasibility.
- **When a French ICF is unavailable at activation**, the site should not proceed with consenting French-language participants until a French version is approved by the REB. This is a common source of enrollment delays.
- **CATALIS Quebec** provides standard ICF clauses in both official languages and a provincial [ICF template](https://catalisquebec.com/en/provincial-tools/) that satisfies MUHC REB expectations. RI-MUHC has an agreement with CATALIS; confirm current template availability with the [Research Facilitator](mailto:Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca).

### Clinical Trial Agreements (CTAs) and other contracts

Clinical trial agreements and confidentiality agreements are contracts — and depending on their structure, they may qualify as contracts of adhesion (standard-form contracts not individually negotiated). Under Bill 96:

- The French version must be provided first or simultaneously with the English version
- If only an English version is provided, a francophone party may apply to have the contract annulled (Civil Code of Quebec, Art. 1432 read with Charter s. 55)
- In practice, industry sponsors vary in their compliance. If a sponsor provides only an English CTA, the RI-MUHC's contracting team should request the French version before signature

Charter **section 55** governs the default rule for contracts: where a contract is drawn up solely in a language other than French, a person may request that the French version govern. This applies to contracts of adhesion; individually negotiated contracts may be in another language if both parties expressly agree.

### Patient-initiated translation of clinical records

Quebec law gives patients a right to receive their clinical record in French. The precise statutory source for this right in a clinical context should be confirmed against the current text of the [Act respecting health services and social services (LSSSS, RSQ c. S-4.2)](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/s-4.2) — specifically s. 15 — rather than the Charter of the French Language alone. Confirm the operative provision with legal counsel or the [Research Facilitator](mailto:Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) before relying on a specific citation.

<Warning>
  If a participant requests translation of their clinical record into French, the cost falls to the institution, not the participant. This cost is rarely budgeted in industry-sponsored CTAs. Raise this with sponsors during contract negotiation if your study generates significant participant-level clinical documentation.
</Warning>

## Research document carve-out

Bill 96 included a provision enabling the government to adopt regulations exempting certain research documents from the French-language requirements — recognizing that scientific and technical documents are frequently produced in English for international publication. As of May 2026, this research carve-out regulation has **not been finalized**. Until it is:

- Assume that the Charter's general language rules apply to participant-facing documents
- For internal scientific documents (protocols, lab manuals, scientific reports), the practical exposure is lower, but the legal position is not fully settled
- Monitor the [OQLF](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/) and Health Canada Modernized CT Regulations consultations, which touch on this question

## Amendment re-translation trigger

Every protocol amendment that modifies participant-facing content triggers a re-translation obligation. This is one of the most common compliance gaps in ongoing studies.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify which amendment sections are participant-facing">
    Participant-facing content includes the ICF, any patient diaries or questionnaires, recruitment advertisements, and any written instructions provided to participants. Protocol sections that affect the schedule of assessments, eligibility, dosing, or safety may also require ICF updates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Determine whether the French ICF is affected">
    If the amendment changes the ICF content — even a minor wording change to a risk section or a visit addition — the French ICF must be updated to match. Amendments that do not touch the ICF do not trigger re-translation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Obtain French translation before REB submission">
    The amended French ICF must be submitted to the REB as part of the amendment package. Do not submit only the English version with a note that the French version is pending.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm version synchronization before consenting participants">
    After REB approval, confirm that all sites are using the same version of both the English and French ICF. Version mismatch between language versions is a protocol deviation and a potential source of consent invalidity.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  If the French and English versions of an ICF drift out of sync — even if the French version was approved at an earlier amendment — the consents obtained under the mismatched versions may be at risk. Conduct a version audit whenever a new amendment is implemented.
</Warning>

## OQLF enforcement

The [Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF)](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/) interprets and enforces the Charter of the French Language. Bill 96 significantly expanded OQLF's investigation and enforcement authority. The OQLF does not publish a clinical-research-specific enforcement standard, but the general Charter obligations apply to institutions conducting activities in Quebec.

Enforcement in a research context is currently uncommon, but reputational and legal risk increases as the OQLF's mandate expands. The most direct exposure for RI-MUHC is in participant-facing documents and institutional agreements.

</Note>
