Quebec's [Charter of the French language](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/c-11), as amended in 2022 by Bill 96, sets the language that the MUHC, as part of Quebec's civil administration, must or may use in its documents and contracts, research documents included.

## Prepare participant documents in English and French

Unless the Research Ethics Board (REB) accepts a justification, your consent form, advertisements and other participant information must be available in English and French, and so, generally, must other participant materials, such as questionnaires, diaries and phone scripts. These requirements come from the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) and the REB. [Informed Consent in Quebec](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form) explains how to have the consent form translated and submitted, and [Recruitment, Screening and Enrolment](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment) covers advertisements, which MUHC Communications approves in both languages.

When an amendment changes a participant document, translate the approved change and submit the translation to the REB before you use it, so that both versions stay the same. Using an improper translation, or not using the current consent form, is a deviation that the REB requires you to report; [Reportable Events and Protocol Deviations](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events) explains how. The RI-MUHC's SOPs recommend a document approval tracking log to follow each submission, approval and implementation.

Based on: [SOP-CR-006, sections 5.1.6](/sops/cr-006#5-1-6), [5.3.7](/sops/cr-006#5-3-7) and [5.4.7](/sops/cr-006#5-4-7); [SOP-CR-007, section 5.4.8](/sops/cr-007#5-4-8); [SOP-CR-009, section 5.3.1](/sops/cr-009#5-3-1); [SOP-CR-026, section 5.3.1](/sops/cr-026#5-3-1); the MUHC REB's [REB-SOP 404.001](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops), section 5.4.1, and REB-SOP 405A.001, section 5.2.3.

## Check which research documents may stay in another language

The Charter lets an agency of the civil administration, such as the MUHC, write some documents drawn up or used in research in another language alone, though it must still use only French whenever it considers that possible. A regulation of the Minister lists them:
- Documents necessary for a clinical trial, such as the research protocol, the investigator's brochure, the procedure schedule, the imaging acquisition guide and the pharmacy manual.
- Economic and financial documents.
- Information that a participant, or another person contributing to the research, sends to provide information.
- Material used for a survey or statistical survey, such as a questionnaire or an interview form.
- A study and its assessment, and the description of a research project, whatever the language in which the study is conducted, when they need to be referred to in another language.
- Documents attached to an application for authorization or for funding.
- Technical or theoretical documents used in research, such as those for experimental trials.

The exception doesn't cover the contracts the MUHC enters into, or the document written to obtain an authorization or funding; the documents attached to that document are covered.

The SOPs' rule for participant materials still applies to materials on this list, such as questionnaires.

Based on: the [Charter of the French language](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/c-11), sections 13.2 and 22.5; the [Regulation respecting the derogations to the duty of exemplarity of the civil administration and the documents drawn up or used in research](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cr/C-11,%20r.%205.1), section 2; [SOP-CR-006, section 5.3.7](/sops/cr-006#5-3-7).

## Check the language rules for contracts

The Charter sets different language rules for different contracts. Ask the Research Agreements Office, which drafts, reviews and negotiates every research agreement, how they apply to yours, including when the MUHC is a party to a contract of adhesion; [Reviewing Clinical Trial Agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements) explains how the office reviews agreements.

Contracts the MUHC enters into, as part of Quebec's civil administration, must be drawn up exclusively in French, and so must the written documents related to them, such as those sent to the MUHC to enter into a contract and those the parties send each other under it.

A version in another language may be attached when the MUHC enters into a contract in Quebec with, for example, a person who doesn't live in Quebec, or a company that isn't required to register in Quebec's enterprise register and whose head office is in a State where French isn't an official language. A contract the MUHC enters into outside Quebec, and the documents related to it, may be drawn up in another language alone.

The Charter also sets a rule for contracts of adhesion, those whose essential clauses one party imposes and the other can't freely negotiate. Since June 1, 2023, every new contract of adhesion must be given to the adhering party in French first; the parties can then choose to be bound by a version in another language only if both expressly wish it.

A bilingual contract, or a French version and another version given at the same time, is also acceptable, provided the French is at least as prominent as the other language and every version is sent by the same means. No party may charge the other for the French version.

The rule doesn't apply to a contract used in relations with persons outside Quebec, which may be drawn up in another language if the parties expressly wish it. That exception applies only to the contract-of-adhesion rule.

Based on: the [Charter of the French language](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/c-11), sections 21 and 21.3 to 21.6, 55 and 91; the Office québécois de la langue française's page on [contracts of adhesion](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/francisation/entreprises/contrats-adhesion.html) (French only); the [*Regulatory Framework for Clinical Research at Santé Québec – McGill University Health Centre: Organization and Good Practices*](https://rimuhc.ca/conduct-research/policies#framework), section 4.1.01.{/* TO CONFIRM (the rimuhc chat; master doc, Decisions for MF): the Framework's link is its planned rimuhc.ca address, to confirm at the rimuhc.ca launch (STYLE-HUB D). */}

## Respect the right to be served in French

The MUHC must use French in an exemplary manner, and everyone has the right to have its health and social services, including medical consultations and reports on a health record, communicate with them in French.

The Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) recognizes the MUHC under the Charter's provision for health and social services institutions that serve mostly people who speak a language other than French. Some recognized bodies may use another language, in addition to French, in their services to the public, their signs and their written communications and documents; everyone keeps the right to be served in French.

Members of professional orders must provide a French copy of any notice, opinion, report or other document they draw up to anyone who is authorized to obtain it and asks for it, without charging for translation.

Based on: the [Charter of the French language](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/c-11), sections 2, 13.1, 29.1 and 30.1, and Schedule I; the OQLF's [list of recognized bodies](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/francisation/admin_publ/organismes-reconnus.aspx) (French only, updated Sept. 29, 2026) and its page on [receiving public services in French](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/francisation/droits_linguistiques/droits/recevoir-services-publics-en-francais.html) (French only, updated Oct. 7, 2026).
