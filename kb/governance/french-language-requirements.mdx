Quebec's [Charter of the French language](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/c-11) sets the language that the MUHC must or may use in its documents and contracts, research documents included. The MUHC is part of Quebec's civil administration under the Charter. Bill 96 amended the Charter in 2022.

## Prepare participant documents in English and French

Unless the [Research Ethics Board (REB)](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) accepts a justification, your consent form, advertisements and other participant information [must be available in English and French](/sops/cr-006#5-3-7). Questionnaires, diaries, phone scripts and other participant materials generally must also be available in both languages.

Once the REB approves the consent form in one language, [have it translated and submit the translation to the REB](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form). [Advertisements](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#have-recruitment-materials-approved) also go to [MUHC Communications](mailto:communications@muhc.mcgill.ca), which approves them in both languages.

When an amendment changes a participant document, translate the approved change and [submit the translation to the REB before you use it](/sops/cr-026#5-3-1), so that both versions stay the same. Using an improper translation, or not using the current consent form, is a deviation. [Report it to the REB](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations).

[The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures recommend a document approval tracking log](/sops/cr-006#5-4-7) to follow each submission, approval and implementation.

## Check which research documents may stay in another language

The Charter lets the MUHC write some documents drawn up or used in research in another language alone. The MUHC must still use only French whenever it considers that possible.

A [regulation of the Minister](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cr/C-11,%20r.%205.1) lists the research documents that may be written in another language alone:
- Documents necessary for a clinical trial, such as the research protocol, the investigator's brochure, the procedure schedule, the imaging acquisition guide and the pharmacy manual.
- Economic and financial documents.
- Information that a participant, or another person contributing to the research, sends to provide information.
- Material used for a survey or statistical survey, such as a questionnaire or an interview form.
- A study and its assessment, and the description of a research project, whatever the language in which the study is conducted, when they need to be referred to in another language.
- Documents attached to an application for authorization or for funding.
- Technical or theoretical documents used in research, such as those for experimental trials, whatever the language in which the research is conducted.

The exception doesn't cover the contracts the MUHC enters into. It doesn't cover the document written to obtain an authorization or funding either. The documents attached to that document are covered.

For questionnaires and other participant materials on this list, the RI-MUHC's [rule on English and French versions](/sops/cr-006#5-3-7) still applies.

## Check the language rules for contracts

The Charter sets different language rules for different contracts.

Contracts the MUHC enters into must be drawn up exclusively in French, because the MUHC is part of Quebec's civil administration. The written documents related to the MUHC's contracts must also be in French. They include the documents sent to the MUHC to enter into a contract and those the parties send each other under it.

A version in another language may be attached when the MUHC enters into a contract in Quebec with, for example:
- A person who doesn't live in Quebec.
- A company that isn't required to register in Quebec's enterprise register and whose head office is in a State where French isn't an official language.

Some other documents related to the MUHC's contracts may also be drawn up in another language alone. One is a document that the MUHC agrees may be drawn up in another language alone, where it is authentic or semi-authentic, or has a legal value that would prevail over that of any French version. Another is a document by which a person or company of the kinds that may receive a version in another language starts the steps toward a contract with the MUHC.{/* Source: the Charter of the French language, s. 21.6, second and third sentences ("The same applies to a written document relating to a contract referred to in section 21 where the agency concerned of the civil administration so agrees and the document is authentic, semi-authentic or has a legal value that would prevail over that of any possible French version. Moreover, a written document sent by a contracting party referred to in subparagraph 1 of the first paragraph of section 21.4 that initiates steps toward the making of a contract referred to in section 21 may be drawn up only in a language other than French."), read on LégisQuébec, Nov. 13, 2026 (evidence/laws-quebec-C-11-excerpt-ss2-13.1-13.2-22-22.5-29.1-29.15-29.16-30.1-55-91-schedI_read-2026-11-13.md). Added Nov. 15, 2026 (fact check facts-21, A5). */}

A contract the MUHC enters into outside Quebec, and the documents related to it, may be drawn up in another language alone.

The Charter also sets a rule for contracts of adhesion, those whose essential clauses one party imposes and the other can't freely negotiate. Since June 1, 2023, every new contract of adhesion must be given to the adhering party in French first. Once the adhering party has received the French version, the parties can choose to be bound by a version in another language only if both expressly wish it.

A contract of adhesion may also be given in either of these forms:
- A bilingual contract.
- A French version and a version in another language, given at the same time.

In both cases, the French must be at least as prominent as the other language, and every version must be sent by the same means. No party may charge the other for the French version.

Contracts of adhesion used in relations with persons outside Quebec are an exception to the French-first rule. They may be drawn up in another language if the parties expressly wish it. The exception covers only the rule for contracts of adhesion.

Every [research agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#the-research-agreements-office) with a third party must be drafted, reviewed and negotiated by the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca). If you are unsure how the Charter's rules apply to your research agreement, including when the MUHC is a party to a contract of adhesion, ask the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).

## Respect the right to be served in French

Everyone has the right to have the MUHC's health and social services communicate with them in French, including in medical consultations and reports on a health record. The MUHC must also use French in an exemplary manner.

The Office québécois de la langue française (OQLF) [recognizes the MUHC](https://www.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/francisation/admin_publ/organismes-reconnus.aspx) under the Charter's provision for health and social services institutions that serve mostly people who speak a language other than French. As a recognized institution, the MUHC may provide its health and social services in the language of its recognition, orally or in writing, as well as in French. Everyone keeps the right to be served in French.

Members of professional orders must provide a French copy of any notice, opinion, report or other document they draw up to anyone who is authorized to obtain it and asks for it, without charging for translation. When the client who called on the professional is a legal person, the client bears the cost of translating a document that another authorized person asks for.
