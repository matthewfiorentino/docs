Health information that the MUHC holds is confidential by law. Quebec's [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1#se:5) (Law 5) allows it to be used or communicated only with the person's express consent or as the act provides. Whenever the research can be done with information that does not identify the person directly, the information must be used or communicated in that form.

Before your study starts, the Research Ethics Board (REB) looks at how the study will protect participants' information, from collection to destruction. When the study needs a privacy impact assessment, the MUHC reviews that protection too.

## Which law applies to the information in your study

Quebec has separate privacy laws, and the one that applies depends on the information and on who holds it.

| Information | Law that governs it |
|---|---|
| Health and social services information held by the MUHC. It is any information that can identify a person, even indirectly, and concerns their health, biological material taken from them during care or the services they received. A name, date of birth, contact information or health insurance number counts too when it appears with such information or is collected when the person registers. | Law 5, the [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1) (R-22.1), most of it [in force since July 2024](https://muhc.ca/news-and-patient-stories/news/put-privacy-first-data-protection-week-2025) |
| Other personal information held by a public body, such as staff records. | The [Act respecting access to documents held by public bodies and the protection of personal information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/A-2.1) (A-2.1) |
| Personal information that a private enterprise in Quebec holds for its own purposes. | The [Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1) (P-39.1) |

Health information that a contractor holds on the MUHC's behalf stays under Law 5. [Law 25](https://www.assnat.qc.ca/en/travaux-parlementaires/projets-loi/projet-loi-64-42-1.html), An Act to modernize legislative provisions as regards the protection of personal information (2021), amended the access act (A-2.1) and the private-sector act (P-39.1). Most of its provisions have been in force since Sept. 22, 2023.

The federal [Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/) (PIPEDA) generally does not apply to hospitals, which provincial law covers, though it may apply in some situations. PIPEDA applies to businesses when personal information crosses provincial or national borders in the course of their commercial activities. The REB also applies [chapter 5 of the *Tri-Council Policy Statement*](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html) (TCPS 2), on privacy and confidentiality.

[Law 5 makes it an offence](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1#se:160) to:
- Access, use or communicate health information in a way the act does not allow.
- Identify or try to identify someone from de-identified data without the MUHC's authorization, or from anonymized data.
- Break a condition of your agreement on how the information may be used, when your access to records was authorized without consent.

For an individual, the fine is $5,000 to $100,000, doubled for a second offence and tripled after that.

## Get authorization before using health records without consent

Before you look at patients' records to find people for your study, or use their records without their consent, the study needs the approval of the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) and MUHC Authorization, which includes access to the records. Being able to see a record as a caregiver does not allow you to use it for research.

A person is attached to the MUHC if they practise their profession in one of its centres, or do research for it under a contract of employment or a contract for services. [Quebec's health ministry](https://www.quebec.ca/sante/professionnels/encadrement-recherche-participants-humains/acces-renseignements-sante-services-sociaux-chercheurs-recherche) gives a research contract, researcher status, research privileges and employment as a researcher as ways to show that link. If you are attached, the steps are these:

1. Ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) to approve the use of the records without consent.
2. In your Nagano submission, [answer the questions on access to participant information](/sops/cr-007#5-4-3). The request for access is part of your initial submission (form F11).
3. Complete the privacy impact assessment, the EFVP (évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée), in French. Use form F17, a short version, for pre-screening, or form F18 for other access without consent. The principal investigator who leads the study at the MUHC signs the EFVP.
4. Once the access is authorized, sign the written agreement with the MUHC that makes the authorization official.
5. Make sure that anyone you retain to help with the project, such as an analyst or a firm, meets the agreement's obligations too.
6. At the end of the period the agreement sets, destroy the information and tell the MUHC that you have.

The REB may waive consent only if your study meets [the conditions in TCPS 2](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html), among them that seeking consent is impossible or impracticable. [Law 5](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1#se:44) requires the request for access to include a detailed description of the project, a privacy impact assessment and the REB's decision.

After the REB approves the use of identifiable information or [human biological materials](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter12-chapitre12.html) without consent, a study may need to contact some of the people concerned, for more information or materials or for reasons related to their welfare. Seek approval of the plan for making contact from the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you contact anyone.

The plan must explain who will contact the people and that person's relationship with them. For a study of human biological materials, the plan should explain the same. Make sure that the plan for follow-up contact complies with applicable privacy law. Any new collection of data or materials from the people you contact needs their consent.

The benefits of the contact must clearly outweigh its risks to the people contacted, and the REB must be satisfied that the plan keeps those risks as low as possible.

The MUHC's [Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) (DIPQ) reviews the EFVP. A planning, programming and research officer in the DIPQ approves it. The EFVP is then sent to the Commission d'accès à l'information (CAI), Quebec's access-to-information commission.

The personne mandatée, the MUHC's authorizing official, gives MUHC Authorization only after the EFVP is approved. MUHC Authorization may be given only if the personne mandatée considers that all of these are true:
- Requiring consent would be unreasonable.
- The project's objective outweighs its impact on privacy, in the public interest.
- The security measures are suitable and meet the information governance rules.
- If information will leave Quebec, the EFVP shows that it will be adequately protected there.

A refusal comes in writing, with reasons.

In the agreement, you agree that the information:
- Will be used only by people who need it for their work and have signed a confidentiality agreement.
- Will be used only for the purposes and data linkages you described.
- Will not be communicated, published or otherwise shared in a form that identifies anyone.

The agreement also sets how long the information is kept, the security measures, what people contacted through their records must be told and your duty to [report problems](#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person). A copy of the agreement goes to the CAI.

The MUHC can revoke your access without delay or formality if it has reason to believe that any of these is the case:
- Generally accepted standards of ethics and scientific integrity are not being followed.
- The security measures are not being followed.
- Another measure in the agreement is not being followed.
- The information's protection is otherwise compromised.

Patients may refuse to let researchers use their information to invite them into a study, and the authorization does not cover their information for that purpose.

Researchers in private companies, researchers outside Quebec and other researchers who are not attached to an institution of Quebec's health and social services network apply instead to Santé Québec's [Research Access Center](https://sante.quebec/en/help-and-resources/centre-dacces-a-la-recherche/) (Centre d'accès pour la recherche). A patient may also refuse, in whole or in part, to let researchers who apply through the Research Access Center see their information.

For information designated under the Act respecting the Institut de la statistique du Québec, researchers attached to a public body within the meaning of that act apply to the Institut instead.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.

## Know when an EFVP is needed

[Whether a study needs an EFVP](/sops/cr-007#5-4-3) depends on how it uses participants' information and where the information goes.

| Situation | EFVP |
|---|---|
| Access to identifiable health information before consent (pre-screening) or without consent | Required |
| Participants' information sent outside Quebec, including to another province (sponsors' or contract research organizations' servers, central labs, cloud platforms, collaborators) | May be required |
| A technology product or service (an app, a wearable device, a data capture system or other software) acquired, developed or altered for the study to collect, keep, use, communicate or destroy participants' information | May be required |
| Records used with the participant's consent, when the information stays in Quebec and no new technology is involved | Not required |
| Anonymized data, which can no longer identify anyone, even indirectly | Not required |

For a multicentre project within Quebec's health network, the lead site must submit one EFVP for the project. Information leaving Quebec is the trigger teams most often miss. If participants' information will leave Quebec, or the study acquires, develops or alters technology to handle it, consider asking [efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) before the protocol is final.

A study may use where people live as a variable, such as their postal codes. If the area it covers includes Eeyou Istchee, Quebec's region 18, the study comes under [the joint review with the Cree Board of Health and Social Services of James Bay (CBHSSJB)](/kb/recruitment/indigenous-research-ethics#check-whether-the-procedure-applies), separately from the EFVP. If you aren't sure whether your study is covered, ask the CBHSSJB's [Research Office](mailto:18tcr.research.committee@ssss.gouv.qc.ca).

## Keep participant information confidential

Before Medical Records Services gives you access to patients' medical records, sign the [MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=105487170812722201) and send it to them. Consider sending it early enough that the whole team can work from the first day of study activation. The MUHC agreement is separate from any confidentiality agreement with the sponsor, which covers the sponsor's proprietary information.

The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) set these rules for participants' information, during the study and when its files are archived:

- Keep the signed consent forms and the participant identification log at the site. Never give the log or the forms to the sponsor, because [external sponsors must not know who the participants are](/sops/cr-009#5-8-6).
- The log links each participant's name, medical record number (MRN) and contact details to their study number. Do not archive the log with the essential documents, and get the sponsor's approval before you destroy it.
- [Participant information should not be saved on electronic shared drives](/sops/cr-015#5-1-13), except temporarily during a transfer.
- [Identify participants by their study number](/sops/cr-014#5-7-4) on case report forms and on anything else sent to the sponsor, unless the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) has approved otherwise. The sponsor's monitor may see identifying information after signing the MUHC confidentiality agreement.
- Keep identifying information inside the institution as far as the law permits, whether in originals or in copies. Let it leave only with the REB's approval and in line with institutional policies, as redacted [certified copies](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#make-a-certified-copy), or when authorized people such as regulators' inspectors require it.
- [Black out identifying information on audit and inspection copies](/sops/cr-017#5-7-6), as required.
- The SOPs recommend [redacting identifying information](/sops/cr-014#5-3-3), such as the name and MRN, writing the study number in its place and having a second team member check the number.
- Keep no identifying information on the validated electronic devices used for the study, such as handheld computers.
- Collect data only for the REB-approved study.
- Pay participants from the study's research account, not directly by the sponsor, so that the sponsor does not have their names. The consent form tells participants that they are paid from the research account.

Medical Records can give monitors [temporary, restricted access](/apps/oacis#requesting-access-for-external-monitors) to participants' records in OACIS, the MUHC's electronic medical record. [Who may see study data, where to store them and how to send them](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security) depends on whether the data are published and on how identifiable they are.

## Report information that is lost or seen by the wrong person

A file emailed to the wrong person, a lost laptop or USB key and data used outside the approved purpose are all confidentiality incidents. [Law 5](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1#se:3) defines a confidentiality incident as any access to, use or communication of information that the law does not allow, any loss of information or any other breach of its protection.

[Report the incident](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) to the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) in Nagano as soon as you become aware of it. As applicable, also report it to the appropriate organizational officials. The REB counts any accidental or intentional re-identification of a participant as a [serious adverse event (SAE)](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#assess-and-report-adverse-events). Report a re-identification to the REB whether it happened at the MUHC or elsewhere.

By signing the MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement, you also agreed to report any improper or illegal activity immediately to your superior or directly to the Security and Confidentiality coordinator. The activity to report includes the use of information assets for abusive or malicious communications.

If your access to records was authorized without consent, your agreement also requires you to inform the MUHC and the [CAI](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/) without delay of:
- Any breach of the agreement's conditions.
- Any failure to follow its security measures.
- Any event that could breach the information's confidentiality.

The MUHC must take reasonable steps to reduce the risk of harm and prevent new incidents. If there is a risk of serious injury, the MUHC must promptly notify Quebec's health minister, the CAI and the people concerned.

## Know who keeps the records after the study

The RI-MUHC's SOPs have Medical Records Services [keep participants' health records](/sops/cr-019#1-2-4) for at least 25 years of inactivity, unless otherwise specified. Your team keeps the study's essential documents for [the period that applies to your study](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).
