Health information that the MUHC holds is confidential by law. Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information (Law 5) allows it to be used or communicated only with the person's express consent or as the act provides, and in a form that does not identify the person directly whenever the research can be done that way.

Before your study starts, the Research Ethics Board (REB) looks at how the study will protect participants' information, from collection to destruction, and so does the MUHC, through a privacy impact assessment, when the study needs one.

## Which law applies to the information in your study

Quebec has separate privacy laws, and the one that applies depends on the information and on who holds it.

| Information | Law that governs it |
|---|---|
| Health and social services information held by the MUHC: anything that can identify a person, even indirectly, and concerns their health, biological material taken from them during care or the services they received. A name, date of birth, contact information or health insurance number counts too when it appears with such information or is collected when the person registers | Law 5, the Act respecting health and social services information (R-22.1), most of it in force since July 2024 |
| Other personal information held by a public body, such as staff records | The Act respecting access to documents held by public bodies and the protection of personal information (A-2.1) |
| Personal information that a private enterprise in Quebec holds for its own purposes | The Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector (P-39.1) |

Health information that a contractor holds on the MUHC's behalf stays under Law 5. Law 25, An Act to modernize legislative provisions as regards the protection of personal information (2021; most provisions in force since Sept. 22, 2023), amended the last two acts. Neither of them applies to health and social services information that the MUHC holds; Law 5 governs it.

The federal Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) generally does not apply to hospitals, which provincial law covers, though it may apply in some situations. It does apply to businesses when personal information crosses provincial or national borders in the course of their commercial activities. The REB also applies chapter 5 of the *Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2), on privacy and confidentiality.

Law 5 makes it an offence to access, use or communicate health information in a way the act does not allow; to identify, or try to identify, someone from de-identified data without the MUHC's authorization or from anonymized data; or, when your access to records was authorized without consent, to break a condition of your agreement on how the information may be used.

For an individual, the fine is $5,000 to $100,000, doubled for a second offence and tripled after that.

Based on: the [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1), sections 2, 4, 5, 160 and 161; the [Act respecting access to documents held by public bodies and the protection of personal information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/A-2.1), sections 2 and 3; the [Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1), sections 1 and 3; the National Assembly's [page on Bill 64](https://www.assnat.qc.ca/en/travaux-parlementaires/projets-loi/projet-loi-64-42-1.html) (Law 25); the MUHC's [Data Protection Week notice](https://muhc.ca/news-and-patient-stories/news/put-privacy-first-data-protection-week-2025) (Jan. 27, 2025); the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada's [PIPEDA requirements in brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/); [TCPS 2, chapter 5](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html).

## Get authorization before using health records without consent

Looking at patients' records to find people for your study, or using their records without their consent, needs the REB's approval and MUHC Authorization, including access to the records, before you look at any record. Being able to see a record as a caregiver does not allow you to use it for research.

You are attached to the MUHC if you practise your profession in one of its centres, or do research for it under a contract of employment or a contract for services. Quebec's health ministry gives a research contract, researcher status, research privileges and employment as a researcher as ways to show that link. If you are attached, the steps are these:

1. Ask the REB to approve the use of the records without consent. The REB may waive consent only if your study meets the conditions in TCPS 2, among them that seeking consent is impossible or impracticable; see [adapting the consent process](/kb/recruitment/consent#adapt-the-consent-process-when-needed).
2. In your Nagano submission, answer the questions on access to participant information. The request for access is part of your initial submission (form F11). Law 5 requires the request to include a detailed description of the project, a privacy impact assessment and the REB's decision.
3. Complete the EFVP (évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée), a privacy impact assessment, in French: form F17, a short version, for pre-screening, or form F18 for other access without consent. The principal investigator (who leads the study at the MUHC) signs it.
4. The EFVP is reviewed in the MUHC's Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality (DIPQ), where the access-to-information senior advisor also works, and approved there by a planning, programming and research officer. It is then sent to the Commission d'accès à l'information (CAI), Quebec's access-to-information commission.
5. Wait for MUHC Authorization. The personne mandatée, the MUHC's authorizing official, gives it after the EFVP is approved, and it may be given only if requiring consent would be unreasonable, the project's objective outweighs its impact on privacy in the public interest, the security measures are suitable and meet the information governance rules and, if information will leave Quebec, the EFVP shows it will be adequately protected there. A refusal comes in writing, with reasons.
6. Sign the written agreement that makes the authorization official. In it, you agree that only people who need the information for their work and have signed a confidentiality agreement will use it, only for the purposes and data linkages you described, and that it will not be communicated, published or otherwise shared in a form that identifies anyone.
7. The agreement also sets how long the information is kept, the security measures, what people contacted through their records must be told, and your duty to report problems ([Report information that is lost or seen by the wrong person](#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person)). A copy goes to the CAI.
8. Make sure that anyone you retain to help with the project, such as an analyst or a firm, meets the agreement's obligations too. The MUHC can revoke your access without delay if it has reason to believe that generally accepted standards of ethics and scientific integrity, the security measures or any other measure in the agreement are not being followed, or that the information's protection is otherwise compromised.
9. At the end of the period the agreement sets, destroy the information and tell the MUHC that you have.

Patients may refuse to let researchers use their information to invite them into a study, and the authorization does not cover their information for that purpose.

Researchers who are not attached to an institution in this way, such as researchers in private companies or outside Quebec, apply to Santé Québec's [Research Access Center](https://sante.quebec/en/help-and-resources/centre-dacces-a-la-recherche/) (Centre d'accès pour la recherche) instead, and patients may refuse, in whole or in part, to let such researchers see their information.

For information designated under the Act respecting the Institut de la statistique du Québec, researchers attached to a public body within the meaning of that act apply to the Institut instead.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

## Know when an EFVP is needed

| Situation | EFVP |
|---|---|
| Access to identifiable health information before consent (pre-screening) or without consent | Required |
| Participants' information sent outside Quebec, including to another province (sponsors' or contract research organizations' servers, central labs, cloud platforms, collaborators) | May be required |
| A technology product or service (an app, a wearable device, a data capture system or other software) acquired, developed or altered for the study to collect, keep, use, communicate or destroy participants' information | May be required |
| Records used with the participant's consent, when the information stays in Quebec and no new technology is involved | Not required |
| Anonymized data, which can no longer identify anyone, even indirectly | Not required |

A multicentre project within Quebec's health network files one EFVP for the project, from the lead site. Information leaving Quebec is the trigger teams most often miss. If participants' information will leave Quebec, or the study acquires, develops or alters technology to handle it, ask [efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) before the protocol is final.

## Keep participant information confidential

Before Medical Records Services gives you access to patients' medical records, sign the [MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=105487170812722201) (sign-in required) and send it to them, early enough that the whole team can work from the first day of study activation. It is separate from any confidentiality agreement with the sponsor, which covers the sponsor's proprietary information.

The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) set these rules for the rest of the study:

- Keep the participant identification log, which links each participant's name, medical record number (MRN) and contact details to their study number, and the signed consent forms at the site. Never give them to the sponsor. Do not archive the log with the essential documents, and get the sponsor's approval before you destroy it.
- Participant information should not be saved on electronic shared drives, except temporarily during a transfer.
- Identify participants by their study number on case report forms and on anything else sent to the sponsor, unless the REB has approved otherwise. The sponsor's monitor may see identifying information after signing the MUHC confidentiality agreement.
- Keep identifying information, originals and copies, inside the institution as far as the law permits. Let it leave only with the REB's approval and in line with institutional policies, as redacted [certified copies](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#make-a-certified-copy), or when authorized people such as regulators' inspectors require it, with identifying information redacted on their copies as required. The SOPs recommend redacting identifying information, such as the name and MRN, writing the study number in its place and having a second team member check the number.
- Keep no identifying information on the validated electronic devices used for the study, such as handheld computers.
- Collect data only for the REB-approved study.
- Pay participants from the study's research account, not directly by the sponsor, so that the sponsor does not have their names. The consent form tells participants this.

Medical Records can give monitors temporary, restricted access to participants' records; the steps are on the page on [OACIS](/apps/oacis#requesting-access-for-external-monitors), the MUHC's electronic medical record. Who may see study data, where to store them and how to send them are on [Storing and Sharing Human Data](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security).

## Report information that is lost or seen by the wrong person

A confidentiality incident is any access to, use or communication of information that the law does not allow, any loss of information, or any other breach of its protection, such as a file emailed to the wrong person, a lost laptop or USB key or data used outside the approved purpose.

Report it to the REB in Nagano as soon as you are aware of it, and, as applicable, to the appropriate organizational officials. The REB treats any re-identification of a participant, accidental or deliberate, as a serious adverse event (SAE).

By signing the MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement, you also agreed to report any improper or illegal activity, including the use of information assets for abusive or malicious communications, immediately to your superior or directly to the Security and Confidentiality coordinator.

If your access to records was authorized without consent, your agreement also requires you to inform the MUHC and the CAI without delay of any breach of its conditions or security measures, and of any event that could breach the information's confidentiality.

The MUHC must take reasonable steps to reduce the risk of harm and prevent new incidents and, if there is a risk of serious injury, promptly notify the Minister, the CAI and the people concerned.

Based on: the MUHC REB's [SOP 405A.001-2](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops), section 5.2.4; [SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019), Appendix 1; the [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1), sections 3, 48 and 108.

## Know who keeps the records after the study

The RI-MUHC's SOPs have Medical Records Services keep participants' health records for at least 25 years of inactivity, unless otherwise specified. Your team keeps the study's essential documents for the period that applies to your study; see [how long study records are kept](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).
