In GCP, quality assurance is the planned, systematic actions that ensure a trial complies with GCP and the applicable regulatory requirements." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">Quality Assurance</Tooltip> (QA) checks the credentials and training of each investigator who submits a study and audits how research is done at the MUHC and the RI-MUHC. It also helps teams through inspections and keeps the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) and their training current. You deal with [QA](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) when you submit a study, when a major or critical deviation needs corrective and preventive action and when an inspector announces a visit.

## Pass the QA check when you submit a study

When you submit a study in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>, [QA checks](/sops/cr-007#5-4-2) that each investigator who submits it holds valid research privileges or researcher status and the RI-MUHC's institutional training at the [level the study requires](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level). QA's check is part of the [institutional feasibility review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#institutional-feasibility-review).

Until your privileges or researcher status and your training meet the requirement, that part of the review isn't approved, and the <Tooltip tip="The person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research: the Director of Innovation, Performance and Quality, who issues MUHC Authorization for each study." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> will not issue <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>. Depending on the type of study, you may be required to renew your institutional training or upgrade to a higher level.

For a study you sponsor yourself, QA also checks that you have a trial master file suited to the start of the study.

## What QA does while your study runs

QA conducts [process audits](/sops/cr-017#5-4-1) across the MUHC and the RI-MUHC to assess where processes need improving, so that SOPs can be revised, tools and templates created for the research community and SOP training improved.

QA may also conduct for-cause audits or investigations when non-compliance is reported. The audit report goes to the RI-MUHC's upper management. A principal investigator, the researcher who leads a study at the MUHC, can also ask [QA](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) to audit the study.

{/* Sources: [SOP-CR-017, sections 5.4.1 to 5.4.3](/sops/cr-017#5-4-1) (5.4.1: process audits "in order to assess the need for process improvement, facilitating SOP revisions and creation of tools/templates for the human research community as well as the resulting improved SOP training"; 5.4.2: "QA may also conduct for-cause audits or investigations when there is reported non-compliance. The resulting audit report is reported to RI-MUHC upper management"; 5.4.3: "a QI/PI may request that an audit be conducted by QA"). */}

## Meet your quality duties during the study

The MUHC sets quality duties for principal investigators in its [*Regulatory Framework for Clinical Research at Santé Québec – McGill University Health Centre: Organization and Good Practices*](https://rimuhc.ca/conduct-research/policies#framework). As a principal investigator, you must:
- Allow and facilitate audits and inspections.
- Cooperate with QA, Health Canada, the Research Ethics Board and sponsors' monitors.
- Put corrective and preventive actions in place when they are required.
- Maintain the integrity of the research.
{/* TO CONFIRM (the rimuhc chat; master doc, Decisions for MF): the Framework's link is its planned rimuhc.ca address, to confirm at the rimuhc.ca launch (STYLE-HUB D). */}

Your study's essential documents [must be available for audit](/sops/cr-015#5-5-1) by QA and by the sponsor's auditor, and for inspection by regulators such as Health Canada. A sponsor can also [audit your study](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#sponsor-audits).

As an investigator, make sure each member of your team has appropriate documented training before you delegate study tasks to them, that is, before they sign the study's Task Delegation Log. Team members should hold the study's training level, or a higher one, before signing the log.

The [corrective and preventive action (CAPA) form](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management#working-through-a-capa) for a major or critical deviation should be filed in the investigator site file. A copy should also be sent to QA, which tracks trends across studies to improve processes.

If your study relies on a vendor, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator is responsible for ensuring that the duties the vendor performs on its behalf are specified in writing and overseen. You can ask [QA](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) to help assess and qualify a vendor.

Other parties may generate study data, such as a testing facility, the pharmacy or radiology. [The investigator should make sure that each party is qualified](/sops/cr-002#5-5-1) to perform the study tasks it carries out. The investigator should also put procedures in place to protect the integrity of the tasks and of the data.

## Tell QA as soon as an inspection is announced

If a regulator such as Health Canada or the US Food and Drug Administration announces an inspection of your study, or an inspector arrives with little or no notice, [inform QA immediately, as required](/sops/cr-017#5-1-4). For a Health Canada inspection, you should tell [QA](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) promptly, [within the deadline counted from the notice of inspection](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/hc-inspection-guide#when-you-receive-the-notice). QA helps you prepare for and host a Health Canada inspection, as required.

## Help keep the SOPs current

If your group sees the need for a new or revised procedure, you should tell [QA](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca).

QA writes the RI-MUHC's two sets of SOPs, or coordinates their writing, and keeps improving them. The high-risk series has codes starting with SOP-CR, and the low-risk series has codes starting with SOP-LR.

QA also runs the process that creates, documents and maintains the RI-MUHC's institutional training on the conduct of human research. That training runs on the TalentLMS, CITI and N2 (Network of Networks) platforms. QA provides the content of the [SOP Reader and Assessment](/training/compliance-requirements#sop-reader-and-assessment), the RI-MUHC's SOP training on TalentLMS. The RI-MUHC's [Training Advisor](mailto:training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca) manages TalentLMS.

{/* Sources: [SOP-CR-001, section 5.1.2](/sops/cr-001#5-1-2) ("The need for a revision to the process in question should be communicated to the QA department"); [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003), section 3 (QA's draft SOP-CR-003_11, accepted text: QA writes or coordinates the writing of "two sets of RI-MUHC SOPs – a high-risk SOP-CR series and a low-risk SOP-LR series" and their continuous improvement; the institutional training process through TalentLMS, CITI and N2; the quiz content; the Training Advisor manages TalentLMS, training-formation@muhc.mcgill.ca). */}

## Register an investigator-initiated trial on ClinicalTrials.gov

The MUHC has an organization account on ClinicalTrials.gov for sponsor-investigators of investigator-initiated trials. To [register your trial](/kb/close-out/publications#trial-registration) there, ask [QA](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) to request the opening of your account. QA makes the request only for sponsor-investigators who hold valid research privileges or researcher status. You remain the responsible party for your study in the registry. One account covers all your studies.

When you create your account, enter "MUHC-RIMUHC" as the "Sponsor" and choose "Principal Investigator" as the "Responsible Party", for insurance purposes. A section then opens where you enter your name. If you choose "Sponsor-Investigator" instead, the registry also enters you as the sponsor, which isn't recommended unless your research is covered by your private insurance.

{/* Sources: [SOP-CR-007, section 5.8.4](/sops/cr-007#5-8-4) ("The Sponsor-Investigator is the responsible party for their study in this registry. The QA department's involvement is solely to request Sponsor-Investigator's account opening for those with valid RI-MUHC Privileges/Researcher Status"; "For insurance purposes … ensure that the MUHC-RIMUHC is indicated as the 'Sponsor' and ensure to select 'Principal Investigator' for 'Responsible Party'"; its Notes: "Only one clinicaltrial.gov account per Sponsor-Investigator is opened for all their studies"). */}
