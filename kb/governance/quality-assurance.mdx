You deal with QA when you submit a study, when a major or critical deviation needs corrective and preventive action and when an inspector announces a visit.

## Pass the QA check when you submit a study

When you submit a study in <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>, QA checks, as part of the institutional feasibility review, that each investigator who submits it holds valid research privileges or researcher status and the RI-MUHC's institutional training at the level the study requires. Check the [training each level requires](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level).

Until your privileges or researcher status and your training meet the requirement, that part of the review isn't approved, and the <Tooltip tip="The person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research: the Director of Innovation, Performance and Quality, who issues MUHC Authorization for each study." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip>, the person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research, will not issue <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>. Depending on the type of study, you may be required to renew your institutional training or upgrade to a higher level.

For a study you sponsor yourself, QA also checks that you have a trial master file suited to the start of the study.

## What QA does while your study runs

QA conducts process audits across the MUHC and the RI-MUHC to find where processes need improving, which helps revise SOPs, create tools and templates and improve SOP training. It may also conduct for-cause audits or investigations when non-compliance is reported; the audit report goes to the RI-MUHC's upper management. A principal investigator, the researcher who leads a study at the MUHC, can also ask QA to audit the study.

Good clinical practice (GCP) builds quality into a trial's design and monitoring; [GCP and Quality by Design](/kb/foundations/gcp-quality-by-design) explains how.

Based on: [SOP-CR-017, sections 5.4.1 to 5.4.3](/sops/cr-017#5-4-1).

## Meet your quality duties during the study

As a principal investigator, you must allow and facilitate audits and inspections, cooperate with QA, Health Canada, the Research Ethics Board and sponsors' monitors, and put corrective and preventive actions in place when they are required. Your study's essential documents must be available for audit by QA and by the sponsor's auditor, and for inspection by regulators such as Health Canada.

As an investigator, you are responsible for making sure that each member of your team has appropriate documented training before you delegate study tasks to them, that is, before the member signs the study's Task Delegation Log. Team members should hold the study's training level, or a higher one, before signing it.

The corrective and preventive action (CAPA) form for a major or critical deviation should be filed in the investigator site file, with a copy sent to QA, which tracks trends across studies to improve processes. [Deviation Management and CAPA](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management) explains how to prepare one, and [Monitoring, Audits, and Inspections](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits) covers the audits sponsors run.

If your study relies on a vendor, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator is responsible for ensuring that the duties the vendor performs on its behalf are specified in writing and overseen. You can ask QA to help assess and qualify a vendor.

## Tell QA as soon as an inspection is announced

If Health Canada or another regulator, such as the US Food and Drug Administration, announces an inspection of your study, you should tell QA promptly. For a Health Canada inspection, QA helps you prepare for and host it; [Hosting a Health Canada Inspection](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/hc-inspection-guide) gives the deadline for telling QA and the steps.

Based on: [SOP-CR-017, sections 5.1.2](/sops/cr-017#5-1-2), [5.1.4](/sops/cr-017#5-1-4) and [5.5.4](/sops/cr-017#5-5-4).

## Help keep the SOPs current

If your group sees the need for a new or revised procedure, you should tell QA.

QA writes, or coordinates the writing of, the RI-MUHC's two sets of SOPs, a high-risk series (codes starting with SOP-CR) and a low-risk series (codes starting with SOP-LR), and keeps improving them.

QA also runs the process by which the RI-MUHC's institutional training on the conduct of human research is created, documented and maintained, through the TalentLMS, CITI and N2 (Network of Networks) platforms. It provides the content of the [SOP Reader and Assessment](/training/compliance-requirements#sop-reader-and-assessment), the RI-MUHC's SOP training on TalentLMS, a platform the RI-MUHC's Training Advisor manages.

Based on: [SOP-CR-001, section 5.1.2](/sops/cr-001#5-1-2); [SOP-CR-003](/sops/cr-003), section 3.

## Register an investigator-initiated trial on ClinicalTrials.gov

The MUHC has an organization account on ClinicalTrials.gov for sponsor-investigators of investigator-initiated trials. To register your trial there, ask QA, which requests the opening of your account; you need valid research privileges or researcher status, and you remain the responsible party for your study in the registry. One account covers all your studies.

When you create your account, for insurance purposes, enter "MUHC-RIMUHC" as the "Sponsor" and choose "Principal Investigator" as the "Responsible Party"; you then enter your name in the section that opens. If you choose "Sponsor-Investigator" instead, the registry also enters you as the sponsor, which isn't recommended unless your research is covered by your private insurance.

Check [when a trial must be registered](/kb/close-out/publications).

Based on: [SOP-CR-007, section 5.8.4](/sops/cr-007#5-8-4).
