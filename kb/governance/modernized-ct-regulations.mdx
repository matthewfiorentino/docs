In December 2025, Health Canada published proposed new clinical trial regulations in [Canada Gazette Part I, Vol. 159, No. 51](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2025/2025-12-20/html/reg2-eng.html) — the most significant overhaul of the Canadian clinical trial regulatory framework in over two decades. The proposals follow a multi-year consultation process and are expected to finalize in late 2026 or early 2027, with a transitional period before coming into force.

This page summarizes what is proposed, what it means for RI-MUHC investigators, and what — if anything — you should be doing now.

## What the proposed regulations change

The current framework, anchored in Division 5 of the *Food and Drug Regulations*, is organized around individual products and single authorization events. A CTA is filed for a specific drug in a specific indication; Health Canada reviews it; a no-objection letter (NOL) issues. The proposed framework replaces this product-centric model with a **lifecycle authorization** approach.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Lifecycle authorization model">
    Under the proposed framework, a clinical trial program — not just a single trial — can be authorized within a defined scope. This allows protocol amendments, adaptive modifications, and new study phases to proceed without a full re-authorization, provided they remain within the approved scope and risk tier.

    For industry sponsors, this reduces the regulatory overhead for complex adaptive or platform trial designs. For investigator-initiated studies, the practical impact depends on how Health Canada defines the authorization scope thresholds — transitional guidance will be critical.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Risk-tiered oversight">
    The proposed framework formalizes risk tiering: trials are assessed against a defined set of risk factors (participant population, investigational product characteristics, evidence base, trial design complexity) and assigned to an oversight tier that determines review timelines, reporting requirements, and monitoring expectations.

    This aligns with the risk-proportionate approach already embedded in [ICH E6(R3)](/kb/gcp-quality-by-design). Trials classified at lower risk tiers may face reduced review timelines and lighter ongoing reporting obligations; higher-risk trials attract more intensive oversight. The tiering criteria have not yet been finalized.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Expanded scope — natural health products and other categories">
    The current Division 5 framework applies primarily to drugs and biologics. The proposed regulations extend the clinical trial framework to additional product categories, including a defined set of natural health products. Separate transitional provisions will cover products previously regulated under different pathways.

    Medical device clinical investigations continue under the Medical Devices Regulations (ISO 14155:2020 as the recognized standard). The proposed regulations do not alter the device ITA pathway.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Explicit accommodation of DCTs and adaptive designs">
    The proposed regulations explicitly recognize decentralized trial elements (remote consent, DTP IP shipping, remote monitoring) and adaptive designs (sample size re-estimation, response-adaptive randomization) within the authorization framework. This removes the current ambiguity under Division 5, where these elements must be addressed through protocol justification without a specific regulatory hook.

    The practical implications for Quebec-specific DCT requirements (OPQ pharmacist obligations, Law 25 PIA) are not addressed in the federal proposal. See [Decentralized Trials](/kb/decentralized-trials) for the current operational framework.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Changes to NOL, CTA-A, and ITA pathways">
    The current 30-day default review clock and the CTA amendment (CTA-A) process are expected to be restructured under tiered review. The specific timelines for each tier have not been finalized. The NOL as currently issued is likely to be replaced by a tier-specific authorization document.

    For ITA submissions (medical device investigations), Health Canada has separately indicated intent to introduce an independent-investigator ITA pathway — currently not available, as the Medical Devices Regulations require the manufacturer to be the applicant. This amendment has not yet been enacted and is tracked separately.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Timeline

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="December 20, 2025 — Canada Gazette Part I publication">
    Proposed regulations published for public consultation. This opened the formal comment period. Text available at [Canada Gazette Part I, Vol. 159, No. 51](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2025/2025-12-20/html/reg2-eng.html).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="March–April 2026 — Consultation period closed">
    The consultation period has closed. The original close date was March 20, 2026; it may have been extended to April 2026. Health Canada is now expected to review feedback before proceeding to Canada Gazette Part II. See the [Health Canada consultation page](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/programs/consultation-modernizing-framework-clinical-trials.html) for any updates.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="April 1, 2026 — ICH E6(R3) effective in Canada">
    This is an independent milestone, not part of the Modernized CT Regulations. E6(R3) is already in effect as of this date under the updated GUI-0100. It is relevant here because the proposed regulations and E6(R3) are designed to align — the risk-proportionate oversight model in the new regulations directly mirrors E6(R3)'s quality-by-design framework. See [GCP and Quality by Design](/kb/gcp-quality-by-design).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Late 2026 / Early 2027 — Canada Gazette Part II (expected)">
    Final regulations are expected to be published in Canada Gazette Part II once consultation feedback is incorporated. This triggers the transitional period. The gap between CG Part I and CG Part II has historically ranged from several months to over a year for complex regulatory reforms.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="TBD — Coming into force + transitional period">
    The final regulations will specify a coming-into-force date and transitional provisions for studies already authorized under the current framework. Ongoing studies authorized under Division 5 will need to confirm whether they must transition or may complete under the existing authorization. Monitor Health Canada and RI-MUHC QA communications for transitional guidance.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How this interacts with ICH E6(R3)

The proposed regulations and ICH E6(R3) are aligned in design intent — both shift from prescriptive compliance to risk-proportionate quality management. E6(R3) is already operative (April 1, 2026); the new CT Regulations will, when finalized, provide the regulatory authorization framework that sits above E6(R3)'s operational requirements.

In practical terms: studies initiated under the current framework should implement E6(R3) now (it is already required). When the new regulations come into force, the authorization pathway and ongoing oversight requirements will change, but the quality management obligations under E6(R3) will remain.

## What investigators should do now

<Steps>
  <Step title="No submission changes required">
    All current CTA, CTA-A, ITA, and NOL processes remain operative. Do not wait for the new regulations before submitting or amending. The proposed framework is not in force.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Implement ICH E6(R3) now">
    E6(R3) is already in effect as of April 1, 2026. This is separate from the proposed CT Regulations. Review the [GCP and Quality by Design](/kb/gcp-quality-by-design) page and confirm your study's quality management approach aligns with E6(R3) requirements.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monitor Health Canada and RI-MUHC QA communications">
    Health Canada will publish transitional provisions and implementation guidance. RI-MUHC QA will issue site-level guidance when the final regulations are close to in force. Subscribe to Health Canada drug and device regulatory updates.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Raise early-phase design questions with [Research Facilitator](mailto:Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca)">
    If you are planning a new investigator-initiated or platform trial expected to launch in 2027 or later, raise protocol design questions with the [Research Facilitator](mailto:Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) now. The new risk-tiered framework may affect authorization strategy, particularly for adaptive or decentralized designs.
  </Step>
</Steps>
