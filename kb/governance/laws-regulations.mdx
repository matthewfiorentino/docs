## Follow good clinical practice

Health Canada requires the sponsor of a drug or natural health product trial to ensure that it is conducted in accordance with good clinical practices. For drug trials, Health Canada implemented [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) on April 1, 2026, and its Annex 2, on decentralized elements, pragmatic elements and the use of real-world data, on Nov. 4, 2026.

ICH E6(R3) applies to interventional clinical trials of investigational products, a term it uses for drugs, medicines, medicinal products, vaccines and biological products, when the trials are intended to be submitted to regulatory authorities. Its principles may also apply, in accordance with local requirements, to other interventional trials of these products that are not intended to support a marketing authorization application.

In ICH E6(R3), the International Council for Harmonisation sets out the principles of good clinical practice and the responsibilities of ethics committees, investigators and sponsors. It encourages a risk-based and proportionate approach to the conduct of a trial, with [quality by design](/kb/foundations/gcp-quality-by-design) to identify the data and processes on which the trial's quality depends.

For a natural health product trial that you initiate, the RI-MUHC's SOPs require compliance with the Natural Health Products Regulations, ICH E6 good clinical practice and provincial and local regulations.

For device trials, Health Canada expects manufacturers to follow the principles of the *Declaration of Helsinki* and TCPS 2 and to conform to good clinical practice as set out in ISO 14155. It considers ISO 14155 generally consistent with the Medical Devices Regulations; where the two are inconsistent, the Regulations take precedence.

Because ISO 14155's good clinical practice requirements are very similar to those of ICH E6, the RI-MUHC's SOPs accept [ICH GCP training](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) in place of ISO 14155 training when a sponsor or sponsor-investigator can't provide it. The current edition is ISO 14155:2026. Health Canada recognizes it and, for three years from its publication in March 2026, the 2020 edition, unless the new edition sets its own transition period.

The RI-MUHC's SOPs require device research to comply with the Medical Devices Regulations, and it should follow ISO 14155. For a device trial that you initiate, other than one with an in vitro diagnostic device, the SOPs require its investigational testing to be conducted in compliance with ISO 14155 (the SOP cites the 2011 edition).

If you initiate a drug trial as its sponsor-investigator, the [same Division 5 requirements apply to you as to a commercial sponsor](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator). The [training you need](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level), good clinical practice included, depends on the level of the studies you work on.

## Check whether your study needs an authorization from Health Canada

Your study cannot begin until Health Canada's authorization, [where it needs one](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization), has been obtained. For a trial that you initiate, the RI-MUHC's SOPs set the steps of the application in [SOP-CR-018](/sops/cr-018) for a drug, [SOP-CR-023](/sops/cr-023) for a natural health product and [SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024) for a device, in vitro diagnostic devices excluded.

Health Canada explains how to apply in its guidance on [clinical trials of drugs](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/clinical-trials.html) and [clinical trials of natural health products](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/clinical-trials.html). It also sets [deadlines for reporting safety events](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines) during a trial.

### Check whether a device study needs an investigational testing authorization

Health Canada's <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)</Tooltip> lets a manufacturer or importer sell or import a Class II, III or IV medical device for investigational testing in human participants. A Class II, III or IV device needs an ITA when it is not licensed in Canada, or when a licensed device is used outside its licensed indications in a study that the manufacturer sponsors or that is meant to support a licence application.

The Medical Devices Regulations class devices from Class I, the lowest risk, to Class IV, the highest, by the rules in their Schedule 1. A device's class depends on its labelling and on how the study uses it. Class I devices need no ITA, but the manufacturer or importer must still hold the records that Health Canada requires, and the REB's approval and the MUHC's requirements still apply.

Health Canada's [classification guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/guidance-document-guidance-risk-based-classification-system-non-vitro-diagnostic.html) explains the rules, with examples such as these. An example doesn't mean that every device of its type is in that class; the manufacturer is responsible for classifying its device by its characteristics and intended purposes.

| Class | Examples named in Health Canada's guidance |
|---|---|
| Class I | Gauze bandages; mechanical and powered wheelchairs |
| Class II | Surgical gloves; air conduction hearing aids; diagnostic dental radiographic (x-ray) units |
| Class III | Shoulder prostheses; computed tomography (CT) x-ray scanners; high-energy direct current defibrillators that are not closed-loop |
| Class IV | Mechanical heart valves; implantable rate-responsive pacemakers; automatic implantable cardioverter defibrillators |

No ITA is needed for:

- A study that uses a licensed device within its licensed indications. If the study is meant to generate data for a licence application, the manufacturer should consult Health Canada's Investigational Testing division.
- A study that the manufacturer does not sponsor and that uses a licensed device outside its indications without aiming to support a licence application (off-label use).
- Use of a device by the physician who is its legal manufacturer, at one site and only on that physician's own patients, when no one else conducts the testing.

Some devices that physicians make and use may be considered covered by their scope of practice. Before testing one, the RI-MUHC's SOPs strongly recommend asking Health Canada's head of investigational testing for advice.

Health Canada's review target for a new ITA application or a request for a revised ITA, other than for a combination product, is 30 calendar days, counted from when screening begins or, after a screening deficiency letter, from when Health Canada receives a complete application. For minor administrative revisions to an ITA, the review target is 15 calendar days. Both are estimated targets, and no authorization follows automatically when one passes.

In a device trial that you initiate and that needs an ITA, other than one with an in vitro diagnostic device, do not begin the study before Health Canada's written authorization is received.

Health Canada explains how to apply for an ITA in its [guidance on investigational testing authorizations](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/investigational-testing-authorizations-guidance/guidance-document.html) and has [separate guidance for in vitro diagnostic devices](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/cover-letter-application-investigational-vitro-diagnostics-devices.html). If you are unsure whether your study needs an ITA, ask Health Canada's Investigational Testing division at [it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca](mailto:it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca).

## Apply Quebec rules at the MUHC

Health and social services information held by the MUHC falls under Law 5, not Law 25. Law 5 also sets the conditions for using health records without consent, including [a privacy impact assessment (EFVP, for évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée) and MUHC Authorization](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent).

Quebec's health ministry, the Ministère de la Santé, de la Prévention et des Aînés, requires each establishment in its network that conducts research to establish a regulatory framework for that research, adopted by the establishment's board of directors and complying with all applicable laws, regulations and standards. In applying the ministry's standards, establishments must also take into account the standards of research ethics and responsible conduct of research recognized nationally and internationally, such as those of Quebec's and Canada's research funding agencies, but Quebec's legal requirements and standards prevail.

The MUHC, an establishment of Santé Québec since Dec. 1, 2024, adopted its [*Regulatory Framework*](/kb/governance/institutional-structure) to meet this requirement.

## Know what Health Canada is changing

Until Health Canada's proposed Clinical Trials Regulations come into force, apply for and amend authorizations under the current regulations. Health Canada published the proposal for consultation in the [*Canada Gazette*, Part I](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2025/2025-12-20/html/reg2-eng.html), on Dec. 20, 2025, and the [consultation](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/programs/consultation-modernizing-framework-clinical-trials.html) ran until April 19, 2026.

As published, the proposal would:

- Apply only to clinical trials of drugs, leaving natural health product trials and device investigations under their current regulations.
- Regulate the conduct of drug trials directly, with risk-based oversight throughout a trial's life cycle; the current regulations oversee trials indirectly, through the sale and import of the drugs tested.
- Have Health Canada issue a contingent authorization within 7 days of a complete application. It would become an authorization after 30 days unless Health Canada notified the sponsor in writing, within that time, that the application was incomplete, that requested information was missing or that there were reasonable grounds not to authorize the trial. Health Canada could confirm it sooner, or extend the 30 days to 60 for a complex trial or where protecting a vulnerability of the study population needs further assessment.
- Cover a trial with one or more drugs, including a master protocol with sub-studies, under a single authorization, with an amendment needed for changes such as adding a sub-study.
- Exempt from authorization a trial that uses only authorized drugs, as authorized, though its sponsor would still have to comply with certain of the proposed requirements, such as those on good clinical practices.
- Accept documented consent, given orally or with an electronic signature, let a broader range of regulated health professionals act as investigators, and count remote locations as part of a trial site.
- Treat the sponsor of an ongoing trial authorized under the current drug regulations, or under the COVID-19 clinical trial regulations, as holding an authorization under the new ones, unless the trial uses only authorized drugs as authorized.

In its [report on the consultation](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/programs/consultation-modernizing-framework-clinical-trials/what-we-heard.html), Health Canada said it expects to publish the final regulations in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II, in spring 2027. It anticipates that they will come into force in spring 2028, 12 months after that publication, as the proposal provides.

Meanwhile, through an [interim policy](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/announcements/interim-policy-modernization-clinical-trial-framework-notice.html) issued on Sept. 14, 2026, Health Canada applies these interpretations to drug trials, in accordance with good clinical practices, until the new regulations come into force:

- The sponsor of a multi-site trial whose sites share one REB needs to give Health Canada that board's contact information only once, as long as it stays up to date, and must keep the records of every site's approval.
- People who are not physicians or dentists may act as the qualified investigator, the person responsible to the sponsor for the trial at a site, if their education, training, experience and scope of practice suit the trial. Medical decisions about the trial outside their licensed scope of practice must then be made by a qualified physician or dentist on the team, licensed in the site's province or territory and responsible for medical oversight and participant care.
- Consent may be documented in new ways, such as real-time audio and video consent discussions and electronic signatures, when the process is appropriately documented, complies with good clinical practices and ICH E6 principles, and ensures that consent is informed and given as [the laws that govern consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-remotely-or-electronically) require, including the Civil Code's rule on written consent.

None of these interpretations changes the sponsor's responsibility to ensure that the trial is conducted in accordance with good clinical practices and all applicable ethical, legal and institutional requirements, including valid REB approval for each participating site before activities start at that site.

Health Canada has also issued guidance for drug trials with [decentralized elements](/kb/conduct/study-operations/decentralized-trials).

## Follow the rules of other countries

If your study takes place wholly or partly in another country, that country's rules apply too, such as its requirements for regulatory submissions, importing study drugs or devices, safety reporting and documentation. Studies that MUHC personnel conduct in other countries using their MUHC affiliation must also be reviewed and approved by the MUHC REB before they start.

In the United States, a study that falls under the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) may need an Investigational New Drug application (IND) or an Investigational Device Exemption (IDE), and the FDA's rules on electronic records and electronic signatures, in Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations, may apply to [the electronic systems a study uses](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#check-electronic-systems-before-you-use-them).

A trial conducted in Canada stays under Canadian law and Health Canada's regulations even when a sponsor also runs it under a US IND or IDE. MUHC and RI-MUHC investigators are not to sign [Form FDA 1572](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#the-qiu-and-the-fda-1572), because it requires investigators conducting studies in Canada to follow US laws.
