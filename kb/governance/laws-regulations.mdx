## Follow good clinical practice

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span></div>

ICH E6(R3) applies to interventional clinical trials of investigational products when the trials are intended to be submitted to regulatory authorities. The guideline uses "investigational products" for drugs, medicines, medicinal products, vaccines and biological products. The guideline's principles may also apply to other interventional trials of these products that are not intended to support a marketing authorization application, in accordance with local requirements.

ICH E6(R3) encourages a risk-based and proportionate approach to the conduct of a trial, with [quality by design](/kb/foundations/gcp-quality-by-design) to identify the data and processes on which the trial's quality depends.

Because ISO 14155's good clinical practice requirements are very similar to those of ICH E6, the RI-MUHC's SOPs accept [ICH GCP training](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) in place of ISO 14155 training when a sponsor or sponsor-investigator can't provide ISO 14155 training or the document. The current edition is ISO 14155:2026.

The RI-MUHC's SOPs require all device research to comply with the Medical Devices Regulations. All device research should also follow ISO 14155.

The [training you need](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) depends on the level of the studies you work on, and includes good clinical practice.

### Follow good clinical practice in a study regulated by Health Canada

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span></div>

Health Canada requires the sponsor of a drug or natural health product trial to ensure that it is conducted in accordance with good clinical practices. For drug trials, Health Canada implemented [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) on April 1, 2026, and its Annex 2 on Nov. 4, 2026. Annex 2 covers decentralized elements, pragmatic elements and the use of real-world data.

If you initiate a drug trial as its sponsor-investigator, the [same Division 5 requirements apply to you as to a commercial sponsor](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator).

For a natural health product trial that you initiate, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs require](/sops/cr-023) compliance with the Natural Health Products Regulations, ICH E6 good clinical practice and provincial and local regulations.

For device trials, [Health Canada expects manufacturers](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/investigational-testing-authorizations-guidance/guidance-document.html) to follow the principles of the *Declaration of Helsinki* and TCPS 2 and to conform to the good clinical practice of ISO 14155. Health Canada considers ISO 14155 generally consistent with the Medical Devices Regulations. Where the two are inconsistent, the Regulations take precedence.

Health Canada recognizes the 2026 edition of ISO 14155. For three years from the 2026 edition's publication in March 2026, Health Canada also recognizes the 2020 edition, unless the 2026 edition sets its own transition period.

For a device trial that you initiate, other than one with an in vitro diagnostic device, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs require its investigational testing](/sops/cr-024#5-1-ri-muhc-compliance-requirements) to be conducted in compliance with the *Declaration of Helsinki*, ISO 14155 and ISO 14971 on the application of risk management to medical devices.

## Check whether your study needs an authorization from Health Canada

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

Your study cannot begin until Health Canada's authorization, [where it needs one](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization), has been obtained. For a trial that you initiate, the RI-MUHC's SOPs set the steps of the application in [SOP-CR-018](/sops/cr-018) for a drug, [SOP-CR-023](/sops/cr-023) for a natural health product and [SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024) for a device, in vitro diagnostic devices excluded.

Health Canada explains how to apply in its guidance on [clinical trials of drugs](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/clinical-trials.html) and [clinical trials of natural health products](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/clinical-trials.html). Health Canada also sets [deadlines for reporting safety events](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines) during a trial.

### Check whether a radiopharmaceutical study is a basic clinical research study

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span></div>

A study of a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical can be a basic clinical research study under Division 3 of the Food and Drug Regulations. The sponsor of such a study applies for the Minister's authorization under Division 3, instead of a Clinical Trial Application.

The radiopharmaceutical may be sold or imported only for use in a basic clinical research study. A radiopharmaceutical that is to be imported may be sold or imported only if its manufacturer has a representative in Canada who is responsible for its sale.

Under Division 3, a basic clinical research study obtains data on the radiopharmaceutical's pharmacokinetics or metabolism, on normal human biochemistry or physiology, or on changes caused to human biochemistry or physiology by aging, disease or medical interventions. The study is not primarily intended to discover, identify or verify the radiopharmaceutical's pharmacodynamic effects, identify adverse reactions, fulfil an immediate therapeutic or diagnostic purpose, or ascertain its safety or efficacy.

A basic clinical research study under Division 3 must also meet all of the following requirements:

- Before the radiopharmaceutical is used in the study, there are sufficient data from testing it in animals and humans to demonstrate its safety in humans.
- The amount of active ingredients, or combination of active ingredients, in the radiopharmaceutical has been shown not to cause any clinically detectable pharmacodynamic effect in humans.
- The total radiation dose each participant incurs in a year will be not more than 50 millisieverts (mSv). That total includes the radiation from multiple administrations of the radiopharmaceutical, from significant contaminants or impurities, and from other procedures used for the study.
- Any concomitant drug used in the study has a drug identification number or, if it is a new drug, a notice of compliance.
- Participants are at least 18 years old and have legal capacity at the time of the study.
- Female participants are confirmed at the outset of the study as not pregnant, on the basis of a pregnancy test, or state in writing that they are not pregnant.
- Female participants are advised that if they are lactating, they are to suspend lactation for 24 hours after the radiopharmaceutical is given.
- The study involves no more than 30 participants.

A study may involve more than 30 participants if the sponsor provides the Minister with a scientific rationale for the increase and the Minister approves it.

[Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations does not apply to the radiopharmaceutical in a basic clinical research study under Division 3](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.03.302.html). Division 3's sections on these studies set their own rules, including on the research ethics board, good clinical practices, adverse reaction reporting and records.

Division 3's rules for these studies don't apply to a radiopharmaceutical manufactured from a bulk process intermediate of biological origin.

### Check whether a device study needs an investigational testing authorization

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span></div>

Health Canada's <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)</Tooltip> lets a manufacturer or importer sell or import a Class II, III or IV medical device for investigational testing in human participants.

Health Canada counts a device that the manufacturer provides to the investigator free of charge as sold, because the Food and Drugs Act's definition of selling is not restricted to commercial or monetary sale.

The Medical Devices Regulations class devices from Class I, the lowest risk, to Class IV, the highest, by the rules in their Schedule 1. A device's class depends on its labelling and on how the study uses it.

Health Canada's [classification guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/guidance-document-guidance-risk-based-classification-system-non-vitro-diagnostic.html) explains the rules, with examples such as these. An example doesn't mean that every device of its type is in that class. The manufacturer is responsible for classifying its device by its characteristics and intended purposes.

| Class | Examples named in Health Canada's guidance |
|---|---|
| Class I | Gauze bandages. Mechanical and powered wheelchairs. |
| Class II | Surgical gloves. Air conduction hearing aids. Diagnostic dental radiographic (x-ray) units. |
| Class III | Shoulder prostheses. Computed tomography (CT) x-ray scanners. High-energy direct current defibrillators that are not closed-loop. |
| Class IV | Mechanical heart valves. Implantable rate-responsive pacemakers. Automatic implantable cardioverter defibrillators. |

For software, Health Canada applies the rules of Schedule 1 through its [guidance on software as a medical device](/kb/design/software-medical-devices#know-how-health-canada-classes-software-as-a-medical-device), which has examples of its own.

Whether a device study needs an ITA depends on the device's class, whether it is licensed in Canada, how the study uses it and who makes it:

| The case | ITA needed? |
|---|---|
| A Class I device. | No. The manufacturer or importer must still hold the records that Health Canada requires, and the REB's approval and the MUHC's requirements still apply. |
| A Class II, III or IV device that is not licensed in Canada. | Yes, unless no sale occurs, as in the last two rows: testing within a single corporate entity, or use by the physician who is the device's legal manufacturer. |
| A licensed Class II, III or IV device used outside its licensed indications, in a study that the manufacturer sponsors or that is meant to support a licence application. | Yes, unless no sale occurs, as in the last two rows: testing within a single corporate entity, or use by the physician who is the device's legal manufacturer. |
| A licensed device used outside its indications, in a study that the manufacturer does not sponsor and that does not aim to support a licence application (off-label use). | No. |
| A licensed device used within its licensed indications. | No. If the study is meant to generate data for a licence application, the manufacturer should consult Health Canada's Investigational Testing division. |
| A device developed, manufactured and tested within a single corporate entity. The testing is limited to use on site, at the corporate entity, at a single institution and solely by the device's legal manufacturer. | No. No sale occurs, and the requirements of the Medical Devices Regulations do not apply. |
| Use of a device by the physician who is its legal manufacturer, at one site and only on that physician's own patients, when no one else conducts the testing. | No. |

Some devices that physicians make and use may be considered covered by their scope of practice. Before testing one, the RI-MUHC's SOPs strongly recommend asking Health Canada's head of investigational testing for advice.

If a trial that you initiate uses an unlicensed device and needs an ITA, [obtain the cooperation of the device's manufacturer](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#only-the-manufacturer-or-importer-can-apply). The manufacturer must be the official applicant and signatory for the ITA application.

Health Canada's review target for a new ITA application or a request for a revised ITA is 30 calendar days, except for a combination product. The target is counted from when screening begins. If Health Canada sends a screening deficiency letter, the target is counted from when Health Canada receives a complete application. For minor administrative revisions to an ITA, the review target is 15 calendar days. Both are estimated targets, and no authorization follows automatically when one passes.

In a device trial that you initiate and that needs an ITA, other than one with an in vitro diagnostic device, do not begin the study before Health Canada's written authorization is received.

An ITA is specific to the device, the protocol and the number of patients named on it. When several studies use the same device, a separate ITA application is required for each study protocol.

After the investigation, a re-usable device that is not yet licensed should be returned to the manufacturer or importer, decommissioned or disposed of appropriately. If the qualified investigator intends to use the device under a different study protocol, the manufacturer or importer must submit a new ITA application.

Health Canada explains how to apply for an ITA in its [guidance on investigational testing authorizations](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/investigational-testing-authorizations-guidance/guidance-document.html) and has [separate guidance for in vitro diagnostic devices](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/cover-letter-application-investigational-vitro-diagnostics-devices.html). If you are unsure whether your study needs an ITA, ask Health Canada's Investigational Testing division at [it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca](mailto:it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca).

## Apply Quebec rules at the MUHC

Health and social services information held by the MUHC falls under Law 5, not Law 25. Law 5 also sets the conditions for using health records without consent, including [a privacy impact assessment (EFVP, for évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée) and MUHC Authorization](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent).

Quebec's health ministry requires each establishment in its network that conducts research to establish a regulatory framework for that research. The framework must be adopted by the establishment's board of directors. The ministry's official name is the Ministère de la Santé, de la Prévention et des Aînés.

In applying the ministry's standards, establishments must also take into account the standards of research ethics and responsible conduct of research recognized nationally and internationally, such as those of Quebec's and Canada's research funding agencies. Quebec's legal requirements and standards prevail over them.

The MUHC has been an establishment of Santé Québec since Dec. 1, 2024. The MUHC adopted its [*Regulatory Framework*](/kb/governance/institutional-structure) to meet the ministry's requirement. In it, the MUHC takes on the responsibility that the ministry delegates to establishments: adopting and maintaining a framework that complies with all applicable laws, regulations and standards.

## Know what Health Canada is changing

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

Until Health Canada's proposed Clinical Trials Regulations come into force, apply for and amend authorizations under the current regulations. Health Canada published the proposal for consultation in the [*Canada Gazette*, Part I](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p1/2025/2025-12-20/html/reg2-eng.html), on Dec. 20, 2025, and the [consultation](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/programs/consultation-modernizing-framework-clinical-trials.html) ran until April 19, 2026.

As published, the proposal would:

- Apply only to clinical trials of drugs, leaving natural health product trials and device investigations under their current regulations.
- Regulate the conduct of drug trials directly, with risk-based oversight throughout a trial's life cycle. The current regulations oversee trials indirectly, through the sale and import of the drugs tested.
- Have Health Canada issue a contingent authorization within 7 days of a complete application. It would become an authorization after 30 days unless Health Canada notified the sponsor in writing, within that time, that the application was incomplete, that requested information was missing or that there were reasonable grounds not to authorize the trial. Health Canada could confirm it sooner, or extend the 30 days to 60 for a complex trial or where protecting a vulnerability of the study population needs further assessment.
- Cover a trial with one or more drugs, including a master protocol with sub-studies, under a single authorization, with an amendment needed for changes such as adding a sub-study.
- Exempt from authorization a trial that uses only authorized drugs, as authorized, though its sponsor would still have to comply with certain of the proposed requirements, such as those on good clinical practices.
- Accept documented consent, given orally or with an electronic signature.
- Let a broader range of regulated health professionals act as investigators.
- Count remote locations as part of a trial site.
- Treat the sponsor of an ongoing trial authorized under the current drug regulations, or under the COVID-19 clinical trial regulations, as holding an authorization under the new ones, unless the trial uses only authorized drugs as authorized.

In its [report on the consultation](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/programs/consultation-modernizing-framework-clinical-trials/what-we-heard.html), Health Canada said it expects to publish the final regulations in the *Canada Gazette*, Part II, in spring 2027. Health Canada anticipates that the final regulations will come into force in spring 2028, 12 months after that publication, as the proposal provides.

Meanwhile, through an [interim policy](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/announcements/interim-policy-modernization-clinical-trial-framework-notice.html) issued on Sept. 14, 2026, Health Canada applies these interpretations to drug trials, in accordance with good clinical practices, until the new regulations come into force:

- The sponsor of a multi-site trial whose sites share one REB needs to give Health Canada that board's contact information only once, as long as it stays up to date, and must keep the records of every site's approval.
- People who are not physicians or dentists may act as the qualified investigator, the person responsible to the sponsor for the trial at a site, if their education, training, experience and scope of practice suit the trial. Medical decisions about the trial outside their licensed scope of practice must then be made by a qualified physician or dentist on the team, licensed in the site's province or territory and responsible for medical oversight and participant care.
- [Consent may be documented in new ways](/kb/recruitment/consent#meet-the-added-conditions-for-a-study-regulated-by-health-canada), such as real-time audio and video consent discussions and electronic signatures, when the process is appropriately documented, complies with good clinical practices and ICH E6 principles, and ensures that consent is informed and given in accordance with the applicable laws governing consent.

None of these interpretations changes the sponsor's responsibility to ensure that the trial is conducted in accordance with good clinical practices and all applicable ethical, legal and institutional requirements, including valid REB approval for each participating site before activities start at that site.

At the MUHC, the laws governing consent include [Quebec's rule on written consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-before-any-study-procedure) for research that could interfere with a person's integrity.

At the MUHC, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs require Human Research Privileges](/sops/cr-500#4-0-definitions) of the qualified investigator of a drug trial, as of every Health Canada-regulated study with an investigational product. [Privileges](/training/compliance-requirements#know-which-credential-you-need) are for physicians and dentists who are members of the MUHC's Council of Physicians, Dentists, Pharmacists and Midwives.

Health Canada has also issued guidance for drug trials with [decentralized elements](/kb/conduct/study-operations/decentralized-trials).

## Follow the rules of other countries

If your study takes place wholly or partly in another country, that country's rules apply too. They include its requirements for regulatory submissions, importing study drugs or devices, safety reporting and documentation. Studies that MUHC personnel conduct in other countries using their MUHC affiliation must also be reviewed and approved by the MUHC REB before they start.

In the United States, a study that falls under the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) may need an Investigational New Drug application (IND) or an Investigational Device Exemption (IDE). The FDA's rules on electronic records and electronic signatures, in Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations, may apply to [the electronic systems a study uses](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#know-who-validates-electronic-systems).

A trial conducted in Canada stays under Canadian law and Health Canada's regulations even when a sponsor also runs it under a US IND or IDE. MUHC and RI-MUHC investigators are not to sign [Form FDA 1572](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#the-qiu-and-the-fda-1572), because it requires investigators conducting studies in Canada to follow US laws.

To check whether FDA or another country's requirements apply to your study, ask [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca).{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 1, 3 to 5, 7 to 11 and 13"). */}
