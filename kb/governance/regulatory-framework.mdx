Clinical research at the MUHC is governed by four overlapping layers of authority. Each layer has a different legal basis, different enforcing body, and different purpose. A requirement from one layer is not cancelled, replaced, or satisfied by a requirement from another — they stack. Understanding where a requirement comes from is usually the fastest way to understand why it exists.

## International layer: GCP and the Declaration of Helsinki

### ICH GCP E6(R3)

Good Clinical Practice (GCP) is an international quality standard for designing, conducting, recording, and reporting clinical trials involving human participants. The current version, **ICH E6(R3)**, was adopted by the ICH Assembly on January 6, 2025. Health Canada implemented E6(R3) effective **April 1, 2026**.

E6(R3) covers the responsibilities of sponsors, investigators, and monitors; the structure of essential documents; the principles of data integrity and source data verification; and the obligations for safety reporting. It introduces a quality-by-design approach and formally endorses risk-based monitoring as the expected model for sponsor oversight.

**Applies to:** All interventional clinical trials involving investigational products regulated by Health Canada, and as best-practice guidance for other interventional studies.

<Note>
Health Canada implemented Annex 2 of E6(R3), which covers non-traditional trial designs including decentralized and real-world data trials, on November 4, 2026.
</Note>

### Declaration of Helsinki

The Declaration of Helsinki, first adopted by the World Medical Association in 1964 and most recently revised in 2024, establishes the ethical principles for research involving human subjects. It provides the ethical foundation on which GCP was built: the obligation to put participant welfare above scientific and societal interests, the requirement for independent ethics review, and the prohibition on research that does not meet minimum standards of scientific quality. The 2024 revision formally incorporated scientific integrity as an explicit ethical requirement.

**Applies to:** All biomedical research involving human participants, regardless of funding source or regulatory status.

### ISO 14155:2020

ISO 14155 is the GCP standard specifically for clinical investigations of medical devices. It mirrors many ICH GCP principles but includes device-specific requirements for risk management, device accountability, and the structure of the Clinical Investigation Plan.

**Applies to:** Medical device studies at the MUHC. ISO 14155 compliance is required alongside the Medical Devices Regulations.

<Note>
None of these documents are Canadian statutes — they have no direct legal force on their own. Their binding effect in Canada comes from the federal layer: Health Canada regulations require compliance with ICH GCP and ISO 14155 as a condition of conducting regulated trials.
</Note>

## Federal layer: Health Canada

Health Canada regulates clinical research involving investigational drugs, natural health products, and medical devices under the authority of the *Food and Drugs Act*. The key subordinate legislation is:

| Regulation | Applies to |
|---|---|
| Division 5, Food and Drug Regulations | Drug and biologic trials |
| Medical Devices Regulations, Part 3 | Medical device studies |
| Natural Health Products Regulations, Part 4 | NHP trials |

These regulations set when a trial needs Health Canada's authorization, and they require SUSARs to be reported to Health Canada on a defined timeline.

The sponsor of a drug trial may proceed if Health Canada does not object within 30 days of receiving the CTA; Health Canada usually sends a <Tooltip tip="Letter from Health Canada confirming a CTA is satisfactory and authorizing the sponsor to proceed." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nol">No Objection Letter</Tooltip>. For a natural health product, Health Canada authorizes the trial by sending a notice. For a medical device, Health Canada's 30-day review of an ITA application is an estimated target, and Health Canada issues the authorization once it finds the application satisfactory.

Health Canada regulations do not differentiate between commercial sponsors and non-commercial Sponsor-Investigators — all ICH GCP sponsor responsibilities apply equally.

**Applies to:** All studies involving Health Canada-regulated investigational products (drugs, devices, NHPs). Foreign trials conducted in Canada under a US-held IND or IDE are also subject to Health Canada regulations.

### TCPS2

The Tri-Council Policy Statement: Ethical Conduct for Research Involving Humans (TCPS2, 2022) is Canada's national research ethics policy. It is a policy condition imposed by CIHR, NSERC, and SSHRC on any institution that receives their funding. Because the RI-MUHC receives tri-council funding, TCPS2 compliance is mandatory. The MUHC <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> has also adopted TCPS2 as binding policy for all research under its jurisdiction, regardless of funding source.

TCPS2 governs research ethics review, informed consent, privacy, conflicts of interest, research involving Indigenous peoples, and special protections for vulnerable populations.

**Applies to:** All federally funded research at the RI-MUHC, and by MUHC REB policy, all research under REB jurisdiction regardless of funding.

## Provincial layer: Quebec

Quebec provincial law creates a distinct layer of obligations that applies because the MUHC is a Quebec public health institution. These requirements are not covered by GCP training.

### Civil Code of Quebec — Articles 20–25

Consent to research in Quebec is governed by the Civil Code, not common law.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Article 20 — Proportionality of risk">
    A capable adult may take part in research that could interfere with their integrity only if the risk is not disproportionate to the benefit that can reasonably be expected, and the project must be approved and monitored by a research ethics committee.

    **Applies to:** Research that could interfere with the integrity of a capable adult.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Article 21 — Minors and incapable adults">
    Governs research with minors and adults who cannot consent. A minor or an adult who cannot consent, and who understands the nature and consequences of the research and objects to taking part, cannot take part, whoever has consented for them. The research must be approved and monitored by a research ethics committee that the Minister of Health and Social Services has formed or designated; the MUHC REB is a designated one.

    **Applies to:** Any study enrolling minors or incapacitated adults.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Article 25 — Limits on compensation">
    Allows only an indemnity for the loss and inconvenience a participant suffers, and no other financial reward. This is stricter than TCPS2 and ICH GCP, which permit payment to healthy Phase I volunteers. In Quebec, compensation must be calibrated to avoid constituting an undue inducement.

    **Applies to:** Research in Quebec that could interfere with a participant's integrity.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

The RI-MUHC's SOPs add that [the treating physician should not be the one who obtains consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#choose-who-obtains-consent), unless the REB approves.

### MSSS Cadre de référence ministériel

The *Cadre de référence ministériel pour la recherche avec des participants humains* (MSSS, 2020) is the provincial framework that establishes governance requirements for human research in Quebec's public health network. It is the legal basis for:

- **The <Tooltip tip="Individual formally authorized to issue the final written authorization for each research project at a Quebec RSSS institution." headline="Personne mandatée (PFM)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne formellement mandatée</Tooltip> (PFM)** — every <Tooltip tip="Quebec's public health and social services network (Réseau de la santé et des services sociaux), including the RI-MUHC." headline="RSSS" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#rsss">RSSS</Tooltip> institution must designate a person authorized by the Board of Directors to issue final written authorization for each research project. No study begins without the PFM's authorization letter.
- **Human Research Privileges and Researcher Status** — formal credentials required before conducting human research at a Quebec public health institution.
- **The triple review** — authorization requires three independent evaluations: scientific review, ethics review, and institutional feasibility review (*convenance institutionnelle*). Authorization issues only when all three are complete and satisfactory.

**Applies to:** All research conducted at the MUHC and other Quebec RSSS institutions.

### Law 25 — Privacy modernization

<Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act to modernize personal information protection law (in force Sept 2023). Requires EFVP before sharing personal data without consent." headline="Law 25 / Loi 25" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#law-25">Law 25</Tooltip> (Act to Modernize Legislative Provisions Respecting the Protection of Personal Information, 2022) governs how personal information held by Quebec organizations may be used, shared, and protected. For research, it creates the **EFVP requirement** (Évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée — privacy impact assessment). An EFVP must be completed before a project begins if it involves:

- Access to identifiable health data without participant consent
- Transfers of personal health data outside Quebec
- Deployment of technology to collect personal data

**Applies to:** Any study at the MUHC involving identifiable personal information, regardless of study type or funding source.

### Law 5 — Health information framework

<Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information (key provisions July 2024). Governs researcher access to health data through RSSS institutions." headline="Law 5 / Loi 5" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#law-5">Law 5</Tooltip> / R-22.1 (Act Respecting Health and Social Services Information, in force July 2024) governs how health and social services information held by Quebec public health institutions may be accessed, used, and shared. It establishes a framework for secondary use of health data held in provincial systems and applies specific requirements to research accessing that data.

**Applies to:** Studies involving health and social services information held by the MUHC or Quebec provincial health systems.

### Pharmacy Act and the OPQ

In Quebec, any medication given to a patient, whether in care or in a research protocol, must be dispensed by a pharmacist. The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures base this rule on the rules of the Ordre des pharmaciens du Québec (OPQ), Quebec's order of pharmacists. It is a professional regulatory requirement specific to Quebec. Good clinical practice guidelines do not require it, and other Canadian provinces have no equivalent.

All drugs and some natural health products used in research must be managed by the MUHC pharmacy. The investigator delegates these tasks to the pharmacy but remains responsible for them. In specific circumstances, the investigator may ask at submission for an RI-MUHC or MUHC nurse to dispense the investigational product. The MUHC Research Pharmacy approves such a request only after checking that the plan complies with Quebec's Pharmacy Act.

**Applies to:** Studies that give participants medications, including investigational drugs, at the MUHC.

## Institutional layer: MUHC and RI-MUHC

The fourth layer is the MUHC and RI-MUHC's own policies, procedures, and governance structures. These exist to operationalize the three layers above into site-specific practice and to add institutional protections that the higher layers leave to individual institutions.

The RI-MUHC SOPs translate federal and provincial requirements into step-by-step procedures calibrated for the MUHC environment. Key institutional requirements include:

- **MUHC Authorization letter** — issued by the Personne mandatée; required before any study activity begins
- **Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status** — administered by the Performance Office; prerequisite for REB submission
- **MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement** — governs access to MUHC electronic systems and patient records
- **QA feasibility review** — confirms training levels and investigator credentials before the Personne mandatée can issue authorization
- **Departmental agreements** — pharmacy, laboratory, and nursing reviews where applicable

## Who enforces what

Non-compliance with one layer does not provide protection against enforcement from another. Each layer is enforced independently.

| Layer | Enforcing body | Consequences |
|---|---|---|
| International (GCP / ISO) | Health Canada — through federal regulations requiring ICH GCP and ISO 14155 compliance | Inspection observations (Critical / Major / Minor); suspension or cancellation of CTA/ITA; data invalidation |
| Federal (Health Canada) | Health Canada CTCP (Clinical Trial Compliance Program, ROEB) | <Tooltip tip="Actions taken to resolve a compliance issue and prevent recurrence: identify, correct, prevent." headline="Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#capa">CAPA</Tooltip> requirements; suspension of CTA/ITA; criminal penalties under the Food and Drugs Act: \$500–\$5,000,000 fine and/or 3 months–2 years imprisonment |
| Provincial (Quebec) | CAI (privacy), MUHC REB (ethics), MSSS (governance) | Fines under Quebec's privacy laws; loss of REB approval; study suspension |
| Institutional (MUHC / RI-MUHC) | Personne mandatée; RI-MUHC QA; MUHC REB | Withholding or revoking MUHC Authorization; QA CAPA; REB suspension; revocation of Human Research Privileges; loss of institutional insurance coverage |

<Tip>
When a requirement seems redundant, the question is not "does another layer cover this?" but "which layer does this requirement come from, and what does it protect against that the others don't?" A study that satisfies GCP has met the international layer — it has not met the federal, Quebec, or institutional layers.
</Tip>
