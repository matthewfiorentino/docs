A payment from a sponsor, shares in a company or a relationship with a team member can influence how a researcher designs, conducts or reports a study, or seem to. Managing these interests protects participants and maintains public confidence in research. Declare a conflict of interest to the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> for each study it could affect, and to your Reporting Officer at McGill University. The two declarations are separate, and making one doesn't make the other.

## Recognize a conflict of interest

A conflict of interest may arise when a researcher's duties in a study clash with personal, institutional or other interests. The clash can be real, potential or perceived. The interests include business, commercial or financial interests of the institution or of the researcher, their family members, friends, or former, current or prospective professional associates.

McGill counts these among the financial interests that can create a conflict:

- Remuneration, such as a salary, consulting fees, retainers, honoraria, bonuses, gifts, speaker's fees, advisory board remuneration and finder's or recruitment fees.
- Equity, such as stocks, stock options or other ownership interests.
- Intellectual property rights, such as patents and copyrights, and the royalties or other payments from them.

A conflict can also come from a relationship with family, friends or professional colleagues, from an academic interest, or from holding two roles at once, such as researcher and treating physician.

At McGill, a conflict exists when you or a related party have a personal interest that you are or should be aware of, and that a reasonably informed and impartial person would see as enough to question your independence, impartiality and objectivity, or your ability to act in McGill's best interests. It also exists when you or a related party only appear to have such an interest.

[Conflicts of interest must be avoided whenever possible](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops). If one can't be avoided, it must be managed. Having a conflict doesn't necessarily mean misconduct, provided the conflict is recognized, disclosed, assessed and addressed. McGill notes that most conflicts can be permitted with a good mitigation plan, but not every conflict is permissible, even when it is disclosed in time.

If you aren't sure whether a situation is a conflict, [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) require you to contact the REB Chair](/sops/cr-007#5-7-2) and follow the REB's directions. McGill requires you to consult your Reporting Officer or, where appropriate, a research ethics board.

## Declare a conflict to the REB that reviews your study

Every investigator and team member must declare to the REB their own real, potential or perceived conflicts of interest, and those of a close relative or partner. As the study's principal investigator, the researcher who leads it at the MUHC, you are also responsible for identifying the conflicts of every member of your research team.

In the REB application, declare any real, potential or perceived personal or institutional conflict of interest that may affect the study. For the MUHC REB, the application is the F11 form in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>. Your declaration must:

- Be in writing, and detailed enough for the REB to evaluate the conflict accurately and objectively.
- Explain why the conflict cannot be prevented.
- Come with a conflict of interest management plan on the REB's template, describing how you propose to manage the conflict.

With the application, also give the study's budget and funding sources, as applicable. The researcher should inform the REB of any incentive for recruiting participants, such as fees or any other benefit.

Declare your conflicts at these times:

- With the initial REB application.
- At each continuing review of the study.
- Whenever a conflict arises, such as when your responsibilities or financial circumstances change.

Communications about a project take place in Nagano. If you write to the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) about a conflict outside Nagano, include the study's REB number in all later correspondence with the REB. The communication is then uploaded to Nagano.

When the REB evaluates your study, you may give it information on any aspect of the study. [You should not take part in its deliberations or in its vote](/sops/cr-007#5-7-3).

A conflict that a researcher hasn't declared can be flagged by anyone, such as the Research Agreements Office, the body that approves the budget, a department head or the REB. An REB member with a declared and confirmed conflict in your study is recused from its review and isn't counted toward the quorum.

If your department head is the investigator or has a conflict of interest, [an academic superior signs the submission form instead](/kb/submission/preparing#get-approvals-start-the-agreements-and-declare-conflicts).

## Tell participants about a conflict

Participants need to know about a conflict of interest to decide freely whether to take part. Declare the conflict in the consent documents if it applies to the study. If the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) asks, the investigator should give participants all the details of the conflict.

If you hold two roles, such as researcher and treating physician, supervisor or employer of the people you recruit, tell participants about the nature of the conflict during the consent process. [The person who obtains consent](/kb/recruitment/consent#choose-who-obtains-consent) should not be the participant's treating physician. If the treating physician is considered the best person to obtain consent, they must explain to the REB why it is necessary. If the REB approves, the physician must disclose their dual role to the participant.

If the REB lets a researcher with a conflict stay involved in part of the study, participants should be told during the consent process about the conflict and the extent of that researcher's involvement. The REB decides the scope and wording of what participants are told.

## Disclose a conflict to McGill

McGill University's [*Regulation on Conflict of Interest*](https://www.mcgill.ca/secretariat/policies-and-regulations) applies to all RI-MUHC researchers, staff and students, including those without a McGill appointment. As soon as you become aware of a conflict, disclose its facts in writing to your Reporting Officer, on McGill's form. Each year, submit an updated declaration for all your ongoing conflicts. If you are new at McGill, disclose all your conflicts to your Reporting Officer within 30 working days.

Your Reporting Officer depends on your position:

| Your position | Your Reporting Officer |
|---|---|
| A member of the academic staff | The chair of your department, or the director of your centre, institute or school, named in your letter of appointment. In a faculty without departments, the dean. |
| The chair of a department or the director of a centre, institute or school | The dean. |
| A postdoctoral fellow or a graduate student | Your supervisor. |
| Anyone else | The person you report to, or who supervises you. |

If your Reporting Officer is also involved in the conflict, disclose it to the first superior above them who isn't.

Tenure-track and tenured academic staff, librarian staff and salaried contract academic staff also submit an annual declaration by March 31: the Statement of Conflict of Interest (Short Form). For a research-related conflict, use Disclosure (Long) Form B: research-related. McGill's Academic Personnel Office keeps the forms on its [Conflict of Interest Reporting page](https://www.mcgill.ca/apo/forms/conflict-interest-reporting).

Except where the law requires otherwise, what you disclose is available only to people with a legitimate need to know it, and to a funding agency whose rules require it.

## What happens after you declare a conflict

The REB reviews each declaration and checks that your management plan is complete and relevant to the study. Its Chair or a delegate then decides whether the conflict is likely to affect, or appear to affect, the conduct or reporting of the research. The REB Chair or delegate also seeks to confirm that the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) has considered, or will consider, the conflict in the study's contracts.

The REB may approve the measures in your management plan or require others, such as:

- Changing your financial interests, or the sponsor's, including giving them up.
- Your recusal from the study.
- Limiting your part in all or some of the research.
- Independent monitoring of the study, such as an independent review of the data, or monitoring of the consent process.
- Disclosing the conflict to institutional bodies, participants, journals, and data and safety monitoring boards.

The REB Chair or delegate has the final say on whether a conflict has been eliminated or appropriately managed. The REB may set conditions before it approves the study, and may reject research whose conflict has not been appropriately managed. Follow all of the REB's requirements.

At McGill, your Reporting Officer reviews your disclosure and meets with you. The Reporting Officer may decide that there is no conflict, that there is one but it is permissible if appropriately addressed, or that it is not permissible.

A permission requires at least that you disclose the conflict to everyone it would affect, and that you promptly report any change that could change the conflict or its management. A decision on a research-related conflict also goes to McGill's Office of the Vice-President (Research and Innovation) for review.

Where a research ethics board has been charged with the conflict, the conflict is dealt with under McGill's policies on the ethical conduct of research with human participants instead. A copy of the research ethics board's decision, and where appropriate of the Research Ethics Appeals Committee's decision, is filed with the relevant chair and dean and with McGill's Office of the Vice-President (Research and Innovation).

## Handle common situations in clinical research

| Situation | Why it can be a conflict | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| You or a related party hold shares, stock options or a consulting arrangement with the company whose product the study tests. | The interest could bias how the study is conducted, analyzed or reported. | Declare it to the REB in the initial application, at each continuing review and whenever a new conflict arises. Disclose it to your McGill Reporting Officer, on Long Form B for a research-related conflict. Declare it in the consent documents where it applies. |
| The sponsor offers a finder's fee or a recruitment bonus. | A payment for each participant enrolled can create pressure to enrol people who may not meet the eligibility criteria. | McGill forbids researchers to accept any personal benefit, including a bonus or milestone payment, for enrolling a number of patients, meeting a recruitment deadline, having participants complete the study, or assessing potential recruits. The investigator should inform the REB of any recruitment incentive. Per-participant fees above actual costs are not permitted. |
| You are the study's sponsor-investigator. | Holding both roles puts the sponsor's and the investigator's duties in one person's hands. | Disclose the dual role at submission. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs require an independent dedicated monitor](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#monitoring-independence) for the study. |
| You recruit your own patients. | The care relationship can create a power imbalance, and patients may feel they cannot decline without affecting their care. | Tell participants about the dual role during the consent process. The person who obtains consent should not be the participant's treating physician. If you are considered the best person to obtain consent, you must explain to the REB why it is necessary. If the REB approves, you must disclose your dual role to the participant. Declare the dual role to the REB if the therapeutic relationship may affect recruitment. |
| Your career, a grant renewal or your publication record depends on the study's results. | An academic interest can affect how the study is designed, conducted or reported. | Declare it to the REB if it may affect the study. If you aren't sure whether McGill counts it as a conflict, consult your Reporting Officer. The REB may require an independent review of the data. |
| A related party, such as a member of your immediate family or someone with whom you have or had a close or intimate relationship, is on your team as a sub-investigator, coordinator or student. | The relationship can bias how you delegate tasks to them or evaluate their work. | Disclose it to your McGill Reporting Officer, who may set conditions, such as changes to how their work is overseen. Declare it to the REB if it may affect the study. |

If your situation is different, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) or your McGill Reporting Officer.

<Warning>
Do not leave a conflict of interest undeclared. The REB may refer a failure to disclose a conflict to the institutional body that reviews allegations of breaches of the responsible conduct of research. At McGill, not complying with the rules on conflicts of interest when you know, or should reasonably know, that you are in a conflict may be a disciplinary offence.
</Warning>

Failing to identify and address a conflict of interest is also among [the breaches of the responsible conduct of research](/kb/governance/research-integrity#what-counts-as-a-breach-of-the-responsible-conduct-of-research) that Canada's federal research agencies list.
