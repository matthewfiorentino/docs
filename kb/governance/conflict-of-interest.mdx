A conflict of interest in research exists when a researcher's personal, financial, or professional interests could — or could be perceived to — influence how they design, conduct, report, or oversee a study. The obligation is to disclose. Disclosure is not an admission of wrongdoing; it is the mechanism that lets the institution, the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>, and ultimately research participants make informed judgements about the research.

## What a conflict of interest is

Under [McGill's Regulation on Conflict of Interest](https://www.mcgill.ca/secretariat/policies-and-regulations), a potential conflict of interest exists whenever a member of the university community is in a position to influence the conduct of research, academic, human resource, business, financial, governance, or other matters in ways that could lead to personal gain for the member or a related party, or give improper advantage to others, to the detriment of the university or other members of the university community.

Three things follow from this definition that are worth making explicit:

1. The conflict does not have to be **financial** — a personal relationship, a professional rivalry, or a reputational stake can all constitute a conflict.
2. The conflict does not have to be **actual** — the appearance of a conflict, as perceived by a reasonable outside observer, triggers the same obligations.
3. Identifying a conflict does **not mean the research cannot proceed** — most disclosed conflicts are manageable. Disclosure is the mechanism, not the disqualification.

## Two distinct disclosure tracks

At the RI-MUHC, COI disclosure operates through two independent channels. They serve different purposes and should not be treated as interchangeable.

**Annual disclosure to supervisor or dean**

**What:** Ongoing financial and non-financial interests that could affect any aspect of a researcher's university responsibilities

**When:** Annually, and whenever a new reportable interest arises

**To whom:** Reporting Officer (typically department chair for academic staff; dean for chairs)

**Form:** McGill Statement of Conflict of Interest (Short Form for academic staff; Long Form B for research-related disclosures)

**Governed by:** [McGill Regulation on Conflict of Interest](https://www.mcgill.ca/secretariat/policies-and-regulations)

<Card title="Track 2 — REB study-level" icon="file-contract">
**Disclosure at study submission and to participants**

**What:** Any conflict specific to a particular research study — financial interest in the sponsor, equity in a company whose product is being tested, finder's fees, or any personal stake in the outcome

**When:** At initial REB submission and whenever a new conflict arises during the study lifecycle

**To whom:** MUHC REB (via <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. **Form:** Disclosed in the Nagano F11 application and in the ICF

**Governed by:** [MUHC REB SOP on Conflicts of Interest](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) · [TCPS2 Ch. 7](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter7-chapitre7.html)
</Card>

</CardGroup>

<Warning>
**The two tracks are additive, not alternative.** A researcher who discloses a financial interest to their department chair has not thereby satisfied the REB disclosure obligation — and vice versa. Each track must be satisfied independently.
</Warning>

## McGill institutional disclosure

All RI-MUHC investigators hold McGill Faculty appointments and are therefore subject to [McGill's Regulation on Conflict of Interest](https://www.mcgill.ca/secretariat/policies-and-regulations). This regulation requires annual disclosure of potential conflicts and immediate disclosure whenever a new reportable interest arises during the year. The disclosure is made to the member's Reporting Officer and is handled with confidentiality.

McGill distinguishes between financial and non-financial conflicts. Financial conflicts include equity holdings, consulting fees, honoraria, royalties, paid advisory roles, and sponsored travel. Non-financial conflicts include personal relationships, supervisory relationships, academic rivalries, and reputational interests. Both categories are subject to disclosure.

For research-related conflicts specifically, the Long Form B is used. [McGill's Academic Personnel Office](https://www.mcgill.ca/apo/forms/conflict-interest-reporting) maintains the current forms and process guidance. Disclosure does not prevent the research from proceeding — after disclosure, the Reporting Officer determines whether any management measures are needed, which could range from enhanced oversight to recusal from specific decisions.

## REB study-level disclosure

The MUHC REB SOP on Conflicts of Interest requires that <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PIs disclose to the REB all details of their research activities including budget agreements, commercial interests, relations with consultants, and any other information relevant to a potential conflict. The REB then determines how any conflict should be managed within the context of that specific study.

Separately, TCPS2 and the MUHC REB SOP require that investigators disclose potential conflicts to research participants. This means the informed consent form must clearly state any financial or other relationships between the investigator and the study sponsor that could reasonably affect a participant's decision about whether to enrol. The disclosure in the ICF is directed at participants, not the institution — it serves the autonomy of the person being asked to consent.

The REB also has its own COI obligations: REB members must recuse themselves from any study in which they have a conflict, and quorum requirements account for the possibility of recusals.

## Common situations in clinical research

| Situation | Why it may be a conflict | What is required |
|-----------|-------------------------|-----------------|
| Financial interest in sponsor company | Equity, stock options, or consulting arrangements with the company whose product is being tested could bias study conduct, analysis, or reporting | Disclosure to McGill (annual, Long Form B) and to MUHC REB at submission; may require disclosure in ICF |
| Finder's fee or recruitment bonus | Per-participant financial incentive from sponsor creates pressure to enrol participants who may not meet criteria | Must be disclosed to REB; Office of Clinical Contracts flags these during budget review. Per-participant fees above actual costs are not permitted |
| Investigator as sponsor-investigator | Holding both sponsor and investigator roles means no independent oversight of one's own study | Disclosure at submission; the 100-series SOPs establish additional governance requirements for this role |
| <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> recruiting their own patients | Therapeutic relationship creates power imbalance; patients may feel they cannot decline without affecting their care | Addressed in the consent process — a person other than the treating clinician should where possible obtain consent. Disclose to REB if the therapeutic relationship may affect recruitment objectivity |
| Academic stake in outcomes | Career advancement, grant renewal, or publication record tied to the study's results | Non-financial conflict — disclosure to McGill Reporting Officer; REB may ask about blinding or independent data review arrangements |
| Relationship with research team member | Supervisory or personal relationship with a Sub-I, <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip>, or student creates risk of biased delegation or evaluation | Disclosure to McGill Reporting Officer; may require oversight adjustments |

## What happens after disclosure

Disclosure triggers a management process, not automatic disqualification. The outcome depends on the nature and severity of the conflict and who is assessing it.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="McGill institutional track — Reporting Officer assessment">
The Reporting Officer reviews the disclosure and determines whether any management measures are needed. Most disclosures result in a management plan rather than prohibition. A conflict that cannot be adequately managed may result in removal from the activity in question.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="MUHC REB track — REB review and conditions">
The REB reviews the disclosed conflict as part of the study application. It may impose conditions (enhanced monitoring, independent data review, specific ICF language) or, in rare cases where a conflict cannot be managed, decline to approve the study or a specific investigator's involvement.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Participant-facing — Disclosure in the ICF">
Where required by the REB, the conflict is disclosed to potential participants in the informed consent form. This allows the participant to factor the investigator's interests into their decision about enrolment. The REB determines the required scope and language of the disclosure.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
**Undisclosed conflicts carry greater risk than disclosed ones.** A conflict that is disclosed and managed does not by itself constitute misconduct. A conflict that is not disclosed — particularly one that subsequently influences study conduct or reporting — is a serious integrity breach and may constitute research misconduct under both McGill policy and the [Tri-Agency Framework](https://rcr.ethics.gc.ca/eng/framework-cadre.html).
</Warning>
