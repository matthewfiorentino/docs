Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI) considerations are no longer optional for most funded research. Most major Canadian and Quebec funders — CIHR, FRQS, CFI, and others — now require EDI plans as part of grant applications, and eligibility for some programs is contingent on demonstrating EDI commitments in team composition and study design.

In 2023–24, 85% of CIHR training and career-support research integrated sex and gender considerations into study design.

Engage the EDI team at the **Design & Planning stage**, before your protocol is finalized and before your grant application is submitted.

<Tip>
  Contact the EDI team early — GBA+ consultation and EDI attestation for grant proposals are time-sensitive. Don't wait until submission to confirm your requirements.
</Tip>

## What the EDI team provides

The RI-MUHC EDI team offers a range of supports for research teams at The Institute:

**GBA+ / SGBA consultation:**
Gender-Based Analysis Plus (GBA+) / Analyse comparative entre les sexes plus (ACS+) is a Tri-Agency requirement for most CIHR and FRQS grant applications. The EDI team provides consultation to help researchers integrate sex, gender, and diversity considerations into study design, eligibility criteria, and outcome measurement.

**Inclusive recruitment and retention support:**
- Guidance on designing recruitment strategies that reach underrepresented populations
- Support for developing accessible consent materials
- Retention strategies for diverse participant cohorts

**Self-assessment tools:**
- Tri-Agency-aligned EDI self-assessment for research teams
- Disability inclusion toolkit
- Neuroinclusion planning resources

**Training and awareness:**
- Online training on unconscious bias and inclusive leadership
- Team-based EDI workshops and facilitated sessions
- Resources aligned with the MUHC's institutional EDI commitments

## Programs for equity-seeking groups

The RI-MUHC EDI initiative supports researchers and research team members from equity-seeking groups, including:

- Women in research leadership
- Racialized researchers and staff
- Indigenous people and researchers
- 2SLGBTQIA+ researchers and participants
- People with disabilities
- Ethnic and linguistic minorities

The EDI team can connect researchers with institutional support programs, mentorship, and resources aligned with their specific needs.

## 3CTN EDI Framework

The Canadian Cancer Clinical Trials Network ([3CTN](https://3ctn.ca/)) published its [EDI Framework and Toolkit](https://3ctn.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/EDI-Framework.pdf) in August 2024. Although developed for the oncology clinical trial context, the framework is applicable across therapeutic areas and provides one of the most operationally specific EDI resources available to Canadian clinical trial sites.

Key elements of the 3CTN framework directly applicable at the RI-MUHC:

**1. Inclusion/exclusion criteria — implicit bias review.** A structured review of a protocol's eligibility criteria to identify requirements that may unintentionally exclude underrepresented populations without scientific justification. Common examples:
- Age caps that exclude older adults from trials where evidence of benefit in the elderly is needed
- English/French language requirements where bilingual research assistance could be provided instead
- Requirement for caregiver accompaniment that disadvantages participants without robust social support
- Geographic access requirements for frequent in-person visits that disadvantage participants without flexible work schedules or without cars

The 3CTN toolkit provides a checklist for this review. The EDI team can assist in applying it to your protocol at the design stage.

**2. Recruitment strategies for underrepresented populations.** The framework identifies specific recruitment adaptations for populations that are systematically underrepresented in clinical trials:

| Population | Recruitment considerations |
|---|---|
| Racialized communities | Community-based recruitment partnerships; materials in community languages; community champion relationships; addressing historical mistrust of medical research |
| Rural and remote participants | Telemedicine options; decentralized visit design; partnerships with rural health centres; realistic travel burden assessment in eligibility criteria |
| Low socioeconomic status | Addressing logistical barriers (transportation, parking, childcare); clearly communicating that participation doesn't affect standard care access; flexible visit scheduling |
| Older adults | Age-appropriate consent materials; larger font; inclusion of caregivers where appropriate; technology support for digital tools |
| Gender-diverse participants | Gender-affirming data collection (sex and gender recorded separately); inclusive language in consent materials and questionnaires; training for study team on gender-affirming communication |
| Immigrants and newcomers | Materials in relevant languages; use of certified medical interpreters for consent; cultural navigation support |

**3. Team composition.** The 3CTN framework recommends active reflection on research team composition — not as a diversity checkbox, but as a scientific quality issue. Research teams that include people with lived experience of the condition under study, or with experience from communities the study aims to enrol, produce studies with stronger recruitment, higher retention, and more relevant outcomes.

## FRQ EDI strategy — what it requires in applications

FRQ (Fonds de recherche du Québec) has committed to having 80% of FRQ-funded research groups reporting active EDI actions by 2026. This target is embedded in the [FRQ EDI Strategy](https://frq.gouv.qc.ca/en/equity-diversity-and-inclusion-strategy/) and translates into specific application requirements across FRQ programs:

- **EDI plan required** in most FRQ career award applications (Chercheur-boursier Junior 1/2/Senior) — the plan should describe how EDI is integrated into the research program, team composition, mentorship practices, and knowledge translation
- **GBA+/SGBA+** considerations are assessed in FRQ health sector competitions alongside scientific merit
- Applicants are expected to demonstrate concrete, not aspirational, EDI commitments — reviewers assess whether the plan is integrated into the research design or simply appended as a compliance item

If your application is to a CIHR Team Grant (including SPOR, Strategy Grants, or Priority Announcements), the **EDI Champion** role is a named and assessed position on the research team. The EDI Champion is accountable for embedding EDI into scientific design, team composition, and dissemination — not for generic awareness activities. Document the EDI Champion's planned activities in the application and ensure they have access to the 3CTN toolkit and the RI-MUHC EDI team's consultation resources.
