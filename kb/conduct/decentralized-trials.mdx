Your protocol is approved and includes decentralized elements. This page covers the conduct obligations for each element. For design decisions made before protocol submission, see Designing a Decentralized Trial.

## Virtual Site Initiation Visits

Virtual SIVs are acceptable. When the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip> is conducted by teleconference or video, the attendee list from the virtual meeting platform is acceptable in place of wet ink signatures on the training log, and must be filed in the <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> alongside the SIV report.

All SIV prerequisites apply regardless of format — Health Canada authorization, <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approval, and <Tooltip tip="Institutional authorization allowing a study to proceed at the RI-MUHC. Issued after ethics approval and feasibility review." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> must all be in place before the SIV can be scheduled. As per [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005).

## Remote monitoring

Remote monitoring visits follow the same documentation requirements as in-person visits. Every remote visit must be documented with a written report and a follow-up monitoring letter, both filed in the TMF and clearly labelled as a Remote Monitoring Visit.

For remote SDV and SDR, all source data shared with the monitor must be de-identified before transfer — nominative information replaced with the participant's study code — and uploaded into a secure, password-protected validated database or transferred via an encrypted channel. As per [SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-013).

Health Canada requires a minimum of annual monitoring regardless of enrolment rate or whether monitoring is remote or on-site. ICH E6(R3) — effective April 1, 2026 in Canada — moves risk-based monitoring from an option to a core expectation: a mix of centralized, remote, and targeted on-site visits tailored to the specific risks of the trial is the expected approach. The traditional model of regular on-site SDV is no longer the default.

## Electronic informed consent (eICF)

Remote consent using a validated electronic platform is permitted. The process must be documented in a written Working Instruction (WI) before any eICF is used, and that WI must have been submitted to the REB alongside the <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> and acknowledged before use with participants.

### Platform requirements

- Validated and compliant with Health Canada GUI-0100 and 21 CFR Part 11
- Can confirm the signer's identity; captures at least two signatures (participant and consent obtainer)
- Can produce a signed hard copy of the consent form for the participant
- All signed eICFs stored and retrievable for **15 years** (HC-regulated studies)
- Documented procedures and user training in place before first use

A compliant eSignature must include: signer's printed name, date and time stamp, unique user ID code, digital adopted signature, signing reason, and audit trail.

### Working Instruction requirements

The WI must describe:
- How the eICF will be explained and discussed with the participant
- Security questions used to confirm identity
- How participant understanding will be assessed
- Whether a paper copy option is available and how it will be provided

<Warning>
**REB acknowledgement required before first use.** All eICFs, amendments, addenda, participant-facing materials, and the written remote consent WI must have been submitted to and acknowledged by the REB before the eICF is used with any participant. The population being recruited must be assessed — if the electronic method could impede consent for any members of the target population, this must be addressed in the REB submission.
</Warning>

For the full eICF requirements in the context of Quebec consent law, LAR rules, and re-consent triggers, see Consent in Quebec. As per [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008).

## Direct-to-participant investigational products

When the protocol includes shipping <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> directly to a participant's home or dispensing through a local pharmacy, Quebec pharmacy law applies without exception — the same as for conventional MUHC pharmacy dispensing. The Quebec pharmacy rule (RSQ, c. S-4.2 / OPQ) requires pharmacist professional oversight regardless of where the IP is dispensed.

For direct-to-home shipping: chain-of-custody must be documented at every step; temperature and storage requirements must be maintained during transit; receipt by the participant must be confirmed; and unused or returned product must be accounted for. If the IP is a controlled substance or has cold-chain requirements, local handling obligations apply at the delivery end. The MUHC pharmacy must have been engaged during study design and a pharmacy agreement must be in place before the study begins. See [Investigational Products in Quebec](/kb/conduct/investigational-products) and [SOP-CR-010](/sops/cr-010).

## Privacy and data transfer

Privacy obligations apply in full regardless of where trial activities occur. Decentralization creates no exemption from Quebec privacy law (<Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act to modernize personal information protection law (in force Sept 2023). Requires EFVP before sharing personal data without consent." headline="Law 25 / Loi 25" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#law-25">Law 25</Tooltip> / P-39.1), the MUHC Confidentiality Agreement, or MUHC information security requirements. When participant health data is transmitted from a home, community, or satellite setting to the main MUHC site, the transfer must use a secure encrypted channel consistent with what was described in the EFVP submission.

Decentralized elements that cross provincial boundaries introduce additional privacy considerations — Quebec privacy law governs data collected in Quebec, but activities in another province may engage that province's framework. As per [SOP-CR-019](/sops/cr-019). See also [Privacy and Confidentiality in Quebec](/kb/conduct/privacy) and [Research Data Security](/kb/conduct/data-security).

## Third-party involvement and agreements

When trial activities are delegated to individuals or organizations at other locations — a home nursing service, a community pharmacy, a local imaging facility, a CRO conducting remote monitoring — a written agreement must be in place before those activities begin. All personnel performing delegated activities must appear on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> with their specific responsibilities, and study-specific training must be completed before they begin.

Health Canada requires written agreements for decentralized activities to address:

- Individual roles and responsibilities
- Required qualifications of personnel
- How activities will be conducted and monitored for protocol compliance
- Professional liability among sponsors, QIs, sub-investigators, and third parties
- Procedures for <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip> reporting
- Indemnity coverage for participants
- Any considerations specific to community or Indigenous community contexts

Records of all agreements must be maintained for **15 years** and must be accessible from the main MUHC location — they may be reviewed during an inspection. As per [SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-013).

## Inspections

Health Canada inspects decentralized trials under the same <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> framework as conventional trials. For a remote inspection, all records must be accessible from the main MUHC location — including records from delegated activities at other locations.

ICH E6(R3) requires that digital health technologies and electronic record systems support immediate access for inspection: audit trails, access logs, and data governance documentation must be inspection-ready from activation, not retrofitted after the fact. Platform selection is therefore an inspection readiness decision, not only a technical one.

For full inspection preparation guidance, see [Monitoring, Audits and Inspections](/kb/conduct/monitoring-audits) and [SOP-CR-017](/sops/cr-017).
