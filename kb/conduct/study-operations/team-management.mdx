Study teams change while a study runs. New members join, others leave or take a leave of absence, and sometimes the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, has to be replaced. Each change is recorded on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>, so that the log shows at any time who may do each study task. Some changes must also be reported to the sponsor, the Research Ethics Board (REB) or Health Canada.

Who may join a team, and which tasks each person may be given, are on [Building Your Research Team](/kb/team/building-your-team).

## Keep the Task Delegation Log up to date

[The log is completed for each team member before they conduct any study task](/sops/cr-002#5-6-1), and it should be kept up to date throughout the study. Any change in staff or in delegated tasks is documented on it. When a person leaves the study, the log's grey area is completed for them.

The principal investigator's signature on the log confirms that each person is [qualified and trained for the tasks delegated to them](/kb/team/building-your-team#delegate-every-task-on-the-task-delegation-log). The principal investigator keeps ultimate responsibility for the conduct of the study, however its tasks are shared out.

<Warning>
Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.
</Warning>

During monitoring visits, the monitor checks that the log is current and that it shows who is working on the study. A log that does not match the team, such as one with no end date for a person who has left or with tasks given to people not yet trained, is a common finding in monitoring visits and regulatory inspections.

## Check the log at every update

The errors below appear most frequently in monitoring reports and Health Canada inspections. Consider checking the log for each of them before the principal investigator signs it, and at every update:

- A task done before the person's entry on the log was signed. Training is documented first, the principal investigator and the person then sign the log, and only then is the task done. [A delegation of the investigator's responsibilities must be documented on the log before the task is done](/sops/cr-002#5-3-6), and a delegation is never recorded after the task.
- A resident on rotation named as a sub-investigator. [The rules for fellows and residents](/kb/team/building-your-team#fellows-and-residents-on-a-research-team) say who may be named a sub-investigator and what they may be delegated. Fellows and longitudinal residents may also be delegated administrative and data-related tasks.
- No backup named for the principal investigator, or a backup without the principal investigator's credentials and training. [Name a backup for the principal investigator](#name-a-backup-for-the-principal-investigator) gives the rules.
- A volunteer given a task that involves participants. Volunteers and other people who are not affiliated with the RI-MUHC or the MUHC may not contact or recruit research participants, or perform procedures on them, because they are not covered by professional or academic liability insurance. Membership in a professional order makes no exception. Volunteers may not access identifiable patient health records, such as those in [OACIS](/apps/oacis), the MUHC's electronic medical record system, either. [Who cannot work with participants](/training/compliance-requirements#who-cannot-work-with-participants) lists everyone the rule covers.
- A training certificate that expires while the study runs. During the study, [the principal investigator must ensure that the people delegated tasks keep the study's level of institutional training](/sops/cr-002#5-3-6). Consider checking the expiry date of each team member's certificates against the expected end of the study as well as its start. A team member whose certificate has expired may not perform delegated tasks until they renew it. [Training and Compliance Requirements](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level) gives each certificate's renewal period.
- The MUHC Pharmacy's own log missing from the study's records. When the MUHC Pharmacy has study tasks, it keeps its own Pharmacy Task Delegation Log. [The completed pharmacy log must be obtained from the pharmacy and added to the study's Task Delegation Log](/sops/cr-002#5-5-2) before the log is archived with the essential documents. Consider confirming with the responsible pharmacist how the pharmacy's log will be handled when the study is activated, rather than at close-out.

## Add a new team member

When someone joins a study that is already running, their training comes first and their signature on the log second.

- Before signing the log, everyone on a study [should hold the study's level of institutional training, or a higher level](/training/compliance-requirements#training-requirements-by-study-level). [The principal investigator must ensure](/sops/cr-002#5-3-4) that each team member has obtained the level of institutional training the study requires. The principal investigator must also ensure that each team member's training is documented before delegating study tasks to them.
- Training on the protocol and the study documents must be documented. [New staff's initial training should be documented on a Training Log](/sops/cr-003), including training they give themselves by reading. Training that gives no certificate or dated printout, such as reading the protocol or the Investigator's Brochure, must be documented on a Training Log.
- An assessment of the new team member's knowledge of the applicable clinical research regulations and guidelines should be conducted during onboarding, to inform their training needs. The type and results of any such assessment must be kept in the training files.
- [Before the study starts, the principal investigator must ensure](/sops/cr-002#5-3-5) that team members' documented training and current CVs and licences are on file and available for audit and inspection.
- The new team member and the principal investigator then sign the log. The principal investigator's signature confirms that the person is qualified and trained for the tasks delegated to them.

When study tasks are delegated to a department or a team on rotation, [its manager or supervisor is added to the log](/kb/team/building-your-team#fill-the-core-roles), not each member of its staff, provided that evidence of the staff's qualification is available. The pharmacy, a laboratory, nursing and imaging are examples of such teams.

When the protocol or a study document such as the Investigator's Brochure is amended, staff must be trained on the amendment in a timely fashion, directly after the REB approves it. [The investigator must ensure that this training is documented](/sops/cr-002#5-1-3).

## Name a backup for the principal investigator

A sub-investigator [should be named as the principal investigator's backup](/sops/cr-002#5-1-2) when the study is being prepared, not identified informally after the fact. On the [Task Delegation Log](/sops/cr-002#appendix-1), the backup is delegated the task "Study oversight in absence of QI".

The backup must hold the same Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status, qualifications and institutional training as the principal investigator, or better. The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) do not allow a sub-investigator without them to serve as the backup, whatever their clinical seniority. A fellow or a resident cannot be the backup.

## Leave of absence

If a team member is on leave for more than 6 months, [make sure that the Task Delegation Log shows them as undelegated and replaced by a backup study member with appropriate qualifications and training](/sops/cr-002#5-4-2) while they are away.

When they return, they must redo their institutional training at the level their studies then require, even if their certificate has not expired. Other training may be required by their supervisor, or if their study level goes up. Make sure their training is updated before they are delegated again on the log. Update the study contact list and notify the sponsor of the change in staff.

## Change the principal investigator

A change of principal investigator during a study involves the sponsor, the REB and, in some studies, Health Canada, and each must be dealt with.

When the role must be reassigned, for example because the principal investigator no longer works at the MUHC, [a new principal investigator should be named](/sops/cr-002#5-6-4), such as a qualified sub-investigator with Privileges or the head of the department with the same qualifications.

Before taking on the role, the incoming principal investigator must hold [the credential required for their profession and study](/training/compliance-requirements#human-research-privileges-and-researcher-status) or a written institutional authorization. Researcher Status covers only research that does not require medical oversight. The incoming principal investigator must also hold [the level of institutional training the study requires](/sops/cr-002#5-3-3) before taking on the role.

1. The outgoing principal investigator delegates all the principal investigator's tasks to the incoming principal investigator on the log, and both sign. The outgoing principal investigator's departure is recorded on the log. The change should be documented on the [Study Team Member Change form](/sops/cr-002#appendix-5).
2. Inform the sponsor. The contract must be revised and signed by both the outgoing and the incoming principal investigator.
3. Inform the REB without delay through an amendment in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> (Form F1). A change of principal investigator is an amendment that [the REB must review and approve before it takes effect](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#know-which-changes-need-an-amendment). Do not treat it as an administrative change.
4. Update the [undertakings and forms Health Canada requires](#update-the-undertakings-and-forms-to-health-canada).
5. Confirm the backup. ### Update the undertakings and forms to Health Canada

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span></div>

For a drug or natural health product trial, the [Qualified Investigator's Undertaking (QIU)](/kb/foundations/glossary#qualified-investigators-undertaking-qiu) must be reissued under the incoming principal investigator's name. The QIU is the Canadian equivalent of the US Food and Drug Administration's form 1572. By signing it, the qualified investigator agrees to conduct the study in accordance with Canadian regulations and guidelines.

For a drug trial, the sponsor must also submit a new Clinical Trial Site Information Form to Health Canada, and keep a new QIU form. For a natural health product trial, a change of qualified investigator at a site after the trial has started there is one of the changes the sponsor notifies to Health Canada, with the QIU form.

For a medical device study, the manufacturer's or importer's records [must contain a written undertaking from each qualified investigator](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/section-81.html). For a Class II to IV device, a change to the list of qualified investigators is one of the changes for which the manufacturer or importer [may request a revised authorization](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/investigational-testing-authorizations-guidance/guidance-document.html#2.5) from Health Canada. Health Canada's authorization names each qualified investigator to whom the device may be sold.
