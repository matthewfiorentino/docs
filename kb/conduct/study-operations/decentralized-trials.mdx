Your protocol is approved and includes decentralized elements. This page covers the conduct obligations for each element. For design decisions made before protocol submission, see Designing a Decentralized Trial.

## Virtual Site Initiation Visits

Virtual SIVs are acceptable. When the <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip> is conducted by teleconference or video, the attendee list from the virtual meeting platform is acceptable in place of wet ink signatures on the training log, and must be filed in the <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">TMF</Tooltip> and <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> alongside the SIV report.

All SIV prerequisites apply regardless of format — Health Canada authorization, <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approval, and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> must all be in place before the SIV can be scheduled. As per [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005).

## Remote monitoring

Remote monitoring visits follow the same documentation requirements as in-person visits. Every remote visit must be documented with a written report and a follow-up monitoring letter, both filed in the TMF and clearly labelled as a Remote Monitoring Visit.

For remote SDV and SDR, all source data shared with the monitor must be de-identified before transfer — nominative information replaced with the participant's study code — and uploaded into a secure, password-protected validated database or transferred via an encrypted channel. As per [SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-013).

Health Canada requires a minimum of annual monitoring regardless of enrolment rate or whether monitoring is remote or on-site. ICH E6(R3) — effective April 1, 2026 in Canada — moves risk-based monitoring from an option to a core expectation: a mix of centralized, remote, and targeted on-site visits tailored to the specific risks of the trial is the expected approach. The traditional model of regular on-site SDV is no longer the default.

## Electronic informed consent (eICF)

Remote consent using a validated electronic platform is permitted. The process must be documented in a written Working Instruction (WI) before any eICF is used, and that WI must have been submitted to the REB alongside the <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">ICF</Tooltip> and acknowledged before use with participants.

### Platform requirements

- Validated and compliant with Health Canada GUI-0100 and 21 CFR Part 11
- Can confirm the signer's identity; captures at least two signatures (participant and consent obtainer)
- Can produce a signed hard copy of the consent form for the participant
- All signed eICFs stored and retrievable for **15 years** (HC-regulated studies)
- Documented procedures and user training in place before first use

A compliant eSignature must include: signer's printed name, date and time stamp, unique user ID code, digital adopted signature, signing reason, and audit trail.

### Working Instruction requirements

The WI must describe:
- How the eICF will be explained and discussed with the participant
- Security questions used to confirm identity
- How participant understanding will be assessed
- Whether a paper copy option is available and how it will be provided

<Warning>
**REB acknowledgement required before first use.** All eICFs, amendments, addenda, participant-facing materials, and the written remote consent WI must have been submitted to and acknowledged by the REB before the eICF is used with any participant. Consider whether the electronic method could impede consent or assent for anyone in the population being recruited.
</Warning>

For the full eICF requirements in the context of Quebec consent law, LAR rules, and re-consent triggers, see Consent in Quebec. As per [SOP-CR-008](/sops/cr-008).

## Direct-to-participant investigational products

When the protocol includes shipping an investigational drug directly to a participant's home or dispensing it through a local pharmacy, Quebec's pharmacy rules apply without exception, as they do at the MUHC pharmacy. The Ordre des pharmaciens du Québec (OPQ), Quebec's order of pharmacists, requires a pharmacist's professional oversight wherever the drug is dispensed.

For direct-to-home shipping: chain-of-custody must be documented at every step; temperature and storage requirements must be maintained during transit; receipt by the participant must be confirmed; and unused or returned product must be accounted for. If the IP is a controlled substance or has cold-chain requirements, local handling obligations apply at the delivery end. The MUHC pharmacy must have been engaged during study design and a pharmacy agreement must be in place before the study begins. See [Investigational Products in Quebec](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products) and [SOP-CR-010](/sops/cr-010).

## Privacy and data transfer

Privacy obligations apply in full regardless of where trial activities occur. Decentralization creates no exemption from Quebec privacy law, the MUHC Confidentiality Agreement, or MUHC information security requirements. When participant health data is transmitted from a home, community, or satellite setting to the main MUHC site, the transfer must use a secure encrypted channel consistent with what was described in the EFVP submission.

Decentralized elements that cross provincial boundaries may raise additional privacy considerations, since Law 5 (the Act respecting health and social services information) governs the health and social services information the MUHC holds, wherever it was collected, while activities in another province may engage that province's framework. See also [Privacy and Confidentiality](/kb/governance/privacy) and [Storing and Sharing Human Data](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security).

## Third-party involvement and agreements

When trial activities are delegated to individuals or organizations at other locations — a home nursing service, a community pharmacy, a local imaging facility, a CRO conducting remote monitoring — a written agreement must be in place before those activities begin. All personnel performing delegated activities must appear on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> with their specific responsibilities, and study-specific training must be completed before they begin.

Health Canada requires written agreements for decentralized activities to address:

- Individual roles and responsibilities
- Required qualifications of personnel
- How activities will be conducted and monitored for protocol compliance
- Professional liability among sponsors, QIs, sub-investigators, and third parties
- Procedures for <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip> reporting
- Indemnity coverage for participants
- Any considerations specific to community or Indigenous community contexts

Records of all agreements must be available or accessible from the main MUHC location, and Health Canada may review them during an inspection; keep them with the study's essential documents for its retention period.

## Inspections

Health Canada inspects decentralized trials against the same <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> requirements as conventional trials, in person or remotely. Whatever the kind of inspection, all records of activities conducted away from the MUHC, delegated ones included, must be accessible for inspection at the main MUHC location.

ICH E6(R3) requires that digital health technologies and electronic record systems support immediate access for inspection: audit trails, access logs, and data governance documentation must be inspection-ready from activation, not retrofitted after the fact. Platform selection is therefore an inspection readiness decision, not only a technical one.

For full inspection preparation guidance, see [Monitoring, Audits and Inspections](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits) and [SOP-CR-017](/sops/cr-017).

## Canadian regulatory considerations

### Scope of the Health Canada DCT guidance

Health Canada published its final [guidance on clinical trials with decentralized elements](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/clinical-trials-decentralized-elements.html) on Oct. 27, 2026, replacing the December 2025 draft. It applies to **drug trials**, biologics and radiopharmaceuticals included. It does not cover:

- Medical device trials (regulated under Part 3 of the Medical Devices Regulations)
- Natural health product trials
- Food-for-special-dietary-purposes studies

If your decentralized trial involves a medical device or natural health product, no current Health Canada guidance is specific to DCT conduct for those products. Health Canada notes that similar principles apply, but it regulates device and natural health product trials through their own regulations; use the drug guidance as a good-practice reference only. Contact [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) before finalizing a DCT protocol for a device or NHP to assess the regulatory posture.

Health Canada's guidance documents are not law, and some flexibility is possible. In a drug trial, an approach that differs from the guidance must be supported by adequate justification and should be discussed with Health Canada in advance.

### Quebec pharmacy law — Bill 67 (OPQ scope expansion)

Quebec's Bill 67, An Act to amend the Professional Code for the modernization of the professional system and to broaden certain professional practices in the field of health and social services (assented Nov. 7, 2024), broadens the professional activities of pharmacists from Dec. 9, 2026, in ways relevant to direct-to-participant IP dispensing in DCTs. Ask the MUHC Research Pharmacy how these changes apply to your study.

Among the changes relevant to clinical research:
- From Dec. 9, 2026, pharmacists may initiate and modify certain drug therapies without a physician's prescription in defined circumstances, a scope expansion that affects how pharmacist oversight is structured in a DCT where IP is dispensed outside the MUHC pharmacy
- The expanded authority does not eliminate the requirement for pharmacist professional involvement in IP dispensing, but it changes the practical workflow for studies where a community pharmacist is engaged as a local dispensing point

If your DCT protocol routes IP through community pharmacies (rather than direct-to-home shipping), the MUHC pharmacy and the community pharmacy must have a written agreement that addresses the expanded pharmacist scope, accountability for GCP-compliant dispensing, and how accountability is maintained under both the clinical trial protocol and OPQ standards.

<Note>
Bill 67 is implemented through OPQ professional frameworks that continue to evolve. Confirm the current OPQ-approved practice scope with the MUHC pharmacy before finalizing direct-to-participant dispensing arrangements.
</Note>

### Provincial telemedicine licensure for virtual visits

When a physician or nurse practitioner at the RI-MUHC conducts a virtual visit with a participant who is physically located in another province, they are practicing medicine (or nursing) in that other province — not in Quebec. Each province regulates medical practice within its borders.

**For participants located in Ontario during a virtual visit:** The College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario (CPSO) has temporary provisions allowing registered Quebec physicians to conduct virtual care for existing patients, but these provisions have evolved since the pandemic. Confirm current CPSO rules before conducting Ontario virtual visits. The situation differs for Nurse Practitioners (regulated separately) and Allied Health Professionals.

**For participants located in other provinces:** Rules vary. Some colleges have cross-border telemedicine provisions; others do not. Provincial nursing regulatory bodies (CNO in Ontario, CRNNS in Nova Scotia, etc.) have their own rules.

**Practical approach for RI-MUHC DCTs:**
- If the study protocol includes virtual visits, the REB submission and the protocol should specify where participants will be located during those visits
- For participants located in Quebec during all virtual interactions, no out-of-province licensure is required
- For studies explicitly enrolling participants from multiple provinces, the QI must hold licensure (or be covered by a telemedicine agreement) in each province where participants will be during virtual visits, or delegate those visits to a sub-investigator who holds appropriate licensure in that province
- Contact the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) early if your protocol involves multi-provincial virtual visits — this is a setup item, not a conduct item
