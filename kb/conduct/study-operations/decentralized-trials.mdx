Once a trial with decentralized elements is approved, some of its activities take place away from the MUHC: a site initiation visit by video, a monitor working remotely, consent signed on a screen, a drug sent to a participant's home or a visit with a participant in another province.

The rules for each activity are the same as at the hospital. What changes is how the site documents the activity, who carries it out and where the records are kept. [Designing a Decentralized Trial](/kb/design/dct-design) covers the decisions made before the protocol is submitted.

## Check which guidance applies

Health Canada's [guidance for clinical trials with decentralized elements](/kb/design/dct-design#check-which-guidance-applies) covers drug trials only. Before you finalize a decentralized element of a device or natural health product trial, ask [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) about the rules that apply to it.

Health Canada's guidance documents are administrative, not legal, instruments, so some flexibility is possible. In a drug trial, an approach that differs from the guidance must be supported by adequate justification. The approach should be discussed with Health Canada in advance.

## Prepare for remote monitoring

Monitoring can be done on site, remotely or both, as [the trial's monitoring plan](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#risk-based-monitoring-under-ich-e6-r3) sets out. Under the good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), ICH E6(R3), the monitoring approach should take decentralized settings into account and be included in the monitoring plan.

The monitor writes a report after each visit, remote or on site, and sends the site a monitoring letter. [Make sure that the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, initials and dates the letter](/sops/cr-013#5-11-3) to document medical oversight. File the initialed and dated letter in the investigator site file. The RI-MUHC's monitoring plan template has a report from a remote monitoring visit clearly marked as such on the report and on the monitoring letter.

[Ensure that no study document with participant identifiers leaves the institution](/sops/cr-013#5-10-5). Documents should be de-identified if the monitor needs a copy for their files. For remote source data verification and source data review, [it is important that all data shared from the site is redacted](/sops/cr-013#appendix-3), with nominative information replaced by the participant's study code. The de-identified information is then uploaded electronically into a secure, password-protected, validated database, sent by email, or transferred through an encrypted channel.

Health Canada requires a minimum of annual monitoring regardless of enrolment rate or whether monitoring is remote or on site.

## Send investigational drugs to participants

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs require that any medication to be administered to a patient, an investigational drug included, be dispensed by a pharmacist](/kb/conduct/study-operations/investigational-products). The rule applies when a trial ships an investigational drug to a participant's home or dispenses it through a local pharmacy, as it does at the MUHC pharmacy.

When a drug is shipped to participants' homes:

- Document the chain of custody at every step.
- Maintain the drug's temperature and storage requirements during transport.
- Confirm that the participant received it.
- Account for unused or returned product.

If the drug is a controlled substance or needs a cold chain, local handling obligations apply where it is delivered. [SOP-CR-010](/sops/cr-010) covers the management of investigational products.

### Check how Bill 67 affects dispensing

Quebec's Bill 67, An Act to amend the Professional Code for the modernization of the professional system and to broaden certain professional practices in the field of health and social services (assented Nov. 7, 2024), broadens the professional activities of pharmacists, members of the Ordre des pharmaciens du Québec (OPQ), from Dec. 9, 2026. The change bears on dispensing investigational drugs to participants in a decentralized trial. Ask the MUHC Research Pharmacy how it applies to your study.

The changes that bear on clinical research include:

- From Dec. 9, 2026, pharmacists may start and adjust certain drug therapies without a physician's prescription, in defined circumstances. The change affects how a pharmacist's oversight is organized in a decentralized trial that dispenses the drug outside the MUHC pharmacy.
- The broader activities don't remove the requirement that a pharmacist take part in dispensing the investigational drug. The broader activities change the workflow for a study that uses a community pharmacist as a local dispensing point.

If the protocol sends the drug through community pharmacies, rather than shipping it to homes, the MUHC pharmacy and the community pharmacy must have a written agreement. The agreement addresses the pharmacists' broader activities, accountability for dispensing in line with good clinical practice, and how that accountability is kept under both the protocol and the OPQ's standards.

<Note>
The OPQ puts Bill 67 into practice through professional frameworks that are still changing. Confirm the practice the OPQ currently approves with the MUHC pharmacy before you finalize arrangements to dispense the drug to participants.
</Note>

## Protect participant information sent from other locations

Privacy obligations apply in full wherever trial activities take place. Decentralization creates no exemption from Quebec's privacy law, the MUHC Confidentiality Agreement or the MUHC's information security requirements. When participants' health information is sent from a home, a community setting or a satellite site to the main MUHC site, the transfer must use a secure, encrypted channel consistent with what was described in the submission for the [privacy impact assessment (EFVP)](/kb/governance/privacy#know-when-an-efvp-is-needed).

Decentralized elements that cross provincial boundaries may raise other privacy considerations. The Act respecting health and social services information governs the health and social services information the MUHC holds, wherever it was collected. Activities in another province may also engage that province's framework. [Privacy and Confidentiality](/kb/governance/privacy) and [Storing and Sharing Human Data](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security) cover both.

### Document and oversee delegated activities in a drug trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span></div>

In a drug trial, the activities that the sponsor or the qualified investigator delegates to service providers [should be documented in written agreements](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/clinical-trials-decentralized-elements/conducting-trial-delegating-activities.html#a3), with the division of responsibilities defined.

The sponsor's agreements with the qualified investigator or the institution, service providers and other parties involved in a drug trial [should be documented before the activities start](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100/good-clinical-practices.html#c.05c). In a drug trial, trial-related documentation should distinguish the activities that need an agreement from those that fall within routine clinical, imaging or laboratory practice, where delegation documentation or an agreement may not be necessary.

A written agreement can set out:

- Individual roles and responsibilities, including who performs which activity and where.
- The qualifications required of the personnel.
- How activities will be conducted and monitored for compliance with the protocol.
- Professional liability among sponsors, qualified investigators, sub-investigators and third parties, local health care providers included.
- Procedures for safety monitoring and timely reporting of <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant who has been given an investigational product, whether or not it is related to that product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">adverse events</Tooltip>.
- Indemnity coverage for participants.
- Any considerations specific to particular groups, communities and Indigenous communities.

In a drug trial, records of all agreements and contracts must be available or accessible from the main location of the trial site. Health Canada may review them during an inspection. Keep them with the study's essential documents for [its retention period](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).

In a drug trial, the qualified investigator must ensure that:

- Delegated activities are conducted in accordance with the approved protocol, applicable regulatory requirements and good clinical practice.
- The people and organizations to whom activities are delegated have the education, training, experience, qualifications and resources needed to perform their responsibilities safely and in compliance with the protocol and regulatory requirements.

In a drug trial, the sponsor and the qualified investigator must also ensure that all parties understand and adhere to the approved protocol, applicable regulatory requirements and any procedures needed to protect participant safety, data integrity and privacy.

In a drug trial, Health Canada's guidance says that routine clinical procedures, such as routine blood tests, medical imaging or standard-of-care procedures by local health care providers, generally don't require trial-specific training and delegation from the qualified investigator. Where feasible, the qualified investigator should confirm that the people who perform these routine procedures are qualified, for example by checking their licence or certification.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs require the principal investigator to sign the Task Delegation Log](/sops/cr-002#5-1-2) to confirm that each staff member is qualified and trained for the study tasks delegated to them. In a drug trial, the qualified investigator should also sign and date the Task Delegation Log before a task is delegated. In a drug trial, the delegation log and the other documents used to manage delegated activities must be available at the main location of the trial site.

## Be ready for an inspection

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span></div>

Health Canada inspects drug trials with decentralized elements for adherence to good clinical practice, as it does other trials. Its inspections may be in person or remote. The main location of the trial site, where the qualified investigator works, is the first point of contact for an inspection. In a drug trial, all relevant information and records of activities conducted remotely, delegated activities included, must be accessible for inspection at the main location of the trial site.

Inspectors may ask the sponsor and the qualified investigator for documents showing how delegated parties were coordinated, overseen and monitored, and how participants' safety was protected.

For a drug that needs specific storage conditions, for example, inspectors may ask for documents showing that it was stored appropriately while transported to remote locations. Health Canada may also inspect other places where study activities took place. Health Canada would not inspect a participant's home except in rare cases of serious and time-sensitive concerns for health or safety.

In a drug trial, the sponsor and the qualified investigator should make sure that the consent process tells participants about a possible visit from monitors working for the sponsor, or from regulatory inspectors.

Under ICH E6(R3), the investigator or institution should make all requested trial-related records available for direct access when the monitor, an auditor, the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) or a regulatory authority asks.

Under ICH E6(R3), the responsible party's approach to the metadata of data of higher criticality should include evaluating each computerized system to ensure that it keeps logs of user account creation, changes to user roles and permissions, and user access. The approach should also ensure that audit trails, reports and logs are not disabled.

[Designing a Decentralized Trial](/kb/design/dct-design#plan-wearables-and-other-digital-health-technologies) covers the audit trail a platform should produce from the day it is activated.

[Monitoring, Audits and Inspections](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits) and [SOP-CR-017](/sops/cr-017) cover the preparation for an inspection.
