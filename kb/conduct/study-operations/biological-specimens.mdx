## QI and team responsibilities

Under [SOP-CR-011](/sops/cr-011), the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI holds primary responsibility for the safe and compliant management of biological specimens throughout the study. This includes ensuring that all staff handling specimens are qualified, trained on the study-specific tasks delegated to them, and aware of the health and safety risks involved.

Before specimen collection begins, the QI must assess the biosafety risks associated with the collection and analysis of specimens and ensure appropriate precautionary measures are in place. Safety information must be made available to all persons who will come into contact with specimens. For biosafety training schedules, contact the RI-MUHC Environmental Health and Safety Officer at ri.ehs@muhc.mcgill.ca.

Make sure the people who handle specimens receive training in the appropriate safety practices, and ensure the safety and well-being of participants while specimens are collected.{/* Source: SOP-CR-011, RI-MUHC Addendum, 1.1.4 ("ensure that these persons receive training in the appropriate safety practices") and 1.1.5 ("Ensure the safety and well-being of subjects during the collection of specimens."). Added Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, list 1). */}

<Warning>
**Delegation applies here.** Specimen handling tasks must appear on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> before any team member performs them. All specimen collection must follow the Sponsor's lab manual, which specifies collection tubes, volumes, timing windows, and processing requirements for each sample type. Deviations from the lab manual — wrong tube, missed window, incorrect processing temperature — must be documented as protocol deviations and reported to the Sponsor.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): is every departure from the sponsor's lab manual a protocol deviation to report to the sponsor (with the storage question below)? All laboratory abnormalities identified in results should be reported to the QI/PI for assessment. The QI's assessment of each one's clinical significance (CS or NCS) should be printed, initialled and dated by the QI. Abnormalities judged clinically significant should be documented as the protocol describes{/* Source: SOP-CR-011, RI-MUHC Addendum, 1.1.3 ("All laboratory abnormalities should be reported to the QI/Investigator for assessment and all assessments (clinical significance e.g. c.s. or n.c.s.) should be printed, initialed and dated by the Investigator. The following laboratory records must be retained in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> throughout the study:

- Normal ranges for all study-specific tests
- Laboratory certification and/or accreditation documentation
- Established quality control and external quality assessment records
- Laboratory results and reports
- X-ray films, digital images, microfilms, or compact disks where applicable

## Storage and temperature monitoring

Specimens must be stored under the conditions specified in the Sponsor's lab manual — typically at defined refrigerator, freezer, or room temperature ranges. Any deviation from required storage conditions is a protocol deviation and must be documented and reported.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): is every departure from the required storage conditions, or from the sponsor's lab manual, a protocol deviation to report to the sponsor? SOP-CR-011 has specimens stored and processed per the protocol or the sponsor's documents and does not classify a departure. In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P3-4, P3-7). */}

[Store specimens in a secure and suitable environment](/sops/cr-011#5-2-1), with access controlled and limited to authorized personnel, and respect the storage time that the protocol or other study documents set. Make sure centrifuges, refrigerators and freezers are calibrated and checked on a regular maintenance schedule, and file the maintenance records with the essential study documents. Prepare an emergency response plan to protect the specimens.

When specimens are to be destroyed, destroy them as the protocol and the institution require, and file the destruction records with the essential study documents.{/* Source: SOP-CR-011, sections 5.1.3 ("Ensure that equipment such as centrifuges, storage refrigerators and freezers are calibrated and checked on a regular maintenance schedule. Retain any documentation connected with maintenance of the equipment with the essential study documentation."), 5.1.4 ("Prepare an emergency response plan to ensure the integrity of the biological specimens."), 5.2.1 ("a secure and suitable environment … Establish and maintain controlled access for authorized personnel."), 5.2.2 ("Ensure adherence to the storage time for biological specimens, as defined in the protocol, or other study documentation."), 5.3.1 and 5.3.2 ("Destroy biological specimens, according to the requirements of the protocol, and of the institution."; "File destruction records with the essential study documentation."). Added Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, list 1). */}

If no centralized temperature monitoring system is used, the QI should ensure that a temperature log is maintained for each refrigerator and freezer that stores study specimens. The log should record the minimum, maximum and actual temperature each day, with the initials of the person who recorded them and an explanation of any temperature excursion.{/* Source: SOP-CR-025, RI-MUHC Addendum ("If no centralized temperature monitoring system is used, the QI/Investigator should ensure that a temperature log is maintained for each refrigerator and freezer used for storage of biological specimens and investigational product. Until Nov. 15, 2026: "Temperature monitoring logs must be maintained for each storage unit" (facts-21, P3-8). */} The standard temperature log form is in [SOP-CR-025](/sops/cr-025). The log is an essential document and must be filed in the ISF.

**Temperature excursions must be reported promptly.** If a storage unit goes outside the acceptable range, document the excursion immediately, notify the Sponsor, and follow their instructions before moving or using affected specimens. Do not assume specimens are still usable — that determination belongs to the Sponsor or central lab.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): a temperature excursion in a specimen freezer: notify the sponsor and follow its instructions? ## Shipping and transport

Biological specimens shipped off-site — to a central laboratory, a Sponsor facility, or another institution — are classified as dangerous goods under Canadian and international regulations. The specific classification determines packaging, labelling, and documentation requirements: Make sure the packaging, labelling and shipping meet the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations for ground transport and the International Air Transport Association's (IATA's) standards for air. Each shipment should be documented, and the documentation kept with the essential study documents.{/* Source: SOP-CR-011, RI-MUHC Addendum, 1.2.1 ("Ensure that appropriate packaging, labelling and shipping standards are applied, according to Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulation (TDG-for ground transportation) and International Air Transportation Association (IATA-for air transportation). The shipping process should be documented and the documentation kept with the essential documentation of the study."). Added Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, list 1). */}

- **Category A infectious substances** (capable of causing permanent disability or life-threatening disease) — require UN 2814 or UN 2900 classification, a Type P620 packaging, specific labelling,{/* Sources: the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations, s. 5.16(1) (Category A or B only "in a means of containment that is manufactured, selected and used in accordance with CGSB-43.125"); Transport Canada, "Shipping infectious substances" (saved Nov. 15, 2026, evidence/web/tc-shipping-infectious-substances_fetched-2026-11-15/), its table of permitted packaging: Type P620 "Intended for transport of: UN2814 and UN2900 – Category A"; Type P650 "Intended for transport of: UN3373 – Category B". Until Nov. 15, 2026: "triple packaging to P650 specification" (facts-21, P3-11). */} and a completed Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods.
- **Category B biological substances** (diagnostic or clinical specimens not meeting Category A criteria) — require UN 3373 classification and triple packaging to P650 specification. No Shipper's Declaration required, but labelling must comply.
- **Exempt human specimens** — minimal risk specimens that meet specific criteria. Reduced packaging and documentation requirements apply.

For most clinical trial biomarker samples, Category B applies. Confirm the correct classification with the Sponsor's lab manual or central lab before the first shipment.

Ground transport within Canada is governed by the federal **Transportation of Dangerous Goods (TDG) Act and Regulations**. Air transport is governed by **IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations**. Both require that the person preparing or shipping specimens is trained and holds a valid certification.

<Warning>
**Dry ice is also regulated.** Specimens shipped on dry ice require an additional UN 1845 designation and specific quantity declarations. This is frequently missed. Confirm dry ice requirements with the Sponsor's lab manual and ensure your TDG/IATA certification covers dry ice shipments.
</Warning>

## TDG and IATA certification

Anyone who handles, offers for transport or transports dangerous goods, including biological specimens, must be adequately trained and hold a valid training certificate, or work in the presence and under the direct supervision of someone who is adequately trained and holds one. The Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations set this requirement. It is not an internal policy.{/* Source: the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations, s. 6.1(1) ("A person who handles, offers for transport or transports dangerous goods must (a) be adequately trained and hold a training certificate in accordance with this Part; or (b) perform those activities in the presence and under the direct supervision of a person who is adequately trained and who holds a training certificate in accordance with this Part."), the whole text fetched from Justice Laws on Nov. 15, 2026 (batches/b106-facts-first-1/run/web/tdgr-fulltext.txt). Until Nov. 15, 2026 the sentence named "prepares, packages, ships, or receives" and dropped the exception (facts-21, P3-15). */}

| Transport mode | Certificate validity | Notes |
|---------------|---------------------|-------|
| Ground (TDG — road/rail within Canada) | 3 years | Governed by Transport Canada TDG Act. Certificate must show employer name and address and be signed by both the employee and supervisor. |
| Air (IATA) | 2 years | Governed by IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations. Required by all major air carriers (FedEx, UPS, DHL). Recertification required before expiry. |

**TDG/IATA training is available through CITI Program.** The course "CITI Canada — Transportation of Dangerous Goods (TDG/IATA)" is available under the RI-MUHC institutional licence at no additional cost. See External Training Resources for registration instructions and access.{/* TO CONFIRM (the Training Advisor): the CITI course's name and whether it is free under the RI-MUHC's licence; SOP-CR-011's addendum (1.2.2): training "is available through the CITI link under Continuing Education in the RI portal". In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P3-18). */}

The certification must be on file in the ISF for any team member who ships specimens. Anyone who handles, offers for transport or transports dangerous goods, or directly supervises someone who does, must give their training certificate, or a copy of it, to an inspector immediately on request.{/* Source: the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations, s. 6.8 ("A person who handles, offers for transport or transports dangerous goods, or who directly supervises another person engaged in these activities, must give his or her training certificate, or a copy of it, to an inspector immediately on request."). Added Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, list 1). */} A team member whose TDG or IATA certificate has expired must not ship specimens until recertified — arrange recertification before expiry, not after. Lapsed shipping certifications are a recurring inspection finding.

## Documentation requirements

Specimen management generates several essential documents that must be maintained in the ISF throughout the study and retained for the required period after close-out. At minimum, the following must be on file:

- **Specimen collection log** — records collection date and time, processing details (centrifuge serial number, speed, duration, temperature), freezer serial number, time placed in storage, and staff initials for each sample at each visit. [SOP-CR-011](/sops/cr-011) Appendix 1 provides the standard form.
- **Shipment log** — records date of shipment, sample type, shipping conditions (ambient, dry ice), courier waybill number, and any issues or deviations.
- **Temperature logs** — daily monitoring records for each storage unit used for study specimens.
- **TDG/IATA certificates** — valid certificates for all team members who ship specimens, filed with training documents.
- **Laboratory accreditation and normal ranges** — current documentation for all labs processing study specimens.

Monitors verify specimen documentation during monitoring visits as part of Source Data Verification. Gaps in the collection log — missing times, unsigned entries, undocumented deviations — are among the most common findings. The log should be completed at the time of collection, not reconstructed later.

## Biobanking and long-term specimen governance

When specimens are stored for future unspecified research rather than for a defined study protocol, additional governance considerations apply.

### Consent models for future use

Consent for biobank specimens can take several forms:{/* TCPS 2 sets the rules for consent to a specific project and for broad consent (chapter 3, articles 3.2 and 3.13; chapter 12 refers to them). It does not name tiered consent: the third item is the Hub's own description of a common practice. Chapters 3, 4, 11 and 12 read Nov. 15, 2026 (evidence/web/tcps2-2022-chapter12_fetched-2026-11-15/). Until that day the lead-in said "Under TCPS2 (2022) Chapter 12" (facts-21, P3-23). */}

- **Specific consent** — consent for a defined research project only. Specimens cannot be used for other purposes without additional consent or REB waiver.
- **Broad consent** — consent for storage and use in future unspecified research, subject to any restrictions the participant specifies. The Tri-Council Policy Statement (TCPS2), article 3.13, requires that the consent process address areas of uncertainty about future uses, describe the governance structure overseeing access, and explain withdrawal procedures. When consent to a specific project and consent to storage for future research are sought at the same time, participants must be offered the option to consent to each separately.{/* Source: TCPS 2, article 3.13, Application ("When seeking consent for a specific research project at the same time as seeking consent for storage of data and human biological materials for future unspecified research, prospective participants must be provided with an option to consent to each separately"). Added Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, page 3, list 1). */}
- **Tiered consent** — participants choose which categories of research they consent to (e.g., cancer research only, publicly funded research only, specific institutions only).{/* TO CONFIRM (QA or the REB office): is tiered consent a model the REB recognizes, and on what source? TCPS 2 (chapters 3 and 12) does not name it (b111, row 48b). In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026. */}

<Note>
**Quebec Civil Code caution.** Article 22 of the Quebec Civil Code requires consent for the use of body parts, tissues, or substances removed during care. The interplay between the Civil Code's consent requirements and TCPS2 broad consent models is an area where institutional REB review and legal counsel should be consulted. Do not assume TCPS2 broad consent alone is sufficient in Quebec without addressing the Civil Code.
</Note>

### Access and release

Biobank specimens held at the RI-MUHC are institutional assets. Release of specimens for external research requires:

- A formal access request reviewed by the biobank governance committee or designate{/* TO CONFIRM (the Research Facilitator or QA): which policy governs the release of RI-MUHC biobank specimens? In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P3-26). */}
- REB approval for the proposed use (or a documented REB waiver where applicable)
- A Material Transfer Agreement (MTA) when specimens leave the institution — managed by the Research Agreements Office
- Documentation of consent scope — confirming the proposed use falls within what participants consented to

### Material Transfer Agreements

An MTA governs the transfer of biological materials between institutions. It specifies permitted uses, intellectual property rights, liability, biosafety requirements, and obligations upon completion of the research (return or destruction of remaining material). MTAs are managed by the Research Agreements Office — investigators should not transfer materials without an executed MTA.{/* TO CONFIRM (the Research Agreements Office): may materials be transferred before the agreement is executed? SOP-CR-007 names Material Transfer Agreements among the agreements the Office drafts. In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P3-27). */}
