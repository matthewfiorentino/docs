## QI and team responsibilities

Under [SOP-CR-011](/sops/cr-011), the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI holds primary responsibility for the safe and compliant management of biological specimens throughout the study. This includes ensuring that all staff handling specimens are qualified, trained on the study-specific tasks delegated to them, and aware of the health and safety risks involved.

Before specimen collection begins, the QI must assess the biosafety risks associated with the collection and analysis of specimens and ensure appropriate precautionary measures are in place. Safety information must be made available to all persons who will come into contact with specimens. For biosafety training schedules, contact the RI-MUHC Environmental Health and Safety Officer at ri.ehs@muhc.mcgill.ca.

<Warning>
**Delegation applies here.** Specimen handling tasks must appear on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> before any team member performs them. Tasks not on the log are not authorized, regardless of the team member's qualifications.
</Warning>

## Collection and processing

All specimen collection must follow the Sponsor's lab manual, which specifies collection tubes, volumes, timing windows, and processing requirements for each sample type. Deviations from the lab manual — wrong tube, missed window, incorrect processing temperature — must be documented as protocol deviations and reported to the Sponsor.

Laboratory abnormalities identified in results must be reported to the QI/PI for clinical assessment. The QI must assess each abnormality for clinical significance (CS or NCS), and that assessment must be printed, initialled, and dated by the QI. Abnormalities deemed clinically significant must be documented as specified in the protocol — this may trigger <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip> reporting obligations under [SOP-CR-012](/sops/cr-012).

The following laboratory records must be retained in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> throughout the study:

- Normal ranges for all study-specific tests
- Laboratory certification and/or accreditation documentation
- Established quality control and external quality assessment records
- Laboratory results and reports
- X-ray films, digital images, microfilms, or compact disks where applicable

## Storage and temperature monitoring

Specimens must be stored under the conditions specified in the Sponsor's lab manual — typically at defined refrigerator, freezer, or room temperature ranges. Any deviation from required storage conditions is a protocol deviation and must be documented and reported.

Temperature monitoring logs must be maintained for each storage unit used for study specimens. The log captures daily minimum and maximum temperatures, the person who checked them, and any deviations. [SOP-CR-011](/sops/cr-011) Appendix 1 provides the standard temperature log form. The log is an essential document and must be filed in the ISF.

<Warning>
**Temperature excursions must be reported promptly.** If a storage unit goes outside the acceptable range, document the excursion immediately, notify the Sponsor, and follow their instructions before moving or using affected specimens. Do not assume specimens are still usable — that determination belongs to the Sponsor or central lab.
</Warning>

## Shipping and transport

Biological specimens shipped off-site — to a central laboratory, a Sponsor facility, or another institution — are classified as dangerous goods under Canadian and international regulations. The specific classification determines packaging, labelling, and documentation requirements:

- **Category A infectious substances** (capable of causing permanent disability or life-threatening disease) — require UN 2814 or UN 2900 classification, triple packaging to P650 specification, specific labelling, and a completed Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods.
- **Category B biological substances** (diagnostic or clinical specimens not meeting Category A criteria) — require UN 3373 classification and triple packaging to P650 specification. No Shipper's Declaration required, but labelling must comply.
- **Exempt human specimens** — minimal risk specimens that meet specific criteria. Reduced packaging and documentation requirements apply.

For most clinical trial biomarker samples, Category B applies. Confirm the correct classification with the Sponsor's lab manual or central lab before the first shipment.

Ground transport within Canada is governed by the federal **Transportation of Dangerous Goods (TDG) Act and Regulations**. Air transport is governed by **IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations**. Both require that the person preparing or shipping specimens is trained and holds a valid certification.

<Warning>
**Dry ice is also regulated.** Specimens shipped on dry ice require an additional UN 1845 designation and specific quantity declarations. This is frequently missed. Confirm dry ice requirements with the Sponsor's lab manual and ensure your TDG/IATA certification covers dry ice shipments.
</Warning>

## TDG and IATA certification

Any team member who prepares, packages, ships, or receives dangerous goods — including biological specimens — must hold a valid training certificate before performing those tasks. This is a legal requirement under the TDG Act, not an internal policy.

| Transport mode | Certificate validity | Notes |
|---------------|---------------------|-------|
| Ground (TDG — road/rail within Canada) | 3 years | Governed by Transport Canada TDG Act. Certificate must show employer name and address and be signed by both the employee and supervisor. |
| Air (IATA) | 2 years | Governed by IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations. Required by all major air carriers (FedEx, UPS, DHL). Recertification required before expiry. |

<Note>
**TDG/IATA training is available through CITI Program.** The course "CITI Canada — Transportation of Dangerous Goods (TDG/IATA)" is available under the RI-MUHC institutional licence at no additional cost. See External Training Resources for registration instructions and access.
</Note>

The certification must be on file in the ISF for any team member who ships specimens. A team member whose TDG or IATA certificate has expired must not ship specimens until recertified — arrange recertification before expiry, not after. Lapsed shipping certifications are a recurring inspection finding.

## Documentation requirements

Specimen management generates several essential documents that must be maintained in the ISF throughout the study and retained for the required period after close-out. At minimum, the following must be on file:

- **Specimen collection log** — records collection date and time, processing details (centrifuge serial number, speed, duration, temperature), freezer serial number, time placed in storage, and staff initials for each sample at each visit. [SOP-CR-011](/sops/cr-011) Appendix 1 provides the standard form.
- **Shipment log** — records date of shipment, sample type, shipping conditions (ambient, dry ice), courier waybill number, and any issues or deviations.
- **Temperature logs** — daily monitoring records for each storage unit used for study specimens.
- **TDG/IATA certificates** — valid certificates for all team members who ship specimens, filed with training documents.
- **Laboratory accreditation and normal ranges** — current documentation for all labs processing study specimens.

Monitors verify specimen documentation during monitoring visits as part of Source Data Verification. Gaps in the collection log — missing times, unsigned entries, undocumented deviations — are among the most common findings. The log should be completed at the time of collection, not reconstructed later.

## Biobanking and long-term specimen governance

When specimens are stored for future unspecified research rather than for a defined study protocol, additional governance considerations apply.

### Consent models for future use

Under TCPS2 (2022) Chapter 12, consent for biobank specimens can take several forms:

- **Specific consent** — consent for a defined research project only. Specimens cannot be used for other purposes without additional consent or REB waiver.
- **Broad consent** — consent for storage and use in future unspecified research, subject to any restrictions the participant specifies. TCPS2 Article 3.13 requires that the consent process address areas of uncertainty about future uses, describe the governance structure overseeing access, and explain withdrawal procedures.
- **Tiered consent** — participants choose which categories of research they consent to (e.g., cancer research only, publicly funded research only, specific institutions only).

<Note>
**Quebec Civil Code caution.** Article 22 of the Quebec Civil Code requires consent for the use of body parts, tissues, or substances removed during care. The interplay between the Civil Code's consent requirements and TCPS2 broad consent models is an area where institutional REB review and legal counsel should be consulted. Do not assume TCPS2 broad consent alone is sufficient in Quebec without addressing the Civil Code.
</Note>

### Access and release

Biobank specimens held at the RI-MUHC are institutional assets. Release of specimens for external research requires:

- A formal access request reviewed by the biobank governance committee or designate
- REB approval for the proposed use (or a documented REB waiver where applicable)
- A Material Transfer Agreement (MTA) when specimens leave the institution — managed by the Research Agreements Office
- Documentation of consent scope — confirming the proposed use falls within what participants consented to

### Material Transfer Agreements

An MTA governs the transfer of biological materials between institutions. It specifies permitted uses, intellectual property rights, liability, biosafety requirements, and obligations upon completion of the research (return or destruction of remaining material). MTAs are managed by the Research Agreements Office — investigators should not transfer materials without an executed MTA.
