[The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-010#1-1-1) require that any medication given to a patient, whether in care or in a study, be dispensed by a pharmacist. An investigational drug is therefore sent to the MUHC Pharmacy, which stores it and dispenses it to participants. The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, delegates these tasks to the pharmacy but remains responsible for the <Tooltip tip="A pharmaceutical form of an active ingredient or placebo, a medical device or a natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product (IP)</Tooltip>.

The SOPs base the rule on Quebec's regulations and the rules of the Ordre des pharmaciens du Québec (OPQ). There is no equivalent requirement in most other Canadian provinces.

## Know what the pharmacy does and what the principal investigator keeps

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span></div>

[All drugs and some natural health products must be managed by the pharmacy](/sops/cr-007#5-4-4). In the Nagano submission, the investigator must answer the questions that start [the pharmacy's feasibility review](/kb/submission/preparing#answer-the-f11-questions-on-services-and-data-access). A pharmacy agreement then comes to the investigator for signature. [The MUHC Pharmacy must be informed and invited to the site initiation visit](/kb/site-activation/site-setup#prepare-for-the-site-initiation-visit) for training on the protocol and the product.

The principal investigator delegates the product's tasks to the pharmacy and remains responsible for them:

- Reception and inventory.
- Accountability.
- Labelling.
- Storage.
- Dispensing to participants.
- Return and destruction.
- Randomization, where the study uses it.

The MUHC Pharmacy has its own SOPs, and it records the tasks delegated to its staff on its own Pharmacy Task Delegation Log.

The principal investigator must:

- Ensure that the pharmacy is given the IP Receipt Log and the IP Dispensing Log, and that both are completed.
- Ensure that an IP Inventory Log and a Dispensing Log are maintained.
- Document in the source documents any modification to, or deviation from, the drug dosage the protocol requires, and report the deviation to the Research Ethics Board (REB).
- Ensure that, if a trained and qualified staff member dispenses the drug, the pharmacy provides a label identical to the product's label, to be applied on the source document form.

The principal investigator should also ensure that the product is used only in accordance with the approved protocol and the Investigator's Brochure or the product monograph. [Document any use of the product that is not in accordance with the approved protocol](/sops/cr-010#5-4-1), and report it to the sponsor as required.

In a study with an investigational product, only the principal investigator and sub-investigators who are physicians can prescribe medication.

## Follow the product from receipt to return or destruction

Every step in the product's path at the site is documented, and the documents are kept with the essential study documents:

- **Receipt.** The shipment is checked against its shipping documentation and inventoried. Any damage or discrepancy is recorded and reported to the sponsor as soon as possible. Keep all the documentation of the product's transport and receipt with the essential study documents throughout the study.
- **Storage.** The product is stored securely, at the conditions the protocol or the sponsor sets, as [Store the product](#store-the-product) explains.
- **Dispensing.** The pharmacy dispenses the product to participants on a prescription from the principal investigator or a sub-investigator who is a physician. Each participant is told how to use the product and to return what they don't use, packaging included. Document what the participant is told in the source documents. Assess participants' compliance with the instructions at intervals appropriate for the protocol.
- **Accountability.** Every unit received, dispensed, returned or destroyed is recorded, as [Keep the accountability records](#keep-the-accountability-records) explains.
- **Return.** Product left at the end of the study, or recalled, is returned to the sponsor or distributor, or handled as the protocol says.
- **Destruction.** Destruction at the site needs the sponsor's written authorization, and the pharmacy destroys the product following its own procedures. [The destruction documentation goes to the sponsor, with a copy for the investigator site file](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).

In a randomized study, [follow the randomization procedures the protocol describes](/sops/cr-010#5-7). Ensure that the randomization code is broken only in accordance with the protocol. Keep all the documents on randomization done by an external system, such as an interactive voice response system. File the randomization documentation with the essential study documents.

In a blinded study, [promptly document and explain to the sponsor any premature unblinding](/kb/foundations/trial-design#unblind-a-participant), whether accidental or because of a serious adverse event, and inform the REB as its procedures require.

## Store the product

Investigational products are stored securely, with access restricted to authorized staff, at the temperature and humidity the protocol or the sponsor sets. Investigational products should be kept in a dedicated area, separate from regular medication. Some products, such as biologics, radiopharmaceuticals and devices, have special storage conditions.

[The principal investigator should ensure that the refrigerators, freezers and rooms used to store the product](/sops/cr-025#1-2):

- Are adequate and undergo regular maintenance.
- Have a single calibrated minimum and maximum thermometer or a centralized monitoring system.
- Are linked to backup power in case of a power outage.
- Have an alarm, or are linked to the central alarm system, so that a malfunction or a temperature excursion is detected quickly.

The appropriate Biomed department should be told of any equipment malfunction: the MUHC's Biomed department for MUHC equipment, and the RI-MUHC's for the equipment of the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM). Where no centralized system monitors the temperature, the principal investigator should ensure that a temperature log is kept for each refrigerator and freezer, and [the RI-MUHC's temperature log](/sops/cr-025#appendix-1) can be used for it. Temperature records are filed with the essential study documents at the end of the study.

Monitor the temperature and humidity, and record them regularly, by hand or with an automatic device. Be prepared to move the product to another storage area if problems arise, and document any storage issue. Keep the storage records within easy access of the product, and make them available to monitors, auditors and others who ask for them.

The site [should have a procedure for correcting changes in the storage environment](/sops/cr-010#5-3-1), and a backup plan that says, for example, who should be notified, with their contact information. The RI-MUHC's SOPs also require backup methods for refrigerators and freezers in case of a power failure, with pharmacy staff trained on these methods under the MUHC Pharmacy's own SOPs.

The principal investigator or a designee is responsible for written procedures for when the alarm sounds or the electricity goes out, and for transferring samples. The study team should be trained on these procedures, and the training should be documented on a central Training Log. A copy of the written procedures should be accessible to all users.

If the temperature goes outside the required range, document the excursion and inform the sponsor immediately. The product must be quarantined until the sponsor confirms whether it is fit for use.

A Health Canada inspector [may review](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/hc-inspection-guide) the shipping records that show the product stayed in its temperature range in transit. The inspector may also review the record of control and traceability kept when the product is moved from the main pharmacy to a research storage location.

## Keep the accountability records

The investigational product is fully accounted for at all times. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-010#1-4-3) do not require drug accountability records for:

- A drug that is the subject of a Phase IV clinical trial.
- A comparator drug that has market authorization, is used as its Canadian monograph sets and hasn't been altered in any way.
- Rescue or concomitant medications that are not the subject of the trial, even if used off-label, such as supportive medications for known clinical uses.

The records include:

- The IP Receipt Log, for every shipment received.
- The IP Dispensing Log, for each product dispensed to each participant. The sponsor provides it, or the site uses an equivalent.
- The IP Inventory Log.
- An accountability log that reconciles all the product received, dispensed and remaining at close-out.
- The temperature records, kept for the duration of the study and archived with the investigator site file at close-out.
- The return and destruction documentation.

For drugs, [the MUHC Pharmacy's own SOP](/sops/cr-010#1-4-1), MON_PEC11_FR01, has the inventory and dispensing log templates in its appendices, in Nagano. For medical devices, the RI-MUHC's SOPs provide a [Medical Device Accountability Log](/sops/cr-024#appendix-1).

Never give investigational product assigned to a participant and not used to another participant, to a person outside the study or to another site. If the accounting shows an inconsistency, follow up with the participant or the pharmacy, for the participant's safety, and document the inconsistency.

Monitors verify participants' accountability records during their visits, and may visit the pharmacy. A Health Canada inspector may review the overall and individual accountability records. [Keep the accountability records for the study's full retention period](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).

## Check the label

At the site, for every investigational product:

- Ensure that the product's label is not hidden, covered, withdrawn or modified without the sponsor's authorization.
- Apply an additional label if the protocol or the institution requires one, without covering the original label.
- Ensure that no information that identifies a participant is on a container or device label when the product is returned to the sponsor.

### Check the label against the regulations

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span></div>

For a drug trial, the sponsor must ensure that the investigational drug bears a label in both official languages, English and French. The label carries the information the Food and Drug Regulations list, such as a statement that the drug is an investigational drug to be used only by a qualified investigator, its expiration date and its recommended storage conditions. The rule is federal and applies to drug trials across Canada.

The sponsor has the overall responsibility for the label. [The principal investigator should also ensure that the label meets the Regulations' requirements](/sops/cr-010#1-2-2).

For a natural health product trial, [the sponsor must ensure](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2003-196/section-75.html) that the natural health product bears a label in both official languages. The label carries the information the Natural Health Products Regulations list, such as a statement that the product is an investigational natural health product to be used only by a qualified investigator, its expiry date and its recommended storage conditions, if any.

For a medical device, [no person may import or sell the device for investigational testing](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-98-282/section-86.html) unless it has a label that sets out the information the Medical Devices Regulations list. The label sets out the name of the manufacturer and the name of the device.

A device's label for investigational testing also sets out the statements "Investigational Device" and "To Be Used by Qualified Investigators Only", or other statements that convey those meanings, in English and French. For an in vitro diagnostic device, the label also states, in English and French, that the device's performance specifications have not been established.

## Ask for a nurse to dispense the product

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span></div>

In specific conditions or circumstances, an RI-MUHC research nurse or an MUHC nurse may be approved to dispense the investigational product directly to participants, without going through the pharmacy. The approval is an exception to the rule that a pharmacist dispenses every medication. Convenience, nights and weekends are not exceptions.

1. At submission in Nagano, the investigator asks for the approval.
2. The MUHC Research Pharmacy checks that the planned dispensing complies with Quebec's *Pharmacy Act*. The pharmacy approves only after that check.
3. If the pharmacy approves, it issues a waiver.
4. The waiver should be kept with the essential documents.

<Warning>
Do not assume that a nurse may dispense the product. The approval must be requested in Nagano at submission, and the waiver is required before the study can begin.
</Warning>

## Medical devices, natural health products and radiopharmaceuticals

The pharmacy's central role applies to drugs, biologics included, and to some natural health products. Other kinds of investigational product follow their own rules, and the RI-MUHC's SOP on investigational products still applies to each of them.

### Medical devices

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span></div>

A study of a Class II, III or IV medical device may need Health Canada's [investigational testing authorization (ITA)](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study). The RI-MUHC's [SOP on investigational testing authorizations](/sops/cr-024) covers the device rules for a device trial that the investigator initiates, other than one with an in vitro diagnostic device. The SOP gives a [Medical Device Accountability Log](/sops/cr-024#appendix-1) as a working document example.

### Natural health products

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span></div>

A natural health product trial is regulated under Part 4 of Health Canada's [*Natural Health Products Regulations*](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2003-196/). [All drugs, and only some natural health products, must be managed by the pharmacy](/sops/cr-007#5-4-4). The product's storage and accountability rules apply whether or not the pharmacy manages it.

### Radiopharmaceuticals

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span></div>

Radiopharmaceuticals have special storage conditions, and their handling follows radiation safety and pharmacy protocols. Consider contacting the MUHC Research Pharmacy early in planning a radiopharmaceutical study.

If your study uses any of these products, consider contacting the MUHC Research Pharmacy and [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) early in planning.
