In a sponsored study, the sponsor usually provides an electronic data capture (EDC) system to collect the study data, and much of a clinical research coordinator's daily work happens there: entering data, answering queries and keeping every change traceable. For RI-MUHC-led studies that use REDCap, see [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/design-support/cord).

## Get access to the EDC

Sponsors use systems such as Medidata Rave, Oracle InForm or Clinical One, Veeva Vault CDMS and Medrio.

Give EDC access only to team members whose training on the system is documented and who have been delegated the task on the Task Delegation Log, each with their own account. Keep the case report form (CRF) completion guidelines, if any, and the training records with the essential documents.

Never share your login, because the system records every action under your name and someone else using your account breaks the trail. When someone leaves the study or no longer needs access, tell the sponsor, so that it can change or end their access.

Participants appear in the EDC by their study number, unless the protocol requires otherwise and the Research Ethics Board (REB) has approved it. Enter no name or medical record number; the [privacy rules](/kb/governance/privacy#keep-participant-information-confidential) explain what stays at the site.

Based on: [SOP-CR-014](/sops/cr-014), sections 5.1, 5.5, 5.7 and 5.8; [SOP-CR-009](/sops/cr-009), section 5.7; the International Council for Harmonisation's guideline [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), sections 2.12.10, 4.2.2 and 4.3.8.

## Enter data promptly

Enter data as soon as possible after they are recorded in the source documents, within the timelines the sponsor sets in its data management plan or its CRF completion guidelines. Data left in paper source for weeks delay queries, invite transcription errors and widen the gap between source and CRF that monitors look for.

Only the data that the REB-approved protocol defines should go into the CRF. Enter laboratory results and vital signs as they become available, and enter serious adverse event (SAE) data promptly after the SAE report itself, as the sponsor's procedures set; the reporting deadlines are on [Reportable Events](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events). Check each entry against its source as you make it.

Many queries come from the system's automatic checks:

- Range checks flag values outside the expected range, such as a heart rate of 350. Check the source, and if the value is right, add a comment explaining it.
- Consistency checks flag fields that contradict each other, such as "no concomitant medications" with a medication entered. Review related fields together.
- Missing-data checks flag blank required fields. If a value is unavailable, use the system's "not done" or "not applicable" option rather than leaving the field blank.
- Date checks flag dates that do not fit, such as an end before a start, an event before consent or a procedure outside its visit window.

Based on: [SOP-CR-014, sections 5.5](/sops/cr-014#5-5-case-report-forms), [5.6](/sops/cr-014#5-6-good-documentation-practices-gdps), [5.7](/sops/cr-014#5-7-transcription-of-source-data-to-paper-crfs) and [5.9](/sops/cr-014#5-9-quality-control-and-modifications-to-clinical-data); [SOP-CR-103](/sops/cr-103), section 5.2; [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), section 4.2.1.

## Answer queries

A query is a request from the sponsor's data managers or monitor to clarify or correct an entry. Some come automatically from the system's checks; others are raised by the data management team or the monitor during review.

1. Read the query and answer the question it asks.
2. If the entry was right, say so and cite the source. If it was wrong, correct it and explain the correction.
3. Send medical questions, such as whether an adverse event is related to the product or whether a result is clinically significant, to the physician investigator or the sub-investigator delegated that assessment.
4. The data manager or monitor closes the query or asks again. A vague answer, such as "will check", brings another query.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Confirmed" | "Value confirmed against the source document dated 15-Mar-2026. The investigator's screening note documents baseline bradycardia." |
| "Updated" | "Corrected from 15-Mar-2026 to 15-Apr-2026: transcription error. The source document shows the visit on 15-Apr-2026." |
| "Will check" | Check first, then answer with the source. |
| "N/A" | "Assessment not done: the participant declined the optional MRI, which protocol section 6.3 allows." |

A query on paper comes on the sponsor's data clarification form. Review it, sign it, return the original to the sponsor, unless you agreed otherwise, and keep a copy in the investigator site file (ISF); print a request sent by email and file it with the source data.

The sponsor's data management plan, monitoring plan or CRF guidelines may set the time you have to answer. For a study that an RI-MUHC investigator sponsors, the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) suggest, in their monitoring plan template, that the site resolve remaining queries within 2 weeks of the monitoring visit.

Monitors may post queries at their visits for the site to resolve, and the number of data management queries is among the factors that may lead the sponsor to monitor more often.

## Correct data and keep the audit trail

The EDC's audit trail records each change: who made it, when, the old and new values and, where needed, the reason. It is the electronic version of the single line drawn through a paper entry.

- Give a reason for every correction unless it is obvious, and use the field to explain what was wrong.
- Nothing is erased. A changed or deleted value stays in the audit trail. The audit trail itself should be changed only in rare cases, such as a participant's personal information entered by mistake, and then with a record of what was done and why.
- Health Canada inspectors and sponsors' auditors can review audit trails, including corrections made long after the event or without an adequate reason.

The RI-MUHC's SOPs add a rule for databases that the site manages, unless the study contract or the data management plan sets otherwise. When data are entered directly into the electronic case report form with no paper record, the audit trail must be computer-generated and time-stamped, and must show, for each change, addition or deletion, its date and time, the user who made it, the reason, and the old and new values.

How to correct paper records is on [Data Integrity](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#record-and-correct-data-properly).

Based on: [SOP-CR-014, sections 5.6](/sops/cr-014#5-6-good-documentation-practices-gdps) and [5.9](/sops/cr-014#5-9-quality-control-and-modifications-to-clinical-data); [SOP-CR-105](/sops/cr-105), sections 2 and 5.3; [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), section 4.2.2 and glossary.

## Reconcile SAE data

SAE data usually go in two places, the sponsor's safety database (through the SAE report form) and the EDC (on the adverse event pages), and the sponsor reconciles the two.

The sponsor's data managers compare them and send reconciliation queries about any difference, such as an SAE in the safety database that is not yet in the EDC, dates or outcomes recorded differently, or a causality assessment in the EDC that differs from the SAE form. A change to a causality assessment is a medical judgment for the physician investigator or a delegated sub-investigator.

If the sponsor collects SAE reports in its EDC, print the electronic case report form (eCRF) report with the proof of the sponsor's receipt and its date, file it in the ISF and record it on the SAE Reporting Log. The RI-MUHC's SOPs recommend completing the RI-MUHC SAE report template first and keeping it in the ISF.

Answer reconciliation queries promptly. For a database your team manages, the RI-MUHC's SOPs require all SAEs to be reconciled before the database is locked, unless the study contract or the data management plan sets otherwise; a sponsor's database follows the sponsor's own procedures.
