In a sponsored study, the sponsor usually provides an electronic data capture (EDC) system to collect the study data. Sponsors use systems such as Medidata Rave, Oracle InForm or Clinical One, Veeva Vault CDMS and Medrio. Much of a clinical research coordinator's daily work happens there: entering data, answering queries and keeping every change traceable. Studies led at the RI-MUHC that use REDCap get support from [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/design-support/cord).

## Get access to the EDC

[Give EDC access only to team members](/sops/cr-014#5-8-3) whose training on the system is documented and who have been delegated the task on the Task Delegation Log. Each needs their own account. [Keep the case report form (CRF) completion guidelines](/sops/cr-014#5-8-2), if any, and the training records with the essential documents.

For a database that the site manages, [create and maintain a list of the users who have access to it](/sops/cr-105#5-2-3), unless the study contract or the data management plan sets otherwise. If access to the database is restricted, keep track of each user's access privileges.

Never share your login, because the system records every action under your name and someone else using your account breaks the trail. When someone leaves the study or no longer needs access, you [should tell the sponsor](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), so that it can change or end their access.

Participants appear in the EDC by their study number, unless the protocol requires otherwise and the Research Ethics Board (REB) has approved it. Enter no name or medical record number. The privacy rules set [what stays at the site](/kb/governance/privacy#keep-participant-information-confidential).

## Enter data promptly

[Enter data as soon as possible](/sops/cr-014#5-7-3) after they are recorded in the source documents, within the timelines the sponsor sets in its data management plan or its CRF completion guidelines.

[Only the data that the REB-approved protocol defines should go into the CRF](/sops/cr-014#5-5-1). Enter laboratory results and vital signs as they become available. Enter serious adverse event (SAE) data promptly after [the SAE report itself](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines), as the sponsor's procedures set. Best practice is to check the source data before you transcribe them into the CRF. Once the data are in the CRF, the site [should check them against the source](/sops/cr-014#5-9-1) for transcription errors, omissions, missed protocol deviations and anything unclear.

Many queries come from the system's automatic checks:

- Range checks flag values outside the expected range, such as a heart rate of 350. Check the source, and if the value is right, add a comment explaining it.
- Consistency checks flag fields that contradict each other, such as "no concomitant medications" with a medication entered. Review related fields together.
- Missing-data checks flag blank required fields. If a value is unavailable, use the system's "not done" or "not applicable" option rather than leaving the field blank.
- Date checks flag dates that do not fit, such as an end before a start, an event before consent or a procedure outside its visit window.

## Answer queries

A query is a request from the sponsor's data managers or monitor to clarify or correct an entry. To answer a query:

1. Read the query and answer the question it asks.
2. If the entry was right, say so and cite the source. If it was wrong, correct it and explain the correction.
3. Send medical questions to the physician investigator or to the sub-investigator delegated medical assessments. Medical questions include whether an adverse event is related to the product and whether a result is clinically significant.

The data manager or monitor closes the query or asks again. A vague answer, such as "will check", brings another query.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Confirmed" | "Value confirmed against the source document dated 15-Mar-2026. The investigator's screening note documents baseline bradycardia." |
| "Updated" | "Corrected from 15-Mar-2026 to 15-Apr-2026: transcription error. The source document shows the visit on 15-Apr-2026." |
| "Will check" | Check first, then answer with the source. |
| "N/A" | "Assessment not done: the participant declined the optional MRI, which protocol section 6.3 allows." |

A query on paper comes on the sponsor's data clarification form. [Review it, sign it and return the original to the sponsor](/sops/cr-014#5-9-7), unless you agreed otherwise. Keep a copy in the investigator site file (ISF). A request sent by email should be printed and filed with the source data.

The sponsor's data management plan, monitoring plan or CRF guidelines may set the time you have to answer. For a study that an RI-MUHC investigator sponsors, the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) provide a [monitoring plan template](/sops/cr-013#appendix-3) in which remaining queries should be resolved by the site within 2 weeks of the monitoring visit.

Monitors may post queries at their visits for the site to resolve. The number of data management queries is among the factors that may lead the sponsor to monitor more often.

## Correct data and keep the audit trail

For each change, the EDC's audit trail records who made it, when, the old and new values and the reason where needed. The audit trail is the electronic version of [the single line drawn through a paper entry](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#record-and-correct-data-properly). Corrections in the EDC follow these rules:

- Give a reason for every correction unless it is obvious, and use the field to explain what was wrong.
- Nothing is erased. A changed or deleted value stays in the audit trail. The audit trail itself should be changed only in rare cases, such as a participant's personal information entered by mistake, and then with a record of what was done and why.
- Health Canada inspectors and sponsors' auditors can review audit trails, including corrections made long after the event or without an adequate reason.

For databases that the site manages, when data are entered directly into the electronic case report form with no paper record, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs require a computer-generated, time-stamped audit trail](/sops/cr-105#5-3-2), unless the study contract or the data management plan sets otherwise. For each change, addition or deletion, the audit trail must show:

- Its date and time.
- The user who made it.
- The reason.
- The old and new values.

For databases that the site manages, the RI-MUHC's SOPs also require that:

- Where an electronic audit trail is not possible, [a paper trail of all modifications, deletions and additions is kept](/sops/cr-105#5-3-1), with the date and time, the reason for each change and the initials of the person who made it.
- [Changes requested after the database is locked](/sops/cr-105#5-3-3) that the audit trail cannot capture are documented clearly.
- [A data loss protection process for the database is created, maintained and documented](/sops/cr-105#5-4-3), and backup and recovery of the database are tested periodically.

## Reconcile SAE data

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

SAE data usually go in two places, the sponsor's safety database (through the SAE report form) and the EDC (on the adverse event pages). The sponsor's data managers reconcile the two databases by comparing them, and send reconciliation queries about any difference, such as:

- An SAE in the safety database that is not yet in the EDC.
- Dates or outcomes recorded differently.
- A causality assessment in the EDC that differs from the SAE form.

A change to a causality assessment is a medical judgment for the physician investigator or a delegated sub-investigator.

If the sponsor collects SAE reports in its EDC, print the electronic case report form (eCRF) report with the proof of the sponsor's receipt and its date. File the printout in the ISF and record it on the SAE Reporting Log. The RI-MUHC's SOPs [recommend completing the RI-MUHC SAE report template](/sops/cr-012#5-4-3) before you transcribe the SAE into the sponsor's eCRF report, and keeping the template in the ISF.

Answer reconciliation queries promptly. For a database your team manages, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs require all SAEs to be reconciled](/sops/cr-107#5-2-1) before the database is locked, unless the study contract or the data management plan sets otherwise. A sponsor's database follows the sponsor's own procedures.
