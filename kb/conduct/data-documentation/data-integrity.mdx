A study record has to be complete in itself. Monitors, auditors and inspectors may review it years after the study, with no one there to explain it. The record has to show who recorded each piece of data, when and what changed. To protect data from people who should not see them, follow the rules for [storing and sharing human data](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security).

## Follow ALCOA+

The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) [require you to record, process and store all study information](/sops/cr-014#5-4-2) following the ALCOA+ principles. For clinical trials of investigational products, the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) sets the first six in its good clinical practice guideline, ICH E6(R3): source records [should be attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate and complete](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html).

- **Attributable.** The record shows who recorded each piece of data and when. Initial and date every entry. Record data only if you are delegated the task on the Task Delegation Log.
- **Legible.** Use permanent black or blue ink, so that entries stay readable for the whole retention period.
- **Contemporaneous.** Record data at the time. Never backdate. Data recorded later carry both dates, the note "late entry", an explanation and your initials. Add them after the existing entries, never between lines or in the margin.
- **Original.** The first place data are recorded is the source.
- **Accurate.** The record reflects what happened, and errors are corrected in a way that keeps the original visible.
- **Complete.** Mark a blank field "Not Applicable" ("N/Ap"), "Not Available" ("N/Av") or "unknown", with an explanation, your initials and the date. [Report a missing element clearly](/sops/cr-014#5-6-1), and note where retrieved information came from in a footnote, with your initials and the date. If a whole paper record is missing, write a note to file.

The SOPs add three more:

- **Consistent.** Data reported to the sponsor match the source records, or the difference is explained.
- **Enduring.** Records last for the whole retention period. Thermal paper degrades faster than a photocopy. Photocopy results printed on thermal paper, such as electrocardiograms (ECGs), promptly. Have the principal investigator who leads the study at the MUHC initial and date both copies. Never record source data on sticky notes.
- **Available.** Records can be found and produced for monitors, auditors and inspectors. Records must be made available to regulatory inspectors within 7 to 14 calendar days, or sooner for a for-cause inspection, for which inspectors may give no notice. Keep a record of where archived files are, ready at all times.

## Record and correct data properly

Records with errors or missing information were among the observations Health Canada cited most often in the [drug clinical trial inspections](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/reports-publications/compliance-enforcement/inspectorate-program-annual-inspection-summary-report-2015-2016.html) it summarized up to 2015–2016. Errors and missing information are also common findings in [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca)'s audits at the RI-MUHC. When you record or correct data:

- Enter data in sequence, without leaving empty spaces.
- [Correct an entry](/sops/cr-014#5-6-2) by drawing a single line through it, so that the original stays readable. Initial and date the correction, and explain it unless the reason is obvious.
- Never use correction fluid or tape, and never scribble over, cover or overwrite an entry.
- Only the person who made the entry, or someone delegated to do so on the Task Delegation Log, may correct it.
- If there is no room to explain a correction on the document, you may write a note to file and file it with the source document or in the notes-to-file section of the essential documents.
- Void information with a single line and the word "VOID", an explanation, your signature and the date.
- Never discard data. Mark a replaced or unused document "Superseded" or "Obsolete", with your initials, the date and a diagonal line across it.
- Use only the current approved version of each document, and keep earlier versions marked as superseded.
- The principal investigator should sign and date the case report form (CRF) where indicated, after the final entry, and sign again if a source form or the CRF is revised afterwards.
- Once the sponsor has collected paper CRFs, make any correction on its data clarification form.

## Know what counts as a source document

A source document is [the first place data are recorded](/sops/cr-014#5-2-1), kept at the site in its original state, on paper or in a validated electronic system. Source documents include:

- Hospital records, among them [OACIS](/apps/oacis), the MUHC's electronic medical record.
- Clinical and office charts.
- Laboratory notes.
- Participants' diaries and evaluation checklists.
- Pharmacy dispensing records.
- Data recorded by automated instruments.
- Certified copies of any of these.

Data transcribed from a source document into a CRF or a database must trace back to it. Monitors check that link during source data verification. Any source document you create or print from OACIS [should be signed and dated](/sops/cr-014#5-4-6) by whoever collects, records, reviews or assesses its data.

[Make sure that the principal investigator assesses each abnormal test result promptly](/sops/cr-014#5-4-5). The principal investigator should mark each one "c.s." (clinically significant) or "n.c.s." (not clinically significant), with initials and the date, judging it in the context of the study rather than of standard care.

If the principal investigator consistently reviews results online and initials and dates the printouts later, a work instruction should describe that oversight, and the printouts should ideally be initialled and dated within 14 days of the results. If it happens for only one participant, a note to file can explain the late documentation.

[The site creates its own visit worksheets](/sops/cr-014#5-2-2) to capture all the data the protocol requires, and any other data the principal investigator considers necessary for participants' safety. Give every source form you create a header and footer with:

- The protocol number.
- The principal investigator's name.
- The visit and its date.
- The participant's number and initials.
- The site number, if there is one.
- The name of the person who checked the blank form and of the person who completed it, each with the date.

Ideally, a manager, a supervisor or a staff member independent of the study [should check each blank worksheet before it is used](/sops/cr-014#5-2-4), so that it captures every procedure the protocol requires. The worksheets should also be checked after source data are recorded, to make sure that good documentation practices were followed.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs suggest tracking other source documents](/sops/cr-014#5-2-3), such as laboratory reports and OACIS printouts, in a separate checklist or with a check or a date on the visit worksheet, and filing each document with the worksheet.

## Make a certified copy

A certified copy is a copy verified to have the same information as the original, including relevant metadata where applicable, either by a dated signature or because a validated process produced it. When you make or use certified copies:

- The person who makes the copy [should be the one who certifies it](/sops/cr-015#5-1-17), by marking it "True and Exact Copy" and adding their initials and the date.
- A copy shared outside the team, such as a source document for a sponsor or an auditor, should have all identifying information redacted.
- When a copy replaces an original, keep the original attached, and never destroy it.
- Documents from other departments or hospitals, such as medical history reports, should be filed as originals or certified copies. [Source documents obtained from external clinics or hospitals should be certified copies](/sops/cr-002#5-8-1), to ensure that they are exact copies. Each document should be marked "Certified Copy", handwritten or stamped, and signed and dated by the person authorized to make the copy.
- For a patient referred from outside the MUHC's patient population, including the Jewish General Hospital, request and obtain certified copies of the relevant source documents from the other institution, and file them in a timely fashion. Record when you asked and when they arrived, because there can be long delays.
- Auditors and inspectors may ask for copies. The team should make certified copies and return the originals to the archive. During an inspection, [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) may help keep track of the copies the inspector requests.

## Know who validates electronic systems

The sponsor or sponsor-investigator [must make sure](/sops/cr-004#5-1-3) that every electronic system used in a study, including the electronic data capture (EDC) system, has been validated and complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations (21 CFR Part 11). 21 CFR Part 11 is the US rule on electronic records, and the RI-MUHC's SOPs apply it.

When you prepare for the sponsor's or sponsor-investigator's site feasibility visit, you should ask RI-MUHC [information technology (IT)](mailto:ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca) to evaluate any software specific to the study and any e-connectivity issues. The request goes through IT Rescue on the RI-MUHC Portal.

Software specific to a study can include an electronic consent system, a mobile app, an outside database or a wearable device. A security review after submission goes to [MUHC Information Security](mailto:securitygovernance@muhc.mcgill.ca).

If the protocol requires clinical data to be transferred to another system, such as SAS, [make sure that the transfer is validated and secure](/sops/cr-014#5-8-4).

In a drug trial regulated by Health Canada, every electronic system used to capture, process, manage or archive clinical trial information [should be adequately validated](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100.html). The evidence of validation should be kept for the retention period and be readily available for inspection. The system should also keep audit trails and produce complete paper copies for the whole retention period.

In a drug trial regulated by Health Canada, records kept in cloud systems are subject to the same requirements as records in the sponsor's own systems. For a hospital system that the sponsor is not responsible for, such as OACIS, an alternative to validation could be considered, such as printing the records.

[Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/design-support/cord) supports REDCap at the RI-MUHC. The RI-MUHC's SOPs consider REDCap unfit for [electronic consent forms](/kb/recruitment/consent#obtain-consent-remotely-or-electronically), because it allows only one signature per form.

## Use electronic source data correctly

When data are first recorded in an electronic system, that electronic record is the source. Before the trial starts, the investigator [should define what the source records are](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), how data will be captured and where they will be kept. The investigator should also avoid transcription steps that serve no purpose. When the source is electronic:

- Entering source data directly into an electronic case report form (eCRF) is unusual. It needs the [approval of the Research Ethics Board (REB)](/sops/cr-014#5-8-6) before you start. The protocol or another study document must describe it. The system must be validated and comply with 21 CFR Part 11. REDCap at the RI-MUHC is meant for data transcribed from source forms.
- The principal investigator [must print, sign and date electronic source documents](/sops/cr-014#5-8-5) to confirm their content, unless they come straight from the sponsor's validated equipment or laboratory into its validated eCRF. File the signed printouts with the study's other source documents.
- Keep the original or a certified copy of the electronic data backup, with its audit trail. Keep a record of all data changes for the retention period.

In an electronic system, [the audit trail keeps every correction](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/edc-query-management#correct-data-and-keep-the-audit-trail).

## Prepare for source data verification

Source data verification is the monitor's check that the CRFs match the source documents. Well-kept records make the check faster and produce fewer queries. To prepare for the monitor's visit:

- Best practice is to check the source data before you transcribe them into the CRF. Once the data are in the CRF, the site [should check them against the source](/sops/cr-014#5-9-1) for transcription errors, omissions, missed protocol deviations and anything unclear.
- The site should not rely on the sponsor alone for quality control. The site's own checks [should come before the monitor's and be independent of them](/sops/cr-013#5-8-11).
- [Keep the participant data binders, the regulatory binder and the essential documents up to date](/sops/cr-013#5-8-6). Consider filing source documents at once, by participant and visit, with the OACIS records you need identified.
- [Correct known errors](#record-and-correct-data-properly) before the visit.
- The Task Delegation Log [should be kept up to date throughout the study](/sops/cr-002#5-6-1). Monitors check that whoever collected and signed data had been delegated the task at the time. Backdated entries on the log are a common finding.
- Keep consent documents easy to check, so that the monitor can confirm that the current approved version was signed before any study procedure.
- [Arrange the monitor's access to OACIS](/apps/oacis#requesting-access-for-external-monitors) before the visit.

## Keep the records

[The retention periods, and when they start](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving), depend on whether Health Canada regulates the study. [Never destroy study files](/sops/cr-015#5-6-1) without consulting the principal investigator and the sponsor or sponsor-investigator. Keep their written permission in the archiving binder. Redact identifying information on source documents before you archive them.
