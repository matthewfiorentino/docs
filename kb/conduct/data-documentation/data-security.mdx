The sensitivity of the data you are working with determines the environment in which it must be stored and the tools you must use to share it.

## Data classification

The RI-MUHC classifies human research data into four categories. The category determines where data can be stored, how it can be shared, and what approvals are required to access it. Understanding these distinctions is essential for protocol design, <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> submissions, and day-to-day data handling.

| Category | Type | Description |
|----------|------|-------------|
| 1 | Aggregate / Statistical | Fully summarized data with no individual identifiers and no reasonable risk of re-identification. Examples: published statistics, summary tables, group averages. No restrictions on storage or sharing once published. |
| 2 | Anonymized | Identifiers have been **irreversibly** stripped with no possibility of re-identification — no linking key exists or ever existed. Anonymized data can be stored more broadly and shared via standard encrypted methods. |
| 3 | De-identified | Identifiers have been removed, but re-identification **remains possible** through a linking key (e.g., a master ID log or participant number list). Only the <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> has access to the key. De-identified data is subject to the same security requirements as identifying data. |
| 4 | Identifying | Contains direct identifiers: name, MRN, RAMQ number, date of birth, or any other information that can directly identify a participant. Strictest storage and sharing controls apply. Requires full <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approval, MUHC Authorization, and typically an EFVP. |

<Warning>
**De-identified is not the same as anonymized.** This is the most common misconception the RI-MUHC sees from research teams. If a linking key exists anywhere — even stored separately — the data is de-identified, not anonymized. The security and EFVP requirements for de-identified data are the same as for identifying data.
</Warning>

<Note>
**Data linkage increases re-identification risk.** Two separately de-identified datasets may each carry low re-identification risk on their own. Merging them can make re-identification substantially easier — for example, combining a rare diagnosis with a date of birth may uniquely identify a patient. Consider re-identification risk whenever combining data sources.
</Note>

For the full classification framework with storage and sharing controls, see the [RI-MUHC Researcher's Guide to Data Security](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=111401908461398782).

## Storage requirements

Storage location depends on data classification. There are three environments: the MUHC network, the RARI network, and cloud services. These are not equivalent — the MUHC network operates behind Ministry (MSSS) firewalls with stricter security controls, while the RARI network has different access requirements. If you are unsure which network your workstation is on, contact ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca.

| Data type | Digital storage | Hardcopy storage | Off-site / USB |
|-----------|----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Aggregate / Anonymized (unpublished) | Any MUHC or RARI network location, or a cloud service that uses encryption, as long as the data are not accessible to the public. | In an RI-MUHC or MUHC monitored area, or under constant attention in a public area; stored in a locked area. | Password-protected laptops and USB keys; hard copies taken off site kept under constant attention. |
| De-identified / Identifying | Stored so that only people on the study's MUHC-approved access list (Nagano) can reach the data; in the cloud, only an encrypted service with two-factor authentication, a Canadian or EU server and MUHC-held encryption keys (NextCloud meets these requirements). | In a room accessible only to specific authorized people, in a locked cabinet; never taken off site. | Password-protected laptops and USB keys, kept under constant supervision or in a locked room on site and encrypted for off-site transport or use. |

<Warning>
**No personal devices.** Research data must never be stored on personal laptops, personal USB drives, or personal cloud accounts (including personal Google Drive, Dropbox, or iCloud). This applies even to de-identified data. The fact that you _can_ download something does not mean you are permitted to.
</Warning>

## Sharing data securely

Only authorized recipients may receive research data: people named in the REB-approved protocol, people who have signed the required legal agreements, or people appropriately delegated by the PI and listed on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>.

### Sending to another MUHC or MSSS recipient

If both sender and recipient have MUHC (muhc.mcgill.ca) or MSSS (ssss.gouv.qc.ca) email addresses, you may send encrypted files via Outlook. Encrypt the file using Excel's built-in encryption or 7-zip before attaching. Send the file by email, but **send the decryption password by a different channel** — SMS or phone. Never send the file and the password in the same email.

### Sending to an external recipient (including McGill)

McGill University (mcgill.ca) email addresses are external to the MSSS network. If the recipient has a mcgill.ca email or any other non-MUHC, non-MSSS address, use **MUHC NextCloud** for file transfer — not Outlook. Encrypt the file before uploading. Send the decryption password by SMS or phone.

<Warning>
**McGill email addresses are external to the MUHC network.** Many RI-MUHC researchers and staff prefer their mcgill.ca accounts, but the RI-MUHC allows McGill email only for unpublished data with no direct or indirect identifiers. Never email de-identified or identifying data from a mcgill.ca address. Encrypt the data, transfer them through MUHC NextCloud and keep them behind MUHC firewalls.
</Warning>

Any data sharing with an external collaborator must also be stipulated in a signed collaboration or data use agreement before transfer takes place. Contact the contracts office if you are unsure whether an agreement is required.

## Personal devices

Research data — including de-identified data — must never be stored or processed on personal devices. This includes personal laptops, home computers, personal tablets, and personal USB drives. If you need remote access to MUHC data, use Citrix to connect to the MUHC network from any device. Contact IT (ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca) to set up appropriate remote access.

## Archiving at study close-out

When a study ends, data must be archived in an institutionally approved environment according to the applicable retention schedule. Health Canada's regulations, the RI-MUHC's SOPs and institutional policies set the [retention periods](/kb/site-activation/isf#retention-and-archiving).

Data must not be moved to personal storage at close-out. Archiving should be planned at the start of the project — a data management plan helps document your archiving strategy, including the file formats and software versions used, so records remain accessible years later. See [SOP-CR-107](/sops/cr-107) (Database Lock and Archiving) for detailed guidance.

## Cybersecurity for research teams

Clinical trial data is a high-value target. Sponsor portals, eConsent systems, EDC platforms, and REDCap instances all hold personal health information that must be protected throughout the trial lifecycle. The following practices address the most common vulnerabilities at the research-team level.

### Phishing and social engineering

Research staff receive communications from a wide range of sponsors, CROs, monitors, labs, and IRBs. This creates an environment where phishing attempts are easy to disguise as legitimate study communication.

- Never open unexpected file attachments from sponsors or CROs without verifying through a second channel (a phone call or a separate email independent of the original message)
- Sponsor portals will never ask you to confirm login credentials via email — portal credential requests by email are phishing attempts
- Report suspicious emails to ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca before clicking anything

### Sponsor portal credential hygiene

Research teams routinely hold active credentials for many sponsor portals simultaneously. This creates credential-accumulation risk — weak or reused passwords, shared credentials, and accounts that remain active after staff depart.

- Use a unique, strong password for each sponsor portal — a password manager is the practical solution for managing this at scale
- Never share portal credentials with colleagues. If a colleague needs access, request a separate account through the sponsor
- When a team member leaves the study or the institution, notify the sponsor to deactivate their portal access. Include portal access deactivation in the Task Delegation Log off-boarding process
- When you are removed from a study, confirm your portal access has been deactivated

### REDCap and institutional platform security

RI-MUHC REDCap instances are managed by [CORD](/kb/design-support/cord). For a Health Canada-regulated study, ask CORD for the validation records that cover your use; inspectors can ask for them. Research team obligations:

- Only users listed on the REB-approved protocol and the Task Delegation Log should have REDCap project access
- Remove user access when team members leave a study — this requires a deliberate action in the REDCap project, not just removal from the TDL
- Enable two-factor authentication (2FA) on your REDCap account
- Do not export REDCap data to personal devices or personal cloud storage (see storage requirements above)

### Validated platforms for eConsent and electronic records

When selecting an eConsent or eICF platform for a decentralized trial, platform security is an inspection-readiness question as much as an IT one. ICH E6(R3) requires that electronic systems used in clinical trials maintain audit trails, access logs, and data governance documentation from activation — retroactive remediation before an inspection is not always possible.

Before adopting any sponsor-provided or investigator-selected digital platform:
- Confirm the platform has a validation status document (IQ/OQ/PQ or equivalent)
- Confirm audit trail and access log retention meets the study's retention period
- Confirm the platform complies with Health Canada GUI-0100 requirements for electronic records

Contact the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) before committing to a new digital platform in a study protocol — platform selection is a setup decision, not a conduct item.
