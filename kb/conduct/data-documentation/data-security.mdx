Where you may keep human data from your study, who may receive them and how you may send them depend on whether the data are published and on how identifiable they are. The RI-MUHC sets controls for each kind of data in its [*Researcher's Guide to Data Security*](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=111401908461398782) (version 8, PDF).

## Know how identifiable your data are

The [MUHC Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) sorts human data into four types.

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| Aggregate | Counts, summary tables and other statistics about groups, with no data on individuals. |
| Anonymized | Data on individuals, irrevocably stripped of direct identifiers, with no code kept to link them back to people and a low or very low risk that anyone could be identified from what remains. |
| De-identified (coded) | Data on individuals whose direct identifiers have been replaced by a code. A key links the code back to the people. |
| Identifying | Data with any direct or indirect identifier, such as a name, address, contact information, medical record number (MRN), health insurance (RAMQ) number, date of birth or postal code. |

<Warning>
Data are anonymized only if no key exists to link them back to people. If a key exists anywhere, even stored separately, the data are de-identified, and they can still identify people.
</Warning>

For health information that the MUHC holds, Quebec's [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1#se:111) (Law 5) counts information as anonymized only if it is, at all times, reasonably foreseeable in the circumstances that it irreversibly no longer allows anyone to be identified, even indirectly.

Indirect identifiers, such as a postal code or a date of birth, can point to a person when they are combined with other information. Linking datasets can also make people identifiable again. [Data linkage needs the approval](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html) of the [Research Ethics Board (REB)](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you do it.

Each type has [its own requirements](/kb/design-support/data-warehouse#requirements-by-data-type) before you can obtain MUHC patient data from the Data Warehouse. When the study uses health records without consent, the [privacy rules](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent) apply too.

## Apply the controls for each kind of data

Each kind of data falls into a security class, and each class has its own controls.

| Security class | Public | Restricted (general) | Sensitive (highly confidential) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it covers | Published processed or analysed data, conclusions and other intellectual property, with no identifying data. | Unpublished aggregate data, and the Data Warehouse's Data Summary and Data Dictionary. | Unpublished processed or analysed data, research conclusions and other intellectual property. Also unpublished data on individuals, with or without identifiers, and lists of participants or patients. |
| Who may receive it | Anyone, within the grant's or publisher's conditions. | The RI-MUHC research team and anyone on the study's MUHC-approved access list in Nagano. | Only people on the study's MUHC-approved access list in Nagano. |
| What you need before you receive or collect it | Nothing | For the Data Summary and Data Dictionary, access to the RI-MUHC Portal. Otherwise, a principal investigator (the person who leads the study at the MUHC) who is an RI-MUHC investigator. | A principal investigator who is an RI-MUHC investigator, REB approval and MUHC Authorization. For de-identified or identifying data obtained without the participants' consent, also an approved privacy impact assessment (EFVP) before MUHC Authorization. |
| Digital storage | No restrictions beyond the grant's or publisher's conditions. | Where the public cannot reach it. | Where only the people on the access list can reach it. |
| Paper | No restrictions beyond the grant's or publisher's conditions. | In a monitored RI-MUHC or MUHC area, or under constant attention in a public area. Off site, under constant attention. Stored in a locked area. | In a room that only specific people can enter, in a locked cabinet. Never taken or used off site. |
| Email | Any email service. | MUHC or McGill email, or a reliable service such as Gmail. | Only as an encrypted file, through a service that uses encryption. |
| Cloud and websites | You may post it on your website if the grant or publisher allows. | A cloud service that uses encryption. | Only encrypted, with a provider that uses encryption and two-factor authentication, keeps its servers in Canada or the European Union and leaves the encryption keys with the MUHC alone, such as NextCloud. |
| Laptops and USB keys | No restrictions beyond the grant's or publisher's conditions. | Password-protected | Password-protected, under constant supervision or locked in a room on site, and encrypted when taken or used off site. |

A principal investigator who is an RI-MUHC investigator holds RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status and up-to-date training on the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) at the appropriate level. The RI-MUHC's SOPs add these rules for study records:

- [Participant information should not be saved on electronic shared drives](/sops/cr-015#5-1-13), except temporarily during a transfer.
- [Research documents that identify participants should be kept apart](/sops/cr-019#5-3-2) from the other study documents.
- [Store completed case report forms apart from participants' identifying information](/sops/cr-014#5-10-5), in a secure area that only study personnel can enter.
- [Limit access to study data and documents](/sops/cr-015#5-1-7) to the research team and the people whom the protocol, the consent form and the Task Delegation Log identify.

In your REB application, describe how you will [protect the information through its whole life cycle](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html), from collection to disposal. In general, identifiable data kept on a computer connected to the Internet should be encrypted. ## Put an agreement in place before data leave or arrive

Before data go to a site outside the MUHC or come from one, all parties [must sign a data sharing or transfer agreement](/sops/cr-106#1-1). Use the template from the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca). McGill University researchers count as external collaborators. [Upload the agreement](/sops/cr-007#5-4-6) both in Nagano and in My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal, so that the contract review starts.

## Archive the data when the study ends

Archive data on individuals in a secure, institutionally approved environment, never on a personal device. Keep access restricted for [the retention period that applies to your study](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).

For a database your team manages, the [RI-MUHC's SOPs require you](/sops/cr-107#5-4-database-archiving) to do the following, unless the study contract or the data management plan sets otherwise:

- Export the data, metadata, audit trails and queries to long-term storage media in an open format, such as CSV or PDF. Label the media with the archive date and how long they must be kept.
- Record the hardware and software used, with their versions, in the database manual or the data management plan.
- Record the user history, including each user's access rights, every change and the dates they were authorized.
- Remove all access to the database except an administrator account that controls the user accounts.
- Keep the archive in a secure environment with backup copies, on storage media that are replicated.
- Test on a regular schedule, such as every five years, that the archived database can still be retrieved and run.

If you are the sponsor-investigator, you [should plan the archiving of the trial master file](/sops/cr-015#5-7-4) from the start of the study.

Clinical data from many RI-MUHC projects cannot go in McGill University's Dataverse repository, because they hold health information on individual participants. As of 2023, the RI-MUHC keeps them on its secure servers in the MUHC's digital environment. The RI-MUHC strongly encourages you to consult [RI-MUHC IT](mailto:ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca) about each project's secure storage and computing space, secure ways to access and share data, and the security of apps and tools that collect data from participants.

To keep the study's systems secure:

- [Give access to an electronic data capture system](/sops/cr-014#5-8-3) only to people delegated that task on the Task Delegation Log, after their training on it is documented.
- When someone leaves the study, remove their access to the systems you control, such as REDCap. You should also tell the sponsor when someone's access to its systems needs to be changed or ended.
- Consider checking unexpected attachments, even from a sponsor or a contract research organization, with the sender by phone or by a separate email before opening them.
- A request for your password by email is a warning sign. Suspicious emails can be reported to [RI-MUHC IT](mailto:ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca).
- Consider turning on two-factor authentication for your REDCap account.

If data are lost, sent to the wrong person or seen by someone who should not see them, [report the incident](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person). [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/design-support/cord) supports REDCap at the RI-MUHC, and can provide its validation records for Health Canada-regulated studies. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator [must make sure that every electronic system used in the study has been validated and complies with the US rule on electronic records](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#check-electronic-systems-before-you-use-them).
