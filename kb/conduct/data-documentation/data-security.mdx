Where you may keep human data from your study, who may receive them and how you may send them depend on whether the data are published and on how identifiable they are. Each kind of data has its own controls, set out in the RI-MUHC's [*Researcher's Guide to Data Security*](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=111401908461398782) (version 8; PDF, sign-in required). The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) add rules for study records.

## Know how identifiable your data are

The MUHC Data Warehouse sorts human data into four types.

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| Aggregate | Counts, summary tables and other statistics about groups, with no data on individuals |
| Anonymized | Data on individuals, irrevocably stripped of direct identifiers, with no code kept to link them back to people and a low or very low risk that anyone could be identified from what remains |
| De-identified (coded) | Data on individuals whose direct identifiers have been replaced by a code; a key links the code back to the people |
| Identifying | Data with any direct or indirect identifier, such as a name, address, contact information, medical record number (MRN), health insurance (RAMQ) number, date of birth or postal code |

<Warning>
Data are anonymized only if no key exists to link them back to people. If a key exists anywhere, even stored separately, the data are de-identified, and they can still identify people.
</Warning>

For health information that the MUHC holds, Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information (Law 5) counts information as anonymized only if it is, at all times, reasonably foreseeable in the circumstances that it irreversibly no longer allows anyone to be identified, even indirectly.

Indirect identifiers, such as a postal code or a date of birth, can point to a person when they are combined with other information. Linking datasets can also make people identifiable again, so linkage needs the approval of the Research Ethics Board (REB) before you do it.

What each type needs before you can obtain MUHC patient data is on the [Data Warehouse page](/kb/design-support/data-warehouse#requirements-by-data-type). When the study uses health records without consent, the [privacy rules](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent) apply too.

## Apply the controls for each kind of data

Each kind of data falls into a security class, and each class has its own controls.

| | Published processed or analysed data, conclusions and other intellectual property, with no identifying data | Unpublished aggregate data, and the Data Warehouse's Data Summary and Data Dictionary | Unpublished processed or analysed data, research conclusions and other intellectual property; unpublished data on individuals, with or without identifiers; lists of participants or patients |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security class | Public | Restricted (general) | Sensitive (highly confidential) |
| Who may receive it | Anyone, within the grant's or publisher's conditions | The RI-MUHC research team and anyone on the study's MUHC-approved access list in Nagano | Only people on the study's MUHC-approved access list in Nagano |
| What you need first | Nothing | Access to the RI-MUHC Portal, for the Data Summary and Data Dictionary; otherwise, a principal investigator (the person who leads the study at the MUHC) who is an RI-MUHC investigator | A principal investigator who is an RI-MUHC investigator, REB approval and MUHC Authorization; for de-identified or identifying data obtained without the participants' consent, also a privacy impact assessment (EFVP) |
| Digital storage | No restrictions beyond the grant's or publisher's conditions | Where the public cannot reach it | Where only the people on the access list can reach it |
| Paper | No restrictions beyond the grant's or publisher's conditions | In a monitored RI-MUHC or MUHC area, or under constant attention in a public area; off site, under constant attention; stored in a locked area | In a room that only specific people can enter, in a locked cabinet; never taken or used off site |
| Email | Any email service | MUHC or McGill email, or a reliable service such as Gmail | Only as an encrypted file, through a service that uses encryption |
| Cloud and websites | You may post it on your website if the grant or publisher allows | A cloud service that uses encryption | Only encrypted, with a provider that uses encryption and two-factor authentication, keeps its servers in Canada or the European Union and leaves the encryption keys with the MUHC alone, such as NextCloud |
| Laptops and USB keys | No restrictions beyond the grant's or publisher's conditions | Password-protected | Password-protected, under constant supervision or locked in a room on site, and encrypted when taken or used off site |

A principal investigator who is an RI-MUHC investigator holds RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status and up-to-date SOP training at the appropriate level; people working for them count too. Clinical data that are confidential become sensitive once they are accessed or collected for research.

Keep the key that links de-identified data back to participants apart from the data, and give access to it only to authorized members of the research team. For double-coded data, a third party holds the key and the research team has no access to it.

<Warning>
Never send data on individuals, even with the identifiers removed, through MUHC, McGill or Gmail email.
</Warning>

Send the password for an encrypted file separately, by text message or phone, never in the same email or thread. To encrypt a file, use a tool such as 7-Zip.

The RI-MUHC's SOPs add these rules for study records:

- Participant information should not be saved on electronic shared drives, except temporarily during a transfer.
- Research documents that identify participants should be kept apart from the other study documents.
- Store completed case report forms apart from participants' identifying information, in a secure area that only study personnel can enter.
- Limit access to study data and documents to the research team and the people whom the protocol, the consent form and the Task Delegation Log identify.

In your REB application, describe how you will protect the information through its whole life cycle, from collection to disposal. In general, identifiable data kept on a computer connected to the Internet should be encrypted. Never store data on individuals, even de-identified data, on your own laptop, phone or USB key, or in a personal cloud account. For remote access to the MUHC network, use Citrix; the RI-MUHC's information technology (IT) team can set it up.

## Put an agreement in place before data leave or arrive

Before data go to a site outside the MUHC, or come from one, all parties must sign a data sharing or transfer agreement. Use the template from the Research Agreements Office ([contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca)). McGill University researchers count as external collaborators. Upload the agreement both in Nagano and in My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal (sign-in required), so that the contract review starts.

Based on: [SOP-CR-106](/sops/cr-106), RI-MUHC Addendum, section 1.1; [SOP-CR-007, section 5.4](/sops/cr-007#5-4-initial-reb-and-institutional-review).

## Archive the data when the study ends

Archive data on individuals in a secure, institutionally approved environment, never on a personal device, and keep access restricted for the retention period that applies to your study; see [how long study records are kept](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).

For a database your team manages, the RI-MUHC's SOPs require you to do the following, unless the study contract or the data management plan sets otherwise:

- Export the data, metadata, audit trails and queries to long-term storage in an open format, such as CSV or PDF, labelled with the archive date and how long it must be kept.
- Record the hardware and software used, with their versions, in the database manual or the data management plan.
- Record the user history, including each user's access rights, every change and the dates they were authorized.
- Remove all access to the database except an administrator account that controls the user accounts.
- Keep the archive in a secure environment with backup copies, on storage media that are replicated.
- Test on a regular schedule, such as every five years, that the archived database can still be retrieved and run.

If you are the sponsor-investigator, plan the archiving of the trial master file from the start of the study.

Clinical data from many RI-MUHC projects cannot go in McGill University's Dataverse repository, because they hold health information on individual participants; the RI-MUHC keeps them on its secure servers in the MUHC's digital environment (as of its research data management strategy, 2023). The RI-MUHC strongly encourages you to consult RI-MUHC IT, project by project, about secure storage and computing space, secure ways to access and share data, and the security of apps and tools that collect data from participants.

Based on: [SOP-CR-107](/sops/cr-107), section 5.4; [SOP-CR-015](/sops/cr-015), section 5.7; the RI-MUHC's *Research Data Management Strategy* (2023).

## Respect First Nations, Inuit and Métis data governance

The RI-MUHC commits, in its research data management strategy, to a distinctions-based approach for research with First Nations, the Métis Nation and Inuit communities and their data. Which data governance applies to each People is on [Indigenous Research Ethics](/kb/recruitment/indigenous-research-ethics#respect-the-data-governance-of-each-people).

Based on: the RI-MUHC's *Research Data Management Strategy* (2023).

## Keep accounts and systems secure

Each person on the team has their own account for every system the study uses, and passwords are never shared. A password manager helps you keep a different strong password for each sponsor portal.

- Give access to an electronic data capture system only to people delegated that task on the Task Delegation Log, after their training on it is documented.
- When someone leaves the study, remove their access to the systems you control, such as REDCap, and tell the sponsor, so that it can end their access to its systems.
- Check unexpected attachments, even from a sponsor or a contract research organization, with the sender by phone or by a separate email before opening them. A request for your password by email is a warning sign; report suspicious emails to RI-MUHC IT.
- Turn on two-factor authentication for your REDCap account.

If data are lost, sent to the wrong person or seen by someone who should not see them, report it as set out in [Report information that is lost or seen by the wrong person](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person). REDCap at the RI-MUHC, including its validation records for Health Canada-regulated studies, is covered on the page of [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/design-support/cord). Checking an electronic platform before a study adopts it is covered on [Data Integrity](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-integrity#check-electronic-systems-before-you-use-them).
