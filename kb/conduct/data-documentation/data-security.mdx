Where you may keep human data from your study, who may receive them and how you may send them depend on whether the data are published and on how identifiable they are. The RI-MUHC sets controls for each kind of data in its [*Researcher's Guide to Data Security*](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=111401908461398782) (version 8, PDF).

## Write a data management plan

A funder, a sponsor or the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) may ask you for a <Tooltip tip="A study-specific document that details all of a study's data management processes, from study start to database lock and archive." headline="Data Management Plan (DMP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#data-management-plan-dmp">data management plan</Tooltip>. Who asks for one, and when, depends on who funds and who runs the study:

| Who asks for a plan | When |
|---|---|
| The federal research agencies: the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR), the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada (NSERC) and the Social Sciences and Humanities Research Council of Canada (SSHRC) | For certain funding opportunities, [the agencies require a plan with the application](https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/interagency-research-funding/policies-and-guidelines/research-data-management/tri-agency-research-data-management-policy), as the call for proposals sets out. The plan is then considered in the adjudication. CIHR adds the requirement to funding opportunities as pilots, so check each [funding opportunity on ResearchNet](https://www.researchnet-recherchenet.ca) to learn whether it requires a plan. |
| The Fonds de recherche du Québec (FRQ) | The FRQ [has not adopted a research data management policy](https://frq.gouv.qc.ca/gestion-des-donnees/) that would require data management plans (French only). |
| The RI-MUHC's SOPs | When data management staff or other members of the research team are responsible for writing or versioning a plan, [the data manager or a designate writes a new plan for each new study](/sops/cr-103#5-2-1), based on the protocol, the scope of work, the contract, the analysis plans, the data flow, the case report forms and any other applicable documents. The sponsor-investigator, the principal investigator and the data management staff involved are [responsible for ensuring that all the study's data management activities at the site meet](/sops/cr-103#responsibilities) all the applicable regulatory, good clinical practice, sponsor and local requirements. Any or all parts of writing or versioning the plan may be delegated to appropriately trained members of the study team. The work remains the ultimate responsibility of the sponsor, the sponsor-investigator or the principal investigator. |
| The sponsor of a sponsored study | The sponsor's plan [may set the timelines](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/edc-query-management#enter-data-promptly) for entering data and answering queries. |

A journal may ask for something else. A clinical trial reported in an International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) journal needs a [data sharing statement, and for some trials a data sharing plan in its registration](/kb/close-out/publications#share-individual-participant-data).

For research the agencies fund that is conducted by and with First Nations, Métis and Inuit communities, collectives and organizations, [the plan must be co-developed with them](/kb/recruitment/indigenous-research-ethics#respect-the-data-governance-of-each-people), in accordance with research data management principles or plan formats that they accept.

For a funding opportunity subject to both the National Security Guidelines for Research Partnerships and the agencies' requirement for a data management plan, researchers [should identify in their Risk Assessment Form the risks that the plan will mitigate](https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/interagency-research-funding/policies-and-guidelines/research-data-management/data-management-plan-guidance), in part or in whole. The risk mitigation plan section of the Risk Assessment Form must also describe how the plan will address the data-related risks.

### What a plan covers

The content and length of a plan depend on the project. A plan for the federal research agencies should describe:

- How data will be collected, documented, formatted, protected and preserved.
- How existing datasets will be used, and what new data the project will create.
- Whether and how data will be shared.
- Where data will be deposited.

A plan for the agencies also says who is responsible for managing the project's data, and what happens if that person leaves the research team. Where appropriate, it gives the data roles of other team members. The plan sets out the ethical, legal and commercial constraints on the data, and the methodological considerations that support or preclude sharing them.

A plan is a living document, and [it should be revised and updated as the research project progresses](https://science.gc.ca/site/science/en/interagency-research-funding/policies-and-guidelines/research-data-management/data-management-plan-guidance). The agencies encourage researchers to consider using the Digital Research Alliance of Canada's [DMP Assistant](https://dmp-pgd.ca/), a free, bilingual online service for writing a plan.

The plan that the RI-MUHC's SOPs describe details every data management process, from the start of the study to the database lock and archiving. [Its sections may include, among others](/sops/cr-103#5-2-4), the database roles and privileges, the design of the case report forms, data entry and the query process with its timelines, the reconciliation of serious adverse events, data validation, database security and archiving. Identify the plan on every page, for example by the study code and title.

Having the first version of the plan finalized, approved, signed and available before the work it describes begins is of [great value to the research team](/sops/cr-103#5-2-6). [Keep the plan current throughout the study](/sops/cr-103#5-3-1), including protocol changes and addenda, and changes of procedure. For each new version, note its author, the date of the change, the reason for it and the new version number.

### Have your plan reviewed

[Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](mailto:cord@rimuhc.ca) can help review the data plan in a study protocol or grant application, including how the data will be collected, cleaned, stored and shared, and how much it might cost. [CORD's fees](/kb/design-support/cord#fees) apply to its support.

[McGill University's Digital Research Services](mailto:drs@mcgill.ca) and [McGill Libraries](mailto:rdm.library@mcgill.ca) also [review data management plans](https://www.mcgill.ca/drs/rdm/mcgill-rdm-strategy-v30/appendix-b-current-available-services-and-support). Digital Research Services can help McGill researchers [prepare their plans](https://www.mcgill.ca/drs/rdm/data-management-plans).

## Know how identifiable your data are

The [MUHC Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) sorts human data into four types.

| Type | What it is |
|---|---|
| Aggregate | Counts, summary tables and other statistics about groups, with no data on individuals. |
| Anonymized | Data on individuals, irrevocably stripped of direct identifiers, with no code kept to link them back to people and a low or very low risk that anyone could be identified from what remains. |
| De-identified (coded) | Data on individuals whose direct identifiers have been replaced by a code. A key links the code back to the people. |
| Identifying | Data with any direct or indirect identifier, such as a name, address, contact information, medical record number (MRN), health insurance (RAMQ) number, date of birth or postal code. |

<Warning>
Data are anonymized only if no key exists to link them back to people. If a key exists anywhere, even stored separately, the data are de-identified, and they can still identify people.
</Warning>

For health information that the MUHC holds, Quebec's [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1#se:111) (Law 5) counts information as anonymized only if it is, at all times, reasonably foreseeable in the circumstances that it irreversibly no longer allows anyone to be identified, even indirectly.

Indirect identifiers, such as a postal code or a date of birth, can point to a person when they are combined with other information. Linking datasets can also make people identifiable again. [Data linkage needs the approval](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html) of the [Research Ethics Board (REB)](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you do it.

Each type has [its own requirements](/kb/design-support/patient-data#requirements-by-data-type) before you can obtain MUHC patient data from the Data Warehouse. When the study uses health records without consent, the [privacy rules](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent) apply too.

## Apply the controls for each kind of data

Each kind of data falls into a security class, and each class has its own controls.

| Security class | Public | Restricted (general) | Sensitive (highly confidential) |
|---|---|---|---|
| What it covers | Published processed or analysed data, conclusions and other intellectual property, with no identifying data. | Unpublished aggregate data, and the Data Warehouse's Data Summary and Data Dictionary. | Unpublished processed or analysed data, research conclusions and other intellectual property. Also unpublished data on individuals, with or without identifiers, and lists of participants or patients. |
| Who may receive it | Anyone, within the grant's or publisher's conditions. | The RI-MUHC research team and anyone on the study's MUHC-approved access list in Nagano. | Only people on the study's MUHC-approved access list in Nagano. |
| What you need before you receive or collect it | Nothing | For the Data Summary and Data Dictionary, access to the RI-MUHC Portal. Otherwise, a principal investigator (the person who leads the study at the MUHC) who is an RI-MUHC investigator. | A principal investigator who is an RI-MUHC investigator, REB approval and MUHC Authorization. For de-identified or identifying data obtained without the participants' consent, also an approved privacy impact assessment (EFVP) before MUHC Authorization. |
| Digital storage | No restrictions beyond the grant's or publisher's conditions. | Where the public cannot reach it. | Where only the people on the access list can reach it. |
| Paper | No restrictions beyond the grant's or publisher's conditions. | In a monitored RI-MUHC or MUHC area, or under constant attention in a public area. Off site, under constant attention. Stored in a locked area. | In a room that only specific people can enter, in a locked cabinet. Never taken or used off site. |
| Email | Any email service. | MUHC or McGill email, or a reliable service such as Gmail. | Only as an encrypted file, through a service that uses encryption. |
| Cloud and websites | You may post it on your website if the grant or publisher allows. | A cloud service that uses encryption. | Only encrypted, with a provider that uses encryption and two-factor authentication, keeps its servers in Canada or the European Union and leaves the encryption keys with the MUHC alone, such as NextCloud. |
| Laptops and USB keys | No restrictions beyond the grant's or publisher's conditions. | Password-protected | Password-protected, under constant supervision or locked in a room on site, and encrypted when taken or used off site. |

A principal investigator who is an RI-MUHC investigator holds RI-MUHC Privileges or Researcher Status and up-to-date training on the RI-MUHC's SOPs at the appropriate level. People who work for that principal investigator meet the requirement too.{/* Source: the *Researcher's Guide to Data Security*, version 8, the note marked with one asterisk under its table: "Must be an RI-MUHC Investigator (or someone working for them) with Privileges and up-to-date SOP training at the appropriate level" (the guide's whole text is among the provided documents; read Nov. 15, 2026). The question to the Data Warehouse team is closed. */} Clinical data that are confidential become sensitive once they are accessed or collected for research.

Keep the key that links de-identified data back to participants apart from the data. Give access to the key only to authorized members of the research team. For double-coded data, a third party holds the key and the research team has no access to it.

<Warning>
Never send data on individuals, even with the identifiers removed, through MUHC, McGill or Gmail email.
</Warning>

When study data files are transferred to or from the study database, [send the password to the recipient in a separate transmission](/sops/cr-106#5-2-7). For any other encrypted file, consider sending the password by text message or by phone rather than in the same email thread. To encrypt a file, use a tool such as 7-Zip.

The RI-MUHC's SOPs add these rules for study records:

- [Participant information should not be saved on electronic shared drives](/sops/cr-015#5-1-13), except temporarily during a transfer.
- [Research documents that identify participants should be kept apart](/sops/cr-019#5-3-2) from the other study documents.
- [Store completed case report forms apart from participants' identifying information](/sops/cr-014#5-10-5), in a secure area that only study personnel can enter.
- [Limit access to study data and documents](/sops/cr-015#5-1-7) to the research team and the people whom the protocol, the consent form and the Task Delegation Log identify.

In your REB application, describe how you will [protect the information through its whole life cycle](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter5-chapitre5.html), from collection to disposal. In general, identifiable data kept on a computer connected to the Internet should be encrypted. Never store data on individuals on your own laptop, phone or USB key, or in a personal cloud account, even when the data are de-identified.

For remote access to the MUHC network, use Citrix. The RI-MUHC's [information technology (IT) team](mailto:ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca) can set it up.

## Put an agreement in place before data leave or arrive

[Upload the agreement](/sops/cr-007#5-4-6) both in Nagano and in My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal, so that the contract review starts.

## Archive the data when the study ends

Archive data on individuals in a secure, institutionally approved environment, never on a personal device. Keep access restricted for [the retention period that applies to your study](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving).

For a database your team manages, the [RI-MUHC's SOPs require you](/sops/cr-107#5-4-database-archiving) to do the following, unless the study contract or the data management plan sets otherwise:

- Export the data, metadata, audit trails and queries to long-term storage media in an open format, such as CSV or PDF. Label the media with the archive date and how long they must be kept.
- Record the hardware and software used, with their versions, in the database manual or the data management plan.
- Record the user history, including each user's access rights, every change and the dates they were authorized.
- Remove all access to the database except an administrator account that controls the user accounts.
- Keep the archive in a secure environment with backup copies, on storage media that are replicated.
- Test on a regular schedule, such as every five years, that the archived database can still be retrieved and run.

If you are the sponsor-investigator, you [should plan the archiving of the trial master file](/sops/cr-015#5-7-4) from the start of the study.

Clinical data from many RI-MUHC projects cannot go in McGill University's Dataverse repository, because they hold health information on individual participants. As of 2023, the RI-MUHC keeps them on its secure servers in the MUHC's digital environment. The RI-MUHC strongly encourages you to consult [RI-MUHC IT](mailto:ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca) about each project's secure storage and computing space, secure ways to access and share data, and the security of apps and tools that collect data from participants.

## Keep accounts and systems secure

Each person on the team has their own account for every system the study uses, and passwords are never shared. Consider using a password manager to keep a different strong password for each sponsor portal.

To keep the study's systems secure:

- [Give access to an electronic data capture system](/sops/cr-014#5-8-3) only to people delegated that task on the Task Delegation Log, after their training on it is documented.
- When someone leaves the study, remove their access to the systems you control, such as REDCap. You should also tell the sponsor when someone's access to its systems needs to be changed or ended.
- Consider checking unexpected attachments, even from a sponsor or a contract research organization, with the sender by phone or by a separate email before opening them.
- A request for your password by email is a warning sign. Suspicious emails can be reported to [RI-MUHC IT](mailto:ri.it@muhc.mcgill.ca).
- Consider turning on two-factor authentication for your REDCap account.

If data are lost, sent to the wrong person or seen by someone who should not see them, [report the incident](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person). [Capture and Optimization of Research Data (CORD)](/kb/design-support/cord) supports REDCap at the RI-MUHC, and can provide its validation records for Health Canada-regulated studies. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-004#5-1-3) require the sponsor or sponsor-investigator to make sure that every electronic system used in the study has been validated and complies with 21 CFR Part 11, the US rule on electronic records.
