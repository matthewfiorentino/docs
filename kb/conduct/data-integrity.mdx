A clinical trial record may be inspected up to 15 years after a study closes — long after the people who ran it have moved on. The record has to stand alone and tell the story of what happened without anyone present to explain it. That is why documentation requirements exist.

## Why the requirements exist

Every documentation requirement in GCP — initialling corrections, prohibiting Wite-Out, requiring contemporaneous entries — exists for one reason: the study record must be able to stand alone. A Health Canada inspector reviewing a trial five years after closure, or a court examining an adverse outcome fifteen years later, will rely entirely on what was written down. No one will be present to explain what was meant or fill in gaps.

This is the 15-year inspection frame. It is not an abstraction. Under Health Canada regulations, clinical trial records must be retained for 15 years after study completion for HC-regulated studies. Sponsor records must be kept for 25 years. The record does not belong to the research team — it belongs to the public interest in safe, well-documented research.

<Note>
**This is different from data security.** Data integrity is about the trustworthiness and auditability of the research record — whether it was documented correctly in the first place. Data security is about protecting the data you hold from unauthorized access or breach. Both matter, but they address different risks. See [Research Data Security](/kb/conduct/data-security) for the storage and sharing framework.
</Note>

## ALCOA+ in practice

ALCOA+ is the foundational framework for good documentation practice in clinical research, defined in ICH GCP E6(R3). The original five principles form the acronym ALCOA; four additional principles were added to address electronic records and modern data management. At the RI-MUHC, ALCOA+ applies across all study records — hospital records in OACIS, data entered in REDCap, paper source documents, and submission records in Nagano.

**ALCOA — the original five principles**

| Letter | Principle | What it means |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| A | Attributable | It must be clear who collected or entered the data, and when. Every entry signed and dated by the person who made it. |
| L | Legible | The record must be readable now and 15 years from now. Permanent ink. No overwriting that obscures the original. |
| C | Contemporaneous | Recorded at the time the activity occurred — not reconstructed from memory later. Backdating is a serious GCP violation. |
| O | Original | The first place data was recorded is the source. Transcriptions must be traceable back to the original source document. |
| A | Accurate | Reflects what actually happened. Errors corrected with a single line through the original, initialled and dated, with the reason noted. |

**+ Four additional principles (ICH GCP E6(R3))**

| Letter | Principle | What it means |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| C | Complete | All data required by the protocol is present. No blank fields without explanation. Missing data is documented as such. |
| C | Consistent | Data is internally consistent across documents. Dates, values, and sequences match across source documents and CRF entries. |
| E | Enduring | Recorded on durable media that will remain readable for the required retention period. Pencil, sticky notes, and erasable ink are not permitted. |
| A | Available | Can be retrieved for audit or inspection when required. Records must be stored where they can be located and accessed on request. |

### Documentation do's and don'ts

The following table covers the most common documentation errors seen during Health Canada inspections and QA audits at the RI-MUHC.

| Good documentation practice | Documentation errors that generate findings |
|-----------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Errors corrected with a single line, leaving the original legible; initialled, dated, and reason noted | Using Wite-Out, correction fluid, or tape over any entry on any document |
| Source data signed and dated by the person who collected it, at the time of collection | Backdating entries, or recording events from memory after the fact without noting the retrospective nature |
| All signature and date fields completed at the time of the activity | Leaving signature or date fields blank, to be completed later |
| Approved, current versions of all documents at the point of use; version history visible | Using superseded versions of SOPs, forms, or templates; no version control on study documents |
| Transcription errors from source data corrected with a visible annotation and reason | Undocumented corrections of any kind — including minor typos in a CRF or case report form |
| Notes-to-File used to document why an entry is missing, late, or out of sequence | Reconstructed entries without any indication they were recorded retrospectively |
| Data recorded in permanent ink; documents stored to prevent deterioration | Pencil entries, sticky notes, or erasable ink on any source document or study record |
| Delegation log reflects actual task assignments; updated when team members join or leave | Team members performing tasks not listed on their delegation log entry |

<Warning>
**The 12 most common Health Canada inspection findings** include incorrect or missing version numbers on study documents, incomplete delegation logs, and undocumented corrections. Version numbering in particular is flagged as a common finding: incorrect versioning of the protocol, ICF, or study documents is a pattern Health Canada inspectors specifically look for. See Monitoring, Audits and Inspections for the full findings list and how to prepare.
</Warning>

## Source documents at the RI-MUHC

A source document is the original record where data was first captured. At the RI-MUHC, source documents include hospital records in OACIS, ICFs, inclusion/exclusion criteria checklists, randomization records, laboratory notes and reports, pharmacy dispensing and storage records, concomitant medication records, participant diaries, communication logs, and data from automated instruments.

**OACIS is the primary source for most clinical data.** Anything documented in OACIS is, by definition, a source document. Data transcribed from OACIS into a CRF or REDCap database must be traceable back to the OACIS record. Monitors will verify this traceability during Source Data Verification (SDV).

For most low-to-moderate complexity studies at the RI-MUHC, REDCap is the eCRF platform. REDCap is validated and compliant with 21 CFR Part 11. For studies requiring more than approximately 1,000 variables, discuss platform requirements with the DW team or sponsor. Any electronic data capture system used in an HC-regulated study must be validated before use — contact IT via the RI-MUHC Portal before the Site Initiation Visit.

<Note>
**Source documents must stay in their original state.** A source document cannot be corrected after the fact by overwriting. If a correction is necessary, the original entry must remain legible. The correction is made alongside — never on top of — the original, with initials, date, and reason.
</Note>

## Certified copies

A certified copy is a legal substitute for an original source document. Monitors, auditors, and Health Canada inspectors may request certified copies for review. A certified copy is only valid if it meets three conditions: it is a complete, accurate reproduction of the original; it was made directly from the original by the person certifying it; and it is marked with the certifier's initials, date, and the statement **"True and Exact Copy."**

The person who makes the copy must be the person who certifies it. This cannot be delegated — certification is a personal attestation that you made this copy from the original and verify its accuracy. QA assists with certified copy control procedures during inspections.

## Document retention

Records must be retained for the full applicable period before any destruction. Destruction requires sponsor authorization for sponsor-held records, and must follow institutional procedures. Early destruction of study records is a serious regulatory violation.

| Study type | Retention period | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|-------|
| HC-regulated studies (drug, device, NHP) — any study subject to Health Canada regulations under Part C Division 5, ITA, or NHP CTA | 15 years after study completion | Sponsor-held records: 25 years. Destruction requires sponsor authorization. |
| Non-regulated studies — prospective observational, retrospective, secondary data analysis | 7 years after study completion | Institutional policy minimum. Individual funding agencies may require longer. |
| Essential documents (ISF/TMF) | Per above, by study type | The Investigator Site File must be complete and current throughout the study. See SOP-CR-015 for ISF/TMF content requirements. |
