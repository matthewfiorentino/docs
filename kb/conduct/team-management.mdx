Research teams change. Staff leave, new coordinators join, a Sub-Investigator takes parental leave, a <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI moves institutions. Every change to the team during an active study has documentation implications — primarily on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>, but also potentially with the Sponsor, the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>, and Health Canada.

<Note>
Before assembling your team, see Building Your Research Team for guidance on who is eligible to be on a research protocol, what each role can be delegated, and the rules governing trainees, volunteers, and observers.
</Note>

## The Task Delegation Log

The Task Delegation Log (TDL) is the central document for team management during a study. It is not completed once at activation and filed — it is a living document that must reflect the actual composition of the team at all times. Every person performing delegated protocol tasks must be on the TDL before conducting those tasks. Every person who stops must have an end date recorded.

The QI/PI's signature on each delegation entry is not a formality — it is their documented assurance that the delegated person is trained and qualified for the specific tasks assigned. This matters because the QI/PI retains ultimate responsibility for the conduct of the study regardless of how tasks are distributed.

During monitoring visits, the monitor verifies that the TDL is current and that it accurately reflects who is working on the study. A TDL that does not match the team's actual composition — missing end dates for departed staff, tasks assigned to people not yet trained — is a common finding in monitoring visits and regulatory inspections.

<Note>
For the Task Delegation Log template and the full delegation requirements, see [SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002) Appendix 1.
</Note>

## New team members joining

When someone new joins an active study, the sequence is fixed: training first, TDL signature second. No study activities can occur before both are complete.

- **Institutional training** must be current and at the appropriate level for the study type. If the new team member does not have a valid certificate at the required level, they must complete it before being delegated tasks.
- **Protocol-specific training** must be documented on a Training Log, regardless of who delivers it — the existing team, the Sponsor, or the CRO. Self-training is permitted and must also be documented.
- **TDL signature** by both the team member and the QI/PI follows once training is documented. The QI/PI's signature confirms the person is qualified and trained for the tasks delegated.

The same requirement applies when the study protocol is amended. All team members must be trained on any amendments in a timely manner — directly after REB approval — and that training must be documented.

<Tip>
Training certificates are valid for two years from the date of completion. TalentLMS sends a reminder 30 days before expiry. An expired certificate means the team member is no longer qualified to perform delegated tasks until renewal is complete.
</Tip>

## Team members leaving

When a team member leaves — whether permanently, on leave, or at the end of a rotation — their end date must be recorded promptly on the TDL. If the departing team member held tasks that need to continue, a replacement must be identified, trained, and delegated before those tasks can resume.

For studies involving an <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip>, the Sponsor must be notified of significant team changes. The Sponsor may have their own notification requirements specified in the monitoring plan or site agreement.

If the QI/PI is moving institutions or can no longer serve in that role, a more extensive process applies — see Change of QI/PI below.

## Fellows and residents

There are important restrictions on what fellows, residents, and rotating teams can be delegated, which must be clearly reflected on the TDL.

**Residents on rotation** cannot be named as Sub-Investigators on a study.

**Fellows and longitudinal residents** may be named as Sub-Investigators on the TDL, but cannot be delegated unsupervised QI/PI tasks and cannot serve as QI/PI backup. Any medical assessments conducted by fellows or longitudinal residents must be co-signed by the QI/PI or a qualifying Sub-Investigator with Research Privileges. The TDL must clearly indicate which tasks require this co-signature, and the department should have a written working instruction specifying how this supervision is documented.

| | Fellows / longitudinal residents |
|-|----------------------------------|
| **Can be delegated** | Protocol-specified tasks under direct documented supervision; administrative and data-related tasks; medical assessments with QI/PI or qualifying Sub-I co-signature |
| **Cannot be delegated** | Unsupervised QI/PI tasks; acting as QI/PI vacation backup; independent medical oversight of the study |

## Service departments and rotating teams

When study tasks are delegated to a department or team on rotation (e.g., pharmacy, laboratory, nursing, imaging), the Manager or Supervisor of that team is added to the TDL. That Manager signs a separate Protocol-Specific Task and <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> Training form ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002) Appendix 2), confirming they have received training and will ensure their staff are qualified.

The Manager's signature on the TDL is sufficient — individual staff members within the rotating team do not each need to be on the site TDL, provided the Manager retains staff CVs and licences in their own department files.

Note that MUHC Pharmacy maintains its own Pharmacy Task Delegation Log. A completed copy must be obtained from the pharmacy and added to the site TDL package before archiving at study close-out.

## Leave of absence

If a team member is on leave for more than 6 months, they must redo their institutional SOP training at the appropriate level upon return — even if their certificate has not technically expired. A refresher quiz (in lieu of redoing the full course) is available via TalentLMS if the team member is confident in their knowledge.

When a QI/PI or Sub-Investigator goes on leave, a qualified backup must be in place and delegated before the leave begins. The backup QI/PI must hold Research Privileges (or Researcher Status for non-MD studies), the same qualifications, and the same training level as the QI/PI. A fellow or resident cannot serve as QI/PI backup regardless of the length or type of leave.

## Change of QI/PI

A change of QI/PI during an active study is one of the more consequential team changes because it involves obligations to the Sponsor, the REB, and Health Canada — all of which must be addressed, not just one. The incoming QI/PI must hold the same Research Privileges, qualifications, and training level as the outgoing QI/PI before assuming the role.

<Steps>

<Step title="Update the Task Delegation Log">
The outgoing QI/PI formally delegates all QI/PI tasks to the incoming QI/PI. Both sign. An end date is recorded for the outgoing QI/PI. Use the Study Team Member Change form ([SOP-CR-002](/sops/cr-002) Appendix 5) to document the change.
</Step>

<Step title="Notify the Sponsor">
The Sponsor must be informed of the change without delay. The Site Agreement and clinical trial contract may need to be revised and re-signed by both the outgoing and incoming QI/PI.
</Step>

<Step title="Submit an REB amendment">
A change of QI/PI is a protocol amendment requiring REB review before taking effect. Submit via <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> (Form F1). The change must not be treated as administrative — the REB must be informed without delay.
</Step>

<Step title="Update the Qualified Investigator Undertaking">
For Health Canada-regulated studies (drug, NHP, medical device), the Qualified Investigator Undertaking (QIU) must be reissued under the incoming QI/PI's name. The QIU is the Canadian equivalent of the FDA 1572 and is the formal regulatory commitment to Health Canada.
</Step>

<Step title="Confirm backup qualifications">
The incoming QI/PI must designate a new backup Sub-Investigator with the same Research Privileges, qualifications, and training level. Update the TDL to reflect the new backup designation.
</Step>

</Steps>

<Warning>
**All five steps must be completed.** Notifying only the Sponsor, or submitting only the REB amendment, is not sufficient. The change is not complete until the TDL, Sponsor, REB, and QIU (if applicable) are all updated.
</Warning>

<Warning>
**If no qualified replacement can be identified before the outgoing QI/PI's departure, enrollment must pause immediately.** Do not allow study activities to continue without a named QI/PI on the TDL. Contact <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> without delay: QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca
</Warning>
