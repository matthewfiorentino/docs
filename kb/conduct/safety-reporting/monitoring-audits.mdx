Three different forms of oversight operate during an active study — monitoring by the sponsor, audits by the sponsor or RI-MUHC <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip>, and regulatory inspections by Health Canada. Each has different triggers, different authority, and different consequences. What they share is a single underlying requirement: the record must be complete, accurate, and accessible at all times.

## Three forms of oversight

Understanding the distinction between monitoring, audits, and inspections matters because the same underlying principle governs all three: the record must be complete, accurate, and readily accessible at any point during or after the study.

|  | **Monitoring** | **Audits** | **Inspections** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator, via CRA | Sponsor (routine or for-cause) or RI-MUHC QA | Health Canada CTCP (or FDA) |
| **When** | Throughout the active study, per the Monitoring Plan | Any time during or after the study | Routine (~4 weeks' notice) or for-cause (no notice) |
| **Trigger** | Scheduled, risk-based under ICH E6(R3) | Risk or for cause | Routine selection or for cause |
| **Purpose** | Routine ongoing oversight — protocol compliance, data integrity, <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> accountability, safety | Independent compliance review — deeper than monitoring; QA process audits are not punitive | Formal regulatory review — inspectors have authority over all documents, facilities, and personnel |
| **Output** | Monitoring Letter; site response required | Audit report; may require <Tooltip tip="Actions taken to resolve a compliance issue and prevent recurrence: identify, correct, prevent." headline="Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#capa">CAPA</Tooltip> | Findings classified Critical / Major / Minor; CAPA required |
| **Authority** | Sponsor contract | Sponsor contract or institutional (QA) | Statutory (Food and Drug Act); obstruction is a criminal offence |

## Monitoring

Monitoring is the ongoing oversight conducted by the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator throughout the active study. The monitor — usually a Clinical Research Associate (CRA) — is the primary communication channel between the Sponsor and the site, and is the site's main resource for protocol questions and logistical issues.

### What the monitor verifies

During monitoring visits the monitor verifies that the study is being conducted per the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>-approved protocol, that the consent process is documented, that source data is accurate and consistent with the <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip>, that IP accountability is complete, and that safety events are being reported correctly and on time. **Source Data Verification (SDV)** is a core monitoring activity: the monitor compares CRF entries against source documents to confirm data integrity.

### Risk-based monitoring under ICH E6(R3)

Monitoring frequency is set out in the Monitoring Plan and adjusted based on site performance under ICH E6(R3)'s risk-based monitoring framework. Factors include study complexity, enrolment rate, staff turnover, number of protocol deviations, and <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> rates. A site performing well may receive less frequent visits; a site with ongoing issues will receive more. Under ICH E6(R3) — effective April 1, 2026 in Canada — a mix of centralized, remote, and targeted on-site visits tailored to the specific risks of the trial is the expected approach. The traditional model of regular on-site SDV is no longer the default.

### Facilitating remote and risk-based monitoring at your site

Under E6(R3), many monitoring activities now happen remotely. This changes what the site needs to provide and when.

**Remote monitoring logistics.** For remote visits, the monitor reviews documents electronically rather than on-site. This typically requires:

- Scanned or electronic copies of source documents, consent forms, and logs
- OACIS access authorization for the monitor — the same letter template as for on-site visits (see [SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-013))
- A secure method for sharing documents (sponsor-provided portal, encrypted file transfer, or direct system access)
- Scheduled availability of the CRC and <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI for questions during the remote review window

**Centralized statistical monitoring.** Sponsors increasingly use centralized monitoring — statistical analysis of data across all sites to detect anomalies such as unusual data patterns, outliers, or missing data clusters. Sites may receive targeted queries or triggered on-site visits based on centralized findings. These are not punitive — they are the expected output of the risk-based model.

**What to expect under risk-based monitoring:**

- **Visit frequency varies.** A site with few deviations, timely data entry, and a clean audit trail will see fewer on-site visits. A site with recurring issues will see more.
- **Monitoring scope may be targeted.** Instead of 100% SDV, the monitor may focus on high-risk data points — primary endpoint data, safety data, and consent documentation.
- **The monitoring plan is the reference.** Each study's Monitoring Plan documents what will be monitored, how, and at what frequency. Review it at the SIV and refer to it when preparing for visits.

### The Monitoring Letter

After each monitoring visit, the monitor sends a written Monitoring Letter summarizing findings, action items, and any deviations identified. **The QI/PI must initial and date the Monitoring Letter** — this documents medical oversight. Each action item must receive a written response. Both the letter and the response are essential <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> documents.

<Warning>
OACIS access for external monitors is not automatic. External monitors require a letter from the QI/PI requesting access for specific participant MRN numbers for the duration of the study. A template letter is in [SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-013). Arrange authorization before the first monitoring visit, not on the day of. Industry sponsors often assume monitors have standard OACIS access — they do not.
</Warning>

## Audits

An audit is a deeper, independent review of site compliance — distinct from the Sponsor's routine monitoring.

### Sponsor audits

A routine sponsor audit verifies that the study complies with applicable standards, that data quality is maintained, and that participant safety is protected. A **for-cause audit** is triggered by a specific concern — unusual <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">adverse event</Tooltip> rates, data quality issues, unexpectedly high enrolment, or suspected protocol non-compliance. For-cause audits tend to be more intensive than routine audits.

### RI-MUHC Quality Assurance audits

RI-MUHC Quality Assurance conducts its own process audits across MUHC and RI-MUHC sites as part of continuous process improvement. QA audits are not punitive — they identify where SOPs or practices need updating. QA may also conduct study-specific audits when there are specific concerns about a study. Contact QA at QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca.

## Health Canada inspections

A Health Canada inspection is a formal regulatory review conducted by the **Clinical Trial Compliance Program (CTCP)** of Health Canada's Regulatory Operations and Enforcement Branch. Inspectors have the authority to review any documents, facilities, or records related to the study — at the site, at the sponsor's offices, or at third-party locations.

### How inspections are triggered

- **Routine:** Typically arrive with approximately four weeks' notice. Selected based on Health Canada's risk-based criteria.
- **For-cause:** Triggered by suspected non-compliance, participant safety concerns, or data integrity issues. May arrive with no notice.
- **FDA-triggered:** A Health Canada inspection can be triggered by an FDA pre-approval inspection for a US-held IND or IDE.

<Warning>
Notify QA within 24 hours of receiving an inspection notice. The QI/PI must contact RI-MUHC Quality Assurance at QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca within 24 hours. QA will assist with preparation, review of inspection documentation, and CAPA response. Four weeks is not as much time as it seems.
</Warning>

<Warning>
Obstruction of inspectors is a criminal offence. Under the Food and Drug Act and Health Canada POL-0030 (November 2021), obstructing an inspector or knowingly providing false or misleading information is a violation subject to fines of \$500 to \$5,000,000 and/or 3 months to 2 years imprisonment. (These amounts reflect POL-0030 guidance; verify against current *Food and Drugs Act* provisions for authoritative figures.) Cooperation with inspectors is not optional.
</Warning>

### Inspection finding classifications

Health Canada classifies inspection findings into three categories. All findings require a formal CAPA response.

| Classification | Definition | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | A condition, practice, or process that adversely affects the rights, safety, or well-being of subjects; or produces or is likely to produce incorrect data; or constitutes a serious violation of applicable legislation. | Undisclosed conflicts of interest; failure to obtain informed consent; fabricated or falsified data; unreported serious adverse events |
| Major | A condition, practice, or process that may adversely affect the rights, safety, or well-being of subjects; or may produce incorrect data; or constitutes a violation of applicable legislation. | Incomplete ISF; unsigned consent forms; significant protocol deviations not reported to the REB; IP accountability gaps |
| Minor | A condition, practice, or process that is unlikely to adversely affect the rights, safety, or well-being of subjects, but that is not in conformance with <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> or applicable legislation. | Documentation inconsistencies; minor administrative gaps; procedural deviations without safety implications |

### When an inspection arrives

<Steps>
  <Step title="Receive inspection notice">
    Routine inspections: approximately 4 weeks' notice. For-cause: may be immediate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contact QA within 24 hours">
    QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca — QA becomes the inspection facilitator from this point.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Preparation phase">
    QA reviews documentation, organizes the ISF, and confirms roles. Follow [SOP-CR-017](/sops/cr-017) Appendix 1 checklist.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspection conduct">
    Inspectors review documents, facilities, and records. Cooperate fully. Do not volunteer information beyond what is asked.
  </Step>
  <Step title="CAPA response">
    All findings require a formal Corrective and Preventive Action response. QA assists with preparation and submission.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Being inspection-ready

An inspection tests whether the study has been conducted properly throughout its entire duration. The four weeks' notice is time to organize and review documentation that should already be complete — not time to catch up on documentation that was never done.

The practices that support good monitoring outcomes — complete ISF, contemporaneous documentation, signed and dated corrections, intact audit trail — are what make an inspection manageable. Inspection-readiness is not a state you achieve before an inspection; it is how a well-run study operates throughout its lifecycle.
