A protocol amendment is any change to the REB-approved study documents after initial approval. Amendments are routine — they happen in most studies, sometimes multiple times. What matters is that they follow the correct sequence: Health Canada authorization (where required) and <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approval before implementation at the site.

<Warning>
Implementing a protocol amendment before REB approval is a protocol deviation. For a change that would otherwise require prior approval, the only exception is a change made to eliminate an immediate safety risk to participants, and even then the REB must be told immediately and, for a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor must send Health Canada the amendment's information within 15 days. Purely administrative changes are handled separately — see [Notification-only changes](#notification-only-changes) below. See [SOP-CR-026](/sops/cr-026) §5.3.1 for institutional procedures.
</Warning>

## What triggers an amendment

Amendments arise from different sources throughout the study. Understanding the trigger helps determine the type of amendment and the urgency of the submission.

| Trigger | Examples | Typical urgency |
|---|---|---|
| **Safety signals** | New adverse event data, SUSAR requiring protocol change, updated <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">IB</Tooltip> with changed risk profile | High — may require immediate safety measures before formal amendment |
| **Enrollment challenges** | Eligibility criteria too restrictive, recruitment slower than projected | Moderate — plan ahead to avoid enrollment gaps |
| **Operational issues** | Visit window impractical, assessment tool unavailable, site logistics | Moderate — address before the issue generates repeated deviations |
| **Regulatory requirements** | Health Canada request, REB condition of approval | Variable — depends on the request |
| **Data or statistical considerations** | Interim analysis results, sample size re-estimation, endpoint change | Variable — often sponsor-driven |
| **Administrative changes** | Sponsor contact information, site address change, a change in administrative research staff | Low: can be submitted to the REB at the annual continuing review |
| **Change of principal investigator or sponsor** | A new principal investigator, a new sponsor | High: an amendment the REB must approve before the change takes effect; the contract is revised |

## Amendment types

### Changes requiring a CTA amendment (CTA-A)

For a drug trial, an amendment to the clinical trial application (CTA-A) must be filed for amendments to the protocol that affect the selection, monitoring or dismissal of participants, the evaluation of the drug's efficacy or its safety evaluation, that alter the risk to participants' health, or that extend the trial's duration. For a drug trial, Health Canada applies the same 30-day NOL mechanism used for original CTAs.

For a drug trial, [the sponsor may implement the amendment](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.05.008.html) if Health Canada does not object within 30 days of receiving the application. ### Notification-only changes

For Health Canada, changes that do not affect participant safety or risk, such as sponsor contact updates, minor formatting corrections and typographical fixes, need only a written notification within 15 calendar days after the change.

The REB's rule is separate. Only administrative changes that do not affect participants, such as a change of address or of administrative research staff, can wait for the annual continuing review. Any other change, even one Health Canada only needs to be told about, needs the REB's approval before it is implemented, unless it is needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants; report such a change to the REB immediately.

In Nagano, this category is submitted as a minor modification.{/* TO CONFIRM (the REB office): is an administrative change filed as a "minor modification" in Nagano? Only the Hub's Nagano guide says so. In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P4-12). */}

<Note>
Some sponsors and international frameworks use the terms "substantial" and "administrative" to classify amendments — this is primarily EU/EMA terminology. In the Canadian framework, the relevant distinction is whether a CTA-A is required (prior HC authorization) or whether notification to HC is sufficient.
</Note>

### IB updates

An updated Investigator's Brochure from the sponsor is not itself a protocol amendment, but it may trigger one. The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) count a new version of the IB as an amendment to the approved study, so [inform the REB of it before it is implemented](/sops/cr-026#5-3-1).{/* Source: SOP-CR-026, section 5.3.1 ("the Investigator must inform the REB about the following ongoing events prior to their implementation", among the "Amendments to an approved study": "Modifications to the IB or PM"). When a new IB is received, the QI/PI must review it and assess whether the new safety information affects the risk-benefit profile for enrolled participants. ## Regulatory sequencing

For Health Canada-regulated studies, both regulatory and ethics approvals are required before implementation. For a drug trial, Health Canada review (CTA-A) and REB review can proceed in parallel. For a natural health product trial, the application to Health Canada names the REB that approved the amended protocol, so the REB approves first. Both must be obtained before the amendment takes effect at the site.

  <Step title="Sponsor prepares the amendment package">

## Re-consent

Not every amendment requires participants to re-sign the consent form. When an amendment revises the consent form, the REB decides, on the researcher's recommendation, whether participants must be re-consented, and how and when they are given the new information.

Where re-consent is required, re-consent each affected participant on the REB-approved amended form at their earliest visit to the site after the REB's written approval and the sponsor's go letter, if there is one, unless the sponsor, the REB or the department, in its own SOP, sets otherwise.{/* Sources: the MUHC REB's SOP 405A.001-2, section 5.1.6 ("When an amendment involves revisions to the consent form, the REB will consider the recommendations of the Researcher in determining if, how and when the new information should be provided to the research participants and whether re-consent is required"); SOP-CR-008, section 5.7.4 ("a trial participant must be re-consented on any REB-approved amended ICF at their earliest visit to the clinical research site, unless there are specific recommendations/requirements from the Sponsor, REB and/or study/department internal SOP, if applicable. Re-consent the study participants affected after written REB approval of the amended ICF is obtained."). Until Nov. 15, 2026: "But any amendment that changes the information a reasonable person would consider important to their decision to continue participating does require re-consent.", and the lists below were headed "Re-consent is required when" and "is typically not required when" (facts-21, P4-20). The lists stay as the Hub's examples. */}

**Re-consent is usually needed when:**{/* TO CONFIRM (the REB office): are these the cases in which the REB usually requires, or does not require, re-consent? No source found gives the two lists (b111, row 41b). In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026. */}
- New risks or side effects are added
- Study procedures change in a way that affects participant burden
- The risk-benefit profile changes based on new safety data
- Eligibility criteria change and affect the basis on which a participant originally enrolled

**Re-consent is usually not needed when:**
- The change is administrative (sponsor contact info, minor formatting)
- The amendment narrows eligibility for new participants but does not affect already-enrolled participants
- The change is to data analysis methods that do not affect the participant experience

When re-consent is required, document the process for each participant: the date of re-consent, the ICF version signed, and confirmation that the participant was given time to consider the new information. Participants who decline to re-consent must be managed per the protocol's withdrawal procedures.{/* TO CONFIRM (QA): SOP-CR-008, section 5.7.4: "Document the re-consent process on an Informed Consent Checklist (Appendix 3)." Which of these details does the checklist ask for? In the master doc, Nov. 15, 2026 (facts-21, P4-21). */}

<Warning>
A participant who has not been re-consented on a current ICF version is participating on an outdated consent. If this is discovered during a monitoring visit, it will be flagged as a deviation. Track ICF versions and re-consent status for all active participants whenever a new version is approved.
</Warning>

## Multi-site coordination

For multi-centric studies, amendment implementation must be synchronized across sites to the extent possible. Practical considerations:

- **REB timelines vary.** In Quebec's public health network, one reviewing REB approves an amendment for all participating institutions; sites in other provinces have their own REBs and timelines. The sponsor may stagger implementation across sites.
- **Version alignment.** All sites should be using the same protocol version within a reasonable timeframe. Track which version each site is operating under.
- **Communication with the sponsor.** Confirm with the sponsor's monitor when the amendment is implemented at your site. The sponsor tracks implementation dates across all sites for regulatory reporting.

## Filing and documentation

All amendment-related documents must be filed in the ISF:

- The amended documents (tracked changes version and clean version)
- The REB approval letter specifying approved versions
- The Health Canada acknowledgment (if applicable)
- The Sponsor's Go Letter (if applicable)
- Training documentation confirming the team reviewed the changes
- Re-consent tracking records (if applicable)
- Superseded document versions (marked "SUPERSEDED," retained in the ISF — not discarded)
