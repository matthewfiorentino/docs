A protocol amendment is any change to the study documents after the <Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip> first approves them. Amendments are routine, and happen in most studies, sometimes several times. [Submit any change to an approved study](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) to the REB: a change to the protocol, the consent form, the <Tooltip tip="A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure (IB)</Tooltip> or product monograph, the participant materials, such as wallet cards, diary cards and recruitment materials, or who the researcher is.

Each amendment needs the REB's approval, and Health Canada's authorization where the trial needs it, before it is implemented at the site. While an amendment is under review, the study continues on the documents the REB has already approved.

<Warning>
Implementing a protocol amendment before REB approval is a protocol deviation. For a change that would otherwise need prior approval, the only exception is a change made to eliminate an immediate safety risk to participants. The REB must then be told immediately. Administrative changes are handled separately, under [Administrative changes](#administrative-changes).
</Warning>

## Know which changes need an amendment

Amendments arise from different sources during a study. The trigger helps you tell the type of amendment and how urgent its submission is.

| Trigger | Examples | Typical urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Safety signals | New adverse event data, a serious unexpected adverse reaction that requires a protocol change, an updated IB with a changed risk profile | High. Immediate safety measures may be needed before the formal amendment. |
| Enrolment challenges | Eligibility criteria too restrictive, recruitment slower than projected | Moderate. Plan ahead to avoid gaps in enrolment. |
| Operational issues | A visit window that is impractical, an assessment tool that is unavailable, site logistics | Moderate. Address the issue before it causes repeated deviations. |
| Regulatory requirements | A request from Health Canada, a condition of the REB's approval | Variable, depending on the request. |
| Data or statistical considerations | Interim analysis results, a re-estimated sample size, a changed endpoint | Variable. Often driven by the sponsor. |
| Administrative changes | A change in sponsor contact information, a change of site address, a change in administrative research staff | Low. If the change has no impact on participants, it can be submitted to the REB at the annual continuing review. |
| Change of principal investigator or sponsor | A new principal investigator, a new sponsor | High. The REB must approve the amendment before the change takes effect. The contract is revised. |

If the amendment results from a sponsor's safety notice or action letter, [append that document to the amendment request](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops).

## Submit the amendment to the REB

In an industry-sponsored study, the sponsor prepares the amended documents and distributes them to all sites. The sponsor asks the investigators to sign and date the amendments and to obtain the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca)'s approval in a timely fashion, within 30 days of receiving them. In a sponsor-investigator study, the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, prepares the amendment.

Submit the amendment for the REB's review through <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>. Include the amended documents with tracked changes and a summary of changes, in the REB submission and in any submission to Health Canada. The REB reviews the amendment and approves it, requests modifications or rejects it. If the REB requests revisions, [forward them to the sponsor or sponsor-investigator for review](/sops/cr-026#5-3-1), where that applies.

Before a revised consent form goes to the REB, update its version number or date on each page and in the file name, and send it to the sponsor for review where that applies.

Give every other revised document a new version number and date in its footer, and update its file name with the version number and date. The REB's approval form records each document by its electronic file name. Newly translated study documents, such as a translated consent form, also go to the REB before they are used.

If the amendment affects an MUHC feasibility review (MUHC resources such as nursing, pharmacy or laboratories, contracts and budgets, or access to health information), indicate it in the Nagano amendment form. For some reviews, Nagano then launches a new feasibility evaluation, and the amendment needs both the REB's approval and that feasibility approval before you implement it.

[Submit revised documents that concern a department of the MUHC or the RI-MUHC](/sops/cr-026#5-2-2), such as a revised contract, laboratory manual or pharmacy manual, to that department as well. An addendum to a pharmacy or laboratory agreement should be submitted to the department concerned for its approval.

### Administrative changes

The REB's rule for administrative changes is its own. Only administrative changes that do not affect participants, such as a change of address or of administrative research staff, can wait for the annual continuing review. Any other change, even one that Health Canada only needs to be told about, needs the REB's approval before it is implemented, unless it is needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants. Report such a change to the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) immediately.

In Nagano, an administrative change is submitted as a minor modification.

## Wait for every approval before you implement the amendment

Do not implement the amendment until you have the REB's written approval letter, which names the approved versions and their dates. Where the trial needs one, Health Canada must also have authorized the amendment: the rules for [drug trials](#get-health-canada-authorization-for-a-drug-trial-amendment), [natural health product trials](#get-health-canada-authorization-for-a-natural-health-product-trial-amendment) and [device trials](#get-a-revised-health-canada-authorization-for-a-device-trial) are below. If the amendment triggered a new MUHC feasibility evaluation, wait for the feasibility approval as well.

A change needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants may be made before the REB's approval, and must be reported to the REB immediately. In a sponsor-investigator study, the sponsor-investigator should also be notified as soon as possible afterwards. In a drug trial, the change and the proposed protocol amendment [should also be submitted promptly to the sponsor for its agreement](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100/application-authorization.html).

In an industry-sponsored study, the sponsor issues a go letter, its written permission to implement the amendment at the site, confirming that both approvals are in place. No regulation requires a go letter, but [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) do not let you implement a change](/sops/cr-026#5-3-1) until it arrives, where the sponsor issues one.

## Implement the amendment at the site

Once every approval is in hand:

1. Update all documents to the approved version, and begin using the new documents.
2. Implement the amendment at each participant's next visit.
3. Remove superseded versions from circulation.
4. Train the team on the changes, and document the training.
5. Update the investigator site file (ISF).

## Re-consent participants on an amended consent form

Not every amendment requires participants to re-sign the consent form. When the amendment includes a change to the consent form, [indicate in it your recommendation](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) on whether the new information should be given to current participants, past participants or both. The REB considers that recommendation when it decides whether participants must be re-consented, and how and when they are given the new information.

[Communicate any important new information that may be relevant to participants' consent](/sops/cr-008#5-7-2) to them in a timely manner. The communication of new information should be documented.

The REB also requires researchers to inform participants of any new information on the research's potential impacts on their long-term health, even if their participation has already ended. Inform former participants by phone, by mail or in person, if applicable. In a clinical trial, when new information is relevant to participants' welfare, [researchers must promptly inform every participant it applies to](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html), former participants included.

Once the REB has approved an amended consent form in writing, and the sponsor's go letter has arrived if there is one, [re-consent each affected participant on it](/sops/cr-008#5-7-4) at their earliest visit to the site. The sponsor, the REB or a study or department SOP may recommend or require something different.

**Re-consent is usually needed when:**
- New risks or side effects are added.
- Study procedures change in a way that affects participant burden.
- The risk-benefit profile changes based on new safety data.
- Eligibility criteria change and affect the basis on which a participant originally enrolled.

**The sponsor, the REB or a study or department SOP may decide that re-consent is not needed, for example when:**
- The change is administrative, such as sponsor contact information or minor formatting.
- The amendment narrows eligibility for new participants but does not affect participants already enrolled.
- The change is to data analysis methods that do not affect the participant's experience.

Document the re-consent process on an Informed Consent Checklist. When re-consent is required, document the process for each participant: the date of re-consent, the consent form version signed, and confirmation that the participant was given time to consider the new information. Give each participant a copy of the signed amended consent form and any revised participant materials the REB approved. File the original signed form, the Informed Consent Checklist and any revised participant materials with the study's source documents.

Follow the protocol's withdrawal procedures for participants who decline to re-consent.

The RI-MUHC's SOPs [recommend a system that controls re-consent](/sops/cr-008#5-7-3): one that tracks every version of the consent form, every REB approval and which participants have been re-consented.

<Warning>
A participant who must be re-consented on an amended consent form, and has not been, is taking part on an outdated consent form. If a monitoring visit finds it, it is flagged as a deviation.
</Warning>

## Review a new version of the IB

An updated IB from the sponsor is not itself a protocol amendment, but it may trigger one. The RI-MUHC's SOPs count a new version of the IB, or of the product monograph, as an amendment to the approved study, so [inform the REB of it before it is implemented](/sops/cr-026#5-3-1).

When a new IB is received, the principal investigator must review it and assess whether the new safety information affects the risk-benefit profile for enrolled participants. If it does, a protocol amendment, a [revision of the consent form](/sops/cr-006#5-4-1), or both may be needed.

Filing an updated IB without reviewing it for new safety signals is a compliance gap, and a common Health Canada inspection finding. Document the principal investigator's review and assessment in the ISF, even if no action is required.

## Coordinate an amendment across sites

In a study at several sites, implementation of an amendment must be synchronized across sites to the extent possible. Timelines differ from site to site. In Quebec's public health network, one reviewing REB approves an amendment for all participating institutions. Sites in other provinces have their own REBs and timelines. The sponsor may stagger implementation across sites.

All sites should be using the same protocol version within a reasonable timeframe. Track which version each site is operating under. Confirm with the sponsor's monitor when the amendment is implemented at your site. The sponsor tracks implementation dates across all sites for regulatory reporting.

In a study in Quebec's public health network, the investigator who submits an amendment to the reviewing REB [must forward the approved amended documents](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) to the mandated person in each public institution where the research takes place. Send a copy of the original document with the approved changes highlighted as well.

In some studies in Quebec's public health network, the MUHC is a participating site and another institution's REB is the reviewing REB. For each amendment the reviewing REB approves, the [Personne mandatée](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca) creates an F1-MEO form in Nagano. [Complete and submit the F1-MEO form](/kb/design/multi-centric#practical-implications) for the Personne mandatée's evaluation. If the amendment affects one of the MUHC's feasibility reviews, wait for that review's approval before you implement the amendment at the MUHC.

## Get Health Canada authorization for a drug trial amendment

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

For a drug trial, an amendment to the <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">clinical trial application</Tooltip> (CTA-A) must be filed for:
- Amendments to the protocol that affect the selection, monitoring or dismissal of participants.
- Amendments to the protocol that affect the evaluation of the drug's efficacy or its safety evaluation.
- Amendments to the protocol that alter the risk to participants' health.
- Amendments to the protocol that extend the trial's duration.
- Changes to the chemistry and manufacturing information that may affect the drug's safety or quality.

[Health Canada reads an amendment that extends the trial's duration](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/clinical-trials/clinical-trial-sponsors-applications.html#a241) as one that extends the treatment period of individual participants. A CTA-A that extends the treatment period must be accompanied by an Investigator's Brochure or equivalent information that supports the extension. A change in the projected duration of the whole trial that does not change participants' duration of treatment is normally not considered to require a CTA-A.

CTA-A amendments include changes to eligibility criteria, dosing, study endpoints, addition of new procedures, or changes to the investigational product's chemistry and manufacturing information that may affect its safety or quality.

[The sponsor may implement the amendment](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.05.008.html) if Health Canada does not object within 30 days of receiving the application. When Health Canada finds the application acceptable, it issues a No Objection Letter (NOL) within that period, as it does for an original Clinical Trial Application (CTA).

Before the amendment is implemented at a site, the sponsor must also send Health Canada the name, address and telephone number of the REB that approved the amended protocol for that site. The sponsor also gives Health Canada the name of any REB that has refused to approve an amendment to the protocol, with its reasons and the date.

Health Canada's review of the CTA-A and the REB's review can proceed in parallel. The REB's approval and Health Canada's authorization must both be obtained before the amendment takes effect at the site.

If the trial or the use of the drug endangers the health of a participant or another person, the sponsor may make the amendment immediately. The sponsor must then send Health Canada the application's information within 15 days of the amendment.

Some changes need only a written notification to Health Canada within 15 calendar days after the change. They are changes to the protocol that do not alter the risk to participants, and changes to the chemistry and manufacturing information that do not affect the drug's quality or safety. Examples are sponsor contact updates, minor formatting corrections and typographical fixes. The REB's own rule for such changes is under [Administrative changes](#administrative-changes).

Some sponsors and international frameworks classify amendments as "substantial" or "administrative", which is mainly the terminology of the European Union's regulators. For a drug trial in Canada, the distinction that counts is whether the change needs a CTA-A, with Health Canada's authorization before the change is made, or a notification only.

## Get Health Canada authorization for a natural health product trial amendment

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

For a natural health product trial, [the sponsor must not make certain amendments unless Health Canada has amended the authorization](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2003-196/section-71.html):
- Amendments to the protocol that affect the selection, monitoring or dismissal of participants.
- Amendments to the protocol that affect the evaluation of the product's efficacy or its safety evaluation.
- Amendments to the protocol that alter the risk to participants' health.
- Amendments to the protocol that extend the trial's duration.
- Amendments to the chemistry and manufacturing information of a synthetically manufactured medicinal ingredient that may affect the product's safety or quality.

Health Canada amends the authorization by sending the sponsor a notice of the amendment. The application to Health Canada names the REB that approved the amended protocol at each site, so the REB approves the amended protocol before Health Canada amends the authorization. The application also names any REB that has refused to approve an amendment to the protocol, with its reasons and the date.

If the trial or the use of the product endangers the health of a participant or another person, the sponsor may make the amendment immediately. The sponsor must then send Health Canada the application's information within 15 days of the amendment.

Some changes need only a notification to Health Canada within 15 days after the change. They are changes to the protocol that do not alter the risk to participants, and changes to that chemistry and manufacturing information that do not affect the product's quality or safety.

## Get a revised Health Canada authorization for a device trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

For a study of a Class II to IV medical device, [any protocol amendment that requires REB approval also requires Health Canada's authorization](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/investigational-testing-authorizations-guidance/guidance-document.html#2.5). The manufacturer or importer requests a revised authorization from Health Canada.

Other significant changes to the device or the investigation, which don't need a revised authorization under that guidance, should also be reported to Health Canada. Health Canada acknowledges them in writing, and may still require a revised authorization.

## File the amendment documents

[File all amendment-related documents in the ISF](/kb/site-activation/isf):
- The amended documents, in their tracked-changes and clean versions.
- The REB's approval letter, which names the approved versions.
- The REB submission, and every communication with the REB about the amendment.
- For an amended consent form, the communications about it with the sponsor or sponsor-investigator and with regulatory authorities as well.
- For a trial that Health Canada regulates, its authorization of the amendment (the NOL, the notice of authorization or the investigational testing authorization) or the record of a notification, where one applies.
- The sponsor's go letter, if there is one.
- The records of the team's training on the changes.
- The re-consent tracking records, if re-consent was required.
- The superseded versions, marked "SUPERSEDED" and kept in the ISF.
