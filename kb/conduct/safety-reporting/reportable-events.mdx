During a study, some events must be reported to the sponsor and the MUHC Research Ethics Board (<Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>), and some to Health Canada. Each report has its own deadline. Missing one of the study's reporting deadlines is itself a deviation. Repeated or deliberate failure to submit required reports to the REB may lead the REB to suspend or terminate the study, or to suspend its recruitment.

## Participant safety and ethical concerns

Report these to [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) even if no protocol deviation has occurred:
- Concerns about participant safety, informed consent or undue risk.
- New information that may adversely affect participants' safety or the conduct of the research.
- A change in the research's risks or potential benefits, such as an interim analysis showing a different rate of response to treatment than expected, a side effect that is more severe or more frequent than expected, or published research showing that an arm of the research has no therapeutic value.
- A change in Health Canada's or the US Food and Drug Administration's (FDA) safety labelling or approval status for a drug, device, natural health product, genetic therapy or biologic used in the study, or its withdrawal from the market.
- An unanticipated problem or other event that could significantly affect the conduct of the research at the site, such as a concern of non-compliance or an institutional feasibility issue.
- A summary of the findings relevant to participants' safety and well-being after a Health Canada inspection, an FDA or other regulatory audit, a sponsor's audit, an internal Quality Assurance audit or another audit at the site.
- A concern a participant raises about their rights as a research participant or about ethical issues related to the research.

Report each one by the REB's deadline in [Key reporting timelines](#key-reporting-timelines). Do not wait for the annual continuing review.

If you make a change to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants, [notify the REB immediately](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops). Report a privacy breach to the REB as soon as you become aware of it.

## Assess and report adverse events

In an interventional study, every adverse event (<Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">AE</Tooltip>) must be assessed and recorded on the [AE Assessment Log](/sops/cr-012#5-1-4) and in the source documents. Record AEs from the day the participant signs the consent form until the end of the study, as the REB-approved protocol defines it.

The principal investigator leads the study at the MUHC. The principal investigator is responsible for assessing every AE and for making sure that the site's safety reporting meets the applicable regulations, good clinical practice (<Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>), and the sponsor's, the REB's and local requirements.

Parts of assessing and reporting AEs may be delegated, and the principal investigator remains ultimately responsible for all of it. Unless [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-012#3-0-responsibilities) say otherwise, any part may go to trained members of the team. [Physician investigators are delegated the assessment of AEs, serious or not](/sops/cr-012#5-1-3). Coordinators and nurses are delegated the collection and reporting of AEs.

An AE becomes a serious adverse event (<Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip>) if it meets any one of these [seriousness criteria](/sops/cr-012#5-2-6):
- Hospitalization, or prolonged hospitalization.
- A congenital malformation.
- Persistent or significant disability or incapacity.
- Death, or a life-threatening event.
- A medically important event, as defined in the REB-approved protocol.

Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.

The sponsor's and the REB's deadlines run from the date the principal investigator learns of the SAE. [Record the date](/sops/cr-012#5-2-9) in the source documents and on the SAE Reporting Log. Report the SAE to the sponsor on the SAE form that the sponsor provides or the protocol specifies, even if the form is only partly completed. If the sponsor provides no form, use the RI-MUHC's [SAE Report Form template](/sops/cr-012#appendix-4).

At a site under the MUHC REB's approval, an SAE also needs an expedited report to [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) if the principal investigator judges that it meets all three of these criteria:
- It is unexpected.
- It is related or possibly related to taking part in the research.
- It suggests that the research puts participants or others at greater risk of harm than was known when the REB approved the study.

[SAEs that don't meet these criteria should be submitted with the REB's annual continuing review](/sops/cr-012#5-5-7).

Report an SAE that meets all three in [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview) on form F3H, by [the REB's deadline](#key-reporting-timelines). Update the REB as soon as more information is available, and attach the follow-up SAE reports.

Information stripped of a participant's direct identifiers can sometimes still identify them, for example when data sets are linked. The REB counts any accidental or intentional re-identification of a participant as an SAE. Report a re-identification to the REB whether it happened at the MUHC or elsewhere. Ask [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) which Nagano form and deadline to use for it.

In a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor reports to Health Canada every SAE that meets Health Canada's criteria. For a drug trial, [Health Canada's criteria](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.05.014.html) cover every serious unexpected adverse drug reaction, wherever it occurs. In a natural health product trial, [the criteria](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2003-196/section-78.html) cover every serious adverse reaction that occurs in Canada, expected or not.

If you are the sponsor-investigator, report an SAE that meets Health Canada's criteria to Health Canada yourself, on a Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences (CIOMS) I form. Report it whether or not it meets the REB's criteria.

### Review and report external SUSARs

The sponsor sends each investigator reports of suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions (<Tooltip tip="A serious adverse reaction to a drug or natural health product whose nature, severity or frequency the Investigator's Brochure or product label does not anticipate; the sponsor reports it to Health Canada, and the site reports it to the REB when it meets the REB's criteria." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#susar">SUSARs</Tooltip>) from sites outside the MUHC REB's approval, called external SUSARs. Each one is reviewed by a physician investigator, either the principal investigator or a sub-investigator. The principal investigator documents their [medical oversight](/sops/cr-012#5-7-2) of each external SUSAR by initialling and dating it.

The principal investigator must make sure that the site has [a written process](/sops/cr-026#5-3-2) for how investigators review documents that need medical oversight, such as SUSARs, and how those documents reach the team members who need them.

Report an external SUSAR to [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) in [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview) on form F3H only if the principal investigator judges that both of these are true:
- It meets the REB's three criteria for an SAE.
- It requires a change to the research (an amendment to the protocol, the <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip> or the <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">informed consent form</Tooltip>) or requires that participants be told immediately for their safety.

Report each one by the REB's deadline in [Key reporting timelines](#key-reporting-timelines). File every external SUSAR in the investigator site file (<Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip>), whether or not it goes to the REB, with any REB correspondence about it. The RI-MUHC's [External SUSAR Reporting Log](/sops/cr-012#appendix-3) is the template for tracking external SUSARs.

### Track a pregnancy during a trial

If a participant in an interventional study becomes pregnant, [collect and follow the prospective and retrospective exposure in utero reports](/sops/cr-012#5-4-5) to learn the outcome, even without an adverse event. The type and duration of follow-up should generally be set in the protocol or a document it refers to.

## Key reporting timelines

Deadlines are in calendar days. [The REB's deadlines](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) run from when the researcher becomes aware of the event. A detailed report to the REB must contain an analysis of the event, its consequences and the corrective measures taken. Remove all participant identifiers from every report to the REB, and use the participant's study number only.

Otherwise, it should be submitted with the annual continuing review. | In a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor reports an SAE that meets [Health Canada's criteria](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.05.014.html). Within 7 days after the sponsor becomes aware of it if it is fatal or life-threatening, then a complete report within 8 days after that. | Minor deviation of one of the [four kinds the REB lists](#protocol-deviations) | As soon as possible | **7 days** if it involves a death or a life-threatening reaction, then a detailed report within 8 days of the first report. Otherwise **15 days**. | Not applicable |
| Other minor deviation | As soon as possible | With the annual continuing review | Not applicable |

## Hospital reporting of serious drug reactions and device incidents

The hospital has its own duty to report to Health Canada, separate from the trial's reporting. The Protecting Canadians from Unsafe Drugs Act (<Tooltip tip="Federal law (2014) under which hospitals report serious adverse drug reactions and medical device incidents to Health Canada. Not covered: natural health products, and reactions or incidents that involve only a drug or device authorized for a clinical trial, investigational testing or special access, or a routine-program vaccine." headline="Vanessa's Law" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#vanessas-law">Vanessa's Law</Tooltip>) requires hospitals to [report serious adverse drug reactions (SADRs) and medical device incidents to Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medeffect-canada/adverse-reaction-reporting/mandatory-hospital-reporting/drugs-devices/guidance.html).

Each report is due within 30 days after the reaction or incident is first documented in the hospital. The duty covers every SADR and medical device incident that the hospital documents, wherever it started and whether or not the patient is admitted.

The Food and Drug Regulations and the Medical Devices Regulations exempt the following, which already have their own reporting schemes:
- A reaction that relates only to a drug authorized for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations.
- A reaction that relates only to a drug sold through the Special Access Program.
- A reaction that relates only to a vaccine given under a provincial routine immunization program.
- An incident that involves only a device under an investigational testing or special access authorization.

The Food and Drugs Act also excludes reactions to natural health products from the hospital's duty.

<Warning>
  The hospital must report a reaction that involves a suspected drug the exemptions do not cover, such as a marketed drug outside the trial's authorization, even if the trial's own drug is also suspected.
</Warning>

### What counts as a serious drug reaction or a device incident

A serious adverse drug reaction is a noxious and unintended response to a drug at any dose that has one of these outcomes:
- In-patient hospitalization, or prolonged hospitalization.
- A congenital malformation.
- Persistent or significant disability or incapacity.
- Death, or a life-threatening event.

For the hospital's report, and for the qualified investigator's report during investigational testing, a medical device incident meets both of these conditions:
- It is related to a failure of a device, a deterioration in its effectiveness or an inadequacy in its labelling or directions for use.
- It has led to death or a serious deterioration in health, or could do so if it recurred.

### What the study team does

When a trial participant has a suspected SADR, report it to the principal investigator and the sponsor as you would any SAE. When the hospital must also report the reaction, any member of the care team can complete and finalize OACIS Form 8888 in O-Word for the hospital's report. The MUHC's [Vanessa's Law Task Force](https://muhc.ca/clinical-documents/vanessa) reviews finalized forms every two weeks and sends them to Health Canada by the hospital's deadline.

A reaction or an incident in a study still needs the hospital's report unless one of the exemptions covers it. For example, the hospital must report an incident with a licensed device that a study uses without an investigational testing authorization.

For a medical device incident, Health Canada accepts online reports through [MedEffect Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medeffect-canada.html). Ask [Quality Assurance (QA)](mailto:QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) how to report one, or which reports apply in a specific situation.

## Protocol deviations

A deviation is any departure from one of these:
- The current REB-approved study documents.
- The RI-MUHC's SOPs.
- The ICH's GCP guideline.
- The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) standard 14155, where it applies.

Every protocol deviation [must be assessed, addressed and documented](/sops/cr-026#5-2-3), whether it is minor, major or critical. Every deviation should be recorded on the [Protocol/GCP/SOP Deviation Log](/sops/cr-026#appendix-1), an essential document in the ISF. Each one should be assessed to identify trends, with corrective and preventive action on processes where it applies. The sponsor's monitors may check the log at monitoring visits.

Report every deviation to the sponsor, whatever its tier. Major and critical deviations also go to [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) by [its deadline](#key-reporting-timelines). Whatever their tier, report these deviations to the REB too:
- A sponsor-approved waiver of an eligibility criterion.
- A change to the approved consent process, such as an improper translation or not using the current consent form.
- A deviation that leads to an SAE.
- Any other deviation that the principal investigator judges jeopardizes participants' safety, the efficacy of the research or data integrity, or could otherwise affect participants' rights, safety or well-being.

If a protocol deviation brings new information that may affect participants' willingness to continue in the study, all study participants must be informed. The REB may require them to renew their consent in writing.

### Classify a deviation as minor, major or critical

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-026#5-3-3) define three tiers of deviation:

| Tier | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | A deviation that shows a deficiency or a departure from the protocol, good clinical practice or the regulations, without being major or critical. | A visit window missed by one day. A minor transcription error. A non-critical document filed late. |
| Major | A marked departure or deficiency that may result in undue health risks to participants or others or could invalidate data, without being critical. | A participant enrolled outside the eligibility criteria. Consent obtained with an outdated consent form. A required safety assessment missed. A dosing error outside tolerances. |
| Critical | A departure that results in fatal, life-threatening or unsafe conditions for participants, or that involves an unauthorized trial or the adulteration, misrepresentation or falsification of records. | Unapproved <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip> given. A safety event concealed from the REB. Data fabricated or altered to conceal a violation. |

The principal investigator [classifies each deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management#classification-in-practice), with input from the <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">clinical research coordinator</Tooltip>. The tier is recorded on the Deviation Log when the deviation is identified. The ICH also [asks each sponsor to set trial-specific criteria](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) for classifying protocol deviations as important. Health Canada's regulations don't specify deviation tiers.

Major and critical deviations should have a [CAPA](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management#working-through-a-capa) in place, recorded on the [CAPA form](/sops/cr-026#appendix-2). The CAPA form should also be sent to [QA](mailto:QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) for trend tracking and process improvement. A critical deviation may lead the REB to suspend or terminate the study, or to suspend its recruitment.
