During a study, some events must be reported to the sponsor and the MUHC Research Ethics Board (<Tooltip tip="A body, not affiliated with the sponsor, whose main mandate is to approve the start of biomedical research involving human participants and to review it periodically, to protect the participants' rights, safety and well-being." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>), and some to Health Canada. Each report has its own deadline. Missing one of the study's reporting deadlines is itself a deviation. Repeated or deliberate failure to submit required reports to the REB may lead the REB to suspend or terminate the study, or to suspend its recruitment.

## Report participant safety and ethical concerns

Report these to [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) even if no protocol deviation has occurred:
- Concerns about participant safety, informed consent or undue risk.
- New information that may adversely affect participants' safety or the conduct of the research.
- A change in the research's risks or potential benefits, such as an interim analysis showing a different rate of response to treatment than expected, a side effect that is more severe or more frequent than expected, or published research showing that an arm of the research has no therapeutic value.
- A change in Health Canada's or the US Food and Drug Administration's (FDA) safety labelling or approval status for a drug, device, natural health product, genetic therapy or biologic used in the study, or its withdrawal from the market.
- An unanticipated problem or other event that could significantly affect the conduct of the research at the site, such as a concern of non-compliance or an institutional feasibility issue.
- A summary of the findings relevant to participants' safety and well-being after a Health Canada inspection, an FDA or other regulatory audit, a sponsor's audit, an internal Quality Assurance audit or another audit at the site.
- A concern a participant raises about their rights as a research participant or about ethical issues related to the research.

Report each one by the REB's deadline in [Key reporting timelines](#key-reporting-timelines). Do not wait for the annual continuing review.

In a clinical trial, if you report a newly discovered risk or an unanticipated issue, [include in the report a plan to eliminate or mitigate any increased risks to participants](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html#8). In a clinical trial, researchers should raise potentially relevant developments with the REB at an early stage, to better determine the appropriate scope and timing of information sharing with participants and regulatory authorities.

If you make a change to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants, [notify the REB immediately](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops). [Report a privacy breach](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person) to the REB as soon as you become aware of it. As applicable, also report it to the appropriate organizational officials as soon as you become aware of it.

## Assess and report adverse events

In an interventional study, every adverse event (<Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant who has been given an investigational product, whether or not it is related to that product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">AE</Tooltip>) must be assessed and recorded on the [AE Assessment Log](/sops/cr-012#5-1-4) and in the source documents. Record AEs from the day the participant signs the consent form until the end of the study, as the REB-approved protocol defines it.

The principal investigator leads the study at the MUHC. The principal investigator is responsible for assessing every AE and for making sure that the site's safety reporting meets the applicable regulations, good clinical practice (<Tooltip tip="A standard for the design, conduct, performance, monitoring, auditing, recording, analysis and reporting of clinical studies that provides assurance that the data and reported results are credible and accurate, and that the rights, integrity and confidentiality of participants are protected." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>), and the sponsor's, the REB's and local requirements.

Parts of assessing and reporting AEs may be delegated, and the principal investigator remains ultimately responsible for all of it. Unless [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-012#3-0-responsibilities) say otherwise, any part may go to trained members of the team.

[Physician investigators are delegated the assessment of AEs, serious or not](/sops/cr-012#5-1-3). [Every investigator who will assess AEs, serious adverse events or suspected unexpected serious adverse reactions must hold Human Research Privileges](/sops/cr-012#5-1-1). Coordinators and nurses are delegated the collection and reporting of AEs.

When a participant is a minor or an adult who can't consent, the principal investigator or a delegated member of the study team [should collect all data related to an AE](/sops/cr-012#5-1-5) from the participant's <Tooltip tip="The person with legal authority to consent for a participant who cannot consent." headline="Legally Appointed Representative (LAR)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#lar">legally appointed representative</Tooltip>, whether the AE is serious or not.

Every discussion of an AE with a participant [must be documented](/sops/cr-012#5-1-4), whether it takes place at a visit, by phone, virtually or by email. The record of the discussion and the updated AE Assessment Log should be kept in the investigator site file.

Delegated team members [should discuss with the principal investigator every AE](/sops/cr-012#5-1-6) that a participant or their legally appointed representative reports. The team member should obtain the principal investigator's assessment on the AE Assessment Log on the same day or, at the latest, 5 days after, and document the discussion in the investigator site file.

Abnormal laboratory or test results [should be reported to the principal investigator](/sops/cr-012#5-1-8) for assessment on the same day or, at the latest, 5 days afterwards. The principal investigator should review the test results, indicate any abnormality as clinically significant or not clinically significant, and initial and date the results as soon as possible.

If the principal investigator reviews test results online and initials and dates them later, the process should be documented, for example in a working instruction, and a copy placed in the investigator site file.

The coordinator who completed the AE Assessment Log and the principal investigator, who provides medical oversight, [must initial and date the log](/sops/cr-012#5-2-8).

An AE becomes a serious adverse event (<Tooltip tip="An adverse event that meets any one of these seriousness criteria: hospitalization or prolonged hospitalization, a congenital malformation, persistent or significant disability or incapacity, a life-threatening event or death, or a medically important event as defined in the REB-approved protocol." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip>) if it meets any one of these [seriousness criteria](/sops/cr-012#5-2-6):
- Hospitalization, or prolonged hospitalization.
- A congenital malformation.
- Persistent or significant disability or incapacity.
- Death, or a life-threatening event.
- A medically important event, as defined in the REB-approved protocol.

<Warning>
Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.
</Warning>

The sponsor's and the REB's deadlines run from the date the principal investigator learns of the SAE. [Record the date](/sops/cr-012#5-2-9) in the source documents and on the SAE Reporting Log. Report the SAE to the sponsor on the SAE form that the sponsor provides or the protocol specifies, even if the form is only partly completed. If the sponsor provides no form, use the RI-MUHC's [SAE Report Form template](/sops/cr-012#appendix-4).

At a site under the MUHC REB's approval, an SAE also needs an expedited report to [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) if the principal investigator judges that it meets all three of these criteria:
- It is unexpected.
- It is related or possibly related to taking part in the research.
- It suggests that the research puts participants or others at greater risk of harm than was known when the REB approved the study.

[SAEs that don't meet these criteria should be submitted with the REB's annual continuing review](/sops/cr-012#5-5-7).

Report an SAE that meets all three in [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview) on form F3H, by [the REB's deadline](#key-reporting-timelines). [The REB requires the report to include](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) all of the following:
- A description of the SAE.
- Previous safety reports on similar events, if available.
- An analysis of the significance of the SAE.
- If applicable, the proposed changes to the conduct of the research or to the consent form, or a list of corrective actions to be taken in response to the SAE.
- A copy of the sponsor's report, if available.

Upload to Nagano any applicable form that the sponsor completed or requires, such as an SAE form. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs also require the F3H form to include](/sops/cr-012#5-5-5):
- Any proposed changes to the protocol or the <Tooltip tip="A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip>, and any other corrective actions the sponsor is to take in response to the SAE.
- A copy of the SAE report sent to the sponsor.
- Information from the meeting of the <Tooltip tip="An independent committee that the sponsor may establish to assess, at intervals, the progress of a clinical study, its safety data and its critical efficacy endpoints, and to recommend to the sponsor whether to continue, modify or stop the study." headline="Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#dsmb">Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)</Tooltip>, if applicable.

Update the REB as soon as more information is available, and attach the follow-up SAE reports.

<Note>
Information stripped of a participant's direct identifiers can sometimes still identify them, for example when data sets are linked. The REB counts any accidental or intentional re-identification of a participant as an SAE. Report a re-identification to the REB whether it happened at the MUHC or elsewhere.
</Note>

Ask [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) which Nagano form and deadline to use for a re-identification.

In a post-marketing (Phase IV) study or another study of a marketed product, report adverse reactions to the sponsor or the product's market authorization holder, so that the sponsor or the holder can report them to Health Canada.

### Report an SAE at another site to the REB

In any study the MUHC REB reviews, some SAEs at sites outside its approval also need a report to [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information). Report one in [Nagano](/apps/nagano/overview) if the principal investigator judges that it meets all four of these criteria:
- It is unexpected.
- It is related or possibly related to taking part in the research.
- It suggests that the research puts participants or others at greater risk of harm than was known when the REB approved the study.
- It requires a change to the research or the <Tooltip tip="The written form that gives a participant the information essential to an informed decision about taking part in a study." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">informed consent form</Tooltip>, or requires that participants be told immediately for their safety.

Report it by [the REB's deadline](#key-reporting-timelines). [The REB requires the report to include](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) the same information as a report on an SAE at a site under its approval, listed under [Assess and report adverse events](#assess-and-report-adverse-events).

### Know who reports an SAE to Health Canada

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

In a drug or natural health product trial [that needs Health Canada's authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization), the sponsor reports to Health Canada every SAE that meets Health Canada's criteria. For a drug trial, [Health Canada's criteria](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.05.014.html) cover every serious unexpected adverse drug reaction, wherever it occurs. In a natural health product trial, [the criteria](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2003-196/section-78.html) cover every serious adverse reaction that occurs in Canada, expected or not.

If you are the sponsor-investigator of a drug or natural health product trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, report an SAE that meets Health Canada's criteria to Health Canada yourself. Report it whether or not it meets the REB's criteria.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs have the sponsor-investigator prepare a Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences (CIOMS) I form](/sops/cr-012#5-6-4) for submission to national health authorities, as appropriate. In a blinded drug or natural health product trial, the RI-MUHC's SOPs also have the sponsor-investigator unblind the reports of unexpected and related SAEs for expedited reporting, especially reports submitted in Europe.

In most blinded studies, the RI-MUHC's SOPs recommend that the sponsor-investigator [break the blind for the one participant](/sops/cr-012#5-6-7) whose serious adverse drug reaction needs an expedited report. Where serious or fatal outcomes are primary endpoints of the study, unblinding could compromise the study's integrity. In that case, an agreement with the health authorities before unblinding may be advantageous.

In a natural health product trial, adverse reaction reports [should be submitted on Health Canada's Adverse Reaction Report Form for Clinical Trials, or a similar form containing the same information](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/clinical-trials.html#a7.3).

### Review and report external SUSARs

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span></div>

## Hospital reporting of serious drug reactions and device incidents

The hospital has its own duty to report to Health Canada, separate from the trial's reporting. The Protecting Canadians from Unsafe Drugs Act (<Tooltip tip="The federal law, the Protecting Canadians from Unsafe Drugs Act (2014), under which hospitals report serious adverse drug reactions and medical device incidents to Health Canada within 30 days after they are first documented in the hospital." headline="Vanessa's Law" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#vanessas-law">Vanessa's Law</Tooltip>) requires hospitals to [report serious adverse drug reactions (SADRs) and medical device incidents to Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medeffect-canada/adverse-reaction-reporting/mandatory-hospital-reporting/drugs-devices/guidance.html).

Each report is due within 30 days after the reaction or incident is first documented in the hospital. The duty covers every SADR and medical device incident that the hospital documents, wherever it started and whether or not the patient is admitted.

The Food and Drug Regulations and the Medical Devices Regulations exempt the following, which already have their own reporting schemes:
- A reaction that relates only to a drug authorized for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations.
- A reaction that relates only to a drug sold through the Special Access Program.
- A reaction that relates only to a vaccine given under a provincial routine immunization program.
- An incident that involves only a device under an investigational testing or special access authorization.

The Food and Drugs Act also excludes reactions to natural health products from the hospital's duty.

<Warning>
  The hospital must report a reaction that involves a suspected drug the exemptions do not cover, such as a marketed drug outside the trial's authorization, even if the trial's own drug is also suspected.
</Warning>

### What counts as a serious drug reaction or a device incident

A serious adverse drug reaction is a noxious and unintended response to a drug at any dose that has one of these outcomes:
- In-patient hospitalization, or prolonged hospitalization.
- A congenital malformation.
- Persistent or significant disability or incapacity.
- Death, or a life-threatening event.

For the hospital's report, and for the qualified investigator's report during investigational testing, a medical device incident meets both of these conditions:
- It is related to a failure of a device, a deterioration in its effectiveness or an inadequacy in its labelling or directions for use.
- It has led to death or a serious deterioration in health, or could do so if it recurred.

## Declare an incident or accident involving a patient

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

An incident or accident can happen to a patient during a study visit, or at another time while they receive health services or social services. Examples are a fall, an error with a medication or a laboratory test, and a problem with equipment.

[Quebec's Act respecting the governance of the health and social services system](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/G-1.021#se:392) requires these people to report any incident or accident as soon as possible after becoming aware of it:
- A personnel member assigned to performing tasks within an institution.
- A person practising in a centre operated by an institution.
- A person undergoing training.
- A person who, under a service contract, provides services to users of an institution on behalf of the institution.

The report must be made on the form provided for that purpose, to the institution's most senior officer or to a person the officer designates. At the MUHC, the form is AH-223, the incident or accident report. If you aren't sure whether the Act's duty applies to you or to an event, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca).

The Act defines the two kinds of event by their consequences for a user, such as a patient:
- An incident is an action or situation that does not have consequences for a user's state of health or well-being, but whose outcome is unusual and could have consequences under different circumstances.
- An accident is an action or situation where a risk materializes and which has or could have consequences for a user's state of health or well-being.

A confidentiality incident, such as a file emailed to the wrong person, is a different event. [Report a confidentiality incident under its own rules](/kb/governance/privacy#report-information-that-is-lost-or-seen-by-the-wrong-person).

The [Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) has its own procedure for declaring an incident or accident on its premises, with definitions close to the Act's. The CIM's procedure applies to its staff and to the CIM's users, such as the study teams that use its services. Anyone working on the CIM's premises who experiences or witnesses an incident or accident to a patient must declare it. To declare one:

1. Inform the supervisor or manager of the event immediately.
2. Complete sections 1 to 9 of form AH-223. The forms are at both CIM receptions and with the managers and supervisors.
3. Submit the completed form, its white and yellow copies, to your reporting supervisor or manager.

The supervisor or manager reviews the form, checks that sections 1 to 9 are accurately completed, and completes sections 10 to 13.

The study's own reports to the sponsor, the REB and Health Canada follow their own criteria and deadlines, in [Key reporting timelines](#key-reporting-timelines). If the event happened outside the CIM's premises, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) whether and how to declare it.

## Classify and report protocol deviations

A deviation is any departure from one of these:
- The current REB-approved study documents.
- The RI-MUHC's SOPs.
- The ICH's GCP guideline.
- The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) standard 14155, where it applies.

Every protocol deviation [must be assessed, addressed and documented](/sops/cr-026#5-2-3), whether it is minor, major or critical. Every deviation should be recorded on the [Protocol/GCP/SOP Deviation Log](/sops/cr-026#appendix-1), an essential document in the ISF. Each one should be assessed to identify trends, with corrective and preventive action on processes where it applies. The sponsor's monitors may check the log at monitoring visits.

Report every deviation to the sponsor as soon as possible, whatever its tier. A deviation that the sponsor's monitor observes [should be reported to the sponsor within 24 hours](/sops/cr-013#5-10-4). In a [multi-centric study](/kb/design/multi-centric#practical-implications) that the MUHC REB reviews, each Quebec site [must notify the sponsor or sponsor-investigator as soon as possible, and within 24 hours for a protocol violation](/sops/cr-026#5-4-5).

Major and critical deviations also go to [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) by [its deadline](#key-reporting-timelines). Whatever their tier, report these deviations to the REB too:
- A sponsor-approved waiver of an eligibility criterion.
- A change to the approved consent process, such as an improper translation or not using the current consent form.
- A deviation that leads to an SAE.
- Any other deviation that the principal investigator judges jeopardizes participants' safety, the efficacy of the research or data integrity, or could otherwise affect participants' rights, safety or well-being.

[Report other minor deviations to the REB with the annual continuing review](/sops/cr-026#5-3-1). The minor deviations that occurred since the previous REB review may be collected and submitted together.

If a protocol deviation brings new information that may affect participants' willingness to continue in the study, all study participants must be informed. The REB may require them to renew their consent in writing.

### Classify a deviation as minor, major or critical

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-026#5-3-3) define three tiers of deviation:

| Tier | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Minor | A deviation that shows a deficiency or a departure from the protocol, good clinical practice or the regulations, without being major or critical. | A visit window missed by one day. A minor transcription error. A non-critical document filed late. |
| Major | A marked departure or deficiency that may result in undue health risks to participants or others or could invalidate data, without being critical. | A participant enrolled outside the eligibility criteria. Consent obtained with an outdated consent form. A required safety assessment missed. A dosing error outside tolerances. |
| Critical | A departure that results in fatal, life-threatening or unsafe conditions for participants, or that involves an unauthorized trial or the adulteration, misrepresentation or falsification of records. | Unapproved <Tooltip tip="A pharmaceutical form of an active ingredient or placebo, a medical device or a natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip> given. A safety event concealed from the REB. Data fabricated or altered to conceal a violation. |

The principal investigator [classifies each deviation](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management#classification-in-practice), with input from the <Tooltip tip="A nurse, health professional or other qualified member of the research team who handles most of the administrative responsibilities and day-to-day activities of a clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">clinical research coordinator</Tooltip>. The tier is recorded on the Deviation Log when the deviation is identified. In the ICH's good clinical practice guideline, the sponsor of a trial of a drug, a vaccine or a biological product [should determine the necessary trial-specific criteria for classifying protocol deviations as important](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html). Health Canada's regulations don't specify deviation tiers.

Major and critical deviations should have a [CAPA](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management#working-through-a-capa) in place, recorded on the [CAPA form](/sops/cr-026#appendix-2). The CAPA form should also be sent to [QA](mailto:QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) for trend tracking and process improvement. A critical deviation may lead the REB to suspend or terminate the study, or to suspend its recruitment.
