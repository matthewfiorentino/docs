During a study, certain events must be reported to the Sponsor, the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip>, and where applicable, Health Canada — within defined timeframes. Missing a reporting deadline is itself a protocol deviation. This page covers adverse events, SAEs, SUSARs, protocol deviations, and ongoing REB submissions.

## Participant safety and ethical concerns

Report concerns about participant safety, informed consent or undue risk to the MUHC REB even if no protocol deviation has occurred, within the REB's deadlines in [Key reporting timelines](#key-reporting-timelines); do not wait for the annual renewal. A change made to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants is reported to the REB immediately, and a privacy breach as soon as you learn of it. The MUHC REB sets these deadlines in its [standard operating procedures](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops).

## Adverse events and SAEs

All adverse events (AEs) must be assessed and documented throughout the study. When an <Tooltip tip="Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the investigational product." headline="Adverse Event (AE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ae">AE</Tooltip> meets seriousness criteria, it triggers a reporting chain to the Sponsor, the REB, and for regulated trials, Health Canada.

An <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> is defined as any event that results in death, is life-threatening, requires hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization, results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity, causes a congenital anomaly, or is a medically important event as defined in the REB-approved protocol.

An SAE in a participant at a site the REB's approval covers needs an expedited report to the REB when it is unexpected, at least possibly related to study participation and suggests that participants or others are at greater risk of harm than previously known. So does any re-identification of a participant, which the REB treats as an SAE. The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) have the other SAEs submitted with the REB's annual continuing review.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify and assess the AE">
    Assess whether the event meets any of the seriousness criteria, and document the assessment in the source documents.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Notify the Sponsor within 24 hours">
    All SAEs must be reported to the Sponsor within 24 hours of the investigator becoming aware, or as otherwise specified in the REB-approved protocol. A complete follow-up report should follow within 8 calendar days.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assess whether an expedited REB report is required">
    Expedited reporting to the REB applies when the SAE is unexpected, at least possibly related to study participation and suggests that participants or others are at greater risk of harm than previously known. It also applies to any re-identification of a participant, which the REB treats as an SAE.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit to REB via Nagano if criteria are met">
    Use form F3H. Report a fatal or life-threatening event within 7 calendar days, then submit a detailed analysis report within 8 days of that first report; report every other reportable SAE within 15 calendar days.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sponsor-Investigator studies: report to Health Canada">
    For Sponsor-Investigator studies, you also report to Health Canada on a CIOMS I form, the reporting form of the Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences, whether or not the event meets the REB's criteria. In a drug trial, report every serious unexpected adverse drug reaction, in Canada or elsewhere; in a natural health product trial, every serious adverse reaction that occurs in Canada, expected or not. Report within 7 days if it's fatal or life-threatening, with a complete report within 8 days after that, and within 15 days otherwise.

    In a post-marketing (Phase IV) study, or another study of a marketed product, also report adverse reactions to the product's market authorization holder, so that it can report them to Health Canada. Industry sponsors handle their own Health Canada reporting.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
**ICH E6(R3) and urgency-based reporting (effective April 1, 2026).** E6(R3) shifts the framing from fixed calendar timelines to urgency- and severity-based reporting — the underlying principle is that the speed of reporting should match the urgency of the safety signal. The 7-day and 15-day REB reporting clocks above reflect current RI-MUHC SOP practice and remain operationally applicable. Sponsors implementing E6(R3) quality management frameworks may specify urgency-tiered reporting thresholds in their monitoring plans. Where sponsor requirements and site SOP timelines differ, report on whichever deadline is earlier.
</Note>

### External SUSARs from the Sponsor

External <Tooltip tip="A serious adverse reaction to a drug or natural health product whose nature, severity or frequency the Investigator's Brochure or product label does not anticipate; the sponsor reports it to Health Canada, and the site reports it to the REB when it meets the REB's criteria." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip> reports received from the Sponsor require a fourth criterion before reporting to the REB: they must also necessitate an amendment to the protocol, <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip>, or <Tooltip tip="The written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision. Both parties must sign." headline="Informed Consent Form (ICF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#icf">informed consent form</Tooltip> — or require immediate notification to participants for safety reasons.

When you receive an external SUSAR from the Sponsor, initial and date the document to confirm medical oversight, then assess whether all four reporting criteria are met. File all external SUSARs in the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> regardless of whether REB reporting is required.

### Track a pregnancy during a trial

The protocol, or a separate document, should generally describe the type and duration of follow-up for participants who become pregnant. If a participant in an interventional study becomes pregnant, collect and follow the prospective and retrospective exposure in utero reports to learn the outcome, even when there's no adverse event.

Based on: [SOP-CR-012, section 5.4](/sops/cr-012#5-4-reporting-all-saes-to-the-sponsor-within-24-hours); the International Council for Harmonisation's guideline [ICH E6(R3)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), appendix B.9.4.

## Key reporting timelines

| Event | Sponsor | REB | Health Canada |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fatal or life-threatening SAE | Within 24 hours, or as the protocol specifies | **7 days** if it meets the REB's criteria, then a detailed report within 8 days | Sponsor-investigator studies, if it meets Health Canada's criteria: 7 days, then a complete report within 8 days |
| Other SAE | Within 24 hours, or as the protocol specifies | **15 days** if it meets the REB's criteria; otherwise with the annual continuing review | Sponsor-investigator studies, if it meets Health Canada's criteria: 15 days |
| Major or critical deviation | As soon as possible | **7 days** if it involves a death or a life-threatening reaction, then a detailed report within 8 days; otherwise **15 days**. Include the corrective and preventive action (CAPA) already taken | Not applicable |
| Sponsor-approved waiver of an eligibility criterion, change to the approved consent process, deviation that leads to an SAE, or other deviation that, in the researcher's judgment, jeopardizes participants' safety, the efficacy of the research or data integrity, or could otherwise affect participants' rights, safety or well-being, whatever its tier | As soon as possible | The same deadlines; include the CAPA already taken if the deviation is major or critical | Not applicable |
| Other minor deviation | As soon as possible | Annual renewal | Not applicable |
| Incident with a device under investigational testing | 72 hours to the manufacturer or importer | 7 or 15 days | 72 hours from awareness (the qualified investigator reports) |

REB submissions via Nagano — use form **F3H** for SAE/SUSAR reporting.

## Vanessa's Law — hospital ADR reporting

<Tooltip tip="Federal law (2014) under which hospitals report serious adverse drug reactions and medical device incidents to Health Canada. Not covered: natural health products, and reactions or incidents that involve only a drug or device authorized for a clinical trial, investigational testing or special access, or a routine-program vaccine." headline="Vanessa's Law" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#vanessas-law">Vanessa's Law</Tooltip> (*Protecting Canadians from Unsafe Drugs Act*, 2014) requires hospitals, not sponsors, to report serious adverse drug reactions (SADRs) and medical device incidents to Health Canada within 30 days after they are first documented in the hospital. It excludes reactions that relate only to drugs authorized for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, drugs sold through the Special Access Program or vaccines given under a provincial routine immunization program, and incidents that involve only devices under an investigational testing or special access authorization, because those already have their own reporting routes. Reactions to natural health products are outside the hospital's obligation.

<Warning>
  A reaction that relates only to the trial's drug authorized under Division 5 goes through the trial's own reporting. If another suspected drug that these exclusions do not cover is involved, such as a marketed drug outside the trial's authorization, the hospital reports it as well, and the sponsor's reports do not replace that report.
</Warning>

| Obligation | Who reports | To whom | Timeline | Form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SAE to Sponsor** | Site principal investigator | Sponsor | Within 24 hours of awareness, or as the REB-approved protocol specifies | Per protocol / Sponsor form |
| **SUSAR to Health Canada (in a natural health product trial, every serious adverse reaction in Canada)** | Sponsor or sponsor-investigator | Health Canada | 7 or 15 days, with a complete report within 8 days after a 7-day report | CIOMS I |
| **SADR to Health Canada (Vanessa's Law), unless it relates only to a drug authorized under Division 5, a drug sold through the Special Access Program or a vaccine given under a provincial routine immunization program** | Hospital (MUHC) | Health Canada | 30 days after it is first documented in the hospital | OACIS Form 8888 in O-Word; the MUHC's Vanessa's Law Task Force sends it |
| **Medical device incident (Vanessa's Law), unless it involves only a device under an investigational testing or special access authorization** | Hospital (MUHC) | Health Canada | 30 days after it is first documented in the hospital | The MUHC's route is not published (ask QA); Health Canada accepts online reports through MedEffect Canada |

### What triggers the hospital's Vanessa's Law obligation

The hospital reports any SADR or medical device incident it documents, wherever it started and whether or not the patient is admitted.

- A **serious adverse drug reaction** is a noxious and unintended response to a drug, at any dose, that requires in-patient hospitalization or prolongs it, causes a congenital malformation, results in persistent or significant disability or incapacity, is life-threatening or results in death.
- A **medical device incident** is an incident related to a failure of a device, a deterioration in its effectiveness, or an inadequacy in its labelling or directions for use, and has led to death or a serious deterioration in health, or could do so if it recurred.

### Practical implications for site teams

- When a trial participant has a suspected SADR, follow your SAE procedures with the principal investigator and the Sponsor. If the reaction may also involve a marketed drug outside the trial's authorization, make sure it is also reported on OACIS Form 8888 in O-Word, which any member of the care team can fill in and finalize.
- For Sponsor-Investigator studies, the principal investigator holds the sponsor's reporting to Health Canada; the hospital's report still applies unless the reaction relates only to a drug authorized under Division 5, a drug sold through the Special Access Program or a vaccine given under a provincial routine immunization program, or the incident involves only a device under an investigational testing or special access authorization (it applies, for example, to a licensed device a study uses without an investigational testing authorization). Reactions to natural health products are outside the hospital's report. Contact QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca if you are uncertain which pathway applies in a specific situation.

<Note>
  **How the MUHC reports a serious adverse drug reaction.** Any member of the care team can fill in and finalize OACIS **Form 8888** in O-Word. The [MUHC's Vanessa's Law Task Force](https://muhc.ca/clinical-documents/vanessa) reviews finalized forms every two weeks and sends them to Health Canada within the 30 days.
</Note>

See [Health Canada guidance on mandatory hospital reporting under Vanessa's Law](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medeffect-canada/adverse-reaction-reporting/mandatory-hospital-reporting/drugs-devices/guidance.html) for the requirements and reporting instructions.

## Protocol deviations

A protocol deviation is any departure from the REB-approved study documents, RI-MUHC SOPs, ICH <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>, or ISO GCP that was not pre-approved. Every deviation — minor, major, or critical — must be documented on the Protocol/GCP/SOP Deviation Log. The Deviation Log is an essential document in the ISF and is reviewed at every monitoring visit.

### The three-tier classification

Every deviation identified at the RI-MUHC is classified as minor, major, or critical. This three-tier classification reflects RI-MUHC institutional practice, consistent with ICH GCP principles — it is not a classification system mandated by Health Canada regulations, which do not specify deviation tiers. Classification is a judgment call — made by the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI with input from the <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">CRC</Tooltip>, and documented on the Deviation Log at the time the deviation is identified.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Minor" icon="circle-info">
    A deviation that shows a deficiency or a departure from the protocol, good clinical practice or the regulations, without being major or critical.

    **Examples:** Visit window missed by one day; minor transcription error; non-critical document filed late.

    **Action:** Log deviation and notify Sponsor. Report to REB at annual Continuing Review, except a sponsor-approved waiver of an eligibility criterion, a change to the approved consent process, a deviation that leads to an SAE, or one that, in the researcher's judgment, jeopardizes participants' safety, the efficacy of the research or data integrity, or could otherwise affect participants' rights, safety or well-being, which goes to the REB within its deadlines. No <Tooltip tip="Actions taken to resolve a compliance issue and prevent recurrence: identify, correct, prevent." headline="Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#capa">CAPA</Tooltip> required — document and review for trends.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Major" icon="triangle-exclamation">
    A marked departure or deficiency, other than a critical one, that may result in undue health risks to participants or others, or could invalidate data.

    **Examples:** Participant enrolled outside eligibility criteria; consent obtained with outdated ICF; required safety assessment missed; dosing error outside tolerances.

    **Action:** A CAPA should be in place. Report to REB within **15 days**, or **7 days** if it involves a death or a life-threatening reaction. Copy CAPA to <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's Quality Assurance department, which checks the credentials and training of each investigator who submits a study, audits research and maintains the SOPs. In GCP, quality assurance is the planned, systematic actions that ensure a trial complies with GCP and the applicable regulatory requirements." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> and notify Sponsor.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Critical" icon="circle-exclamation">
    A departure that results in fatal, life-threatening or unsafe conditions for participants, or that involves an unauthorized trial or the adulteration, misrepresentation or falsification of records.

    **Examples:** Unapproved <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> administered; safety event concealed from the REB; data fabricated or altered to conceal a violation.

    **Action:** A CAPA should be in place. Report to REB within **15 days**, or **7 days** if it involves a death or a life-threatening reaction. Copy CAPA to QA and notify Sponsor immediately. May trigger study suspension.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
