Running a clinical study at the MUHC involves more than following the protocol and meeting regulatory deadlines. It means operating within a specific institutional environment — Quebec provincial law, MUHC policies, and a set of practical realities that <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip> training alone does not cover. This section explains the layers of obligation that apply throughout an active study and how they fit together.

## The regulatory and institutional layers

Every requirement in clinical research at the MUHC comes from one or more layers of obligation. These layers are additive, not competing — meeting GCP requirements does not satisfy the provincial consent requirements, and meeting provincial requirements does not automatically satisfy MUHC institutional policies. Each layer applies independently.

| Layer | Governing frameworks | What it means in practice |
|---|---|---|
| International | ICH GCP E6(R3), Declaration of Helsinki, ISO 14155 | The baseline for all clinical research worldwide. Participant protection, data integrity, monitoring, and reporting obligations. Covered by GCP and ISO training. |
| Federal (Canada) | Health Canada Food and Drug Regulations (Division 5), Natural Health Products Regulations, Medical Devices Regulations, TCPS2 | <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">CTA</Tooltip>/<Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip> submission and authorization, <Tooltip tip="A serious adverse reaction to a drug or natural health product whose nature, severity or frequency the Investigator's Brochure or product label does not anticipate; the sponsor reports it to Health Canada, and the site reports it to the REB when it meets the REB's criteria." headline="SUSAR" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#susar">SUSAR</Tooltip> reporting timelines, clinical trial registration. |
| Provincial (Quebec) | Civil Code of Quebec, Act Respecting Health and Social Services Information (<Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information (key provisions July 2024). Governs researcher access to health data through RSSS institutions." headline="Law 5 / Loi 5" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#law-5">Law 5</Tooltip>), <Tooltip tip="Quebec's Act to modernize personal information protection law (in force Sept 2023). Requires EFVP before sharing personal data without consent." headline="Law 25 / Loi 25" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#law-25">Law 25</Tooltip>, MSSS research ethics framework | Consent rules in the Civil Code differ from those of common law provinces. Law 5 requires an EFVP when a study asks for access to health records without consent. MSSS mandatory paragraphs must appear in consent forms. |
| Institutional (MUHC) | MUHC research policies, <Tooltip tip="The person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research: the Director of Innovation, Performance and Quality, who issues MUHC Authorization for each study." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip> authorization requirements, MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement, OPQ pharmacy regulations | Every team member must sign the MUHC Confidentiality Agreement before accessing participant records. Medications, including investigational drugs, must be dispensed by a pharmacist. <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> from the Personne mandatée is required before any study procedures begin — <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> approval alone is not sufficient. |

<Note>
In Quebec, the Civil Code governs who can consent to research and under what conditions. A minor 14 or over may consent alone only if the REB finds that the research involves only minimal risk and that the circumstances justify it. For clinical trials, the MSSS's mandatory paragraphs must appear in the consent form; without them, the REB rejects it.
</Note>

## The arc of study conduct at the MUHC

A study moves through a sequence of stages from activation to close-out, and some obligations apply at every stage.


<Steps>
  <Step title="Foundations (throughout)">
    Privacy and data integrity obligations apply throughout the entire study, not at a specific stage. The MUHC Confidentiality and Security Agreement must be signed before any team member accesses participant records. ALCOA+ data standards apply from the first source document entry. Study records are kept for a retention period that depends on the study ([Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving)).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Study activation (before first participant)">
    A study that has received REB approval and MUHC Authorization is authorized to run, but is not running yet. Recruitment begins only after all of these are in place: Health Canada's authorization, if the study needs it; the REB approval letter; the MUHC Authorization letter from the Personne mandatée; the sponsor's <Tooltip tip="Monitoring visit to train the site team on the protocol and confirm site readiness, after REB approval and MUHC Authorization." headline="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#siv">SIV</Tooltip>; documented training; documented task delegation on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>; and the sponsor's go letter, its written permission to start, where there is one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recruitment and screening">
    Recruitment materials, such as posters, phone scripts and social media content, need the sponsor's and the REB's approval before use, and any material a patient may see then needs MUHC Communications' approval too. Pre-screening access to OACIS records requires an EFVP and MUHC authorization.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Enrolment and consent">
    Consent in Quebec is governed by the Civil Code. The participant's treating physician, or anyone else with a long-standing relationship with them, should not obtain their consent; if the treating physician is the best person to obtain it, they must explain why to the REB and, if the REB approves, tell the participant about their dual role. For clinical trials, the consent form must include the MSSS's mandatory paragraphs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Study visits and procedures">
    In Quebec, any medication given to a patient, in care or in research, must be dispensed by a pharmacist, and all drugs and some natural health products used in research must be managed by the MUHC pharmacy. The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, delegates these tasks to the pharmacy but remains responsible for them.

    A research nurse may dispense an <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip> only with the MUHC Research Pharmacy's approval, requested at submission. Convenience, nights and weekends are not exceptions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ongoing oversight (continuous)">
    Safety reporting, REB annual renewals, protocol amendments, monitoring visits, and Task Delegation Log maintenance run continuously in parallel throughout the active study. A lapse in REB renewal is not retroactively corrected — there will be no ethics coverage for the lapse period, which Health Canada inspectors treat as a major observation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Study close-out">
    Close-out does not end a study's obligations. The <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">ISF</Tooltip> must remain organized and inspection-ready for the full retention period. Post-study <Tooltip tip="An adverse event meeting seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, or congenital anomaly." headline="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sae">SAE</Tooltip> reporting continues for the follow-up duration defined in the protocol.
  </Step>
</Steps>
