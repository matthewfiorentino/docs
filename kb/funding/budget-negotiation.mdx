Developing a clinical trial budget with an industry sponsor can be one of the most unfamiliar parts of the process for investigators. This guide covers what to prepare, what line items to include, how to negotiate the exhibit (payment terms), and common mistakes to avoid — with Canadian and Quebec-specific context throughout.

<Note>
  The CIM Budget Specialists are available to help any RI-MUHC researcher negotiate an industry-sponsored budget. Engage them early — during feasibility, before your Site Agreement is signed. See the [CIM advisory service](#cim-budget-advisory-service) at the bottom of this page.
</Note>

## Before you negotiate

Before sitting down with a sponsor, gather these documents. Missing any of them slows the process and leads to incomplete budgets.

| Document | Why you need it |
|---|---|
| **Protocol** (draft or final) | The table of assessments is the foundation — every procedure listed must have a corresponding budget line |
| **Informed Consent Form (ICF)** | Contains visit details, patient compensation commitments, and procedures described to participants |
| **Sponsor's proposed budget** | The starting point for negotiation — compare line by line against the protocol's table of assessments to identify gaps |
| **Exhibit / financial appendix** | The payment terms — payment schedules, holdback, invoicing instructions, currency, and archiving language |
| **Third-party quotes** | Quotes from departments or external vendors your study will use — nursing, pharmacy, ophthalmology, pathology, imaging, laboratory services |

<Tip>
  Compare the protocol's table of assessments against the sponsor's proposed budget procedure by procedure. Any procedure listed in the protocol but missing from the budget is a cost the site will absorb unless it is added during negotiation.
</Tip>

## Canadian context

Several features of the Canadian research environment affect how budgets are structured. Understanding these before you negotiate gives you a stronger position.

### Overhead rates across Canadian institutions

The RI-MUHC charges **30% overhead on direct costs** for industry-sponsored studies, excluding startup, REB, and pharmacy fees. This is consistent with the national standard. The 30% rate applies to industry-sponsored trials only — overhead for investigator-initiated trials and foundation-funded studies differs. See [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#institutional-overhead) for the full funding-source breakdown.

| Institution | Overhead rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **RI-MUHC** | 30% | Excludes startup, REB, and pharmacy fees |
| **VCHRI** (Vancouver) | 30% | 40% on non-clinical-trial service contracts |
| **University of Calgary** | 30% | Confirmed as consistent with national average |
| **McMaster University** | 30% | May be higher depending on project type |
| **UBC** | 25% | Published rate; some agencies have negotiated different rates |

The overall Canadian range is 25–40%, with 30% being the most common. This is notably lower than comparable U.S. institutions, which contributes to Canada's cost competitiveness for clinical trials.

### Tax-exempt status

The RI-MUHC is a hospital authority that is a registered charity. Under CRA policy, industry-sponsored clinical trial services provided by such institutions are **exempt supplies** — meaning **GST/HST is not charged** on payments from pharmaceutical sponsors.

This has two practical implications:

- **Do not include GST/HST in your budget.** The RI-MUHC does not charge tax on research services to sponsors. Including it inflates your budget and may confuse the negotiation.
- **This is a competitive advantage.** Sponsors pay less total at Canadian non-profit research institutes than at for-profit CROs that must charge tax. You do not need to "sell" this, but it is useful context when sponsors compare site costs internationally.

Canadian hospital authorities can also claim rebates on GST/HST paid on research inputs (base rebate of 50%, with additional public service body rebates bringing total recovery to approximately 83% of federal GST/HST).

### Health Canada fees

There is **no fee** to submit a Clinical Trial Application (CTA) to Health Canada, and no fees for operational requests related to clinical trials. This is a notable advantage over some other jurisdictions and should not be confused with REB fees, which are separate and provincial.

### REB fees

REB fees at the RI-MUHC are set by the provincial MSSS fee schedule and are **non-refundable** regardless of REB outcome. For a single-site industry study, the minimum first-year cost is approximately **\$8,886** (scientific review + ethical review + site authorization), plus annual monitoring fees in subsequent years. See the [full MSSS fee schedule](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#reb-fees) on the Study Budgets and Agreements page.

### Quebec-specific tools

Quebec has invested in provincial infrastructure for clinical trials through **CATALIS**, an organization that coordinates research optimization across the province. CATALIS operates the **FAST TRACK** evaluation service (targeting clinical trial authorization in 8 weeks or less) and maintains provincial reference tools including standardized consent form clauses and regulatory templates. Quebec also promotes the pan-Canadian model Clinical Trial Agreement (mCTA) and model Confidentiality Disclosure Agreement (mCDA) to streamline contract negotiation.

## Key cost categories

Every industry-sponsored trial budget should account for four categories of costs. The items below are most commonly missing from sponsor-proposed budgets — verify each is present before negotiating. Open the accordions for full detail on each category. For acceptable rate ranges, see [Reference benchmarks](#reference-benchmarks) below.

| ★ Line item | Category | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Lab and imaging setup fees | Site costs | One-time departmental workflow setup — get departmental quotes before signing |
| Protocol amendments | Site management | Budget for 2–3 at ~\$2,500 each; this line is often absent in initial sponsor budgets |
| Re-consent effort | Site management | Each ICF amendment = coordinator + PI time per active participant |
| Document translation | Site management | Required for non-English/French speakers or Quebec regulatory submissions |
| Audit support | Site management | Staff time for sponsor, Health Canada, or internal audits |
| Screen failures | Patient costs | Reimburse all completed procedures — push to remove ratios; \$50–\$250+ per failure |
| Re-screening | Patient costs | Often omitted entirely; required if the protocol permits repeat screening of failed participants |
| Unscheduled visits | Patient costs | Must include PI, coordinator, and patient stipend components |
| Dry ice and specimen shipping | Patient costs | Per-shipment cost for biological samples sent to central labs |
| Patient transportation | Patient costs | Include if the site is expected to arrange travel for participants |
| SAE reporting | Patient administration | Coordinator + PI time per event — significant in large or long trials |
| IND safety report reviews | Patient administration | PI time to review and acknowledge sponsor safety reports — often missing entirely |

    - **Data entry fees** — CRF completion, EDC data entry, query resolution, and data cleaning
    - **Pharmacy administration** — Per-visit dispensing, accountability, and annual fees
    - **SAE reporting** ★ — Coordinator and PI time for preparing, submitting, and following up on serious adverse event reports. In large trials with frequent SAEs, this cost is significant.
    - **IND safety report reviews** ★ — PI time to review and acknowledge safety reports from the sponsor

## Negotiating the budget

### What is non-negotiable

Certain costs must be in every industry-sponsored budget. These are institutional requirements — they are not optional and cannot be waived.

- **Startup, REB, and pharmacy fees** — Non-refundable. Must be paid before study activities begin, ideally upon fully executed Clinical Trial Agreement.
- **Overhead (30%)** — Applied to direct costs. Excludes startup, REB, and pharmacy fees. This is the RI-MUHC institutional rate, consistent with the national standard.

### What to always include

These line items should appear in every budget. If the sponsor's template does not include them, add them.

- **Study coordinator and PI fees on every patient visit** — Not just treatment visits. Screening, re-screening, unscheduled visits, and follow-up visits all require coordinator and PI time.
- **Re-consenting** — When protocol amendments require participants to sign a new ICF, the time to conduct re-consent must be budgeted.
- **Local labs, pathology, imaging** — Any protocol procedures performed by MUHC departments outside the research team. Get departmental quotes early.
- **Archiving and close-out visit** — These happen at the end, but must be budgeted at the start.
- **Patient stipends and reimbursement** — As described in the ICF.

### Optional but recommended

These items are not always required but are common sources of unbudgeted cost. Including them upfront avoids budget amendments later.

| Line item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Lead site submission fees | If your site is leading a multi-site Canadian submission |
| On-site and remote monitoring fees | Staff time to prepare for and host monitoring visits |
| Amendment processing fees | ~\$2,500 per amendment for re-training, re-consent, and REB submission |
| SAE reporting fees | Coordinator and PI time per event — adds up in large trials |
| Screen failure and re-screening fees | See [negotiating screen failures](#screen-failures) below |
| Unscheduled visit fees | Include PI, coordinator, and patient stipend components |
| Advertising and recruitment costs | If the site is expected to recruit actively |
| Invoice uploading to sponsor portal | Administrative time for sponsor-specific invoicing systems |
| Contingency (8–15%) | Scale by study complexity — see the [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#contingency) page for tiers |

## Negotiating the exhibit

The exhibit (financial appendix) governs how and when you get paid. This is where most of the actual negotiation happens.

### Payment models

There are two primary funding models for industry-sponsored trials in Canada:

- **Activity-based funding (per-patient/per-visit)** — The most common model. Fixed per-unit fees are negotiated for each visit or procedure. Payment is triggered upon completion of milestones (enrollment, visit completion, data entry). This is what most sponsor budgets propose.
- **Position-based funding** — The annual cost of a study coordinator is paid upfront, with specific procedures and pass-through costs billed separately. More common in academic or investigator-initiated research, but occasionally proposed for large or complex industry trials.

Understand which model the sponsor is proposing before negotiating individual line items.

### Payment timing

- **Startup fees** — Should be payable upon fully executed CTA, not upon first patient enrolled. Always have the exhibit state this explicitly.
- **Payment schedule** — Clarify whether payments are automatic (triggered by milestones in the EDC) or invoiceable (you must submit an invoice). If invoiceable, confirm the submission process, portal, and mailing address.
- **Payment terms** — Standard is 30–60 days from receipt of correct invoice. Push back on anything longer than 60 days — across the industry, 23% of sites report payment delays of 90 days or longer.
- **Payment frequency** — Monthly or quarterly? For studies with slow enrollment, infrequent payments create cash flow problems. Negotiate a schedule that reflects your enrollment timeline.
- **Currency** — Confirm whether the budget is in CAD or USD. This affects every line item and must be clear before signing. If the budget is in USD, consider how exchange rate fluctuations will be handled.
- **Upfront participant payment** — Ideally, negotiate one full upfront participant visit payment that can be refunded if no participants enroll. This protects the site during slow-enrollment periods.

### Holdback

Many sponsors withhold a percentage of earned payments until data cleaning or database lock — typically 10–14%. As of recent industry surveys, approximately 76% of clinical trial agreements still include holdback provisions.

- **Push to reduce or eliminate holdback.** The practice originated when data was physically transported between sites and sponsors; it is less justified in the era of electronic data capture and real-time monitoring.
- If holdback cannot be removed, negotiate it down to **5%** and request release on a **per-subject basis** as each participant's data is cleaned — not withheld in bulk until study end.
- Clarify the trigger for holdback release. "Database lock" can be months or years after last patient last visit.

### Screen failures

Sponsors often cap screen failure reimbursement at a fixed ratio — for example, paying for 1 screen failure per 3 participants randomized (1:3 ratio).

- **Negotiate to remove ratios entirely.** Every screen failure consumes real site resources regardless of how many participants are eventually randomized.
- If ratios cannot be removed, negotiate based on **realistic failure rates for your patient population**. Actual screen failure rates vary widely: 20–30% in some cancer trials, up to 70–80% in Alzheimer's and CNS trials. A 3:1 ratio may be adequate for some indications and grossly insufficient for others.
- Ensure screen failure payments include **all completed procedures** — not just a flat fee. If a screen failure completed labs, imaging, and an ECG, each of those should be reimbursed.
- Typical screen failure reimbursement ranges from \$50 to \$250+ depending on procedures completed.

### Other exhibit terms

- **Address of payee** — Confirm the RI-MUHC institutional address is listed, not a personal address.
- **Invoicing instructions** — Confirm where and how invoices are to be submitted. Some sponsors require portal-based invoicing with specific formatting.
- **OCC file number** — Include the file number provided by the Contracts Office on all correspondence.

## Finalizing the budget

Once negotiations are complete:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the final documents">
    The sponsor will send clean final copies of the budget and exhibit. Review them carefully to confirm they match what you agreed to — not a prior draft.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Forward to your Contract Officer">
    Reply to the sponsor and copy your Contract Officer at the Research Agreements Office ([contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca)). Include the approved documents as the final version and reference your OCC file number.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Final CTA review">
    The Contract Officer conducts a last review of the full CTA, which includes your final budget, before the signature process begins.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify before signing">
    Before the agreement is signed, review the attached budget one last time to confirm it is the version you approved. Errors at this stage are costly to correct.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Common mistakes

<Warning>
  The most common source of budget problems is late engagement. If CIM services, departmental resources, or third-party vendors are part of your protocol, their costs must be in the initial budget — not added after the Site Agreement is signed.
</Warning>

- **Underestimating coordinator time** — Every study procedure requires coordinator effort: screening, consenting, data entry, query resolution, monitoring visit prep, SAE reporting, and regulatory maintenance. Budget for all of it — this is the single most underestimated cost at Canadian sites.
- **Accepting flat visit payments** — A 3-hour screening visit should not pay the same as a 30-minute follow-up. Differentiate by visit complexity.
- **Missing amendment costs** — The average industry trial undergoes multiple amendments. Each one costs approximately \$2,500 in re-training, re-consent, and REB submissions. Budget for at least 2–3.
- **Forgetting ancillary department fees** — Pharmacy dispensing, lab processing, imaging, dry ice, specimen shipping, and patient transportation are frequently omitted.
- **Weak unscheduled visit terms** — Vague or missing language on unscheduled visit compensation leaves sites absorbing real costs.
- **Not addressing cash flow** — Accepting 90-day payment terms, high holdback percentages, and infrequent payment schedules creates cash flow problems, especially for sites with multiple concurrent trials.
- **Overlooking archiving** — 15 years of document storage for drug trials (7 years for most other studies) is a real cost that must be negotiated upfront.
- **Ignoring upfront costs** — If ethics review or enrollment is slow, sites fall into deficit before the first patient is enrolled. Secure an upfront startup payment and ideally one full participant visit payment in advance.
- **Allowing non-enrolling studies to stay open** — Keeping a low-performing study open drains coordinator time, pharmacy resources, and regulatory maintenance effort without corresponding revenue. Build in a mechanism to renegotiate or close.
- **Not benchmarking your rates** — Provincial and commercial benchmarking data exists for common clinical trial procedures. Ask your CIM Budget Specialist whether comparable rate data is available for your study type before accepting a sponsor's proposed rates.
- **Not leveraging tax-exempt status** — Canadian hospital research institutes are exempt from charging GST/HST on sponsor payments. Understand this advantage and ensure it is reflected correctly in the exhibit.

## Reference benchmarks

Use these as reference points during negotiation — not as fixed rates, but as a sanity check on what is standard in Canada.

| Metric | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Overhead rate | 25–30% | 30% is the Canadian institutional standard |
| REB fees — ethical review (MSSS) | \$6,348 | Per project, non-refundable. Indexed annually on April 1. |
| REB fees — first-year total (single site) | ~\$8,886 | Scientific review + ethical review + site authorization |
| Health Canada CTA fee | \$0 | No application fee in Canada |
| Screen failure reimbursement | \$50–\$250+ per failure | Should reflect actual procedures completed |
| Screen failure ratio (sponsor-imposed) | 1:3 to 1:5 | Push to remove ratios; negotiate based on actual rates |
| Holdback | 5–14% of earned payments | Push for 5% or elimination; request per-subject release |
| Payment terms | 30–60 days | Push back on anything beyond 60 days |
| Budget contingency | 8–15% of total | Scale by study complexity: 8–10% low, 10–12% moderate, 12–15% high |
| Amendment processing cost | ~\$2,500 per amendment | Re-training, re-consent, REB submission |
| Archiving period | 15 years (drug trials) | 7 years for most other study types |
