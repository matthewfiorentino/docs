A research agreement sets out who does what in a study, who pays for it and who bears which risks. Every research agreement for a study with a third party must be drafted, reviewed and negotiated by the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca). A third party can be a private company, a non-profit organization or any other organization.

The agreement's budget is the principal investigator's to prepare and negotiate: [Study Budgets](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) explains what it must include.

## Know when the study needs an agreement

A research agreement is required when:

- The study receives an agreement from a collaborator or a collaborating institution.
- The study works with collaborators outside the RI-MUHC. McGill University researchers count as external.
- The study receives or sends human samples or human data.
- Funds go to or come from any external party for the study.
- The investigator receives devices or materials, such as software, for the study.
- The investigator uses third-party services.

The agreements a study most often needs:

| Agreement | When it is needed |
|---|---|
| Clinical trial agreement | Between the sponsor or sponsor-investigator, the investigator and the institutions. The agreement sets out the parties' responsibilities and the study's financial terms. |
| Agreement with a contract research organization (CRO) or an academic research organization (ARO) | When the sponsor transfers some of its duties to one. The agreement documents the duties transferred and those the sponsor keeps. The sponsor remains responsible for the quality and integrity of the trial data. |
| Third-party agreement | When a laboratory, an imaging service or another service provider carries out study tasks. All the third party's tasks are set out in the agreement or an addendum to it. |
| Confidentiality agreement | Before the study team receives confidential study information, such as the protocol, from a sponsor. |
| Data sharing or transfer agreement | Before data go to a site outside the MUHC, or come from one. All parties must sign it. Get its template from the Research Agreements Office. |
| Material transfer agreement | When human samples or other materials go to or come from another institution. |

For a study a sponsor initiates, [the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) require](/sops/cr-102#1-1-1) a confidentiality agreement between the sponsor, the institution and the investigator, signed and dated before the team receives confidential study documents. The institution requires the Research Agreements Office to review and approve it before it is signed, and the investigator must then submit the signed agreement in Nagano. For a study a sponsor-investigator initiates, a confidentiality agreement between the sponsor-investigator and the principal investigator [should be signed and dated](/sops/cr-004#5-1-1) before the team receives any confidential study information. Consider checking how long the confidentiality obligations last, and that the agreement lets you discuss the study with the institutional colleagues who need to be involved, such as the pharmacy or the people preparing the submission to the Research Ethics Board (REB).

## Submit the agreement in Nagano and My Contracts

Upload every research agreement both to <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> and to My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal. The upload starts the Research Agreements Office's review. Once the contract is submitted in Nagano, email the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) to tell it the contract was submitted. For an investigator-initiated study, ask the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) for the latest contract template.

For sponsored research, the principal investigator must submit, through My Contracts:

- A description of the project.
- A budget and a timeline.
- A copy of the contract, where there is one.
- All the forms required for the review.
- The source of funding, where there is one.

All responsibilities should be set out in the agreement, such as the sponsor-investigator's or the investigator's responsibilities under good clinical practice. A service provider the sponsor chooses may carry out activities that are the investigator's responsibility, and the responsibility for them stays with the investigator.

Once the agreement is signed, the investigator should obtain a signed copy for the investigator's confidential file. The Research Agreements Office keeps the original. Keep the copy for [the retention period the RI-MUHC's SOPs set](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving). The investigator should send every amendment to an agreement to the Research Agreements Office.

## Sign the agreement at the right time

Researchers should never sign a binding contract or agreement before the Research Agreements Office has reviewed it. Investigators must not sign on behalf of the RI-MUHC.

A research contract with a company must involve the principal investigator, who signs it. Besides the company and the principal investigator, each contract must be signed for the MUHC by its President and Chief Executive Officer, and for the RI-MUHC by its Executive Director, or by their designated representatives.

A clinical trial agreement can be signed once the REB has approved the study, even if other feasibility reviews are still under way. [Every agreement must be fully executed before MUHC Authorization](/kb/submission/submission-overview#institutional-feasibility-review).

When the contract involves the MUHC's services, data, users or facilities, the RI-MUHC and the principal investigator must consult the MUHC before signing it.

A researcher may sign a research contract with a sponsor in their own name, or for a company in which they hold a majority interest or influence, and also sign a separate contract with the RI-MUHC for the same research or part of it. The researcher must declare that situation to the RI-MUHC. The situation is a [conflict of interest](/kb/governance/conflict-of-interest), which the Research Agreements Office manages.{/* Added from the page's own source (V3-TRACE P27; batch B02's review, I2c). ## The Research Agreements Office

The Research Agreements Office protects the rights and interests of the RI-MUHC, the investigator and the research team. The office drafts, revises and negotiates every kind of research agreement: clinical trial agreements, data and material transfer agreements, collaboration and inter-institutional agreements, confidentiality agreements, service agreements and licensing agreements, among others.

The office acts as the liaison between the investigator and the other parties. The office reviews each agreement against the RI-MUHC's policies and procedures and its standard legal clauses. Once all the signing parties agree on the legal terms, it makes its recommendations to the person with authority to sign the contract.

The Research Agreements Office also answers legal questions about research agreements. The Portal's Research Agreements section has a guide to My Contracts.

## Read the clauses of a clinical trial agreement

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Industry-sponsored studies</span></div>

The Research Agreements Office negotiates the legal terms of a clinical trial agreement, and the principal investigator approves the budget. The principal investigator is still responsible for conducting the study at the site, so consider reading what the agreement commits the institution and the team to. Consider looking in particular at the clauses below, and raise any concern with the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).

| Clause | What it sets | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Indemnification | Who bears the financial responsibility if something goes wrong: an injured participant, a regulatory penalty or a third party's claim. The sponsor usually indemnifies the institution and the investigator for claims that arise from the investigational product, the protocol or the sponsor's actions, when the site followed the protocol and the law. The institution stays responsible for its own negligence and deviations. | That the indemnification is not one-sided, and that the institution does not take on the product's own risks. |
| Publication | When and how the investigator may publish. The sponsor usually reviews manuscripts before submission, often for 30 to 90 days, and may ask for a short delay, often 30 to 60 more days, to file a patent. In a multi-site trial the sponsor often coordinates the main publication. | That the investigator keeps the right to publish whatever the results. A clause that lets the sponsor block publication indefinitely is a concern. |
| Data ownership and access | Who owns the study data and who may use them after the study. In most industry-sponsored studies the sponsor owns the data, and the site keeps access to its own source documents. | That the investigator keeps access to the site's own data for as long as the protocol and the retention rules require. Any secondary use the investigator plans is set out before the study starts. |
| Intellectual property | Who owns inventions from the study. Usually each party keeps what it brought, and what the study generates goes to the sponsor. | That the investigator's and McGill's existing intellectual property is protected, and that the institution's licensing rights are clear. |
| Termination | When either party may end the agreement early. Sponsors usually keep the right to end the study at any time with written notice. | That the sponsor pays the costs incurred up to termination and provides for an orderly wind-down, and that enrolled participants are cared for after an early end. The site keeps the right to withdraw if participants are at risk or the sponsor does not meet its obligations. |
| Insurance | The sponsor's clinical trial insurance for participant injury. | The type and amount of coverage, a period after the study for late injuries, and the institution and investigator named as additional insureds or at least not excluded. |

Quebec's health ministry released the [Provincial Model Clinical Trial Agreement](https://catalisquebec.com/en/resources-for-health-institutions/) (mCTA), which CATALIS Québec, a not-for-profit organization mandated by the Government of Quebec, adapted to Quebec's regulatory context from the pan-Canadian model of the Canadian Clinical Trials Coordinating Centre. The ministry released it to speed up the signing of clinical trial agreements between sponsors and the institutions of Quebec's health network.

If a sponsor submits the mCTA without any changes, Quebec's public health institutions accept it as is. CATALIS also publishes a Provincial Model Confidentiality Agreement.

When a sponsor proposes its own agreement, the Research Agreements Office compares it with the mCTA to find where it departs from standard terms.

Quebec contract law is civil law. The Civil Code makes good faith a duty in every contract, clinical trial agreements included, and the Research Agreements Office checks that each agreement is enforceable under Quebec law.{/* NOT CHECKED (LégisQuébec refuses scripts; no saved copy of the Civil Code's articles 6, 7 and 1375): the good-faith duty. ## Cover the study as its sponsor

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

For a study you sponsor yourself to be covered by the RI-MUHC's insurance policy, the RI-MUHC [should be named as the sponsor](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#cover-a-study-you-sponsor-yourself) on the agreement, on any regulatory documents, such as Health Canada's no-objection letter or authorization, and on ClinicalTrials.gov. You keep all the sponsor-investigator's responsibilities for conducting the research under the RI-MUHC's SOPs.

Physicians are covered by the Canadian Medical Protective Association (CMPA), [within the limits of its assistance](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#physicians-and-the-cmpa).

## When a participant is injured

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

Care for the participant comes before anything else. Make sure the participant receives appropriate medical care, record the event as an adverse event in the source documents, and assess whether it is serious.

Report every serious adverse event to the sponsor within 24 hours of the principal investigator learning of it, unless the REB-approved protocol sets a different timeline.

The rest of the investigator's reporting duties are on [Reportable Events and Protocol Deviations](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events).

The MUHC's civil liability insurance includes [coverage that applies to research activities](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#know-what-covers-your-study). A study without MUHC Authorization never had the MUHC's institutional insurance. If it is unclear whether the sponsor's insurance or the institutional coverage applies, contact the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) at once, before you discuss coverage with anyone else.

Under Quebec's Civil Code, a person who causes harm through their fault must repair it.

When harm comes from the site's fault, such as a deviation or negligence, the institution or the investigator may be liable.

When harm comes from risks of the product or the protocol that were disclosed in the consent process and that the sponsor agreed to cover, the sponsor's indemnification and insurance apply. The sponsor's insurance usually covers harm from the investigational product, from procedures the protocol requires and from the sponsor's own fault, but not harm from the site's own negligence or deviations.{/* NOT CHECKED (LégisQuébec refuses scripts; article 1457 not in the saved copies): the civil-liability rule. The signed consent form is the record of the risks the participant was told about.

The sponsor must address the costs of treating participants for trial-related injuries, in line with the regulatory requirements that apply. Most clinical trial agreements in Canada have no no-fault compensation clause.
