One study can involve several kinds of insurance. The institution's insurance, a physician's coverage and a sponsor's insurance can all apply, and some students are covered by academic insurance. Which one applies depends on who does what in the study, who sponsors it and where it runs. The [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) answers questions about a study's insurance.

## Know what covers your study

The MUHC holds civil liability insurance, which includes coverage that applies to research activities. The [Direction des assurances du réseau de la santé et des services sociaux (DARSSS)](https://www.darsss.ca/fr/darsss-qui-sommes-nous) administers it. Quebec's health ministry designated the DARSSS to offer damage insurance services to the institutions of the province's health and social services network, and the DARSSS manages the network's compensation plan. Only the network's public institutions benefit from the insurance the DARSSS manages.

The procedures in the MUHC's [*Regulatory Framework for Clinical Research at Santé Québec – McGill University Health Centre: Organization and Good Practices*](https://rimuhc.ca/conduct-research/policies#framework) comply with the specific rules that apply to the MUHC's civil liability insurance coverage by the DARSSS.

When a user of the MUHC's services claims damages, the DARSSS defends the MUHC and its staff, but not physicians. Physicians have [their own coverage](#physicians-and-the-cmpa), from the Canadian Medical Protective Association (CMPA).

A study without <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip> isn't covered by the MUHC's institutional insurance. The [institutional feasibility review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#institutional-feasibility-review), which runs in parallel with the ethics review by the Research Ethics Board (REB), analyzes the liability insurance coverage that applies to each study.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

If a participant is injured during a study, care and reporting come first. [When a participant is injured](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#when-a-participant-is-injured) gives the steps, and says when to contact the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).

## Meet the insurance duties of the principal investigator

The principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, holds duties on insurance and indemnification under the *Regulatory Framework*. The principal investigator:

- Must ensure that adequate liability insurance coverage is held, including the institutional coverage that applies to research activities.
- Must understand the indemnification agreements that apply.
- Must not sign agreements that inappropriately transfer liability.
- Cannot delegate their ultimate legal responsibility, even when delegating specific tasks.

An indemnification clause requires one party to compensate another for certain kinds of specified harm or loss. [Researchers should never sign a binding agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#sign-the-agreement-at-the-right-time) before the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) has reviewed it.

The consent form protects participants' rights too. [Do not use any language in the consent form](/sops/cr-006#5-5-1) that makes participants or their legally appointed representative waive, or appear to waive, any legal rights. Do not use language that releases, or appears to release, the investigator, the institution, the site, the sponsor or their agents from liability for negligence, such as "we are not responsible for …".

## Know who on the team is covered

Different insurance covers different members of a study team:

| Team member | What applies |
|---|---|
| Physicians | The CMPA covers medical doctors, within its own limits ([Physicians and the CMPA](#physicians-and-the-cmpa)). |
| Students | Students covered by academic insurance may take part in a study, including working with participants, if they meet the training requirements. Without valid academic insurance, a student may not contact or recruit participants, or perform procedures on them. |
| Volunteers and other people not affiliated with the RI-MUHC or the MUHC | Because they are not covered by professional or academic liability insurance, they may not contact or recruit participants, or perform procedures on them, at the RI-MUHC or the MUHC. |
| Patient partners | Consider asking the Research Agreements Office how the patient partners on your team are covered. |

[Who cannot work with participants](/training/compliance-requirements#who-cannot-work-with-participants) lists everyone the rule on liability insurance covers. Each team member also needs [an agreement with the RI-MUHC](/kb/team/hiring-hr-support#agreements-by-role) before any study task.

## Physicians and the CMPA

Physicians are covered by the CMPA. [The CMPA generally assists only with civil actions arising in Canada for medical professional work related to research performed in Canada](https://www.cmpa-acpm.ca/en/membership/protection-for-members/principles-of-assistance/clinical-research). The CMPA does not generally assist with civil legal matters arising from research with healthy volunteers.

To be eligible for the CMPA's assistance, the research must be approved by a Canadian-based independent research ethics board that operates according to applicable regulations and guidelines, such as the *Tri-Council Policy Statement*. Where applicable, the research must also have regulatory approval from Health Canada, for example for research on a drug, a device or software.

The CMPA does not generally assist with civil legal matters where the allegations arise from the design, delivery or outcome of the research. CMPA members are expected to turn to the research sponsor for indemnification for those allegations.

Where the research involves creating a medical device, including software, the CMPA member should look to the developer for indemnification. Where there is no sponsor or developer, the member acting as an independent researcher or medical device developer assumes liability for civil actions related to the design, delivery or outcome of the research or the creation of the medical device.

Multinational studies carry a greater risk of civil actions brought outside Canada. CMPA members should seek indemnification or appropriate liability protection for civil actions that could arise outside Canada.

CMPA members may be eligible for assistance with civil actions arising from the acts or omissions of research assistants employed by CMPA members or by CMPA-eligible clinics or facilities. The assistance may be available where the research assistant works under the direct supervision of a physician researcher and does not see and treat patients independently.

The CMPA's assistance with disputes arising from research contracts is generally limited to situations where the member's professional medical work is called into question, or their ability to practise medicine is jeopardized.

## Declare certain studies to the DARSSS

Some studies must be declared to the DARSSS so that it can confirm the insurance coverage, or comment on it where needed. The DARSSS lists four kinds. Each applies when the insured institution is the study's sponsor, or acts as its sponsor, coordinator or academic research organization (ARO):

- The study recruits participating sites or participants outside Canada.
- The study involves pregnant women.
- The study is clinical research related to COVID-19.
- The study is fundamental research or an early clinical trial (Phase I or II) involving healthy participants.

The DARSSS also processes requests to extend or modify a declared study, and closes declared studies once they are completed.

Consider writing to the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) as soon as you know that your study falls into one of these kinds.

## Cover a study you sponsor yourself

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

For a study you sponsor yourself to be covered by the RI-MUHC's insurance policy, the RI-MUHC [should be named as the sponsor](/sops/cr-007#5-1-2) on the agreement, on any regulatory documents, such as Health Canada's no-objection letter or authorization, and on ClinicalTrials.gov. You keep all the sponsor-investigator's responsibilities for conducting the research under the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs).

For insurance purposes, [the ClinicalTrials.gov record should name "MUHC-RIMUHC" as the sponsor](/kb/governance/quality-assurance#register-an-investigator-initiated-trial-on-clinicaltrials-gov).

As the sponsor, [address the costs of treating trial participants for trial-related injuries](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#when-a-participant-is-injured), in line with the regulatory requirements that apply.

If you find a gap in the study's insurance coverage, consider asking the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) whether the study needs additional clinical trial insurance.

## Check the insurance and the agreement with a sponsor

When another party sponsors the study, the sponsor's insurance and the agreement's clauses also decide who bears which risks. If the applicable regulatory requirements call for it, [the sponsor should provide insurance or indemnify the investigator and the institution](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) against claims arising from the trial. Claims that arise from malpractice or negligence are the exception.

Where one is required, the insurance statement is one of the study's essential documents, filed by both the qualified investigator and the sponsor. The statement documents that compensation for study-related injury will be available, and that the sponsor has evidence of sufficient insurance for the duration of the study and any follow-up period.

The DARSSS validates research agreements and interprets how their terms apply, to help the network's institutions keep their research activities covered. The DARSSS also issues certificates of insurance for research activities in the network's institutions when formal agreements govern them.

The [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) negotiates the agreement, its indemnification and insurance clauses included. [Read the clauses of a clinical trial agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#read-the-clauses-of-a-clinical-trial-agreement) says what to check in each one.
