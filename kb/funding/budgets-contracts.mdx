Contracts and financial agreements are not administrative formalities that follow a research decision — they are part of how that decision becomes real. Getting these in place before submission is both a regulatory requirement and a protection for everyone involved. All contracts and agreements for studies conducted at the MUHC/RI-MUHC must be handled through the Research Agreements Office.

## Research contracts and agreements

A contract defines who is responsible for what, who bears risk if a participant is harmed, who owns the data, and what happens to study materials when the study ends. All new study contracts are submitted through <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>; once submitted, notify the contracts group at [contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).

Depending on the type of study, agreements may be required with one or more of the following:

| Agreement type | When required |
|---|---|
| Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator agreement | All industry-sponsored studies and most Sponsor-Investigator initiated studies. Covers conduct obligations, financial terms, publication rights, and data ownership. |
| CRO or ARO agreement | When a Contract Research Organization or Academic Research Organization (e.g., CIM) is involved. Duties transferred to the CRO must be documented in writing; the Sponsor retains overall responsibility for data quality and integrity. |
| Third-party agreements | External laboratories, imaging services, nursing agencies, or other service providers delegated protocol tasks. All must have a signed agreement before tasks begin. |
| Confidentiality Disclosure Agreement | When sharing sensitive study information with external collaborators — including collaborators at McGill University — before a full agreement is in place. |
| Data Transfer Agreement | When transferring participant data to or from external institutions or jurisdictions. Required by Quebec privacy law for personal health information. |

## Budget planning

The financial aspects of a study must be documented and agreed upon before submission. Research budgets at the MUHC must account for direct and indirect costs associated with the use of MUHC facilities and services. Financial agreements between the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI typically cover:

- Fees for the QI/PI, Sub-Investigators, and other research team members
- Payment per participant or per visit, and payment for participants who complete the study
- Reimbursement of expenses to participants (transportation, parking)
- Reimbursement of MUHC service departments — nursing, pharmacy, laboratory, radiology, and other clinical services used for research
- Costs for third-party service providers
- Costs of treatment of participants in the event of a trial-related injury
- Archiving costs — retention periods are 15 years for drug trials and 7 years for most other studies; negotiate before study start

### Institutional overhead

The RI-MUHC sets overhead on eligible direct costs in its Institutional Cost Recovery Policy (2026). The standard rate is 35 per cent, unless the funder or sponsor sets another rate. Researchers cannot waive, reduce or negotiate it themselves; any exception needs the prior written approval of the RI-MUHC's Chief Operating Officer. Ask the Pre-Awards Office which costs are eligible.

| Funding type | Overhead rate |
|---|---|
| Industry-funded clinical trials | 35% of all eligible direct costs |
| Tri-Agency grants (CIHR, NSERC, SSHRC) | No added overhead on the Tri-Agency portion, which the federal Research Support Fund covers; other portions, such as matching funds, carry the highest rate the funder allows |
| US National Institutes of Health (NIH) grants | 8% of direct costs, excluding equipment |
| Unrestricted donations and fundraising transfers from an MUHC foundation | 15% |
| Industry funds transferred through an MUHC foundation with a charitable receipt | 35% |
| Other funders and sponsors, such as charities and other foundations | The highest rate the funder allows, or 35% if it sets none |

Flag startup costs (initiation visits, site setup, initial regulatory submissions) separately when submitting to the Research Agreements Office, and ask the Pre-Awards Office whether they count as eligible direct costs.

### REB fees

In Quebec, the **MSSS** (Ministère de la Santé et des Services sociaux) sets the REB and authorization fees for studies funded wholly or partly by private enterprise; they apply across all RSSS institutions, including the RI-MUHC. Fees are indexed annually on April 1 against the Quebec Pension Plan pension index. The current schedule is effective **April 1, 2026** (2% index applied).

| Fee item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Scientific review | \$647 |
| Ethical review | \$6,475 |
| Site authorization | \$1,942 per institution |
| Annual ethics monitoring | \$647 × sites (max 10) per renewal |
| Annual authorization monitoring | \$647 per institution per renewal |
| Major protocol amendment | \$647 per amendment |

These fees apply to studies funded wholly or partly by private enterprise, including investigator-initiated trials with private or mixed funding, and are **non-refundable**; each review is billed whatever the REB decides. The MUHC REB counts any private-law body as a private enterprise, not-for-profit organizations included. The institution may choose not to bill when the private funding is $19,420 or less.

### Staff cost structure

Staff costs at the RI-MUHC are calculated using institutional salary scales. The key parameters:

- **1 FTE** = 1,820 hours per year (35 hours/week × 52 weeks)
- **Benefits** = 19% of base salary
- **Cost to study** = (base salary × 1.19) × % FTE allocated
- **Multi-year studies** = 5% annual cost-of-living increase, compounded year over year

### Contingency

A contingency buffer is recommended for all study budgets. Scale by study complexity:

| Complexity | Range | Typical study types |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 8–10% | Observational, retrospective, simple cross-sectional |
| Moderate | 10–12% | Prospective interventional, Phase II–III, multi-visit |
| High | 12–15% | Phase I, first-in-human, complex interventional |

Contingency is applied to the staff and services subtotal, before overhead and REB fees.

For industry-sponsored studies, the budget must also be negotiated with the sponsor before the Site Agreement is signed. Once you have the institutional parameters above, use the negotiation guide to work through the sponsor's proposed budget line by line.
