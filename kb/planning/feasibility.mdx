Before the site and the research team agree to conduct a study, they assess whether it is feasible: scientifically, operationally and financially. Feasibility costs least to check at the design stage, before the protocol is final and before contracts or budgets are negotiated. A study that passes ethics review but cannot enrol participants, or that needs equipment the site doesn't have, stalls at activation.

Feasibility assumes the project is research. If you aren't sure that your project needs the Research Ethics Board's (REB) review, check with [Is It Research?](/is-it-research) first: quality assurance and quality improvement projects follow a different path. [My Study Roadmap](/my-roadmap) gives a personalized startup checklist for your study.

## Before the Site Feasibility Visit

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

[The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-004#3-0-responsibilities) make the sponsor or <Tooltip tip="An individual who both initiates and conducts, alone or with others, a clinical trial, and under whose immediate direction the investigational product is administered to, dispensed to or used by a participant." headline="Sponsor-Investigator" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sponsor-investigator">sponsor-investigator</Tooltip> and the principal investigator responsible for ensuring that the study's feasibility meets all the applicable regulatory, good clinical practice, sponsor and local requirements.

When a sponsor initiates a study, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs require](/sops/cr-102#1-1-1) a confidentiality agreement between the sponsor, the institution and the investigator, signed and dated before the team receives confidential study documents, such as the protocol. The institution requires the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) to [review and approve the agreement](/sops/cr-102#1-1-2) before it is signed. Once it is signed, the investigator must submit it in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic platform through which studies conducted at the MUHC are submitted for review and managed, from the initial submission to the study completion report." headline="Nagano" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>. A copy of the signed and dated agreement should be kept with the essential study documents.

When a sponsor-investigator brings a study to the site, a confidentiality agreement between the sponsor-investigator and the principal investigator [should be signed and dated](/sops/cr-004#5-1-1) before any confidential study information, such as the protocol, is received. A legal advisor often needs to review a confidentiality agreement before it is signed.

Once you have the protocol, assess its feasibility to find out what the site will need. [The issues to consider](/sops/cr-004#5-1-2) may include, among others:

- Whether the protocol is technically and ethically feasible.
- Whether it is compatible with local health care practice.
- Access to the target population, for screening and enrolment.
- The time and resources the study needs.
- Whether the research team and the support services are available and committed.

The site should conduct this preliminary assessment with the Study Feasibility Checklist, in preparation for the sponsor's or sponsor-investigator's Site Feasibility Visit (SFV). [Feasibility checklist](#feasibility-checklist) gives what it covers.

Secure, adequate and temperature-controlled space for storing the investigational products, under the right conditions, should be identified. A qualified person responsible for managing the investigational products, and procedures for their destruction where required, must be available. The facilities must be adequate for every procedure of the protocol and have secure space for the site's data management.

<Warning>
The sponsor or sponsor-investigator is to ensure that all electronic software used in the study has been validated and complies with 21 CFR Part 11. The study team should contact IT through "IT rescue" on the RI-MUHC Portal to have any study-specific software evaluated and any connectivity issues assessed.
</Warning>

## The Site Feasibility Visit

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

The sponsor, the sponsor-investigator or a contract research organization conducts the Site Feasibility Visit (SFV). The visit checks that the principal investigator has access to the right study population, the right facilities and equipment, and qualified, experienced staff to enrol the number of participants the protocol asks for within its timeframe.

When the sponsor asks for a visit, the <Tooltip tip="A nurse, health professional or other qualified member of the research team who handles most of the administrative responsibilities and day-to-day activities of a clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">clinical research coordinator</Tooltip>, or a delegate, does these tasks:

- Compiles the information the sponsor asks for.
- Helps the principal investigator complete the Study Feasibility Checklist, so that every aspect of feasibility is assessed before the site agrees to take part.
- Organizes the visit's meeting and makes sure its follow-up requirements are met.

During the visit, [the sponsor, the sponsor-investigator or the monitor may assess](/sops/cr-004#5-3-4), among other things:

- The qualifications and experience of the principal investigator and the sub-investigators.
- Whether the research team and the supporting departments and service providers can conduct the study.
- The team's knowledge of the therapeutic area, <Tooltip tip="A standard for the design, conduct, performance, monitoring, auditing, recording, analysis and reporting of clinical studies that provides assurance that the data and reported results are credible and accurate, and that the rights, integrity and confidentiality of participants are protected." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">good clinical practice</Tooltip>, Health Canada's regulations, the *Tri-Council Policy Statement* and, where they apply, the US Food and Drug Administration's (FDA) regulations.
- Whether the team members are available and qualified.
- Whether a third party or vendor is needed, such as an external nursing agency for nights.
- The investigator's workload and competing studies.
- The population that may be eligible, including how many people must be screened to reach the site's enrolment target.
- How the team communicates and responds, such as returning calls and providing documents on time.
- The site's procedures for managing clinical research, its SOPs and work instructions.
- The documents that confirm training.
- Whether the site can meet the study's timelines.
- How confidential records are stored.

Ensure that the documents or information that support these points are available at the visit. Once the visit is done and the study is judged feasible at the site, a signed and dated copy of the agreement must be kept with the study's essential documents.

Sponsor-investigators and monitors [should refer to SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-004#5-3-2) for a report template to document the visit. Once the report is completed, a copy should be placed in the trial master file.

## Feasibility checklist

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

The Study Feasibility Checklist ([SOP-CR-004, Appendix 1](/sops/cr-004#appendix-1)) is the site's own assessment of a study. The site should complete it in preparation for the Site Feasibility Visit, and before it agrees to take part. Each part asks the site to confirm or evaluate a list of points:

| Part | What the site checks |
|---|---|
| 1. Scientific, technical, safety and ethical aspects of the protocol | The protocol has been evaluated for scientific merit and is technically feasible. It is compatible with the medical field, local and federal authorities, international good clinical practice guidelines and the site's requirements, and with local ethical practices. Its eligibility criteria are realistic and well defined. Its safety reporting and its schedule of procedures are well defined. If needed, the comparator drug is available in the region. |
| 2. Participants | The target population is at the site. The eligibility criteria raise no foreseeable enrolment issues. Competing studies at the site have been evaluated. The number of participants available to recruit within the time limits is confirmed, from medical files or a computerized listing. Participants available outside the site, through advertising, have been estimated if needed, and so has potential participants' willingness to take part given the protocol's requirements. The period and frequency of treatments or tests are acceptable. |
| 3. Personnel availability | The principal investigator has the therapeutic expertise and research experience to oversee the protocol, and RI-MUHC Privileges and training at a level suited to the study. The team is qualified and experienced, with valid RI-MUHC training for the protocol. The principal investigator or a sub-investigator has time to see and treat the patients and to document medical oversight, such as initialling and dating medically important study documents and safety reports. The principal investigator or a delegate has time to supervise the team, to produce, review and submit the study data, and to deal with the sponsor. The principal investigator has time to review the data's quality control, such as monitoring letters, and to keep evaluating the team's training and the delegation of tasks. |
| 4. Resources | The delegations of authority the study needs, and the staff available for the study's duration. Other technical and professional staff available, such as specialist physicians or teams on rotation. Third parties available, such as external facilities for specialized tests not done at the MUHC. The budget, for the team's pay. |
| 5. Facilities and equipment | Space for the team, for recruiting participants, for procedures and follow-up, for storing participants' study records securely and for storing study supplies securely. Clinical supplies and maintained medical equipment for the protocol. Access to emergency services. Secure, temperature-controlled space for the investigational product, in the pharmacy or elsewhere. Local laboratories and other services, such as the operating room, ophthalmology, radiology and respirology, compatible with the study, with written agreements with other site services. Space for monitoring. The IT infrastructure for an electronic case report form or other electronic system, where one is used. |
| 6. Financial resources | The costs of research team training, study advertising, participant compensation, any equipment to buy, and the MUHC's pharmacy, laboratory, radiology, medical records and other services. The costs of the Centre for Innovative Medicine's (CIM) services for sponsor-investigators, such as project management, data management or monitoring, and for investigators, such as study submission, coordinators, nurses and respiratory therapists. The costs of the CIM's platforms, such as specimen collection, processing and shipping, imaging, electrocardiograms, exercise physiology, pulmonary tests, the sleep lab, apheresis, bronchoscopy and endoscopy, the operating room, 3D printing, simulation and ultrasound. Clinical research space. Archiving services, such as Iron Mountain, for 15 or 7 years ([Study Close-Out](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving) gives the retention periods). Institutional overhead and the REB's study review fees. Start-up costs. |
| 7. Others | Any other point the site specifies. |

Of these parts, personnel availability is the one most often underestimated: check both that qualified staff exist and that each person has time set aside for each task. [Study Budgets](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) gives the institutional overhead rates by source of funding and the REB fees set by Quebec's health ministry.

## Budget and financial agreements

The budget is negotiated after the feasibility assessment and before the site agreement is signed. The principal investigator is responsible for the budget and its financial review. They negotiate it, respecting the rates the hospital's departments set, and they approve it.

No institutional office reviews the budget. For a study the CIM runs, the CIM's budget specialists review it before the principal investigator approves it. For an industry-sponsored study, the RI-MUHC offers guidance, such as a budget tool. Once agreed, the budget is written into the agreement. The Research Agreements Office reviews the agreement's terms to protect the researcher, and it does not negotiate or approve the budget.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-004#5-2-1) say the site must be aware of the financial implications of any research it takes part in. The agreements may set the financial terms, such as the fees for the team, the payment per participant or visit, the payment schedule, the reimbursement of participants' expenses and of supporting departments.

[Ensure that adequate funding for long-term storage](/sops/cr-015#5-3-2) of the study's essential documents is negotiated in the study budget before the study starts.

<Note>
  In the good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), the financial aspects of a trial [should be documented in an agreement between the sponsor and the investigator or institution](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html).

  Give [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) information on the study's budget and funding sources, as applicable, when you submit the study for review. When a study is funded in whole or in part by private enterprise, also include the private enterprises' contact information and the budgets with the submission.
</Note>

REB fees can affect whether a study is financially viable. Under directive 2023-016 of Quebec's health ministry, the REB's review and the authorization fees are billed to the private enterprise that funds a study, wholly or partly. The MUHC REB counts any body governed by private law as a private enterprise, not-for-profit organizations included, so funding from a private foundation can make a study billable. If your study is in this case, its budget must account for these fees before the site agreement is signed. [Study Budgets](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts#budget-the-reb-and-authorization-fees) gives the fee schedule and what to confirm with your sponsor before you submit.

In the REB submission form, identify the study's source of funding, or select the option for a study with no funding. If you don't give the billing contact information when you submit, you must follow up with the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) or the RI-MUHC's finance department.

## What happens next

Once feasibility is confirmed and the site has agreed to take part, the study moves on to its submission, its agreement and its initiation. [SOP-CR-007](/sops/cr-007) covers the submission, the agreement and MUHC Authorization, and [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005) covers site initiation.

The study is submitted in Nagano for the REB's review and <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, which the Personne mandatée issues once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>. Where the study needs Health Canada's authorization (a Clinical Trial Application for a drug or natural health product, or an Investigational Testing Authorization for a device), the RI-MUHC's SOPs have the sponsor obtain it first and send its letter to the site, which includes it in the initial Nagano submission. The order can differ for natural health products and some devices.

[All agreements for studies conducted at the MUHC must be drafted](/sops/cr-007#5-1-2) by the RI-MUHC's [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca). To start the office's review, upload the research agreement to both Nagano and My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal. [Researchers should never sign a binding contract or agreement](/sops/cr-007#5-4-6) before the Research Agreements Office has reviewed it. Investigators are not to sign on behalf of the RI-MUHC.

The clinical trial agreement, or its equivalent, can be negotiated before the submission or during the reviews. It can be signed once the REB has approved the study, even if other feasibility reviews are still under way, and it must be fully executed before MUHC Authorization. [Research Agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#sign-the-agreement-at-the-right-time) explains the timing.

In a study with a sponsor or a sponsor-investigator, once all approvals are in place, the sponsor or sponsor-investigator schedules the Site Initiation Visit (SIV) to train the team, confirm that the site is ready and authorize the start of enrolment.

## Canadian clinical trial networks

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

Joining an established trial network can speed up site activation, give access to shared protocols and funding, and lighten the work of setting up a study. These networks are relevant to RI-MUHC investigators:

| Network | Focus | What it offers |
|---|---|---|
| [**3CTN**](https://3ctn.ca/) (Canadian Cancer Clinical Trials Network) | Oncology | National infrastructure for cancer trials, and an equity, diversity and inclusion tool kit. |
| [**MICYRN**](https://www.micyrn.ca/) (Maternal Infant Child and Youth Research Network) | Pediatrics and maternal health | A national network of maternal and child health research organizations, the RI-MUHC among them. It runs KidsCAN Trials and promotes the uptake of the SPIRIT-C 2026 and CONSORT-C 2026 reporting guidelines for pediatric trials. |
| [**KidsCAN Trials**](https://www.micyrn.ca/ipct) | Medicines for children | A MICYRN program, and a national hub to coordinate research, training and knowledge transfer in the safe therapeutic use of medicines for children. |
| [**PERC**](https://perc-canada.ca/) (Pediatric Emergency Research Canada) | Pediatric emergency medicine | A network of health care researchers at 15 Canadian children's hospitals, the Montreal Children's Hospital among them, for multi-centre research in pediatric emergency medicine. |
| [**CTN+**](https://www.ctnplus.ca/) (CIHR Pan-Canadian Network for HIV and STBBI Clinical Trials Research) | HIV, sexually transmitted and blood-borne infections | A funded network with shared protocols, and access to existing cohorts and infrastructure. |
| [**N2 Canada**](https://n2canada.ca/) (Network of Networks) | Clinical research capacity across Canada | An alliance of Canadian research networks and organizations, offering standard operating procedures, education programs, a Permission to Contact Toolkit and Best Practice Guidelines. |
| [**Clinical Trials Ontario (CTO)**](https://ctontario.ca/) | All study types in Ontario | CTO Stream, a single ethics review by one CTO Qualified REB for all Ontario sites of a multi-site study. Bilingual consent templates and an REB Qualification Program. |
| [**CATALIS Quebec**](https://catalisquebec.com/en/) | All study types in Quebec | Quebec infrastructure optimization, the Provincial Model Clinical Trial Agreement, and standard tools and training. |

The RI-MUHC has an institutional agreement with CATALIS Quebec. If your study is part of a multi-site Quebec network or uses shared CATALIS tools, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) what the agreement covers and whether other institutional authorization steps are needed.

For pediatric studies at the Montreal Children's Hospital, part of the Glen site, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) early in the design about opportunities with MICYRN and PERC. Joining a network can affect where the ethics review takes place, what the budget is expected to cover and how long protocol development takes.
