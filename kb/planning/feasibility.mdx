Before agreeing to conduct a study at The Institute, the site and research team must assess whether it is feasible — scientifically, operationally, and financially. Feasibility is cheapest to evaluate at the design stage, before the protocol is finalized and before contracts or budgets are negotiated. A study that passes ethics review but cannot enrol participants, or requires equipment the site does not have, will stall at activation.

<Tip>
  If you haven't yet connected with the Research Facilitator about your planned study, complete the [Clinical Research Intake questionnaire](/kb/planning/planning-overview) first. It produces a personalized startup checklist and connects you with the right support.
</Tip>

<Note>
  Feasibility presumes this is research. If you are not yet sure whether your project requires REB review at all, run it through the [Is It Research?](/is-it-research) screening tool first — QA/QI projects follow a different path.
</Note>

## Before the Site Feasibility Visit

When a Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator approaches the site with a study, the first step is to sign a confidentiality agreement before receiving any confidential study documents, including the protocol. Legal review of the agreement is often required before signing.

Once you have the protocol, conduct a preliminary self-assessment before the Sponsor's Site Feasibility Visit (SFV). The main questions to consider are:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Science and ethics" icon="microscope">
    Is the protocol scientifically and ethically sound, and compatible with local healthcare practice?
  </Card>
  <Card title="Patient population" icon="users">
    Does The Institute have access to the target patient population within the required timeline?
  </Card>
  <Card title="Team capacity" icon="user-group">
    Does the team have the time, qualifications, and experience to conduct this study?
  </Card>
  <Card title="Facilities and equipment" icon="building">
    Does the site have the required infrastructure, storage, and equipment?
  </Card>
  <Card title="Financial viability" icon="dollar-sign">
    Are the proposed budget and payment terms acceptable to the institution?
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  Any electronic software used in the study must be validated and compliant with 21 CFR Part 11. Contact IT via "IT rescue" on the RI-MUHC Portal to evaluate study-specific software before the SFV.
</Warning>

Use the **Study Feasibility Checklist** (Appendix 1 of [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004)) to structure this assessment and bring it to the SFV.

## The Site Feasibility Visit

The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator conducts the Site Feasibility Visit (SFV) to evaluate whether the site has the right population, facilities, equipment, and team to meet the study's requirements within the required timeline.

The <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#crc">Clinical Research Coordinator</Tooltip> (or delegate) is responsible for compiling the information requested by the Sponsor, assisting the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI with completing the Feasibility Checklist, facilitating the SFV meeting, and ensuring all follow-up requirements are met.

During the SFV, the Sponsor will typically assess:

- QI/PI and Sub-Investigator qualifications and experience
- Team knowledge of <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>, Health Canada regulations, and TCPS2
- Availability of team members and supporting departments
- Patient population available for screening and enrolment
- Ability to meet study timelines
- Secure storage for records and investigational products

<Note>
  Before the SFV, ensure secure, temperature-controlled storage for investigational products has been identified, and that a qualified person responsible for <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> management is named.
</Note>

## Feasibility checklist

The Study Feasibility Checklist (Appendix 1 of [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004)) must be completed before any feasibility assessment. It covers protocol review, site and team capacity, ethical considerations, and financial terms.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Part 1 — Scientific, technical, safety, and ethical aspects">
    Confirm the protocol has been evaluated for scientific merit and is technically feasible. Assess compatibility with the medical field, local and federal authorities, ICH GCP guidelines, and site requirements. Verify that eligibility criteria are realistic and well defined, that safety reporting requirements are clear, and that the schedule of procedures is complete. If a comparator drug is required, confirm it is available in Quebec.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Part 2 — Participant population">
    Confirm the target population is present at your site. Review the eligibility criteria and identify whether any criteria will create enrolment barriers in your patient population. **Check for competing studies** — other trials at your site recruiting from the same population will reduce your available pool. Confirm the number of potentially eligible participants using medical files or an OACIS query before committing to an enrolment target. If advertising or off-site recruitment is required, estimate its feasibility separately.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Part 3 — Personnel availability">
    This is the section most commonly underestimated. Assess not just whether qualified staff exist, but whether they have **dedicated time** for each obligation:

    - **QI/PI**: time to see and treat study participants; time to document medical oversight (initialling and dating medically important study documents and safety reports); time to supervise the team; time to review and submit study data; time to review data QC (e.g., monitoring letters); time to interact with the Sponsor; time for ongoing evaluation of team training and task delegation
    - **Sub-investigators**: availability for delegated medical assessments and co-signature requirements
    - **CRC/delegate**: availability to generate, review, and submit study data; time to interact with the Sponsor

    Confirm the QI/PI holds RI-MUHC Privileges appropriate for the study level and that the team's training is current and valid for this protocol.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Part 4 — Resources">
    Evaluate delegations of authority required for this study — confirm all tasks can be covered by qualified team members, accounting for the full study duration. Assess whether additional technical or professional personnel are needed (e.g., MD Specialists or Teams on Rotation under SOP-CR-002 Appendix 2/3). Identify any 3rd-party requirements — external testing facilities for specialized tests not conducted at the MUHC — and confirm access before committing to the protocol.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Part 5 — Facilities and equipment">
    Assess working space for the team, dedicated space for participant procedures and follow-up, secure storage for study records, and secure storage for study materials and supplies. Confirm access to emergency services. Verify that a secure, temperature-controlled space for the <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ip">investigational product</Tooltip> is available (pharmacy or equivalent). Check compatibility of local laboratories and other MUHC services with study requirements (OR, Ophthalmology, Radiology, Respirology). Confirm space is available for monitoring activities.

    **IT infrastructure:** If the study uses an eCRF or other electronic system, contact IT via "IT rescue" on the RI-MUHC Portal to evaluate study-specific software and assess connectivity requirements before the SFV. Electronic systems must be validated and compliant with 21 CFR Part 11.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Part 6 — Financial resources">
    Budget for all applicable cost categories before agreeing to participate. The checklist covers:

    - Research team training and study advertising
    - Participant compensation
    - Equipment purchases
    - MUHC service fees: Pharmacy, Laboratory, Radiology, Medical Records, and other applicable departments
    - **CIM services** — Sponsor-Investigator services (project management, data management, monitoring); Investigator services (study submission, CRCs, nurses, RTs); Platform services (specimen processing/shipping, imaging, ECG, exercise physiology, pulmonary, sleep lab, apheresis, bronchoscopy, OR, simulation)
    - Clinical research space
    - **Archiving services** — Iron Mountain or equivalent, budgeted for 15 years (regulated studies) or 7 years (non-regulated studies)
    - Institutional overhead and <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> study review fees
    - Start-up costs

    See [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) for the institutional overhead rates by funding source and the full MSSS REB fee schedule.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Budget and financial agreements

The budget negotiation happens after the feasibility assessment and before the site agreement is signed. The Finance team at RI-MUHC must review and approve the budget before any agreement is executed.

<Note>
  Health Canada Division 5 and ICH E6(R3) require that financial arrangements and any payments to investigators be documented in the study agreement and disclosed to the REB as required.
</Note>

**REB billing fees are a financial viability consideration.** Under Directive ministérielle 2023-016, REB review and authorization fees are billed to the Sponsor for any study funded by a private enterprise — a category that now includes foundation funding (such as the MUHC Foundation or other private foundations), not just industry. If your study falls into this category, the Sponsor's budget must account for these fees before the site agreement is signed. See [Study Budgets and Agreements](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) for the full fee schedule and what to confirm with your Sponsor before submitting.

## What happens next

Once feasibility is confirmed and the site has agreed to participate, the study moves into initiation. The sequence below is governed by [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign the Site Agreement">
    The Clinical Trial Agreement (or equivalent) is negotiated and executed between the Sponsor and the institution. Finance must review and approve the final budget before signing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the Nagano submission">
    The study is submitted via Nagano for REB review and <Tooltip tip="Institutional authorization allowing a study to proceed at the RI-MUHC. Issued after ethics approval and feasibility review." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>. Health Canada submission (<Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">CTA</Tooltip> or <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip>) runs in parallel if required for the study type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare for the Site Initiation Visit">
    Once all approvals are in place, the Sponsor schedules the Site Initiation Visit (SIV) to train the team, confirm site readiness, and authorize the start of enrolment.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Canadian clinical trial networks

Joining an established trial network can accelerate site activation, provide access to shared protocols and funding, and reduce the burden of study setup. The following networks are relevant to RI-MUHC investigators.

| Network | Focus | What it offers |
|---|---|---|
| [**3CTN**](https://3ctn.ca/) (Canadian Cancer Clinical Trials Network) | Oncology | National infrastructure for cancer trials; site accreditation; EDI framework; access to industry partnerships |
| [**MICYRN**](https://www.micyrn.ca/) (Maternal Infant Child Youth Research Network) | Pediatrics and maternal health | Network of Canadian pediatric research sites; developed SPIRIT-C 2026 and CONSORT-C 2026 pediatric reporting guidelines; supports KidsCAN trials |
| [**KidsCAN**](https://www.micyrn.ca/clinical-trial-initiatives) (Pediatric Cancer and Blood Disorders) | Pediatric oncology and hematology | Collaborative investigator-initiated trial platform through MICYRN |
| [**PERC**](https://perc-canada.ca/) (Pediatric Emergency Research Canada) | Pediatric emergency medicine | National network for ED-based pediatric studies; established infrastructure and shared protocols |
| [**CHEER**](https://cheerchildhealth.ca/) (Child Health Evidence and Evaluation for Research) | Pediatric health systems and evaluation | Multi-provincial pediatric research; supports MICYRN/CHEER single-REB pilot across provinces |
| [**CTN+**](https://www.ctnplus.ca/) (Canadian Trials Network) | HIV, sexually transmitted and blood-borne infections | Funded network with shared protocols; access to existing cohorts and infrastructure |
| [**N2 Canada**](https://n2canada.ca/) (National Neurosurgery Trials Network) | Neurosurgery and neurocritical care | Collaborative RCT platform for neurosurgical indications |
| [**Clinical Trials Ontario (CTO)**](https://ctontario.ca/) | All study types in Ontario | CTO Stream: single-REB review for multi-site Ontario studies; bilingual consent templates; site qualification program |
| [**CATALIS Quebec**](https://catalisquebec.com/en/) | All study types in Quebec | Quebec infrastructure optimization; provincial Master CTA; standard tools and training |

<Note>
RI-MUHC has an institutional agreement with CATALIS Quebec. If your study is part of a multi-site Quebec network or uses shared CATALIS tools, contact the [Research Facilitator](mailto:Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) to confirm what the agreement covers and whether additional institutional authorization steps are required.
</Note>

For pediatric studies at the Montreal Children's Hospital (part of the Glen site), ask the Research Facilitator about applicable MICYRN, PERC, and CHEER network opportunities early in the design phase — network affiliation can affect ethics review routing, budget expectations, and protocol development timelines.
