When you both initiate and conduct a drug trial that Health Canada regulates under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, you are its sponsor. Health Canada's regulations don't differentiate between a commercial sponsor and a sponsor-investigator, and the same requirements apply. The steps below set up an investigator-initiated trial (IIT) of a drug. [Sponsor-Investigator Studies](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator) explains the role as a whole.

As the sponsor, you apply to Health Canada for authorization, report serious unexpected adverse drug reactions to it, ensure that every site is monitored and keep the trial's records for the period the regulations set. Before you file the application, consider checking that your team and the institution can support these obligations.

## Set up the trial, step by step

### 1. Develop the protocol and the Investigator Brochure

Develop the protocol and the <Tooltip tip="Compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip>, under [SOP-CR-102](/sops/cr-102). The application to Health Canada must contain both, among other documents. For a drug marketed in Canada, [a reference to the most recent Canadian product monograph](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/clinical-trials/clinical-trial-sponsors-applications.html#a232) and its version date may be submitted if an updated brochure is not available.

As the sponsor-investigator, you should find out whether the holder of the product's licence or marketing authorization has a brochure available. Where regulators permit it, a basic product information brochure, such as the product's labelling, may be an appropriate alternative, if it includes current, comprehensive and detailed information on every aspect of the product that might matter to the investigator.

### 2. Check the insurance

For the RI-MUHC's insurance to cover a study you sponsor yourself, the RI-MUHC should be named as the sponsor on the agreement, on regulatory documents such as Health Canada's No Objection Letter and on ClinicalTrials.gov. [Research Agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#cover-the-study-as-its-sponsor) explains it.

Physicians are covered by the Canadian Medical Protective Association (CMPA). [The CMPA generally assists only with civil actions arising in Canada for medical professional work related to research performed in Canada](https://www.cmpa-acpm.ca/en/membership/protection-for-members/principles-of-assistance/clinical-research). The CMPA does not generally assist with civil legal matters arising from research with healthy volunteers.

The CMPA does not generally assist with civil legal matters where the allegations arise from the design, delivery or outcome of the research. CMPA members are expected to turn to the research sponsor for indemnification for those allegations. Where there is no sponsor, the member acting as an independent researcher assumes liability for civil actions related to the design, delivery or outcome of the research.

Confirm with the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) that the sponsor's liability is covered, and arrange additional clinical trial insurance if there is a gap. Industry sponsors carry their own insurance, so this gap is common for investigator-initiated trials.

### 3. Apply to Health Canada

File the <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip> with Health Canada, under [SOP-CR-018](/sops/cr-018). Health Canada first screens the application for completeness. You may sell or import the drug for the trial at a site once all of these conditions are met:

- Health Canada has not sent you a notice of objection within 30 days after it received the application.
- The site's Research Ethics Board (REB) has approved the protocol and the informed consent form.
- Health Canada has the site's information, if you didn't give it with the application.

Health Canada typically issues a <Tooltip tip="Letter from Health Canada confirming a CTA is satisfactory and authorizing the sponsor to proceed." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nol">No Objection Letter (NOL)</Tooltip> when it raises no objection.

[The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-007#5-1-1) have the sponsor obtain Health Canada's approval first, for a trial of a new drug in Phases I to III, or in Phase IV with conditions. The sponsor should then forward a copy of the No Objection Letter to the investigators, to be filed in their investigator site file and submitted to the REB. Do not begin any trial activity before you receive the No Objection Letter.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.

### 5. Set up the safety reporting

As the sponsor, you must inform Health Canada of every serious unexpected adverse drug reaction, inside or outside Canada, within [its deadlines](#meet-the-sponsor-obligations-once-the-trial-starts). You must also keep records of all adverse events with the drug, inside or outside Canada. Before the first participant is enrolled, consider setting up these systems:

- A way to report serious unexpected adverse drug reactions to Health Canada within its deadlines. The sponsor-investigator [reports them on a Council for International Organizations of Medical Sciences (CIOMS) I form](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#know-who-reports-an-sae-to-health-canada).
- A safety database or another structured way to keep records of every adverse event, at every site.

Reports of serious unexpected adverse reactions to investigators, their institutions and the REBs [should be made in a manner that reflects how urgently action is needed](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) and that takes into account the evolving knowledge of the product's safety profile, in accordance with the applicable regulatory requirements.

## Plan the safety monitoring

The [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html) requires researchers to give the REB an acceptable plan for monitoring safety, efficacy or effectiveness (where feasible) and validity. The plan must describe:

- How participant safety will be monitored, and what will be done if it is threatened.
- How the intervention's efficacy will be monitored, where feasible, and what will be done if it is greater than expected.
- The criteria for removing participants from the study for safety reasons.
- The study-wide stopping rules, if any, by which the study may be stopped or amended for inferior safety, superior efficacy or futility.
- How information relevant to participants' welfare or consent will be reported clearly and promptly to the REB.

The REB assesses whether the plan addresses the study's foreseeable risks and potential benefits. In multi-site research, when new information at one site could be relevant to participant welfare and consent at other sites, principal investigators must ensure that it is shared with researchers at each site and with the REBs. The plan may include an independent data and safety monitoring board (DSMB), but doesn't have to.

Not every trial needs an independent DSMB. TCPS 2 says the following factors should be considered when deciding whether a study should have one:

- How great the foreseeable harms of the research to participants are.
- Whether the participants' circumstances make them vulnerable in the context of research.
- Whether interim analysis of the data is feasible.
- How complex the study is.
- Conflicts of interest.

A study whose possible outcomes for participants are severe, such as irreversible harm or death, is more likely to need an independent DSMB and stopping rules. Studies with several sites, blinded data, long-term data collection or many participants may need one whether or not they have study-wide stopping rules. A single-site study with unblinded data collection and clear stopping rules may not. [The good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) also lets the sponsor consider an independent data monitoring committee to assess the trial's progress and recommend whether to continue, modify or stop it.

An independent DSMB brings together people with the relevant scientific, ethical or community expertise, who are not involved in the trial's conduct. A charter usually describes its duties, which may include applying stopping rules and recommending changes to the trial. In a blinded study, it can see the unblinded data to make its recommendations. Consider including at least one biostatistician and one clinician with relevant expertise.

## Meet the sponsor obligations once the trial starts

Once the trial is under way, the sponsor-investigator is responsible for these obligations to Health Canada:

| Obligation | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Reporting serious unexpected adverse drug reactions | Inform Health Canada of each one, whether it occurred inside or outside Canada: within 15 days after becoming aware of it, or within 7 days if it is fatal or life-threatening. For a fatal or life-threatening reaction, submit a complete report within 8 days after informing Health Canada, with an assessment of the importance and implication of the findings. |
| Safety reports on request | Health Canada asks for a [Development Safety Update Report (DSUR)](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#development-safety-update-report-dsur) only on request. |
| Information Health Canada requests | Submit information on the drug or the trial, or samples of the drug, within 2 days after receiving Health Canada's request, when Health Canada has the reasons the regulations list, such as a risk to the health of a participant. Submit any information or records kept for the trial, or samples of the drug, within 7 days after receiving a request made to assess the drug's safety or the health of participants or other persons. |
| Amendments that need authorization | Amendments to the protocol that affect the selection, monitoring or dismissal of participants, the evaluation of the drug's efficacy, the risk to participants' health or the drug's safety evaluation, amendments that extend the trial, and changes to the chemistry and manufacturing information that may affect the drug's safety or quality need Health Canada's authorization. You may proceed if Health Canada sends no objection within 30 days after receiving the application, and once you have sent Health Canada, for each site, the name and contact information of the REB that approved the amended protocol. [Study Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#get-health-canada-authorization-for-a-drug-trial-amendment) gives every condition. |
| Amendments made immediately | If the trial endangers the health of a participant or another person, an amendment that needs authorization may be made immediately, with its information sent to Health Canada within 15 days after the amendment. |
| Changes that need only a notice | Other changes to the protocol that do not alter the risk to participants' health, and other changes to the chemistry and manufacturing information that do not affect the drug's quality or safety, need only a written notice to Health Canada within 15 days after the change. |
| Stopping the trial | If you stop the trial, in its entirety or at a site, [inform Health Canada and every qualified investigator](/kb/close-out/close-out#tell-health-canada-that-the-study-has-ended) within the deadlines the regulations set. |
| Monitoring | Ensure that every site continues to be monitored for compliance with the protocol, data integrity and participant safety. |

## Funding considerations

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span></div>

The Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR) requires the following of the clinical trials it funds:

- Registration in a publicly available, free, searchable registry that meets the World Health Organization's standards. The RI-MUHC's SOPs require registration before participant recruitment begins.
- For grants funded from Jan. 1, 2022, publications of the results open access from the date of publication, summary results public by [CIHR's deadline](/kb/close-out/publications#cihr-funded-trials) and the registration number in every publication's abstract.

Nominated principal investigators who receive CIHR grant funds for clinical trial research after Jan. 1, 2022, must meet these requirements to remain eligible for any new CIHR funding.

The registry's name, its web address and the registration number [must appear in the REB-approved consent form](/sops/cr-007#5-8-5).
