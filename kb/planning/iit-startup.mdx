## Set up the trial, step by step

### 1. Develop the protocol and the Investigator Brochure

Develop the protocol under [SOP-CR-102](/sops/cr-102). You are responsible for ensuring that it is [written in compliance with all the applicable requirements](/sops/cr-102#responsibilities): regulatory, good clinical practice, sponsor and local.

Health Canada's application for a drug or a natural health product trial must contain an <Tooltip tip="A compilation of the clinical and nonclinical data on an investigational product that are relevant to its study in human participants." headline="Investigator's Brochure (IB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ib">Investigator's Brochure</Tooltip>, and an application for a Class III or IV device should include one. What each product may use in its place is in its section: [drug](#apply-to-health-canada-for-a-drug-trial), [natural health product](#apply-to-health-canada-for-a-natural-health-product-trial) or [device](#get-the-ita-through-the-manufacturer).

### 2. Check the insurance

For the RI-MUHC's insurance to cover a study you sponsor yourself, the RI-MUHC [should be named as the sponsor](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#cover-a-study-you-sponsor-yourself) on the agreement, on regulatory documents such as Health Canada's No Objection Letter and on ClinicalTrials.gov.

The MUHC's [*Regulatory Framework for Clinical Research at Santé Québec – McGill University Health Centre: Organization and Good Practices*](https://rimuhc.ca/conduct-research/policies#framework) requires the principal investigator to [ensure that adequate liability insurance coverage is held](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#meet-the-insurance-duties-of-the-principal-investigator), including the institutional coverage that applies to research activities. Physicians are covered by the Canadian Medical Protective Association (CMPA), [within the limits of its assistance](/kb/funding/research-liability-insurance#physicians-and-the-cmpa).

[Every agreement for a study conducted at the MUHC or the RI-MUHC must be drafted by the Research Agreements Office](/sops/cr-007#5-1-2). As the sponsor, secure an agreement with every party involved. Ensure direct access to the <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's or sponsor-investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines across all the study's sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">trial master file</Tooltip> and the <Tooltip tip="The qualified or principal investigator's collection of study documentation that demonstrates compliance with regulations and guidelines, so that the conduct and integrity of the study and the safety of participants at the site can be verified." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file</Tooltip> for monitoring, auditing and inspection.

Questions about the study's insurance go to the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).

## Add the steps for a drug trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

When you both initiate and conduct a drug trial that Health Canada regulates under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, you are its sponsor, and [the same Division 5 requirements apply to you as to a commercial sponsor](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#what-the-two-roles-mean).

In a drug trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, you apply to Health Canada for it as the sponsor and report serious unexpected adverse drug reactions to it. A trial of a marketed drug, for a use within its notice of compliance or its drug identification number, needs no application, and most of Division 5 doesn't apply to it.

In every drug trial under Division 5, you ensure that every site is monitored and keep the trial's records for the period the regulations set. In every drug trial under Division 5, submit the information, records or samples Health Canada requires of you as the sponsor within [the time the regulations set](#meet-the-sponsor-obligations-once-the-trial-starts). Before you file an application, consider checking that your team and the institution can support these obligations.

### Apply to Health Canada for a drug trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

For a drug marketed in Canada, [a reference to the most recent Canadian product monograph](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/clinical-trials/clinical-trial-sponsors-applications.html#a232) and its version date may be submitted if an updated Investigator's Brochure is not available.

As the sponsor-investigator, you should find out whether the holder of the product's licence or marketing authorization has a brochure available. Where regulators permit it, a basic product information brochure, such as the product's labelling, may be an appropriate alternative, if it includes current, comprehensive and detailed information on every aspect of the product that might matter to the investigator.

File the <Tooltip tip="An application to Health Canada for authorization to sell or import a drug for a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip> with Health Canada, under [SOP-CR-018](/sops/cr-018). The application must contain the protocol and the Investigator's Brochure, among other documents. Health Canada first screens the application for completeness. You may sell or import the drug for the trial at a site once all of these conditions are met:

- Health Canada has not sent you a notice of objection within 30 days after it received the application.
- The site's REB has approved the protocol and the informed consent form.
- Health Canada has the site's information, if you didn't give it with the application.

Health Canada typically issues a <Tooltip tip="A letter that Health Canada issues when it has found a Clinical Trial Application satisfactory." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nol">No Objection Letter (NOL)</Tooltip> when it raises no objection.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-007#5-1-1) have the sponsor obtain Health Canada's approval first, for a trial of a new drug in Phases I to III, or in Phase IV with conditions. The sponsor should then forward a copy of the No Objection Letter to the investigators, to be filed in their investigator site file and submitted to the REB. Do not begin any trial activity before you receive the No Objection Letter.

[Ensure that the drug is manufactured, handled and stored in accordance with the applicable good manufacturing practices](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#know-who-ensures-the-product-meets-good-manufacturing-practices).

### Set up the safety reporting for a drug trial

In a drug trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, you must inform Health Canada, as the sponsor, of every serious unexpected adverse drug reaction, inside or outside Canada, within [its deadlines](#meet-the-sponsor-obligations-once-the-trial-starts). In every drug trial under Division 5, you must also keep records of all adverse events with the drug, inside or outside Canada. ### Meet the sponsor obligations once the trial starts

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

Once a drug trial that needs Health Canada's authorization is under way, the sponsor-investigator is responsible for these obligations to Health Canada:

| Obligation | What it requires |
|---|---|
| Reporting serious unexpected adverse drug reactions | Inform Health Canada of each one, whether it occurred inside or outside Canada: within 15 days after becoming aware of it, or within 7 days if it is fatal or life-threatening. For a fatal or life-threatening reaction, submit a complete report within 8 days after informing Health Canada, with an assessment of the importance and implication of the findings. |
| Safety reports on request | Health Canada asks for a [Development Safety Update Report (DSUR)](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#development-safety-update-report-dsur) only on request. |
| Information Health Canada requests | Submit information on the drug or the trial, or samples of the drug, within 2 days after receiving Health Canada's request, when Health Canada has the reasons the regulations list, such as a risk to the health of a participant. Submit any information or records kept for the trial, or samples of the drug, within 7 days after receiving a request made to assess the drug's safety or the health of participants or other persons. |
| Amendments that need authorization | Amendments to the protocol that affect the selection, monitoring or dismissal of participants, the evaluation of the drug's efficacy, the risk to participants' health or the drug's safety evaluation, amendments that extend the trial, and changes to the chemistry and manufacturing information that may affect the drug's safety or quality need Health Canada's authorization. You may proceed if Health Canada sends no objection within 30 days after receiving the application, and once you have sent Health Canada, for each site, the name and contact information of the REB that approved the amended protocol. [Study Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#get-health-canada-authorization-for-a-drug-trial-amendment) gives every condition. |
| Amendments made immediately | If the trial endangers the health of a participant or another person, an amendment that needs authorization may be made immediately, with its information sent to Health Canada within 15 days after the amendment. |
| Changes that need only a notice | Other changes to the protocol that do not alter the risk to participants' health, and other changes to the chemistry and manufacturing information that do not affect the drug's quality or safety, need only a written notice to Health Canada within 15 days after the change. |
| Stopping the trial | If you stop the trial, in its entirety or at a site, [inform Health Canada and every qualified investigator](/kb/close-out/close-out#tell-health-canada-that-the-study-has-ended) as the regulations require. |
| Monitoring | Ensure that every site continues to be monitored for compliance with the protocol, data integrity and participant safety. |
| Records | Keep all the trial's records for the [retention period](/kb/close-out/close-out#supply-reconciliation-and-archiving) the regulations set. |

The rows on information Health Canada requests and on records also apply to a drug trial that needs no authorization.

Beyond these obligations to Health Canada, [the Investigator's Brochure should be reviewed at least once a year](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) and revised as needed, following the sponsor's documented procedures.

### Apply to Health Canada for a natural health product trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

A natural health product trial needs Health Canada's authorization, unless the trial uses the product [as its label approved by Health Canada describes](/paths#check-whether-health-canada-must-authorize-the-study). As the sponsor-investigator, you apply and hold the authorization.

For a trial of a marketed natural health product that you initiate, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs require an application](/sops/cr-023#5-1) if the trial differs from the product monograph in any of these:

- The indication and clinical use.
- The target population.
- The dosage regimen.
- The route of administration.
- The dosage form.

[The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-023#5-1) have you contact Health Canada's [Natural and Non-prescription Health Products Directorate (NNHPD)](mailto:hc.nhpd-cta-dec-dpsn.sc@hc-sc.gc.ca) before you prepare the application, to confirm:

- Which templates are required for the chemistry, manufacturing and control information.
- The number of copies and the formats required, paper and electronic.
- Which directorate the application is submitted to.

Prepare the application under [SOP-CR-023](/sops/cr-023#5-2-2) and [Health Canada's guidance on clinical trials of natural health products](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/natural-non-prescription/legislation-guidelines/guidance-documents/clinical-trials.html). The application must contain, among other documents:

- A copy of the protocol.
- The statement of the risks and anticipated benefits to participants' health, as it will appear in each consent form.
- An attestation, signed and dated by the REB of each site, that it has reviewed and approved the protocol and a consent form containing that statement.
- An Investigator's Brochure.

Because the application names the REB that approved the protocol at each site, the REB approves a natural health product trial before Health Canada authorizes it. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-023#5-2-2) also list a signed Qualified Investigator Undertaking form among the documents required for the submission.

In place of an Investigator's Brochure, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-023#5-2-2) accept the Natural Health Product Monograph or Labelling Standard, with the chemistry, manufacturing and control information. If the manufacturer has already given Health Canada information on the product in a dosage form or strength that the monograph doesn't specify, a letter authorizing a cross-reference to that information may be submitted instead of the brochure.

Health Canada authorizes the trial by sending you a notice of authorization. [Ensure that no trial activities begin before the Notice of Authorization (NOA) is received](/sops/cr-023#5-2-3). As the sponsor, you [should then forward a copy of the NOA to the investigators](/sops/cr-007#5-1-1), to be filed in their investigator site file and submitted to the REB.

Before each site starts the trial, send Health Canada the site's Clinical Trial Site Information form, the signed Qualified Investigator Undertaking form and the REB's attestation form or approval letter for the site, [at least as long before the site starts as the regulations require](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#the-qiu-and-the-fda-1572).

### Meet the sponsor obligations in a natural health product trial

### Get the ITA through the manufacturer

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span></div>

A study of a Class II, III or IV medical device that needs Health Canada's authorization gets it through an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for investigational testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">investigational testing authorization (ITA)</Tooltip>. [Whether a device study needs an ITA](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization) depends on the device's class, whether it is licensed in Canada, how the study uses it and who makes it.

[Only the manufacturer or the importer of a device can apply for an ITA](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator#only-the-manufacturer-or-importer-can-apply), so a device trial you sponsor yourself needs the manufacturer's cooperation. The manufacturer can delegate the regulatory correspondence and the clinical oversight to you.

[The RI-MUHC's SOP on ITAs](/sops/cr-024#5-5) lists what the application contains for a Class II device and for a Class III or IV device, and its [Appendix 2](/sops/cr-024#appendix-2) is an ITA checklist.

For a Class III or IV device, Health Canada may issue the authorization before the REB has approved the study. The investigational testing cannot begin until the REB's approval has been obtained and submitted to Health Canada. For a Class II device, the REB's approval must be obtained before the study begins, but it is not sent to Health Canada.

In a device trial that you initiate and that needs an ITA, other than one with an in vitro diagnostic device, do not begin the study before Health Canada's written authorization is received. [A copy of the ITA should be forwarded to the investigators](/sops/cr-007#5-1-1), to be filed in their investigator site file and submitted to the REB.

### Label the device and keep each investigator undertaking

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span></div>

### Know what the manufacturer must do in a device trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span></div>

During a device trial, the Medical Devices Regulations keep these obligations of the manufacturer or importer:

- Keeping distribution records.
- Handling complaints, with documented procedures and records.
- Reporting incidents to Health Canada.
- Recalls, with documented procedures and records.
- Implant registration, for the devices that need it.

No one may advertise a device that is under investigational testing unless they hold the ITA to sell or import it. The advertisement must say clearly that the device is under investigational testing, and for what purpose.

[Ensure that the qualified investigator reports an incident](/sops/cr-024#5-11-2) that meets the Regulations' criteria to Health Canada and to the manufacturer within [the deadline in Key reporting timelines](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#key-reporting-timelines).

A change to the protocol, the investigators, the sites or the device may need [a revised authorization from Health Canada](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments#get-a-revised-health-canada-authorization-for-a-device-trial), which the manufacturer or importer requests. Where a change needs a revised authorization, [do not implement the change before the new authorization is obtained](/sops/cr-024#5-8-3).
