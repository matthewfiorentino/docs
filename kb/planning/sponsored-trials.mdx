Most industry-sponsored trials reach the RI-MUHC through a company, or a contract research organization working for it, that has already picked the RI-MUHC as a possible site. Knowing how the company chooses its sites, and what it is responsible for, explains much of what the site team meets during the study.

## Know who is responsible for what

In an industry-sponsored trial, the <Tooltip tip="An individual, company, institution or organization that takes responsibility for the initiation, management and arrangement of the financing of a clinical trial." headline="Sponsor" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sponsor">sponsor</Tooltip> is usually a pharmaceutical, biotechnology or medical device company. The sponsor writes and owns the protocol, funds the study and is responsible for the safety reporting and the quality of the data throughout. For a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor applies to Health Canada for its authorization. For a device study, the manufacturer or importer of the device holds the [investigational testing authorization](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization), where the study needs one.

Many sponsors hand much of the work of a multi-site trial, such as site selection, contracts, monitoring and data management, to a <Tooltip tip="A person or an organization, commercial, academic or other, contracted by a sponsor to perform one or more of the sponsor's study-related duties and functions." headline="Contract Research Organization (CRO)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#contract-research-organization-cro">contract research organization (CRO)</Tooltip>. When a CRO is involved, it is usually the site's main contact.

The sponsor may transfer any of its trial activities to a CRO, but the sponsor keeps the [ultimate responsibility for them](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html), including the protection of participants' rights, safety and well-being and the reliability of the trial data. A CRO's oversight of a site can be close for that reason.

The site's research team, the <Tooltip tip="The investigator who leads the study team at a site and is responsible for conducting the study there." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip> who leads the study at the MUHC and the staff, conducts the study. It recruits participants, obtains their consent, carries out the study procedures, collects the data and reports events. The team follows the sponsor's protocol, and its members keep their own professional and institutional obligations. An investigator who delegates tasks keeps the overall responsibility for them.

## How a sponsor chooses a site

Sponsors, usually with a CRO's help, choose the sites. They mostly find candidate sites and contact them, rather than wait for sites to apply. A principal investigator's track record still affects whether a sponsor contacts them.

Sponsors look at several things when they choose a site:

| What sponsors look at | What it means |
|---|---|
| The patient population | Access to the type and number of patients the protocol describes. A site with a large cardiology program is competitive for cardiac trials. |
| The principal investigator's expertise and record | Publications in the disease area, and earlier sponsored trials that show the investigator knows good clinical practice, can lead a team and meets enrolment targets. |
| Infrastructure | The space, equipment, pharmacy and data systems the protocol needs. |
| Readiness for regulations and procedures | Well-kept regulatory binders, trained staff and documented standard operating procedures (SOPs). A site that has had monitoring visits or audits without significant findings carries less risk for the sponsor. |
| Professional connections | Contacts made at specialty conferences, with co-investigators on earlier studies or during the investigator's training. |

CROs also keep their own databases and networks of sites. Principal investigators can register with large CROs to show that they are available for new studies.

Sponsors track whether each site meets its enrolment commitments. A site that keeps enrolling fewer participants than it committed to is unlikely to be chosen again by the same sponsor. Knowing what was promised during feasibility, and raising recruitment problems as soon as they appear, protects the site's standing.

## Answer the feasibility questionnaire

When a sponsor or a CRO has picked the RI-MUHC as a possible site, its first formal step is usually a site feasibility questionnaire. The questionnaire asks about the site's patients, staff, infrastructure and regulatory status, and about the principal investigator's experience. The sponsor uses it to decide whether to visit the site. It typically covers:

- The estimated number of eligible patients the site could screen each year for the protocol.
- The principal investigator's qualifications, the date of their good clinical practice training and their experience of similar trials.
- Whether key staff are available, such as research coordinators, research nurses and a pharmacist if needed.
- Facilities: clinic space, pharmacy, freezer capacity and laboratory capabilities.
- The data systems the site uses, such as REDCap or other electronic data capture systems.
- The institution's review process for the Research Ethics Board (REB) and its other approvals.
- Competing studies that might affect enrolment or the staff's availability.

A sponsored trial can run at the MUHC with or without the [Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM)](/cim/services) running it. If you are considering having the CIM run your study, contact the [CIM](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you answer the questionnaire. The CIM can help assess the institution's feasibility and coordinate the answer. For the studies it runs, it supports contract review, budget negotiation and activation. If the CIM will not run the study, it does not usually take part at the questionnaire stage. If you are unsure how to answer a feasibility request, or whether the CIM suits your study, the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help.

If the answer is promising, the sponsor or its CRO usually conducts a [site feasibility visit](/kb/planning/feasibility#the-site-feasibility-visit) to see the facilities, meet the team and check that the site matches what the questionnaire said.

For the visit, consider preparing organized regulatory binders, a trained team that knows the protocol, and a principal investigator who has read the protocol closely. If the sponsor selects the site, [the budget and the agreement are negotiated](/kb/planning/feasibility#budget-and-financial-agreements) before the study is activated.

## Investigator-initiated trials

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

Not every study at the RI-MUHC has a company as its sponsor. In an investigator-initiated trial, an investigator at the RI-MUHC develops the research question and the protocol. An investigator who both initiates and conducts a clinical trial is its <Tooltip tip="An individual who both initiates and conducts, alone or with others, a clinical trial, and under whose immediate direction the investigational product is administered to, dispensed to or used by a participant." headline="Sponsor-Investigator" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#sponsor-investigator">sponsor-investigator</Tooltip>, with the obligations of both a sponsor and an investigator. [Sponsor-Investigator Studies](/kb/planning/sponsor-investigator) explains those obligations, including the role a company can still play.

## What a sponsored trial means for the site team

For research coordinators, research nurses and other staff new to a sponsored trial, some features of the study follow from how it reached the RI-MUHC:

| What you will meet | Why |
|---|---|
| The protocol is fixed. | The sponsor designed and owns it. A change needs [an amendment](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments). The investigator [must not implement a deviation from the approved protocol](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/deviation-management) without the sponsor's prior agreement and the REB's approval. Two exceptions apply: a deviation needed to eliminate an immediate hazard to participants, and one for an administrative aspect of the study. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs require every deviation to be reported to the sponsor](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#classify-and-report-protocol-deviations), whatever its tier. After a deviation made to eliminate an immediate hazard, the investigator [must notify the REB immediately](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/reportable-events#report-participant-safety-and-ethical-concerns). In a clinical trial of an investigational product, the investigator [should also inform the sponsor promptly](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) after a deviation made to eliminate an immediate hazard. |
| Monitors visit the site. | Sponsors have an [obligation to monitor](/sops/cr-013#1-0-purpose) the quality of every aspect of their studies. The sponsor's monitor [checks, among other things](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/monitoring-audits#what-the-monitor-verifies), that the data recorded match the source documents and that the study follows the approved protocol. |
| Enrolment targets matter. | The principal investigator committed to enrolling a number of participants during feasibility, and that commitment was part of why the site was chosen. Falling well short delays the sponsor's trial and affects the site's standing for future studies. |
| For a study the CIM runs, the CIM is the sponsor's administrative contact. | Budget questions, contract amendments and communication with the sponsor go through the CIM team for the administrative side. Only a study that engaged the CIM from the start has the CIM in this role. |
