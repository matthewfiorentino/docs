Designing a study well means getting the right people involved early. This page maps the six areas where design decisions need to be made and identifies who at the Institute can support you in each.

For study type classification, randomization, blinding, and comparator design, see [Trial Design and Study Types](/kb/trial-design) in the 101 series.

## Support across the six design areas

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Methodology and biostatistics">
    The methodological foundation shapes every subsequent decision in a study. The research question needs to be precisely framed before the protocol can be written; the study design determines what statistical analysis is appropriate.

    A statistical analysis plan should be finalized before data collection begins. The Biostatistics Consulting Unit (BCU) can advise on sample size, power calculations, and analysis strategy early in the design process, when changes are still low-cost. Waiting until after data collection to engage a biostatistician is one of the most common and most avoidable study design problems.

  <Accordion title="Data management and technology">
    Data management planning is a submission requirement, not an afterthought. Before the first form is built, you need to determine what data you are collecting, where it will live, who will have access, and how it will be protected. For studies involving identifiable health data, Quebec's **Act 25 (Loi 25)** requires a privacy impact assessment (ÉFVP).

    If your study involves any electronic platform — eCRF, e-consent, mobile app, or external database — IT feasibility must be confirmed before submission. Pre-contract queries go to the Digital and IT Division; post-submission or platform security questions go to MUHC Security and Governance.

    The CORD unit supports the full REDCap lifecycle at The Institute. If you are accessing MUHC clinical or administrative data for a retrospective or feasibility study, the RI-MUHC Data Warehouse is the route in.

    **Who to contact:**
    - CORD Unit — REDCap database creation, <Tooltip tip="Document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor for each participant." headline="Case Report Form (CRF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crf">CRF</Tooltip> support, data management: [cord@rimuhc.ca](mailto:cord@rimuhc.ca)
    - RI-MUHC Data Warehouse — MUHC clinical and administrative data access for research: [info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - Pascal St. Onge — Director, Digital and IT (IT feasibility for electronic platforms, pre-submission): [pascal.st-onge@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:pascal.st-onge@muhc.mcgill.ca)

  <Accordion title="Funding and pre-awards">
    Funding timelines often precede REB submission timelines significantly — some major grant competitions (CIHR, FRQS) have application deadlines that fall months before a study can be submitted for ethics review. Engaging the Pre-Awards Office early is one of the highest-leverage steps you can take at the design stage.

    The RI-MUHC Clinical Trial Budget Template (launched January 2026) is the recommended starting point for all budget builds. It covers staff salaries with built-in salary scale lookups, time and effort matrices, MUHC service costs, and produces both a project cost summary and a CIHR-format budget. The template is available on the RI-MUHC Portal.

    **Who to contact:**
    - Pre-Awards Office — grant applications, funding strategy: [pre-awards.rimuhc@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:pre-awards.rimuhc@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - Josie Campisi, M.Sc. — Research Facilitator: [Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:Research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca)

  <Accordion title="Participant considerations, PWLLE and EDI" id="participant-considerations">
    Recruitment feasibility is one of the most common reasons studies are delayed or never complete enrolment. Before finalizing eligibility criteria, confirm that the target population exists at the proposed sites in the required numbers and that the recruitment strategy is realistic within the study timeline.

    Consent design deserves attention at the protocol stage, not as a final pre-submission task. For studies involving vulnerable populations — minors, adults incapable of consenting, emergency situations — the consent framework has legal implications under Quebec's Civil Code.

    **Integrating patient perspectives (PWLLE):**
    Integrating the voices of persons with lived or living experience (PWLLE) strengthens the relevance and impact of your study. The MUHC Patient Engagement Office (Centre for Applied Ethics) can help identify, onboard, and support PWLLE collaborators throughout the study lifecycle.

    - Patient Engagement Office (MUHC): [contactpp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:contactpp@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - Research Facilitator (The Institute): [research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca)

    **Equity, Diversity and Inclusion (EDI):**
    EDI considerations are increasingly tied to funding eligibility — most major funders now require them in teams and study design. In 2023–24, 85% of CIHR training and career-support research integrated sex and gender considerations.

    The RI-MUHC EDI team provides:
    - GBA+ / SGBA consultation for grant proposals
    - Inclusive recruitment and retention support
    - Self-assessment tools for Tri-Agency-aligned EDI strategies (disability inclusion, neuroinclusion toolkits)
    - Online training on unconscious bias and inclusive leadership
    - Programs supporting women, racialized groups, Indigenous people, 2SLGBTQIA+ people, people with disabilities, and ethnic minorities

    Contact: [edi.ri@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:edi.ri@muhc.mcgill.ca) — [EDI page on the RI Portal ↗](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:399)
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Regulatory and ethics">
    The regulatory pathway needs to be identified at the design stage, not resolved at submission. Studies involving investigational drugs require a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">CTA</Tooltip> under Health Canada Division 5; studies involving investigational medical devices require an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip>. These run in parallel with <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> review — Health Canada has its own **30-day** default review clock — and must be submitted before a study can be activated.

    For studies involving identifiable health data, the <Tooltip tip="Required under Quebec Law 25 before communicating personal information without consent for research. Non-compliance: up to $25M or 4% of worldwide turnover." headline="ÉFVP — Privacy Impact Assessment" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#efvp">ÉFVP</Tooltip> required under Act 25 must be initiated early. See [Regulations and Guidelines](/kb/planning/regulations) for the full regulatory framework.

    **Who to contact:**
    - MUHC Research Ethics Board: [reb@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca)

  <Accordion title="Team, operations and CIM">
    The <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#pi">principal investigator</Tooltip> must hold valid **Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status** at the RI-MUHC before a study can be submitted. All team members listed on the <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip> must hold current compliance training appropriate to their study role. These are prerequisites for Nagano submission — missing privileges or training delays all review processes simultaneously.

    CIM engagement is optional but worth evaluating early. The McConnell Centre for Innovative Medicine provides research staff, infrastructure, and <Tooltip tip="All planned and systematic actions to ensure trial data are generated, documented, and reported in compliance with GCP and SOPs." headline="Quality Assurance (QA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qa">QA</Tooltip> services at both the Glen site and the Montreal General Hospital. For Phase I studies, oncology protocols, and studies requiring IV administration, routing through CIM is the standard path.

    **Hiring and HR — People & Culture:**
    All study team members must have an appropriate employment or affiliation agreement with the RI-MUHC before performing any study tasks. Volunteers, students without valid academic insurance, and non-affiliated individuals cannot be delegated study tasks.

    - **Talent Acquisition** — Bilingual recruitment and workforce planning, EDI-informed hiring strategies. You lead the selection process, guided by best practices. Consult Talent Acquisition before advertising any position.
    - **HR Service Centre** — One-stop shop for all employee and trainee lifecycle transactions: onboarding, role changes, leaves, and offboarding via the HR Hub application. [ri.hr.centre@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:ri.hr.centre@muhc.mcgill.ca)
    - **HR Advisors** — Performance management, dispute resolution, disability case management, and work permits. Each research program has a dedicated HR partner.
