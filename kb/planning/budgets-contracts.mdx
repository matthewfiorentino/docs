Contracts and financial agreements are not administrative formalities that follow a research decision — they are part of how that decision becomes real. Getting these in place before submission is both a regulatory requirement and a protection for everyone involved. All contracts and agreements for studies conducted at the MUHC/RI-MUHC must be handled through the Research Agreements Office.

## Research contracts and agreements

A contract defines who is responsible for what, who bears risk if a participant is harmed, who owns the data, and what happens to study materials when the study ends. All new study contracts are submitted through <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>; once submitted, notify the contracts group at [contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca).

Depending on the type of study, agreements may be required with one or more of the following:

| Agreement type | When required |
|---|---|
| Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator agreement | All industry-sponsored studies and most Sponsor-Investigator initiated studies. Covers conduct obligations, financial terms, publication rights, and data ownership. |
| CRO or ARO agreement | When a Contract Research Organization or Academic Research Organization (e.g., CIM) is involved. Duties transferred to the CRO must be documented in writing; the Sponsor retains overall responsibility for data quality and integrity. |
| Third-party agreements | External laboratories, imaging services, nursing agencies, or other service providers delegated protocol tasks. All must have a signed agreement before tasks begin. |
| Confidentiality Disclosure Agreement | When sharing sensitive study information with external collaborators — including collaborators at McGill University — before a full agreement is in place. |
| Data Transfer Agreement | When transferring participant data to or from external institutions or jurisdictions. Required by Quebec privacy law for personal health information. |

## Budget planning

The financial aspects of a study must be documented and agreed upon before submission. Research budgets at the MUHC must account for direct and indirect costs associated with the use of MUHC facilities and services. Financial agreements between the Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator and the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI typically cover:

- Fees for the QI/PI, Sub-Investigators, and other research team members
- Payment per participant or per visit, and payment for participants who complete the study
- Reimbursement of expenses to participants (transportation, parking)
- Reimbursement of MUHC service departments — nursing, pharmacy, laboratory, radiology, and other clinical services used for research
- Costs for third-party service providers
- Costs of treatment of participants in the event of a trial-related injury
- Archiving costs — retention periods are 15 years for drug trials and 7 years for most other studies; negotiate before study start

<Tip>
  Contact the Research Facilitator at [research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) at the study planning stage — before submitting to a Sponsor.
</Tip>

### Funding types and overhead

The RI-MUHC applies institutional overhead on eligible costs. The rate depends on the funding source. Overhead is calculated on the total of staff, services, and contingency, but **not** on REB fees.

| Funding type | Overhead rate | <Tooltip tip="US equivalent of the Canadian Qualified Investigator (QI)." headline="Principal Investigator (PI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#pi">PI</Tooltip> salary included? |
|---|---|---|
| Investigator-Initiated (IIT) — peer-reviewed grants (CIHR, FRQS), self-funded | 0% | No |
| Foundation / Charity — Heart and Stroke, MUHC Foundation, cancer societies | 15% | No |
| Industry-Sponsored — pharmaceutical, biotech, device companies | 30% | Yes |

<Note>
  **Startup costs (industry-sponsored studies):** Study startup activities in Year 1 — initiation visits, site setup, initial regulatory submissions — are typically negotiated at 0% overhead. Flag these costs separately when submitting to the Research Agreements Office so they can be carved out before overhead is applied.
</Note>

### CIM submission and budget services

The CIM also provides fee-for-service submission support for industry-sponsored studies: initial ethics submission (lead site: $1,350; participating site: $785), annual ethics renewals ($395), and budget negotiation services ($830 for 1–2 arms; $1,350 for 3+ arms). These fees are not applicable to IIT or foundation-funded studies. Contact CIM directly at [clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:clinical.research@muhc.mcgill.ca) for the full CIM fee schedule (v2.0, May 2025).

### Contingency

A contingency buffer accounts for costs that cannot be precisely predicted at budgeting time: unanticipated protocol deviations, scope creep, additional monitoring visits, or price increases in subsequent years.

| Complexity level | Range | Recommended | Typical study types |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 8–10% | 9% | Observational, retrospective chart review, simple cross-sectional |
| Moderate | 10–12% | 11% | Prospective interventional, Phase II–III, multi-visit, registry |
| High | 12–15% | 13.5% | Phase I, first-in-human, complex interventional (<Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> management, biologics, devices) |

## REB billing fees

Under Directive ministérielle 2023-016, Quebec hospitals are required to bill private enterprise Sponsors for REB review and authorization services. **The definition of "private enterprise" has been expanded by the MUHC REB and now includes foundation-funded studies** — such as those funded by the MUHC Foundation or other private foundations — not only industry-sponsored studies.

**The fees are billed to the Sponsor — not to the PI personally.** Your obligation is accurate funding declaration at submission and ensuring the Sponsor's budget accounts for these costs.

REB billing fees apply from the first REB action through study closure. This includes initial review and all subsequent actions — amendments, renewals, and safety reports. Fees continue to apply even if a study is abandoned after submission but before completion.

The current fee schedule is effective April 1, 2025 (2.6% index applied):

| Fee item | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Scientific review | $634 | One per project. Recognized across HSSN institutions. |
| Ethical review | $6,348 | One REB conducts the review. HSSN institutions recognize each other's reviews. |
| Site authorization | $1,904 per institution | Each participating HSSN institution with a board of directors bills this amount. |
| Annual ethics monitoring | $634 × sites (max 10) per renewal | Billed on the renewal anniversary date. |
| Annual authorization monitoring | $634 per institution per renewal | Billed by each institution on receipt of the evaluating REB's renewal decision. |
| Major protocol amendment | $634 per amendment | REB determines whether a change qualifies as major. |

Before submitting a privately-funded study, confirm two things with your Sponsor:

1. The Sponsor's budget explicitly accounts for REB billing fees for the full anticipated lifecycle of the study.
2. The Sponsor's billing contact information is available and ready to include in your Nagano submission.

Questions about REB fees should be directed to the Research Agreements Office.

## Sponsored trials and Sponsor-Investigator studies

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Industry-sponsored trials">
    In an industry-sponsored trial, the sponsor — typically a pharmaceutical, biotechnology, or medical device company — owns the protocol, funds the study, and bears ultimate regulatory responsibility. The sponsor designs the study, holds any applicable Health Canada authorization, and is accountable for data integrity and safety reporting.

    Sponsors rarely manage operational work themselves. Most delegate site selection, contract management, monitoring, and data oversight to a **contract research organization (CRO)**. The sponsor's accountability does not transfer to the CRO; delegation changes who does the work, not who is responsible for it.

    In industry-sponsored studies, the Sponsor typically carries liability for harm arising from the investigational product. The Clinical Trial Agreement will specify the Sponsor's indemnification obligations and usually requires the Sponsor to provide a Certificate of Insurance naming the MUHC as an additional insured. Verify this is in place before the study starts.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Sponsor-Investigator studies">
    When you both initiate and conduct a study, you hold the obligations of a Sponsor and a Qualified Investigator simultaneously. A Sponsor-Investigator must:

    - Develop the protocol ([SOP-CR-102](/sops/cr-102))
    - Submit and hold the <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">CTA</Tooltip>, NHP CTA, or <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip> ([SOP-CR-018](/sops/cr-018) / [SOP-CR-023](/sops/cr-023) / [SOP-CR-024](/sops/cr-024))
    - Develop a risk-based monitoring plan and arrange an **independent monitor** — you cannot monitor your own study
    - Design the CRFs, data management plan, and set up the database ([SOP-CR-100](/sops/cr-100) series)
    - Report SUSARs and safety events directly to Health Canada
    - Maintain both the <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#tmf">Trial Master File</Tooltip> (TMF) and the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#isf">Investigator Site File</Tooltip> (ISF)
    - Register the study on ClinicalTrials.gov before the first participant is enrolled

    For Sponsor-Investigator studies, the institutional insurance picture is more complex. The RI-MUHC must be correctly identified in the contract and regulatory documents for institutional coverage to apply. Discuss the insurance framework with the Research Agreements Office early in planning, not at submission.

    Contact [QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) early in study planning if you are considering a Sponsor-Investigator study.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Insurance and liability

<Warning>
  Insurance and liability in clinical research is not straightforward — who is responsible for what depends on the type of study, the Sponsor arrangement, and the specific situation. For anything beyond the general principles below, contact the Research Agreements Office before making assumptions.
</Warning>

**DARSSS coverage** protects the institution and its employed personnel — it does not cover physicians. For physician investigators, the Canadian Medical Protective Association (CMPA) provides professional liability coverage for clinical activities. Whether CMPA coverage extends to a specific research activity depends on the nature of that activity and the study context. Physician investigators should confirm their coverage with CMPA directly for any study where they are uncertain.
