When a researcher both initiates and conducts a study, they hold the obligations of a Sponsor and a Qualified Investigator simultaneously. Understanding what that means before committing to a study is what makes the difference between a manageable project and an overwhelming one.

## How researchers become Sponsor-Investigators

Most clinical research at the MUHC is industry-sponsored: a pharmaceutical or device company holds the Sponsor role, develops the protocol, submits to Health Canada, runs the monitoring program, and bears primary accountability for participant safety reporting and data integrity. The site QI/PI is a responsible local partner, but someone else is managing the regulatory infrastructure above them.

Some studies work differently. An investigator has their own scientific question — a drug approved for one indication that they want to test off-label in another population, a modified surgical technique, a new diagnostic biomarker, a care pathway that has never been formally studied. They apply for funding to pursue it. When that funding comes through, they are now the Sponsor. They initiated the study; there is no company holding the protocol above them, no CRO providing monitoring, no Regulatory Affairs department handling Health Canada submissions.

Funding for Sponsor-Investigator studies typically comes from grants (CIHR, FRQS, foundation grants, hospital foundation funding), departmental budgets, or philanthropic sources. Occasionally a company will supply an investigational product at no cost without taking on the Sponsor role — the investigator still holds all regulatory accountability. **The source of funding does not determine Sponsor status; initiating and holding accountability for the study does.**

Common examples at the MUHC: a surgeon formalizing a modified technique as an RCT; an oncologist running a Phase II study of a drug combination not yet approved for that indication; a rheumatologist testing a repurposed drug in a new population; a researcher validating a new diagnostic tool. In all of these, there is no industry Sponsor to develop the protocol, submit to Health Canada, or report safety events to regulators. The investigator does all of it.

<Warning>
The decision point is early. Investigators sometimes do not realize the full scope of Sponsor obligations until they are already committed to a study — often after a grant has been awarded. The time to understand whether a study requires a CTA, an independent monitor, and a GMP-compliant IP supply chain is before writing the grant, not after receiving it.
</Warning>

## What the dual role actually means

A Sponsor-Investigator is simultaneously responsible for two distinct sets of obligations that in an industry study are held by separate organizations.

**As Sponsor**, they are responsible for: developing the protocol; submitting and holding the Health Canada authorization (CTA, NHP CTA, or ITA as applicable); maintaining the Trial Master File; developing and executing a monitoring plan using an independent monitor; managing the investigational product supply chain including GMP compliance; and reporting SUSARs and safety events directly to Health Canada.

**As QI/PI**, they hold all the standard site-level responsibilities: obtaining REB approval, conducting the study per protocol, obtaining informed consent, maintaining the ISF, and ensuring the team is trained and delegated appropriately.

None of these Sponsor-level obligations are reduced or waived because the Sponsor is also the investigator. Health Canada does not distinguish between commercial and non-commercial sponsors. The same Division 5 requirements that apply to a multinational pharmaceutical company apply to an academic investigator running a single-site investigator-initiated trial.

## The additional obligations, compared

The table below maps each major obligation to show what is handled by the industry sponsor in a commercial study, and what the Sponsor-Investigator must handle themselves.

| Obligation | Industry-sponsored study | Sponsor-Investigator study |
|-----------|--------------------------|---------------------------|
| Protocol development | Sponsor writes the protocol and provides it to the site. | You develop the protocol. Must meet REB, Health Canada, and ICH E6(R3) requirements. SOP-CR-102 |
| Health Canada submission | Sponsor submits and holds the CTA, NHP CTA, or ITA. Site QI signs the QIU. | You submit and hold the CTA, NHP CTA, or ITA. You sign the QIU as both Sponsor and QI. SOP-CR-018 / SOP-CR-023 / SOP-CR-024 |
| Monitoring | Sponsor provides a CRA and monitoring plan. Monitor is independent of the site. | You develop a risk-based monitoring plan and arrange an independent monitor. You cannot monitor your own study. SOP-CR-013 |
| Data management | Sponsor provides CRF design, database, and data management infrastructure. | You design the CRFs, develop the data management plan, and set up and maintain the database. SOP-CR-100, SOP-CR-103, SOP-CR-104, SOP-CR-105 |
| Safety reporting to Health Canada | Sponsor reports SUSARs and safety events directly to Health Canada. | You report SUSARs and safety events to Health Canada. You are the Sponsor of record. SOP-CR-012, SOP-CR-026 |
| Investigational product | Sponsor sources, manufactures, and supplies the IP under GMP. Site delegates IP management to pharmacy. | You are responsible for sourcing an IP that meets GMP standards and arranging its supply to the site. Site pharmacy still manages dispensing and accountability. SOP-CR-010 |
| Trial Master File | Sponsor maintains the TMF. Site maintains the ISF. | You maintain both the TMF (Sponsor-level documents) and the ISF (site-level documents), or a combined file for single-site studies. SOP-CR-015 |
| Trial registration | Sponsor registers the study and keeps the registry record current. | You register the study on ClinicalTrials.gov before the first participant is enrolled and keep it current throughout. The registry name, URL, and registration number must appear in the REB-approved ICF. SOP-CR-007, SOP-CR-026 |

## Monitoring independence

The monitoring requirement is one of the most practically significant obligations for Sponsor-Investigators, and the one most commonly underestimated at the planning stage. Under ICH E6(R3) and RI-MUHC SOPs, a Sponsor-Investigator must develop a written, risk-based monitoring plan and arrange for an independent monitor to execute it. The monitor must be independent of the site operations — they cannot be on the site team and they cannot be the QI/PI themselves.

In practice this means contracting an external Clinical Research Associate, engaging a CRC from an affiliate institution, or working with a Clinical Research Organization. The monitor is not listed on the site Task Delegation Log — they are part of the Sponsor-Investigator structure, not the site team. Any site-level quality control — review of source documents, for example — must be conducted by the site, not the monitor, to preserve that independence.

<Warning>
You cannot monitor your own study. A QI/PI who acts as their own monitor is not independent. This is not a technicality — it is a fundamental GCP requirement. If a Health Canada inspection or an audit finds that monitoring was not conducted independently, the study data is at risk. Arranging monitoring before study activation is not optional.
</Warning>

The monitoring plan must describe the strategy (on-site, remote, or a combination), the rationale for the approach, how the monitor will be trained, and what events would trigger a revision to the plan. SOP-CR-013 (Study Monitoring and Communication) includes a monitoring plan template and describes the monitor's required qualifications and training documentation.

## Investigational product and GMP

For drug and natural health product studies, the Sponsor — whether a company or a Sponsor-Investigator — is responsible for ensuring that the investigational product meets Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards. In an industry study, the company manages this and the site pharmacy handles local dispensing and accountability. In a Sponsor-Investigator study, you are responsible for sourcing an IP from a GMP-compliant manufacturer or obtaining it through a mechanism that satisfies Health Canada's requirements.

This has real planning implications. A drug being used off-label for a new indication may be commercially available, but confirmation that it meets the applicable standards for research use, how it will be labelled, how accountability will be documented, and whether pharmacy at the MUHC is equipped to handle it must all be resolved before submission. IP management at the site is still delegated to an appropriately trained pharmacist under SOP-CR-010 — but the Sponsor-Investigator retains accountability for the supply chain above the pharmacy level.

For studies involving a controlled substance, a CTA must be submitted to Health Canada and an NOL obtained before applying separately for an exemption under the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act (CDSA). The CDSA exemption authorizes the specific activities with the controlled substance that would otherwise be prohibited by law, and is valid for two years.

## The QIU and the FDA 1572

For Health Canada-regulated drug studies (Division 5), the Qualified Investigator must complete and sign a **Qualified Investigator Undertaking (QIU)** form. The QIU commits the investigator to conducting the study in accordance with Canadian regulations and guidelines. In a Sponsor-Investigator study, the same person signs as both Sponsor and QI.

The QIU is the Canadian equivalent of the US FDA Form 1572. **RI-MUHC investigators should not sign the FDA 1572.** That form commits the signatory to following US FDA regulations — foreign law that takes precedence over Canadian law when the two conflict. The RI-MUHC and MUHC REB operate under ICH E6, Health Canada Food and Drug Regulations Part C Division 5, TCPS2, and the Declaration of Helsinki. They are not governed by foreign regulations unless contractually obligated.

<Note>
If a US sponsor requests a 1572, use the RI-MUHC ICH GCP Attestation Form (Appendix 4 of SOP-CR-002) as a substitute. It captures the equivalent information — investigator qualifications, site details, Sub-Investigator names, REB information — without committing to US regulations. Canadian sites can participate in US IND studies without being listed under the IND, and data collected at Canadian sites can be used to support a US marketing application. Contact QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca for guidance on any specific sponsor request.
</Note>

## TMF and ISF

In an industry study, the sponsor maintains the Trial Master File and the site maintains the Investigator Site File. These are parallel structures — the TMF holds Sponsor-level documents (monitoring reports, regulatory submissions, SUSAR logs, the master randomization list), the ISF holds site-level documents (signed consent forms, delegation log, local regulatory approvals, lab certifications).

A Sponsor-Investigator maintains both. For single-site studies, the TMF and ISF can be combined into one file, with Sponsor-level and site-level sections clearly distinguished. For multi-site studies — where the Sponsor-Investigator is the QI/PI at their own site but other sites are also participating — best practice is to maintain a separate TMF (covering the whole study) and ISF (covering the MUHC site only). Each participating site maintains its own ISF.

The structure and required contents of both files at RI-MUHC are governed by SOP-CR-015. The retention and archiving requirements apply equally to the TMF and ISF; both must be accessible for the full retention period, which begins at study closure.

## The 100-series SOPs

The SOP-CR-100 to 107 series exists specifically for Sponsor-Investigators. These SOPs cover the Sponsor-side operational infrastructure that a commercial company's data management and regulatory teams would normally handle. A QI/PI on an industry-sponsored study does not need these SOPs. A Sponsor-Investigator does.

| SOP | Topic |
|-----|-------|
| SOP-CR-100 | CRF Design — how to design case report forms that capture protocol-required data accurately and completely |
| SOP-CR-101 | Study Analysis and Reporting — statistical analysis plan, study report structure |
| SOP-CR-102 | Protocol Development — ICH E6-compliant protocol structure, verification checklists for protocol and IB content |
| SOP-CR-103 | Data Management Plan — required for all Sponsor-Investigator studies; also relevant for 21 CFR Part 11 compliance when electronic source data is used |
| SOP-CR-104 | Database Set-up — validation, user access, audit trail requirements |
| SOP-CR-105 | Database Maintenance and Management — ongoing data quality, query management, system change control |
| SOP-CR-106 | File Transfer — secure transfer of data and documents between sites or to third parties |
| SOP-CR-107 | Database Lock and Archiving — procedures for locking the database after final data cleaning and archiving study data |

Contact QAclinicalresearch@muhc.mcgill.ca early in study planning if you are considering a Sponsor-Investigator study. The QA team can help clarify which of these obligations apply given your specific study type, regulatory pathway, and funding source, and can identify infrastructure gaps before they become submission delays.
