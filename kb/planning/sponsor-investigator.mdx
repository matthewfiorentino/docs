## Know when you become a sponsor-investigator

In an industry-sponsored study, a company holds the sponsor's role. It develops the protocol, applies to Health Canada where the study needs it, runs the monitoring and is responsible for the safety reporting and the integrity of the data. The site's principal investigator is a local partner, and the company manages the regulatory work above them.

Some studies start from an investigator's own question instead: a drug approved for one indication that they want to test in another population, a modified surgical technique, a new diagnostic biomarker, a way of organizing care that has never been formally studied. The investigator applies for funding, and once the study goes ahead they are its sponsor.

No company holds the protocol above them, no contract research organization monitors the study and no company's regulatory affairs department prepares Health Canada's applications. The investigator does all of it, for example a surgeon formalizing a modified technique as a randomized controlled trial, or an oncologist running a Phase II study of a drug combination not yet approved for that indication.

Funding for these studies usually comes from grants, such as those of the Canadian Institutes of Health Research (CIHR), the Fonds de recherche du Québec – Santé (FRQS) and foundations, from departmental budgets or from philanthropy. A company may supply the investigational product at no cost, or contribute a grant, without taking on the sponsor's role. The investigator then holds all the sponsor's obligations.

For the RI-MUHC's insurance to cover a study you sponsor yourself, the RI-MUHC should be named as its sponsor on the agreement, on regulatory documents and on ClinicalTrials.gov. [Research Agreements](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements#cover-the-study-as-its-sponsor) explains it. You still keep all the sponsor-investigator's responsibilities.

Consider settling, before you write the grant application, whether the study needs a <Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#cta">Clinical Trial Application (CTA)</Tooltip>, an independent monitor and a supply of the investigational product that meets good manufacturing practices. Investigators sometimes discover the full scope of the sponsor's obligations only once they are committed to a study, often after a grant has been awarded.

## What the two roles mean

A sponsor-investigator holds two sets of obligations that, in an industry-sponsored study, belong to separate organizations. None of the sponsor's obligations is reduced or waived because the sponsor is also the investigator. In a drug trial, [Health Canada's regulations](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/compliance-enforcement/good-clinical-practices/guidance-documents/guidance-drugs-clinical-trials-human-subjects-gui-0100.html) don't differentiate between a commercial sponsor and a sponsor-investigator, and the same requirements apply.

As the investigator, you keep every duty of a site's principal investigator: obtaining the approval of the <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#reb">Research Ethics Board (REB)</Tooltip>, conducting the study according to the protocol, obtaining informed consent, keeping the <Tooltip tip="The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance, study integrity, and participant safety." headline="Investigator Site File (ISF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#isf">investigator site file</Tooltip>, and making sure the team is trained and its tasks delegated.

The table shows who does each of the sponsor's tasks in each kind of study, and the RI-MUHC's standard operating procedure (SOP) for each.

## Monitoring independence

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

Monitoring is the obligation sponsor-investigators most often underestimate when they plan a study. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-013#5-2) make the sponsor-investigator responsible for a systematic, prioritized, risk-based approach to monitoring. Ensure that a monitoring plan is in place, and that an independent, dedicated monitor is assigned to conduct the quality control it describes.

<Warning>
Do not monitor your own study. The monitor should stay independent of the study's operations, so neither you nor a member of your site team can be the monitor.
</Warning>

The monitor is part of the sponsor-investigator's team, not the site team, so the monitor is not listed on the site's <Tooltip tip="Lists each team member, their qualifications, and specific trial tasks authorized by the QI." headline="Task Delegation Log (TDL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tdl">Task Delegation Log</Tooltip>. The site's own quality control, such as the review of its source document forms, should be done, initialled and dated by the site, not by the monitor.

Sponsor-investigators at the RI-MUHC may delegate monitoring to a contract research organization or to an academic research organization, such as the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM). They keep the ultimate responsibility for it. Other options include an external clinical research associate or a qualified research coordinator from an affiliated institution.

The monitoring plan describes the monitoring strategy, whether on-site, remote or a combination, and the reasons for it. It should also identify the study's risks and how they are controlled, the processes for dealing with non-compliance, and the events that would call for a revision of the plan. [SOP-CR-013](/sops/cr-013#appendix-3) includes a template of a monitoring plan.

## Investigational product and GMP

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

In a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor, whether a company or a sponsor-investigator, must ensure that the investigational product is manufactured and stored in accordance with good manufacturing practices (GMP). In an industry-sponsored study, the company manages this, and the site pharmacy handles the local dispensing and accountability. In a study you sponsor yourself, you must obtain the product from a manufacturer that follows GMP, or through another means that meets Health Canada's requirements.

In a drug trial, [ensure that the drug bears a label](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.05.011.html) with the information the Food and Drug Regulations list, in both official languages. In a natural health product trial, ensure that the product bears the label the Natural Health Products Regulations set, in both official languages.

A drug used for a new indication may be commercially available. Before you submit the study, consider settling whether it meets the standards that apply to research use, how it will be labelled and how its accountability will be documented.

For research that dispenses or tests a drug or natural health product, marketed or not, [complete Nagano's questions](/sops/cr-007#5-4-4) that trigger the MUHC pharmacy's feasibility evaluation. The pharmacy agreement then comes to you for signature during the review. At the site, the investigational product is still delegated to a trained pharmacist, under [SOP-CR-010](/sops/cr-010), and you keep the responsibility for the supply above the pharmacy.

For a trial with a controlled substance, the qualified investigator also needs an exemption under section 56(1) of the Controlled Drugs and Substances Act. [Health Canada issues the exemption](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/health-concerns/controlled-substances-precursor-chemicals/exemptions/application-form-exemption-use-controlled-substance-clinical-studies.html) only after a Clinical Trial Application has been submitted and a <Tooltip tip="Letter from Health Canada confirming a CTA is satisfactory and authorizing the sponsor to proceed." headline="No Objection Letter (NOL)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nol">No Objection Letter</Tooltip> issued. The exemption authorizes the qualified investigator to buy (if applicable), possess and administer specific quantities of the substance for one protocol, and it is valid for 2 years from its issue.

With the completed exemption application, a copy of the protocol and the No Objection Letter must be provided.

## The QIU and the FDA 1572

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies regulated by Health Canada</span></div>

For a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, the qualified investigator must complete and sign Health Canada's **Qualified Investigator Undertaking (QIU)** form. By signing it, the qualified investigator agrees to conduct the study in accordance with Canadian regulations and guidelines, and acknowledges their responsibilities as the regulatory bodies define them.

For a natural health product trial that needs Health Canada's authorization, the sponsor must [send Health Canada a signed QIU form with the site's information](/sops/cr-023#5-4-2) at least 15 days before the site starts the study. For a device under investigational testing, the Medical Devices Regulations require a written undertaking from each qualified investigator.

In a drug or natural health product trial you sponsor yourself, you sign the undertaking as the qualified investigator, and as the sponsor you keep it in the trial's records. For a device, the undertaking is among the records the manufacturer or importer must possess.

The QIU is the Canadian version of the US Form FDA 1572. **MUHC and RI-MUHC investigators are not to sign Form FDA 1572**, because it requires investigators conducting studies in Canada to follow US laws, and foreign laws are not to overrule Canadian laws when a study is conducted in Canada. The RI-MUHC and the MUHC REB operate in compliance with the good clinical practice guideline (E6) of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH), Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations, the *Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2) and the Declaration of Helsinki. They are not governed by another country's regulations unless they are obliged to be by a contractual agreement.

<Note>
If a US sponsor asks you to sign a Form FDA 1572 and does not provide a Canadian version of its investigator commitment form, [the RI-MUHC's SOPs recommend](/sops/cr-002#5-3-8) completing and signing the RI-MUHC's ICH GCP attestation form ([SOP-CR-002, Appendix 4](/sops/cr-002#appendix-4)) instead. With the QIU, it gives the sponsor the information the 1572 asks for, without the commitment to follow US regulations. Canadian sites can take part in a study under a US investigational new drug application (IND) without signing a 1572, and the data they collect can still support the marketing application. For advice on a sponsor's request, write to [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca).
</Note>

## TMF and ISF

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

In an industry-sponsored study, the sponsor keeps the <Tooltip tip="The sponsor's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites." headline="Trial Master File (TMF)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#tmf">trial master file</Tooltip>, which holds the essential documents from every participating site. Each site keeps its own investigator site file, with the study-specific essential documents for that site. The trial master file holds the sponsor's documents, such as monitoring reports, regulatory submissions, logs of serious unexpected adverse reactions and the master randomization list. A sponsor-investigator sets up, maintains and updates the trial master file. If you are also the principal investigator at a site of a multi-centre study, best practice is to keep both a trial master file, for the whole study, and an investigator site file, for the MUHC site. Each participating site keeps its own investigator site file. For a single-site study, [only one combined file is required](/sops/cr-007#3-0-responsibilities). ## Annual safety reporting: DSUR and PSUR

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

Industry sponsors produce annual safety reports that bring together the safety information collected at every site worldwide. A sponsor-investigator holds the sponsor's role directly, so a report that Health Canada asks for falls to you. Study planning often overlooks this.

### Development Safety Update Report (DSUR)

The **Development Safety Update Report (DSUR)** is an annual safety report for an investigational drug still in development. Its format and content are set by the ICH's guideline E2F, which Health Canada has adopted. The DSUR:

- Covers 12 months, counted from the Development International Birth Date (DIBD), the date of the sponsor's first authorization to conduct a clinical trial in any country.
- Summarizes the safety data collected worldwide during the period, including serious unexpected adverse reactions, new information from non-clinical studies, the updated assessment of benefits and risks, and any action taken in response to safety findings.
- Goes to Health Canada only when Health Canada asks for it (since Dec. 4, 2015). A sponsor may also send one voluntarily, with a justification in the cover letter, when important new safety information on a drug needs to be conveyed.
- Follows the E2F structure even for a single-site study of one indication, although such a DSUR is simpler than a company's.

The sponsor-investigator prepares the DSUR, or engages a qualified pharmacovigilance consultant to do it. No company prepares it for you. Consider budgeting the time it takes, and including its cost in grant applications.

When Health Canada asks for a DSUR, it should be sent with Health Canada's DSUR Checklist to its Office of Submissions and Intellectual Property, in electronic format: through the Common Electronic Submission Gateway in eCTD format or, if that isn't possible, in the "non-eCTD electronic-only" format on a disc. Health Canada's [DSUR notice](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines/guidance-document-development-safety-update-report-dsur-international-conference-harmonisation-topic.html) gives the details. Give your CTA's submission tracking number.

### PSUR and the PSUR-RRP pilot

The **Periodic Safety Update Report (PSUR)** is for marketed products, as part of safety surveillance after approval, while the DSUR is for investigational products still in development. Many sponsor-investigator studies at the RI-MUHC test a drug already approved for another indication. The relevant report is then the DSUR, because the product is not approved for the use under study.

Health Canada launched the [PSUR Regulatory Review Pilot (PSUR-RRP)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/product-vigilance/questions-answers-implementation-risk-prioritized-periodic-safety-update-report-regulatory-review-pilot-psur-drug-products.html) to test a risk-prioritized review of PSURs for marketed products. The pilot affects mainly companies with marketed products, and it doesn't change how Health Canada handles DSURs from sponsor-investigators.

If you are unsure whether your study may need a DSUR, a PSUR or both, write to [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) before the study is activated. The answer depends on the product's regulatory status (approved or investigational), the study design and the authorization the study holds from Health Canada.

## Investigator-led device studies: the ITA

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies that need Health Canada authorization</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Studies you sponsor yourself (sponsor-investigator)</span></div>

A study of a Class II, III or IV medical device that needs Health Canada's authorization gets it through an <Tooltip tip="Health Canada's authorization for a manufacturer or importer to sell a Class II, III or IV medical device to qualified investigators for testing." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#ita">investigational testing authorization (ITA)</Tooltip>, not a CTA. [Laws, Regulations and Guidelines](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization) explains which device studies need one. Academic investigators need to understand one constraint of the ITA before they plan a device study.

### Only the manufacturer or importer can apply

Under the Medical Devices Regulations, only the manufacturer or the importer of a device can apply for an ITA, and a senior official of the manufacturer must sign the application. If an investigator-initiated trial involves an unlicensed device, an ITA is required, and the investigator must obtain the cooperation of the manufacturer, which must be the official applicant and signatory. The manufacturer can delegate the regulatory correspondence and the clinical oversight to the investigator.

So a sponsor-investigator of a drug trial applies for the CTA on their own, while a sponsor-investigator of a device study cannot obtain an ITA without the manufacturer, even if the manufacturer has no other role in the study.

### Consequences for academic device research

The constraint affects several common situations in academic device research:

- An investigator who tests a licensed device for a new indication, in a study the manufacturer doesn't sponsor and that won't support a licence application, needs no ITA: Health Canada treats it as off-label use. An investigator who tests an unlicensed device needs the manufacturer's cooperation for the ITA, even if the manufacturer has no financial interest in the study.
- Whoever modifies a device can become its manufacturer: Health Canada's definition of a manufacturer includes the person responsible for modifying a device, and an academic institution can be a manufacturer if it designed or owns the device.
- A researcher who develops a new device prototype must determine whether it meets the regulatory definition of a device that needs an ITA. The [Investigational Testing division](mailto:it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca) can help determine whether an ITA application is required.

### Pending Health Canada amendment

Health Canada has proposed an amendment to the Medical Devices Regulations that would let investigators apply for an ITA on their own, without the manufacturer's consent, for devices already legally marketed in Canada. The amendment had not been finalized as of May 2026.

**Do not plan a device study on the assumption that the amendment is in force.** The current rule applies: only the manufacturer or importer applies. Write to [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) and the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) at the earliest stage of planning a device study, to assess the ITA and the manufacturer's involvement it needs.

### Pre-ITA meeting with Health Canada

For a novel or complex device study, the manufacturer or importer can ask Health Canada for a pre-ITA application meeting, to discuss the study design, the device's classification and the ITA's requirements before applying. Health Canada encourages these meetings particularly for novel Class III and IV devices and combination products. The manufacturer or importer may invite the qualified investigators who will take part in the study, and sends the request in writing to the [Investigational Testing division](mailto:it-ee@hc-sc.gc.ca). Consider involving Quality Assurance at least 6 months before you expect the application to be submitted, if a pre-ITA meeting is warranted.
