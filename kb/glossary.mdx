This glossary covers the terms and acronyms you will encounter across the RI-MUHC clinical research SOPs and Knowledge Base. Entries marked **Quebec** are specific to Quebec's regulatory or legislative framework. Entries marked **RI-MUHC** are institutional definitions specific to the RI-MUHC context.

<Note>
This glossary contains approximately 200 terms. Use your browser's search (Ctrl+F / Cmd+F) to locate a specific term quickly.
</Note>

## A

<AccordionGroup>
  <a id="adr"></a>
  <Accordion title="Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)">
    In the pre-approval clinical experience with a new medicinal or natural health product, all noxious and unintended responses related to any dose. For marketed products: a noxious, unintended response at doses normally used for prophylaxis, diagnosis, or therapy. A causal relationship between the product and the event is at least a reasonable possibility.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="ae"></a>
  <Accordion title="Adverse Event (AE)">
    Any untoward medical occurrence in a participant administered an investigational product, whether or not related to that product. Includes any unfavorable and unintended sign, symptom, or disease temporarily associated with use of an investigational product.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="ALCOA+">
    The principles governing good documentation practice: **Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, Accurate** — plus Complete, Consistent, Enduring, and Available. All research records must meet ALCOA+ standards.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Annotation (CRF/eCRF)">
    A representation of the variables and tables in a paper CRF or data entry screens, mapping data fields to the study database.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Applicable Regulatory Requirements">
    Any laws or regulations governing the conduct of clinical trials of investigational products in a given jurisdiction.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Approached Participant">
    A patient or volunteer who has been identified for a study, has had the informed consent process initiated, has been given a consent form, and has been invited to participate.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Approval (REB)">
    The affirmative decision of a Research Ethics Board that a trial has been reviewed and may be conducted at the institution within the constraints set by the REB, the institution, GCP, and applicable regulatory requirements.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Audit">
    A systematic and independent examination of trial-related activities and documents to determine whether activities were conducted and data recorded and reported according to the approved protocol, SOPs, GCP, and applicable regulatory requirements.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Audit Certificate">
    A declaration by an auditor confirming that an audit has taken place.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Audit Report">
    A written evaluation by the sponsor's auditor of the results of an audit.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Audit Trail">
    Documentation that allows reconstruction of the course of events in a clinical trial.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## B

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Bioanalysis">
    The measurement of a drug level or its metabolites in a biological sample such as plasma, serum, urine, or other human biological matrix.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Biologic">
    A drug prepared from a biological starting material derived from a microorganism, virus, animal, human, or plant. Examples include vaccines, blood derivatives, hormones, enzymes, recombinant DNA products, and gene therapies.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Biological Product">
    A product derived from living organisms and used to prevent, treat, or diagnose disease in humans or animals, or for development, experiment, or investigation purposes.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Biological Sampling">
    The collecting, processing, and analyzing of biological samples from study participants.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Blinding / Masking">
    A procedure in which one or more parties to the trial are kept unaware of treatment assignments. **Single-blinding:** the participant is unaware. **Double-blinding:** participant, investigator, monitor, and sometimes data analyst are all unaware.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## C

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Calibration">
    A set of operations that establish, under specified conditions, the relationship between values indicated by a measuring instrument and the corresponding values realized by standards. Results may be recorded in a calibration certificate or report.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="crf"></a>
  <Accordion title="Case Report Form (CRF)">
    A printed, optical, or electronic document designed to record all protocol-required information to be reported to the sponsor on each trial participant.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="CATALIS Québec (Quebec)">
    The clinical trial facilitation platform operated by Clinical Trials Quebec, enabling coordinated multi-site submission and review across Quebec RSSS institutions. The FAST TRACK stream provides an accelerated single-window process, coordinating scientific, ethics, and feasibility evaluations simultaneously.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Causality / Attribution Assessment">
    The investigator's determination, based on clinical judgment, of whether there is a reasonable causal relationship between an investigational product and an adverse event. Attribution may be: related, not related, or unknown.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Certified Copy">
    A paper or electronic copy of an original record that has been verified by a dated signature, or generated through a validated process, to be an exact copy with all the same attributes as the original.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="CIM — RI-MUHC (RI-MUHC)">
    The McConnell Centre for Innovative Medicine at the RI-MUHC. CIM provides operational infrastructure and support services for industry-sponsored clinical trials at the Glen site, including study coordination, pharmacy, regulatory affairs support, and participant care facilities. Most industry-sponsored studies at the RI-MUHC run through CIM.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Civil Code of Quebec / C.c.Q. (Quebec)">
    Provincial legislation governing research with human participants in Quebec. Articles 20–25 set binding conditions for research affecting personal integrity, including consent requirements, protections for minors and incapacitated adults (Art. 21), and prohibitions on financial compensation beyond actual loss reimbursement (Art. 25).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clean Database">
    A database from which errors have been eliminated following query resolution and review, in preparation for database lock.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Development Plan (CDP)">
    A high-level document prepared by the sponsor that outlines the strategy for developing an investigational product from early research through to regulatory approval, including the planned sequence of clinical trials.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Equipoise">
    The genuine uncertainty within the expert medical community about which of the interventions in a trial is superior. Must exist at the outset of a Phase III trial: if one option is clearly better, it is not ethical to assign participants to the other.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Hold">
    An order issued by the FDA to a sponsor to delay a proposed clinical investigation or suspend an ongoing one. When a study is on clinical hold, no new participants may be enrolled.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Investigation Plan (CIP)">
    The ISO-GCP term for the document equivalent to a protocol in a drug trial. Used in medical device studies. Synonymous with protocol.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Research Associate (CRA)">
    Also called a monitor. A person employed by a sponsor or CRO responsible for verifying that a trial is being conducted in accordance with the protocol at the site. Works with the site CRC to review all data and documentation.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="crc"></a>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)">
    A nurse, health professional, or qualified team member who handles most administrative responsibilities and day-to-day activities of a clinical trial. Acts as liaison between the site and the sponsor. Also called: trial coordinator, study coordinator, research nurse, protocol nurse.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Study Agreement (CSA) (RI-MUHC)">
    A written, signed, dated agreement between parties setting out task delegation, obligations, and financial matters. Also known as a Clinical Trial Agreement (CTA). Note: CSA is preferred at the RI-MUHC to avoid confusion with Clinical Trial Application.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Study Report (CSR)">
    A written description of a study of any therapeutic, prophylactic, or diagnostic agent in human participants, integrating clinical and statistical descriptions, presentations, and analyses into a single report.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Trial">
    Any research project that prospectively assigns human participants to intervention or comparison groups to study cause-and-effect relationships between a medical intervention and a health outcome. The terms clinical trial and clinical study are synonymous.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="cta"></a>
  <Accordion title="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)">
    An application submitted to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a clinical trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations. Required for Phase I–III drug trials.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="cov"></a>
  <Accordion title="Close-Out Visit (COV)">
    A final monitoring visit conducted by the sponsor's monitor at the investigational site to verify that all required aspects of study closure have been addressed prior to database lock.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Coded Data (De-identified)">
    Participant data assigned a random code with direct identifiers held separately. Single-coded: key is available only to authorized researchers. Double/multiple-coded: key is held by a third party not available to researchers.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE)">
    A standardized grading system published by the US National Cancer Institute for classifying adverse events. Grades 1–5 reflect severity from mild to death. Widely used as the AE grading scale in oncology and other clinical trial protocols.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Continuing Review">
    Periodic ethics review of an ongoing study conducted by the REB/CER after initial approval. Required at least annually under TCPS2. Ensures the risk-benefit balance remains acceptable as new information emerges.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Contract Research Organization (CRO)">
    A person or organization contracted by a sponsor to perform one or more sponsor study-related duties and functions.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="capa"></a>
  <Accordion title="Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA)">
    A series of actions taken to resolve a compliance issue and prevent recurrence. Steps: identify the issue, correct it, prevent recurrence.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## D

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Data Management Plan (DMP)">
    A document describing how clinical trial data will be collected, processed, cleaned, and archived. Defines roles, systems, timelines, and quality control procedures.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)">
    See Independent Data-Monitoring Committee.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Database Lock">
    Action taken to prevent further changes to a clinical trial database after review, query resolution, and confirmation that the database is ready for analysis.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="tdl"></a>
  <Accordion title="Delegation Log">
    Also called a Task Delegation Log. A document listing each study team member, their qualifications, and the specific trial tasks authorized by the Qualified Investigator. Required under GCP. A core element of the Investigator Site File.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Direct Access">
    Permission to examine, analyze, verify, and reproduce any records and reports important to evaluation of a clinical study. Parties with direct access include regulatory authorities and sponsor auditors.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Documentation">
    All records, in any form — written, electronic, magnetic, optical, scans, x-rays, ECGs — that describe or record the methods, conduct, and results of a study, the factors affecting it, and the actions taken.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Drug Accountability">
    The process of being responsible for investigational product at the study site by following safe pharmaceutical practices. The investigator is responsible for IP accountability, including receipt, storage, dispensing, return, and destruction records.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Drug Identification Number (DIN)">
    A number assigned by Health Canada to a drug product authorized for marketing in Canada.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## E

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="E-consent">
    Informed consent obtained or documented through electronic means, including digital signatures, electronic consent platforms, or remote video-assisted consent processes. Permitted under ICH GCP E6(R3) and TCPS2 with appropriate safeguards.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Electronic Case Report Form (eCRF)">
    An electronic version of the Case Report Form used to capture and transmit participant data to the sponsor.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Electronic Data Capture (EDC)">
    A computerized system for collecting clinical trial data electronically rather than on paper.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Electronic Medical Record (EMR) (RI-MUHC)">
    A digital record of patient health information maintained within a single institution. At the RI-MUHC, the primary EMR is OACIS.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Essential Documents">
    Documents which individually and collectively permit evaluation of the conduct of a study and the quality of the data produced. For drug/NHP: ICH-GCP E6, section 8. For devices: ISO-GCP, Annex E.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Exclusion Criteria">
    A list of criteria, any one of which excludes a potential participant from a study.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="efvp"></a>
  <Accordion title="Évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (ÉFVP) (Quebec)">
    A Privacy Impact Assessment required under Quebec Law 25 before communicating personal information without consent for research, or when deploying systems that access personal data. Must be engaged early in study planning. Non-compliance penalties: up to $25 million or 4% of worldwide turnover.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## F – G

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="FAST TRACK (Quebec)">
    The accelerated submission and review stream within the CATALIS platform for multi-site studies in Quebec. Coordinates scientific, ethics, and feasibility evaluations simultaneously to reduce site activation timelines.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Fonds de recherche du Québec — Santé (FRQS) (Quebec)">
    Quebec's provincial health research funding agency. Funds health research across Quebec institutions including the RI-MUHC. FRQS-funded projects are subject to the same ethics governance framework as federally funded research.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="gcp"></a>
  <Accordion title="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)">
    An international standard for the design, conduct, monitoring, auditing, recording, analysis, and reporting of clinical studies. Provides assurance that data are credible and accurate, and that participant rights and confidentiality are protected. Drug studies follow ICH GCP E6(R3); medical device studies follow ISO 14155.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Good Documentation Practice (GDP)">
    A standard for effective control and management of documentation following ALCOA+ principles.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## H – I

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Health Canada (HC)">
    The federal department responsible for helping Canadians maintain and improve their health. Regulates drugs, medical devices, and natural health products, including clinical trial applications under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Human Research (RI-MUHC) (RI-MUHC)">
    At the RI-MUHC, any research involving human participants or biological material, human embryos, fetuses, fetal tissue, reproductive materials, and stem cells from living or deceased individuals.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="ICH GCP E6(R3)">
    The current version of the ICH Good Clinical Practice guideline, in effect in Canada as of April 1, 2026. Core shift: quality by design — identifying critical-to-quality factors before the study begins and building proportionate controls. Also formalizes risk-based monitoring, electronic data governance, and decentralized trial elements.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Impartial Witness">
    A person independent of the study who attends the informed consent process when the participant or their representative cannot read.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Inclusion Criteria">
    The criteria that prospective participants must meet to be eligible for enrollment in a study.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="dsmb"></a>
  <Accordion title="Independent Data-Monitoring Committee (IDMC)">
    Also called a Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB). An independent committee that assesses at intervals the progress of a clinical study, safety data, and critical efficacy endpoints, and recommends whether to continue, modify, or stop the study.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Independent Ethics Committee (IEC)">
    An independent body that reviews biomedical research involving human participants to protect their rights, safety, and well-being. The IEC is the international term; the Canadian equivalent is Research Ethics Board (REB); the French-language equivalent at the MUHC is Comité d'éthique à la recherche (CER).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Informed Consent">
    A process by which a participant voluntarily confirms willingness to participate after being informed of all aspects relevant to their decision. Under Quebec's Civil Code and TCPS2, consent must be ongoing — not a one-time event.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="icf"></a>
  <Accordion title="Informed Consent Form (ICF)">
    A written document providing the participant with information essential to making an informed decision about participation. Both the participant and the person obtaining consent must sign.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Inspection">
    The act by a regulatory authority of conducting an official review of documents, facilities, records, and other resources related to a clinical trial.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="ip"></a>
  <Accordion title="Investigational Product (IP)">
    A pharmaceutical form of an active ingredient or placebo, a medical device, or a natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="ita"></a>
  <Accordion title="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)">
    Authorization from Health Canada required to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="ib"></a>
  <Accordion title="Investigator Brochure (IB)">
    A compilation of clinical and nonclinical data on the investigational product relevant to the study, informing site investigators of the product's known safety and efficacy profile.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="isf"></a>
  <Accordion title="Investigator Site File (ISF)">
    The QI's collection of documentation demonstrating compliance with regulations, study integrity, and participant safety. Should mirror the sponsor's Trial Master File. See ICH-GCP section 8 (drugs) or ISO-14155 Annex E (devices).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Investigator-Initiated Study">
    A research study developed, initiated, and conducted by an investigator who assumes the combined obligations of both sponsor and investigator.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## L – M

<AccordionGroup>
  <a id="law-25"></a>
  <Accordion title="Law 25 / Loi 25 (Quebec)">
    *An Act to modernize legislative provisions as regards the protection of personal information*, in force September 2023. Requires a Privacy Impact Assessment (ÉFVP) before communicating personal information without consent for research. Non-compliance penalties: up to $25 million or 4% of worldwide turnover.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="law-5"></a>
  <Accordion title="Law 5 / Loi 5 (Quebec)">
    *An Act respecting health and social services information*, with key provisions in force July 2024. Creates a dedicated framework for health data access. Affiliated researchers access data through their institution's information officer; unaffiliated researchers apply through Santé Québec.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Legally Acceptable Representative (LAR)">
    An individual or body authorized under applicable law to consent on behalf of a prospective participant. In Quebec, governed by the Civil Code including provisions for minors (Art. 21) and incapacitated adults.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Loi sur les services de santé et les services sociaux (LSSSS) (Quebec)">
    Quebec's Act respecting health and social services. Governs the organization of the Quebec health network (RSSS) and the obligations of its institutions, including research governance.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Medical Device">
    A device within the meaning of the Food and Drugs Act used for a medical purpose, not including devices intended for use in relation to animals.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Medical Oversight">
    A physician's supervision of protocol tasks that are medical in nature: physical examinations, prescription scripts, adverse reaction assessments, and medical follow-up.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Monitoring">
    The act of overseeing the progress of a clinical trial and ensuring it is conducted, recorded, and reported in accordance with the protocol, SOPs, GCP, and applicable regulatory requirements.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Monitoring Plan (MP)">
    A plan developed by the sponsor or monitor, tailored to the participant protection and data integrity risks of the specific study. Describes monitoring strategy, responsibilities, and methods.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="muhc-auth"></a>
  <Accordion title="MUHC Authorization (RI-MUHC)">
    The institutional authorization allowing a study to proceed at the RI-MUHC. Distinct from REB/CER approval. Issued after scientific evaluation, ethics approval, and feasibility review are complete. No study activity — including participant identification — may begin without it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Multi-centrique Framework (Quebec)">
    A reference framework serving as an agreement between Quebec public RSSS institutions so that multi-site studies undergo a single ethical review, recognized by each participating institution. Administered through CATALIS.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## N – O

<AccordionGroup>
  <a id="nagano"></a>
  <Accordion title="Nagano (RI-MUHC)">
    The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. Used to submit study applications, track review progress, manage amendments, and generate institutional authorizations. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="nol"></a>
  <Accordion title="No Objection Letter (NOL)">
    A letter issued by Health Canada when a Clinical Trial Application has been deemed satisfactory, authorizing the sponsor to proceed with the study.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Note to File (NTF)">
    A document added to the study file to explain, clarify, or document an event, deviation, or decision not captured in standard records. Maintains a complete and accurate audit trail.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Notice of Compliance (NOC)">
    A notification issued by Health Canada to a manufacturer following satisfactory review of a drug submission. Constitutes market authorization in Canada.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Observational Study">
    A study in which participants are assessed for health outcomes without the investigator assigning them to a specific intervention. Includes cohort studies, case-control studies, and patient registries.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## P – Q

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Participant">
    An individual — patient or healthy volunteer — who participates in a clinical study as a recipient of the investigational product or as a control. Also: study participant, research participant, study patient.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Patient-Reported Outcomes (PRO)">
    Measures of a patient's health status reported directly by the patient, without interpretation by a clinician. PRO instruments must be validated and are subject to the same data management standards as other CRF data.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="personne-mandatee"></a>
  <Accordion title="Personne formellement mandatée (PFM) (Quebec)">
    An individual formally authorized by the Board of Directors of a Quebec RSSS institution to issue the final written authorization for each research project. No study may begin — not even participant identification — without the PFM's written authorization.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Pharmacokinetics / Pharmacodynamics (PK / PD)">
    **PK:** how the body affects a drug — absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion. **PD:** how the drug affects the body — the relationship between drug concentration and effect. Common in Phase I and II trials.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="pi"></a>
  <Accordion title="Principal Investigator (PI)">
    See Qualified Investigator. PI is primarily US terminology; the Canadian regulatory equivalent is Qualified Investigator (QI).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Privileges — Research (RI-MUHC)">
    General institutional permission for an investigator to conduct human research at the RI-MUHC. A prerequisite for being named as QI on a study.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Protocol">
    A document describing the objectives, design, methodology, statistical considerations, and organization of a trial. In ISO-GCP (device studies), referred to as the Clinical Investigation Plan (CIP).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Protocol Amendment">
    A written description of a change or formal clarification to a protocol, requiring REB and, where applicable, Health Canada approval before implementation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Protocol Deviation / Violation">
    An incident involving non-compliance with the protocol that may or may not have a significant effect on participant rights, safety, welfare, or data integrity.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="qi"></a>
  <Accordion title="Qualified Investigator (QI)">
    As defined by Health Canada: the person responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at a site, who is entitled to provide health care under provincial law and is a physician in good standing. Only one QI per study per Canadian site.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Qualified Investigator's Undertaking (QIU)">
    A form submitted to Health Canada as part of the CTA package, signed by the Qualified Investigator, acknowledging their responsibilities for the conduct of the clinical trial at the site.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="qa"></a>
  <Accordion title="Quality Assurance (QA)">
    All planned and systematic actions established to ensure a trial is performed, and data generated, documented, and reported in compliance with SOPs, GCP, and applicable regulatory requirements.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Quality by Design">
    A core principle of ICH GCP E6(R3). Requires quality to be built into study design from the outset by identifying critical-to-quality factors and implementing proportionate controls.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## R

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Randomization">
    The process of assigning trial participants to treatment or control groups using an element of chance, to reduce bias.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Re-consent">
    The process of obtaining renewed informed consent when new information emerges that could affect a participant's willingness to continue. Initial consent does not authorize participation under conditions that did not exist at the time of signing.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Recruitment">
    The processes and activities used to identify and enroll patients or volunteers into a clinical trial, from an eligible base population through to signed consent and formal enrollment.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="reb"></a>
  <Accordion title="Research Ethics Board (REB) (RI-MUHC)">
    A body — also called a Comité d'éthique à la recherche (CER) at French-language institutions — whose mandate is to approve and periodically review biomedical research involving human participants. At the RI-MUHC, the ethics committee is referred to as the CER.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Retrospective Study">
    Research conducted by reviewing records from the past, such as medical records, administrative databases, or biobank samples, rather than following participants forward in time.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Risk-Based Monitoring (RBM)">
    A monitoring approach in which monitoring activities are tailored to the specific participant protection and data integrity risks of a study rather than applied uniformly. Formalized in ICH GCP E6(R2) and expanded in E6(R3).
  </Accordion>
  <a id="rsss"></a>
  <Accordion title="RSSS (Quebec)">
    Réseau de la santé et des services sociaux — Quebec's public health and social services network. Comprises the CIUSSS, CISSS, CHUs, and affiliated research institutes including the RI-MUHC. The RSSS structure determines which governance rules apply to research.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## S

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Santé Québec (Quebec)">
    The provincial body designated as the central access point for health data for unaffiliated researchers under Law 5. Researchers not affiliated with a RSSS institution who require access to health and social services data must apply through Santé Québec.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="sae"></a>
  <Accordion title="Serious Adverse Event (SAE)">
    Any adverse event meeting the seriousness criteria: death, life-threatening, hospitalization, disability, congenital anomaly, or other medically important condition.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="siv"></a>
  <Accordion title="Site Initiation Visit (SIV)">
    A monitoring visit conducted as a final evaluation of the site and training of site team members on the study protocol and processes. Conducted after REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="SOP Authorized Signatory (RI-MUHC)">
    An administrator, investigator, or research team member qualified by experience, skills, and training to provide final approval of SOPs at the RI-MUHC.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Source Data">
    All information in original records and certified copies necessary for reconstruction and evaluation of the trial.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Source Documents">
    Original documents, data, and records including hospital records, clinical charts, laboratory notes, pharmacy dispensing records, participant diaries, and recorded data from automated instruments.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sponsor">
    An individual, company, institution, or organization that takes responsibility for the initiation, management, and/or financing of a clinical trial.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sponsor-Investigator">
    An individual who both initiates and conducts a clinical trial, carrying the combined obligations of both sponsor and investigator.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) (RI-MUHC)">
    Detailed written instructions to achieve uniformity in the performance of a specific function. At the RI-MUHC, 33 clinical research SOPs govern study conduct, organized into the CR series, Sponsor-Investigator series (100s), and 500 series.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Sub-Investigator">
    A qualified team member designated and supervised by the QI to perform specific investigator tasks as described on the Task Delegation Log. At least one Sub-Investigator should be delegated on each study as backup for the QI.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="susar"></a>
  <Accordion title="SUSAR">
    **Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction.** A serious adverse drug or NHP reaction that is not identified in nature, severity, or frequency in the Investigator's Brochure or product label. Must be reported to Health Canada and the REB within specific timelines.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## T – W

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Task Delegation Log">
    See Delegation Log.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="TCPS2">
    The Tri-Council Policy Statement (second edition, current version 2022): Canada's national standard for ethical conduct in human research. Governs all research at institutions receiving CIHR, NSERC, or SSHRC funding. Three core principles: respect for persons, concern for welfare, and justice.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Therapeutic Misconception">
    The tendency for research participants to experience a research intervention as personalized treatment rather than as a scientific investigation. Named in TCPS2 as a risk to genuine informed consent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Therapeutics Products Directorate (TPD)">
    The division within Health Canada responsible for evaluating and monitoring the safety, effectiveness, and quality of pharmaceutical drugs and medical devices for human use. The Canadian federal authority that must authorize drug CTAs.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Transport of Dangerous Goods (TDG)">
    Canadian federal regulations governing the classification, packaging, labelling, and transport of dangerous goods, including biological specimens and infectious substances.
  </Accordion>
  <a id="tmf"></a>
  <Accordion title="Trial Master File (TMF)">
    The sponsor's collection of human research documentation demonstrating compliance across all study sites. Should mirror the ISF at each site.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Triple Evaluation (Quebec)">
    The three-part authorization process required before any research project can begin at a Quebec RSSS institution: (1) scientific evaluation; (2) ethics evaluation by the CER; (3) institutional feasibility (*convenance*) including the ÉFVP. All three must be complete and positive before authorization is issued.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Unanticipated Adverse Device Effect (UADE)">
    Any serious adverse effect on health or safety caused by a device that was not previously identified in nature, severity, or degree of incidence in the investigational plan, or any other unanticipated serious problem associated with a device.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Vulnerable Participants">
    Individuals whose willingness to participate may be unduly influenced by expected benefits or fear of retaliation, including: students, subordinate personnel, detained persons, patients with incurable diseases, nursing home residents, impoverished persons, minors, and those incapable of giving consent.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Wet Signature">
    A handwritten signature applied in ink by the signatory in person, as opposed to an electronic or digital signature. Required for many essential documents in clinical research.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
