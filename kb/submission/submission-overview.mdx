Every study under the MUHC's responsibility goes through three reviews before it can start: a scientific review, an ethics review by the Research Ethics Board (REB) and an institutional feasibility review. Many drug, medical device and natural health product trials also need Health Canada's authorization. Once the study has been approved in all three reviews, the <Tooltip tip="Individual formally authorized to issue the final written authorization for each research project at a Quebec RSSS institution." headline="Personne mandatée (PFM)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip>, the person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research, issues MUHC Authorization.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

## How a study is reviewed

Before you submit, confirm that your project is research. A quality assurance or quality improvement project used only for assessment, management or improvement doesn't need REB review; the [Is It Research?](/is-it-research) tool helps you decide and explains how to get an exemption letter.

You submit your study in <Tooltip tip="The RI-MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (RI-MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> with the F11 form, which starts the institutional feasibility review and, where one applies, the ethics review. If no recognized peer committee has already reviewed the science, the REB also carries out the scientific review. The institutional feasibility review starts at the same time as the REB's review. A trial that needs Health Canada's authorization also has its own application to Health Canada.

Review time depends on the study's level of risk, how complete your submission is, whether revisions are needed and the REB's workload. There are no submission deadlines, so enter your study in Nagano as soon as it's ready. Staff of the Centre for Applied Ethics first check each submission for completeness and the quality of its documents, and assess its level of risk.

Full-board review is the default. Studies that involve no more than minimal risk are eligible for delegated review, which runs continuously.

Studies with more than minimal risk are reviewed at a full-board meeting of one of the REB's six specialized panels, which meet about twice a month; the meeting calendar is in Nagano. A new project that could interfere with the integrity of a minor or of an adult who can't consent cannot go through delegated review at its first review.

Incomplete submissions are a common cause of delay, so check yours against [Preparing Your Nagano Submission](/kb/submission/preparing) before you submit.

## Scientific and ethics review by the REB

The REB is responsible for the scientific review, which checks that the study is methodologically sound: its design, data collection, statistical methods and the team's qualifications. You request it in your Nagano submission. The REB may recognize a peer review already done by a recognized committee, such as a granting agency's; a peer-reviewed funded project isn't reviewed again at the MUHC if you provide documentation of that review, such as the reviewers' comments and your responses.

Your study needs review by an REB of Quebec's health and social services network (the MUHC REB or, for a multi-site study, the evaluating REB) and the MUHC's authorization when it:
- Recruits participants at the MUHC, in person or through the MUHC's technology, or among its staff.
- Uses health records stored at the MUHC.
- Takes place at least partly at the MUHC, including by MUHC-affiliated researchers abroad.
- Involves an investigator or sponsor-investigator affiliated with the MUHC.
- Uses the MUHC's staff services, such as nursing or pharmacy, its technical platforms or its other human, material or financial resources.
- Uses or creates a data repository or tissue bank hosted at the MUHC or held by an MUHC-affiliated researcher.
- Is presented by its sponsor or researcher, openly or by implication, as involving the MUHC or an MUHC affiliation.

Research that relies only on information that is publicly available through a legal mechanism and protected by law, or that is in the public domain with no reasonable expectation of privacy, doesn't need REB review. If you're unsure, ask the Research Facilitator before you submit.

A study at more than one public institution in Quebec is reviewed by one Quebec evaluating REB, and each institution still does its own [feasibility review and authorization](/kb/design/multi-centric).

In a study with [sites in other provinces](/kb/design/cross-border-studies), each site submits to its own REB. For those sites, some networks run streamlined ethics review, such as Clinical Trials Ontario (CTO) and, for child health research, the Canadian Collaboration for Child Health: Efficiency and Excellence in the Ethics Review of Research (CHEER). Ask the Research Facilitator early about your study's arrangement.

After its review, the REB decides to:

| Decision | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Approve | The study is approved as submitted. |
| Approve with modifications | Not an approval yet. The study cannot start until you've answered every condition and the REB has issued its final approval. |
| Defer | The REB needs more information or major changes, and reviews the study again once you resubmit. |
| Disapprove | The study cannot go ahead as proposed. |

Answer the REB's questions by the deadline in [Tracking Your Submission and Responding to Requests](/kb/submission/tracking-responding), or the study may be closed.

You can ask the REB to reconsider a decision when you can justify your grounds, and you have the right to be heard at a full board meeting. If the REB maintains a disapproval, it may offer a second hearing before a separate quorum, whose decision is final.

If you decline that hearing, you can appeal, on procedural or substantive grounds, to another REB of Quebec's health and social services network, chosen jointly by the REB and you; the appeal committee's decision is final.

## Institutional feasibility review

In the institutional feasibility review:
- Quality Assurance checks the Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status, and institutional training, of each investigator who submits the study.
- The Research Agreements Office reviews the study's agreements, which you upload to both Nagano and My Contracts and which must be fully executed before MUHC Authorization.
- The Director of Professional Services must approve the study's use of MUHC resources.
- Nursing, laboratory and pharmacy services review the study when it uses them.
- Clinical departments give their approval, and an information security review covers any software, platforms or databases the study uses.
- Any other site-specific review the study calls for takes place.

Answer Nagano's questions on nursing, laboratory and pharmacy use accurately, because the answers trigger those reviews. All drugs and some natural health products must be managed by the pharmacy, and you then receive a pharmacy agreement to sign. If the study needs OPTILAB-MUHC laboratory services, a laboratory feasibility review and a laboratory agreement follow. If the study uses MUHC nursing services, upload to Nagano a nursing feasibility form signed by the nurse manager.

You should never sign a binding contract or agreement before the Research Agreements Office has reviewed it, and investigators must not sign on behalf of the RI-MUHC.

At the Montreal Neurological Institute-Hospital (the Neuro), some processes differ, including contracts managed through McGill University's contracting office and Neuro resources such as neuroimaging platforms and surgical suites; ask its administrative offices.

If the study uses identifiable health information held by the MUHC without participants' consent, pre-screening included, the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC, completes an évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP), the privacy impact assessment that Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information requires. Complete it in French and submit it in Nagano with your F11, using form F17 for pre-screening before consent or form F18 for research using identifiable records without consent.

The MUHC's Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality reviews and approves the EFVP. A study at several Quebec institutions files one EFVP, from the lead site. Check [when an EFVP is needed](/kb/recruitment/privacy).

Studies funded wholly or partly by private enterprise pay the [review and authorization fees](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) set by the Ministry of Health and Social Services when the private funding exceeds the exemption amount. For the MUHC REB, private enterprise means any private-law body, not-for-profit organizations included.

## Health Canada authorization

Plan the Health Canada application during study design.

The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs) have the sponsor obtain Health Canada's approval first, whether a No Objection Letter (NOL) for a drug, a Notice of Authorization (NOA) for a natural health product or an Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA) for a device. The sponsor sends the letter to the site, which includes it in the initial Nagano submission.

The order can differ. An application for a natural health product trial must include each site's REB attestation, so the REB approves first. For a Class III or IV device, Health Canada needs evidence of the REB's approval before the trial starts, ideally with the ITA application. Either way, the study cannot begin until Health Canada's authorization, where the trial needs one, has been obtained.

The RI-MUHC's Regulatory Affairs Specialist, Lori Anderson ([lcoaconsulting@gmail.com](mailto:lcoaconsulting@gmail.com)), can help on a fee basis; researchers cover the specialist's costs, and the Pre-Awards Office handles the invoicing. For a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor files the application; if you initiated the study as its sponsor-investigator, that's you. For a device, the manufacturer or importer holds the authorization.

| Trial | What Health Canada requires |
|---|---|
| A drug, unless the trial uses a marketed drug within its authorized use | A Clinical Trial Application (CTA). The sponsor may proceed if Health Canada doesn't object within 30 days of receiving the application, and once each site's REB has approved the protocol and consent form. |
| A medical device | An ITA for an unlicensed Class II, III or IV device, or for a licensed device used in a manufacturer-sponsored study to support a new indication. For a Class III or IV device, the application also includes the institution's written approval for the testing to take place there. Class I devices, and licensed devices used within their licensed indications, need no ITA. |
| A natural health product, unless the trial stays within a licensed product's recommended use | Authorization under the Natural Health Products Regulations. |

## Before recruitment, and keeping approval valid

Recruitment begins only after all of:
- Health Canada's authorization, if the study needs it.
- The REB approval letter and the MUHC Authorization letter.
- The sponsor's site initiation visit.
- Documented training, and task delegation on the Task Delegation Log.
- The sponsor's go letter, its written permission to start (it often arrives by email), where there is one.

File the printed REB approval letter in the investigator site file, as the RI-MUHC's SOPs require until a compliant electronic document system is in place, ideally the day you receive it; the MUHC Authorization letter should be filed there too. Note the renewal date in your calendar at the same time.

The sponsor or sponsor-investigator must [register a clinical trial in a public registry](/kb/close-out/publications) before the first participant is recruited.

REB approval lasts up to a year. Request the annual continuing review in Nagano at least a month before the approval expires; MUHC Authorization is revalidated with each renewal.

[Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments) to the protocol, consent form or other approved documents need the REB's approval or acknowledgement, the approval of any MUHC feasibility review the change affects, the sponsor's go letter if the sponsor issues one and, for a Health Canada-regulated trial, Health Canada's authorization when the change needs one, before you put them into effect. One exception is a change made at once to remove an immediate hazard to participants. It must then be reported to the REB immediately, and the sponsor or sponsor-investigator should be told as soon as possible.

The REB also lets administrative changes that don't affect participants, such as a change of address or of administrative research staff, wait for the annual continuing review; changes to the consent form or other participant materials still need its approval first.

If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk. Submit the continuing review request at once, tell the REB if research-related treatment must continue for participants' safety, and record the lapse as a deviation. Renewal isn't retroactive, and if no request reaches the REB within 30 days of expiry, the REB may end the study. The RI-MUHC's [SOPs](/sops/cr-007#5-5-annual-renewal-continuing-review) warn that inspectors would consider a lapse a major observation.

Major changes that affect feasibility or the use of resources may need a new assessment, and in rare cases, such as resource constraints or institutional priorities, the Personne mandatée may reassess or withdraw the authorization. When the study ends, or is suspended or stopped early, notify the REB and the Personne mandatée with form F10 in Nagano; for a completed study, do it in a timely way, ideally within 30 days of the close-out visit.

If your study doesn't fit these steps, the Research Facilitator can help you plan its review.
