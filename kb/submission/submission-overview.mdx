Every study under the MUHC's responsibility needs a scientific review, an ethics review by the Research Ethics Board (REB) and an institutional feasibility review before it can start. Many drug, medical device and natural health product trials also need Health Canada's authorization. Once the study has been approved in all three reviews and by Health Canada where it needs that authorization, MUHC Authorization is issued by the <Tooltip tip="The person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research: the Director of Innovation, Performance and Quality, who issues MUHC Authorization for each study." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip>, the person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research.

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

## How a study is reviewed

Before you submit, confirm that your project is research. A quality assurance or quality improvement project used only for assessment, management or improvement [doesn't need REB review](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html). The [Is It Research?](/is-it-research) tool helps you decide and get an exemption letter if you need one.

[Submit your study](/sops/cr-007#5-4-1) in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip> with the F11 form. The F11 starts the institutional feasibility review at the same time as the ethics review, where one applies. If no recognized peer committee has already reviewed the science, the REB also carries out the scientific review. A trial that needs Health Canada's authorization also has its own [application to Health Canada](#health-canada-authorization).

Review time depends on the study's level of risk, how complete your submission is, whether revisions are needed and the REB's workload. There are no submission deadlines, so enter your study in Nagano as soon as it's ready. Before the REB reviews a submission, staff of the [Centre for Applied Ethics](https://muhc.ca/cae/research-ethics) check it for completeness and the quality of its documents, and assess its level of risk.

Full-board review is the default. Studies that involve no more than minimal risk are [eligible for delegated review](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops), which runs continuously. A new project that could interfere with the integrity of a minor or of an adult who can't consent cannot go through delegated review at its first review.

Studies with more than minimal risk are reviewed at a full-board meeting of one of the REB's six specialized panels, which meet about twice a month. The meeting calendar is in Nagano.

Incomplete submissions are a common cause of delay, so check yours against [Preparing Your Nagano Submission](/kb/submission/preparing) before you submit.

## Scientific and ethics review by the REB

The scientific review checks that the study's design, data collection and statistical methods are sound, and assesses the team's qualifications.{/* TO CONFIRM (the Centre for Applied Ethics): what the REB's scientific review assesses. The list (design, data collection, statistical methods, the team's qualifications) comes only from the 2025 Researcher Handbook, a lead that is never cited (FACTS "Submission, review and authorization"). Primary sources found: SOP-CR-007_08 §5.4.7 ("scientific value and validity"); REB-SOP 403.001-1 §5.1.5 ("The methodology is scientifically sound and capable of answering the research question") and §5.1.1 (the REB may require documents showing the researcher's qualifications); the MSSS Cadre de référence (2020) §2.2.1 (peers, "conformément aux normes scientifiques pertinentes en vigueur dans la discipline concernée"). */} Request the scientific review in your Nagano submission. The REB [may recognize a peer review](/sops/cr-007#5-4-7) already done by a recognized committee, such as a granting agency's. A peer-reviewed funded project isn't reviewed again at the MUHC if you provide documentation of its peer review, such as the reviewers' comments and your responses.

A study needs review by an REB of Quebec's health and social services network and the MUHC's authorization when it:
- Recruits participants at the MUHC, in person or through the MUHC's technology, or among its staff.
- Uses health records stored at the MUHC.
- Takes place at least partly at the MUHC, including by MUHC-affiliated researchers abroad.
- Involves an investigator or sponsor-investigator affiliated with the MUHC.
- Uses the MUHC's staff services, such as nursing or pharmacy.
- Uses the MUHC's technical platforms or its other human, material or financial resources.
- Uses or creates a data repository or tissue bank hosted at the MUHC or held by an MUHC-affiliated researcher.
- Is presented by its sponsor or researcher as involving the MUHC or an MUHC affiliation, openly or by implication.

If research relies only on information that is publicly available through a legal mechanism and protected by law, or that is in the public domain with no reasonable expectation of privacy, it [doesn't need REB review](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter2-chapitre2.html). If you're unsure, ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you submit.

The MUHC REB reviews a study whose only site in Quebec is the MUHC. A study at more than one public institution in Quebec is reviewed by one Quebec evaluating REB, and each institution still does its own [feasibility review and authorization](/kb/design/multi-centric).

In a pan-Canadian study with [sites outside Quebec](/kb/design/cross-border-studies), submit for those sites under each jurisdiction's rules, and submit the Quebec sites in Nagano.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 14 to 34", answer 15). After its review, the REB decides to:

| Decision | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Approve | The study is approved as submitted. |
| Approve with modifications | The study isn't approved yet and cannot start until you've answered every condition and the REB has issued its final approval. |
| Defer | The REB needs more information or major changes, and reviews the study again once you resubmit. |
| Disapprove | The study cannot go ahead as proposed. |

Answer the REB's questions [within three months of its last message to you](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#answer-the-reb-within-three-months), or the REB closes the file.

If you can justify your grounds, you can ask [the REB](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information) to reconsider a decision. [Researchers have the right to be heard](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops) at a full board meeting. If the REB maintains a disapproval, it may offer a second hearing before a separate quorum, whose decision is final.

If you decline the second hearing, you can appeal on procedural or substantive grounds. The appeal takes place at another REB of Quebec's health and social services network, chosen jointly by the REB and you. The appeal committee's decision is final.

## Institutional feasibility review

In the institutional feasibility review:
- [Quality Assurance](mailto:QAClinicalResearch@muhc.mcgill.ca) checks that each investigator who submits the study holds Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status and the level of institutional training the study requires.
- The [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca) reviews the study's agreements. Upload them to both Nagano and My Contracts. A clinical trial agreement can be signed once the REB has approved the study, even if other feasibility reviews are still under way. [Every agreement must be fully executed before MUHC Authorization](/sops/cr-007#5-2-5).{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13"): no contract is signed without REB approval; the other feasibility reviews needn't be done; the Personne mandatée checks every review before MUHC Authorization; her example was an industry-sponsored study. A confidentiality agreement is signed before the protocol arrives (SOP-CR-102 Addendum §§1.1.1 to 1.1.2; SOP-CR-004_08 §5.1.1), so the sentence names the clinical trial agreement. Consistent with SOP-CR-007_08 §§3.0 and 5.2.5. */}
- The Director of Medical and Professional Services (DMPS) must approve the study's use of MUHC resources. The DMPS may also have the final say when a department's review raises a question of burden on the hospital's services, for example in radiology.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 1, 3 to 5, 7 to 11 and 13"). */}
- Nursing, laboratory and pharmacy services review the study when it uses them.
- An information security review covers the software, platforms or databases the study uses.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 14 to 34", answer 26). */}
- [The heads of the departments the study affects approve it](/kb/submission/preparing#get-approvals-start-the-agreements-and-declare-conflicts).
- Any other site-specific review the study calls for takes place.

You should never sign a binding contract or agreement before the Research Agreements Office has reviewed it. Investigators must not sign on behalf of the RI-MUHC.

Answer Nagano's questions on nursing, laboratory and pharmacy use accurately, because the answers trigger those reviews. All drugs and some natural health products must be managed by the pharmacy. When your answers trigger the pharmacy feasibility review, you receive a pharmacy agreement to sign. If the study needs [OPTILAB-MUHC laboratory services](mailto:clinicallaboratoryresearchinquiries@muhc.mcgill.ca), a laboratory feasibility review and a laboratory agreement follow.

If the study uses MUHC nursing services, upload to Nagano a nursing feasibility form signed by the nurse manager. The form doesn't have to be ready at the initial submission.

At the Montreal Neurological Institute-Hospital (the Neuro), some processes differ, including contracts managed through McGill University's contracting office and Neuro resources such as neuroimaging platforms and surgical suites. A study needs a privacy impact assessment if it uses identifiable health information held by the MUHC without participants' consent, including for pre-screening. Quebec's [Act respecting health and social services information](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-22.1) requires this assessment, the évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP). The EFVP must be completed in French and signed by the principal investigator, who leads the study at the MUHC.

Submit the EFVP in Nagano with your F11, on form F17 for pre-screening before consent or form F18 for research using identifiable records without consent. The MUHC's [Directorate of Innovation, Performance and Quality](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) reviews and approves the EFVP. A study at several Quebec institutions files one EFVP, from the lead site. Check [when an EFVP is needed](/kb/governance/privacy).

Studies funded wholly or partly by private enterprise pay the [review and authorization fees](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) set by Quebec's health ministry when the private funding exceeds the exemption amount. For the MUHC REB, private enterprise means any private-law body, not-for-profit organizations included.

## Health Canada authorization

Plan the Health Canada application during study design.

[The RI-MUHC's standard operating procedures (SOPs)](/sops/cr-007#5-1-1) have the sponsor obtain Health Canada's approval before the study is submitted to the REB. Health Canada's approval takes the form of a No Objection Letter (NOL) for a drug, a Notice of Authorization (NOA) for a natural health product or an Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA) for a device.

The sponsor sends the letter to the site, which includes it in the initial Nagano submission. If a drug trial's No Objection Letter hasn't arrived yet, the REB can still review the submission, but won't approve the study without the letter.

The order can differ. An application for a natural health product trial [must include each site's REB attestation](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2003-196/), so the REB approves the trial before Health Canada authorizes it. For a Class III or IV device, [Health Canada needs evidence of the REB's approval](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/medical-devices/application-information/guidance-documents/investigational-testing-authorizations-guidance/guidance-document.html) before the trial starts, ideally with the ITA application.

Whatever the order, the [Personne mandatée](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca) waits for Health Canada's letter before issuing MUHC Authorization to a trial that needs one.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 1, 3 to 5, 7 to 11 and 13"). */} The trial cannot begin until it has both Health Canada's authorization and MUHC Authorization.

For a drug or natural health product trial, the sponsor files the application. If you initiated the study as its sponsor-investigator, you file the application yourself. For a device, the manufacturer or importer holds the authorization.

| Trial | What Health Canada requires |
|---|---|
| A drug, unless the trial uses a marketed drug within its authorized use | A [Clinical Trial Application (CTA)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._870/section-C.05.006.html). The sponsor may proceed if Health Canada doesn't object within 30 days of receiving the application, and once each site's REB has approved the protocol and consent form. |
| A medical device | An ITA for [some studies of a Class II, III or IV device](/kb/governance/laws-regulations#check-whether-a-device-study-needs-an-investigational-testing-authorization). |
| A natural health product, unless the trial stays within a licensed product's recommended use | Authorization under the Natural Health Products Regulations. |

The RI-MUHC's Regulatory Affairs Specialist can help for a fee that researchers pay: Lori Anderson, [lcoaconsulting@gmail.com](mailto:lcoaconsulting@gmail.com). The Pre-Awards Office handles the invoicing.

## Before recruitment, and keeping approval valid

Recruitment begins only after all of the following:
- Health Canada's authorization, if the study needs it.
- The REB approval letter and the MUHC Authorization letter.
- The sponsor's site initiation visit.
- Documented training, and task delegation on the Task Delegation Log.
- The go letter, where the sponsor issues one. A go letter is the sponsor's written permission to start the study at the site, or to implement an amendment, often sent by email.

File the printed REB approval letter in the investigator site file, ideally the day you receive it. [The RI-MUHC's SOPs](/sops/cr-007#5-4-11) require the printed copy until a document management system that complies with Part 11 of Title 21 of the US Code of Federal Regulations is in place. The MUHC Authorization letter should also be filed in the investigator site file. When you file the REB approval letter, note the renewal date in your calendar.

The sponsor or sponsor-investigator must [register a clinical trial in a public registry](/kb/close-out/publications) before the first participant is recruited.

REB approval lasts up to a year. Request the annual continuing review in Nagano at least a month before the approval expires. MUHC Authorization is revalidated with each renewal. A lapse in REB approval voids MUHC Authorization. The [Personne mandatée](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca) may reassess or withdraw MUHC Authorization at any time, for example after a for-cause audit.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 14 to 34"). */}

If REB approval lapses, do not enrol anyone, and suspend study activities unless stopping them would put participants at risk. Submit a request for continuing review. [Tell the REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) if research-related treatment must continue for participants' safety and well-being. Record the lapse as a deviation, with its reasons and the steps taken to prevent another lapse.

A renewal isn't retroactive, so the period of the lapse has no ethics approval. If no request reaches the REB within 30 days of expiry, the REB may end the study. Regulatory inspectors [would consider a lapse a major observation](/sops/cr-007#5-5-5).

[Amendments](/kb/conduct/safety-reporting/study-amendments) to the protocol, the consent form or other approved documents need all of the following before you put them into effect:
- The REB's approval or acknowledgement.
- The approval of any MUHC feasibility review the change affects.
- The sponsor's go letter, if the sponsor issues one.
- For a Health Canada-regulated trial, Health Canada's authorization, when the change needs one.

One exception is a change made before approval to remove an immediate hazard to participants. [Report the change to the REB immediately](/sops/cr-026#5-7) after you make it. The sponsor or sponsor-investigator should be told as soon as possible after the change.

If an administrative change doesn't affect participants or their well-being, the REB lets it [wait for the annual continuing review](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops). Administrative changes include a change of address and a change of administrative research staff. Changes to the consent form or other participant materials still need the REB's approval before you use them. The Personne mandatée reviews an amendment that changes the study's feasibility.

When the study ends, is suspended or is stopped before its planned end, notify the REB and the Personne mandatée with form F10 in Nagano. For a completed study, notify them in a timely way, ideally within 30 days of the close-out visit. For a suspension or a stop before the planned end, notify them promptly and explain the reasons in detail.

If your study doesn't fit these steps, the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help you plan its review.
