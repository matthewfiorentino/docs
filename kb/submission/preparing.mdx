## Choose the project type

When you create the project in Nagano, its type sets which version of the F11 you complete. The type cannot be changed once the project has its Nagano number. If you're unsure which type applies, ask the [REB](mailto:reb@muhc.mcgill.ca) or the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you create the project. If you need to recreate the project because you chose the wrong type, write to [personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca), the office of the <Tooltip tip="The person whom an establishment of Quebec's health network formally mandates to authorize the research conducted under its auspices." headline="Personne mandatée" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#personne-mandatee">Personne mandatée</Tooltip>, the person the MUHC has mandated to authorize research.

| Project type in Nagano | Choose it for | F11 form |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical Trial, Clinical Research | Clinical trials, which evaluate the safety or effects of one or more interventions on health outcomes, and other clinical research. | F11-H |
| Banque / Registre (bank or registry) | Creating a bank or registry: biological samples or data collected, stored and distributed for future research. A project that only uses an existing bank or registry is non-interventional research instead, and uses the F11-NIR.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 14 to 34"). */} | F11-BR |
| Recherche fondamentale (fundamental research) | Experimental or theoretical research for new knowledge, with no practical application planned. | F11-NIR |
| Recherche psychosociale, recherche qualitative, sciences infirmières (psychosocial or qualitative research, nursing sciences) | Research on how psychological and social factors shape behaviour and well-being, research that collects and analyzes non-numerical data, or research that guides nursing practice. | F11-NIR |
| Recherche rétrospective sur dossier (retrospective health records research) | Using data already in patients' health records, with no contact with participants and no new data collected. | F11-HRR |
| Recherche en épidémiologie, soins et services de santé, santé internationale (epidemiology, health services or international health research) | Research on the health of populations, on health services or on health worldwide. | F11-NIR |
| Recherche en imagerie (imaging research) | Research whose main question concerns imaging, or that relies on imaging. | F11-NIR |

Submit a protocol with every F11, even for retrospective health records research.

For a study at more than one public institution in Quebec, follow [Multi-Centric Studies in Quebec](/kb/design/multi-centric), whether the MUHC leads or takes part. When the MUHC REB is to review the study for every site, submit the F0 (Request to Act as REB of Record) form with the F11.

To create a single-site clinical research project and complete its F11-H, follow the steps in the [Nagano guide](/apps/nagano/overview).

## Name the principal investigator

The principal investigator is the person who leads the study at the MUHC. Who can be named principal investigator depends on whether the study needs medical oversight:

| The study | Who can be named principal investigator |
|---|---|
| Needs medical oversight, such as an interventional clinical trial | A physician or dentist of the MUHC's Council of Physicians, Dentists, Pharmacists and Midwives (CPDPM) with Human Research Privileges. |
| Doesn't need medical oversight | A researcher with Researcher Status, such as a PhD researcher or a nurse, or a physician or dentist with Privileges. |

[Privileges are required](/sops/cr-500#4-0-definitions) to lead a study that Health Canada regulates with an investigational drug, medical device or natural health product.

A principal investigator at the Montreal Neurological Institute-Hospital (the Neuro) whose study runs there must obtain McGill Research Privileges and institutional training under the Neuro's and McGill's procedures instead.

A clinical trial should ideally also list at least one sub-investigator as a <Tooltip tip="A qualified member of a study team who is designated and supervised by the qualified or principal investigator at a site, called a sub-investigator in the RI-MUHC's SOPs." headline="Co-Investigator" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#co-investigator">co-investigator</Tooltip> in Nagano, so that medical oversight continues if the principal investigator becomes unavailable. Being named principal investigator in Nagano has no bearing on who can be the lead author. Whoever wrote the protocol can be listed as a co-investigator or collaborator if they aren't the principal investigator.

When the REB contacts a research team, it generally emails the principal investigator named in Nagano. Keep the principal investigator's contact details in Nagano accurate.

## Check credentials and training

A Sub-Investigator [should be named as the principal investigator's backup](/sops/cr-002#5-1-2). The backup must hold the same Privileges or Researcher Status and institutional training as the principal investigator, or better. Fellows and residents cannot be the backup, because they are not eligible for Privileges or Researcher Status.

Check [who on the team needs Privileges or Researcher Status, and which training each study level needs](/training/compliance-requirements). Before you submit, renew any credential or training that is [close to expiry](/training/compliance-requirements#validity-and-the-90-day-rule).

Before the study is submitted, the principal investigator [should be trained on Nagano](/sops/cr-007#5-2-1) and have that training documented, even if self-taught. The principal investigator should also make sure that any team member who submits the study has the level of institutional training the study requires.

If a coordinator or another delegate submits the study for the principal investigator, the Principal Investigator's Commitment and Signature form must be completed. Submitting to the REB is one of the tasks on the [Task Delegation Log](/sops/cr-002#task-delegation).

Do not perform a study task until your training for it is documented and the task has been delegated on the Task Delegation Log.

## Prepare the documents

[Upload these documents](/sops/cr-007#5-4-8) to Nagano with the F11:
- The current protocol and a protocol summary.
- The consent form, reviewed and approved by the sponsor if there is one. Follow the [rules for preparing the consent form](/kb/recruitment/consent#prepare-the-consent-form) for its English and French versions and its translation.
- Every other document participants will see, including recruitment material such as letters, phone scripts, advertisements, posters, social media posts and wording for MUHC screens. Submit each one in English and French, [unless the REB accepts a justification](https://muhc.ca/cae/muhc-research-ethics-board-reb-standard-operating-procedures-sops).
- Information on participants' compensation, and the recruitment plan and methods if the study has them.
- The current Investigator's Brochure or its equivalent and other safety information, as applicable. For a phase IV trial, the product monograph takes the place of the brochure.
- The principal investigator's curriculum vitae.
- Health Canada's letter, for a trial that needs one. [For some trials, the letter can come after the submission](/kb/submission/submission-overview#health-canada-authorization).
- The agreement and budget, if there are any.
- Any other document required locally.

The submission should also include the results of [any earlier peer review](/kb/submission/submission-overview#scientific-and-ethics-review-by-the-reb) and of any other REB's review.

Put a version number and date on the protocol, the consent form and the Investigator's Brochure. Put the version number in the file name of every document you upload too, because the file name is what appears on the REB's approval letter.

Check [what advertisements must include](/kb/recruitment/recruitment-enrollment#have-recruitment-materials-approved).

## Plan the safety monitoring of a clinical trial

<div className="hub-scope"><span className="hub-scope-label">Applies to</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Drug studies (Level I)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Medical device and surgery studies (Level II)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Natural health product studies (Level III)</span><span className="hub-scope-tag">Prospective studies not regulated by Health Canada (Level IV)</span></div>

For a clinical trial, the [*Tri-Council Policy Statement* (TCPS 2)](https://ethics.gc.ca/eng/tcps2-eptc2_2022_chapter11-chapitre11.html) requires researchers to give the REB an acceptable plan for monitoring safety, efficacy or effectiveness (where feasible) and validity. The REB needs the study's design, stopping rules and monitoring mechanisms at its initial review. The researcher or the sponsor is responsible for establishing the plan. The plan must describe:

- How participant safety will be monitored, and what will be done if it is threatened.
- How the intervention's efficacy will be monitored, where feasible, and what will be done if it is greater than expected.
- The criteria for removing participants from the study for safety reasons.
- The study-wide stopping rules, if any, by which the study may be stopped or amended for inferior safety, superior efficacy or futility.
- How information relevant to participants' welfare or consent will be reported clearly and promptly to the REB.

The REB assesses whether the plan addresses the study's foreseeable risks and potential benefits. Every clinical trial that poses more than minimal risk to participants should have criteria for removing participants who are experiencing harm. Researchers are responsible for ensuring that the REB receives the results of any interim analyses that affect participants' welfare or consent.

In multi-site research, when new information at one site could be relevant to participant welfare and consent at other sites, principal investigators must ensure that it is shared with researchers at each site and with the REBs. The plan may include an independent data and safety monitoring board (DSMB), but doesn't have to. Appointing an independent DSMB does not change the researchers' responsibility to monitor safety, efficacy and validity throughout the study.

Not every trial needs an independent DSMB. These factors should be considered when deciding whether a study should have one:

- How great the foreseeable harms of the research to participants are.
- Whether the participants' circumstances make them vulnerable in the context of research.
- Whether interim analysis of the data is feasible.
- How complex the study is.
- Conflicts of interest.

A study whose possible outcomes for participants are severe, such as irreversible harm or death, is more likely to need an independent DSMB and stopping rules. Studies with several sites, blinded data, long-term data collection or many participants may need one whether or not they have study-wide stopping rules. A single-site study with unblinded data collection and clear stopping rules may not.

In a drug trial, [the good clinical practice guideline of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH)](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products/drug-products/applications-submissions/guidance-documents/international-council-harmonisation/guidelines.html) also lets the sponsor consider an independent data monitoring committee to assess the trial's progress and recommend whether to continue, modify or stop it.

An independent DSMB brings together people with the relevant scientific, ethical or community expertise, who are not involved in the trial's conduct. A charter usually describes its duties, which may include applying stopping rules and recommending changes to the trial. In a blinded study, it can see the unblinded data to make its recommendations. Consider including at least one biostatistician and one clinician with relevant expertise.

## Answer the F11 questions on services and data access

Your answers on pharmacy, nursing and laboratory services start [those services' feasibility reviews](/kb/submission/submission-overview#institutional-feasibility-review). Take particular care with these F11 answers:
- If the Centre for Innovative Medicine (CIM) or your own research nurse covers the study's nursing, [answer "No" to MUHC nursing services](/sops/cr-007#5-4-9). If you answer "Yes", upload a nursing feasibility form signed by the nurse manager. The form doesn't have to be ready at the initial submission.
- If a clinical trial plans to recruit participants not covered by the Régie de l'assurance maladie du Québec (RAMQ) for in-person visits at the MUHC, declare it in the F11. The RAMQ administers Quebec's health insurance plan. Residents of other provinces are among the people the plan doesn't cover.

If you aren't sure whether the study will use a service, ask that service or the [Personne mandatée's office](mailto:personne.mandatee@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you submit.

If the study will use identifiable health information held by the MUHC without consent, including for pre-screening, submit the privacy impact assessment with the F11. The assessment is the <Tooltip tip="The privacy impact assessment, or évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée, that Quebec's Act respecting health and social services information requires with a researcher's request for access to health and social services information without consent, including access before consent for pre-screening." headline="EFVP (privacy impact assessment)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#efvp">évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (EFVP)</Tooltip>. The EFVP must be completed in French and signed by the principal investigator. Data collected with identifiers and anonymized afterward still count as identifiable.

If the access is authorized, the principal investigator also signs [a written agreement with the MUHC that makes the authorization official](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent).

A missing EFVP, or [the wrong EFVP form](/kb/submission/submission-overview#institutional-feasibility-review), is among the most common causes of late-stage delays in feasibility review. If the F11's data access section has a wrong answer, the F11 is re-opened for you to correct it, and the correction triggers an EFVP review.{/* The Research Facilitator, through MF, Nov. 13, 2026 (FACTS "The Research Facilitator, Nov. 13, answers 14 to 34"). */} Check [when an EFVP is needed](/kb/governance/privacy). If you're still unsure, write to [efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:efvp@muhc.mcgill.ca) or ask the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) before you submit.

## Get approvals, start the agreements and declare conflicts

The head of each department the study affects must approve it. Get the signed approvals before you submit, so that other reviews don't start unnecessarily. Some types of research don't need the department heads' approval.

A department is affected if the study recruits its patients or might use its resources. For example, the head of the intensive care unit must approve a study whose participants might need the intensive care unit after receiving a study drug, even if the study isn't mainly conducted there.

Your department head also signs the submission form's MUHC Authorizing Signatures with you. If the department head is the investigator or has a conflict of interest, [an academic superior signs instead](/sops/cr-007#5-4-8). Also obtain feasibility agreements from the managers of the clinical services the study will use. If you haven't discussed the study with them before you submit, you may be asked to do so during the review.

Submit the study's agreements in My Contracts on the RI-MUHC Portal and in Nagano, including any clinical trial agreement. The upload to My Contracts starts the review by the [Research Agreements Office](mailto:contracts@muhc.mcgill.ca), which runs while the REB reviews the study.

The REB often reads draft agreements, so you should upload drafts as soon as you have them. If a draft becomes available after you submit, [tell your REB panel](https://muhc.ca/cae/page/contact-information).

Check [what to look for in a clinical trial agreement](/kb/funding/clinical-trial-agreements) and [the rules on signing agreements](/kb/submission/submission-overview#institutional-feasibility-review).

Name the study's source of funding in the F11. If private enterprise funds the study wholly or partly, give the private funders' contact information and the budgets at submission. [Review and authorization fees](/kb/funding/budgets-contracts) may apply to a privately funded study.

In the REB submission, declare any real, potential or perceived personal or institutional [conflict of interest](/kb/governance/conflict-of-interest) that may affect the study. Include the conflicts of every member of the research team, and of their close relatives or partners. McGill University's own conflict of interest disclosure is separate from the REB declaration.

## After you submit

Answer the REB's questions [by its deadline](/kb/submission/tracking-responding#answer-the-reb-within-three-months), or the REB closes the file.

After the REB approves your recruitment material, any of it that a patient may see [also needs approval](/sops/cr-009#5-3-1) from [MUHC Communications](mailto:communications@muhc.mcgill.ca).

<Warning>
Do not start any research activity, including recruitment, data collection or analysis, until you have both REB approval and MUHC Authorization.
</Warning>

Recruitment also waits for every other [start condition](/kb/submission/submission-overview#before-recruitment-and-keeping-approval-valid).

If your study doesn't fit these steps, the [Research Facilitator](mailto:research.facilitator@muhc.mcgill.ca) can help you prepare it.
