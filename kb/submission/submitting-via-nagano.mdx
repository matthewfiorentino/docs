Before you open Nagano and create a project, three decisions will shape everything that follows: which project type you choose, who is listed as PI, and whether your study requires an ÉFVP form. Getting these right at the start avoids delays late in feasibility review. Make these decisions before beginning the F11 form.

## Choosing a project type

The project type you select in Nagano determines which version of the F11 submission form is generated. Each type maps to a different form with different sections and questions. Choosing the wrong type early means either missing required fields or seeing sections that do not apply — and changing it later can require duplicating work.

| Project type | Choose this if… | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical Trial / Clinical Research | Your study involves human participants and follows the standard clinical research pathway — including interventional clinical trials and most other types of clinical research. | F11-H |
| Bank / Registry | You are creating a new bank or registry. If your project only uses an existing bank or registry as a data source, this is usually not the right type. | F11-BR |
| Retrospective chart review | Your project uses data already documented in institutional health records, with no participant contact and no new data collection. | F11-HRR |
| Fundamental research | Your study is experimental or theoretical, generating new knowledge without a primary practical application at the outset. | F11-NIR |
| Psychosocial / qualitative / nursing sciences | Your project is non-interventional and aligns primarily with psychosocial, qualitative, or nursing sciences approaches. | F11-NIR |
| Epidemiology / health services / global health | Your project is population-based, health services-oriented, or global health-focused. | F11-NIR |

<Warning>
  If you are unsure which type applies, contact the Research Facilitator before creating the project. Changing project type after the F11 is underway can create inconsistencies in your submission record.
</Warning>

## Who to list as PI

The person listed as PI in Nagano is determined by whether the study requires **medical oversight** — not by who wrote the protocol or who will be lead author.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Clinical / interventional (MD required)">
    Medical oversight is required. The PI must be an MD — a CPDP member in good standing with current RI-MUHC Human Research Privileges and institutional training at the appropriate level for the study.

    The study author can be listed as a co-investigator or collaborator if they are not the PI.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Clinical / non-interventional">
    Medical oversight is not required. The PI may be a non-MD. The PI must hold RI-MUHC Researcher Status and have current institutional training at the appropriate level.

    Note that a physician holding Human Research Privileges may also lead non-interventional studies — Privileges covers all study types.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

<Info>
  **Privileges and Researcher Status are PI-level designations.** Team members listed on the Task Delegation Log do not hold Privileges or Researcher Status, but do require current institutional training at the appropriate level for the study type.
</Info>

## The ÉFVP trigger

The data access section of the F11 is one of the most consequential to complete accurately. ÉFVP requirements that are missed or miscategorized are the most common cause of late-stage delays in feasibility review — the issue surfaces only when the institutional review team reads the submission, not at the time of filing.

The key question is whether your study requires access to identifiable patient information, and under what conditions.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Submit F17 alongside the F11" icon="file-search">
    **Accessing identifiable information before consent** — pre-screening using OACIS or other MUHC health records to identify potential participants before approaching them.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Submit F18 alongside the F11" icon="file-x">
    **Accessing identifiable information without consent** — research using identifiable records where consent will not be obtained (chart reviews, registry linkages). This triggers a formal ÉFVP evaluation.
  </Card>
  <Card title="ÉFVP may not apply" icon="file-check">
    **Anonymized or aggregated data only** — if data can be obtained in a fully anonymized format (e.g. through the RI-MUHC Data Warehouse), ÉFVP may not be required depending on the specific scenario.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  When in doubt, clarify before submitting. Contact the ÉFVP committee at [EFVP@muhc.mcgill.ca](mailto:EFVP@muhc.mcgill.ca) and/or the Research Facilitator. A wrong answer in the data access section discovered during feasibility review will require an amendment and restart that portion of the review.
</Warning>

## Multi-site submission considerations

How you submit depends on whether the MUHC is the lead or a participating site.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="MUHC as lead site">
    Your Nagano submission covers all participating Quebec RSSS institutions. The MUHC REB conducts the scientific and ethics review, and its decision is recognized at participating sites. Each site still completes its own institutional feasibility review and obtains its own MUHC Authorization from its Personne mandatée.

    For industry-sponsored multi-site trials, **CATALIS FAST TRACK** may compress the combined authorization process to eight weeks or less.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="MUHC as participating site (MEO)">
    The reviewing REB is the lead institution's — not the MUHC REB. Your Nagano submission is a participating-site (MEO) file. Scientific and ethics review are handled by the lead site; your local MUHC feasibility review and Authorization by the Personne mandatée are still required.

    Contact the Research Facilitator before starting a MEO submission — the workflow differs from a standard F11 submission.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Key decisions before opening the F11 form

Work through these questions before creating your project in Nagano:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Determine your study type classification">
    Use the [Study Type and Training Classifier](/training/compliance-requirements) to confirm whether your study is Level I (drug), Level II (device), Level III (NHP), Level IV (observational), or Level V (retrospective). This drives which F11 pathway and which training requirements apply.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select the correct project type">
    Match your study to the project type table above. When in doubt, contact the Research Facilitator. The project type determines which version of the F11 is generated and cannot easily be changed mid-submission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm PI eligibility">
    Verify that the PI holds current Privileges (for interventional/medical oversight studies) or Researcher Status (for non-interventional studies), and that credentials are not within 90 days of expiry at the time of submission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Determine whether an ÉFVP form is needed">
    Identify all sources of personal health information in your study and under what conditions they will be accessed. Determine whether F17, F18, or neither is required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your Health Canada submission status">
    For drug trials (Level I): the CTA must be submitted to Health Canada in parallel with Nagano. The No Objection Letter (NOL) must be in hand before MUHC Authorization. For device trials (Level II): obtain the ITA from Health Canada. MUHC Authorization cannot be issued until Health Canada authorization is in place.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify all services your study will use">
    Review which MUHC service departments your study will need: pharmacy, nursing, laboratory, DPS approvals. Answer the F11 service questions accurately — underreporting causes delays when gaps are found during feasibility review.
  </Step>
</Steps>
