Researchers at the RI-MUHC can work with MUHC patient data in two ways. While they plan a study, they can explore synthetic patient data in MDClone, which needs no approval from the Research Ethics Board (REB). Once the study is approved, they can request real patient data from the MUHC Data Warehouse.

| What differs | Synthetic data in MDClone | Real data from the Data Warehouse |
|---|---|---|
| What you work with | Synthetic patient data that keep the statistical properties and relationships of real clinical data. They contain no real patient records and no identifiable information. | Real patient data: aggregate, anonymized, de-identified or identifying. |
| Suited to | Exploring patient populations, estimating cohort sizes, testing inclusion and exclusion criteria, assessing feasibility, preliminary statistics, research hypotheses, and grant and REB applications. | Publication-ready analyses, validated research datasets, and projects that need real patient-level data for validation, publication or detailed statistical analyses. |
| How you get it | You explore the data and build cohorts yourself, once your account is approved. | You submit a formal data request, and the Data Warehouse team extracts the data after reviewing and processing the project. |
| Approvals | REB approval is not required to generate or explore synthetic data. | REB approval and MUHC Authorization, except for aggregate data, which can be requested at any time. |

Many projects begin in MDClone to explore feasibility and define cohorts before a formal Data Warehouse request is submitted to obtain real patient data.

## Explore synthetic data in MDClone

MDClone is a secure self-service platform for exploring synthetic patient data. Researchers use it to explore patient populations, assess a project's feasibility, refine cohort definitions and develop research hypotheses before they request access to real patient data. MDClone can also be used to learn and to teach with realistic clinical data.

Synthetic data are artificially generated. They preserve the statistical properties and relationships of the original clinical data while protecting patients' privacy. Researchers can export the synthetic datasets they generate in MDClone.

As of November 2026, MDClone holds synthetic data from these domains, and new clinical domains are added as they become available:

- Demographics.
- Conditions.
- Encounters.
- Laboratory and pathology.
- Medications.
- Radiology.
- SARDO, the MUHC's cancer tumour registry.
- Surgeries and procedures.

Synthetic data are commonly used for exploratory analyses, cohort discovery, hypothesis generation and study planning. Depending on the research methodology, they may also support other research activities. A study that needs real patient data should obtain the appropriate approvals and submit a Data Warehouse request.

### Get access to MDClone

Access to MDClone is available to all active RI-MUHC staff and members who hold valid MUHC credentials. They include:

- Principal investigators.
- Research staff and trainees affiliated with an RI-MUHC principal investigator.
- Other RI-MUHC members with a valid UNI number, the MUHC credential that looks like MAMO1234.

If you have no UNI number, confirm your eligibility and request a UNI number through a C2 request, with your principal investigator, before you apply for MDClone access.

MDClone is accessible only through the MUHC network. Before your first login, make sure you can connect on-site at an RI-MUHC facility, or remotely through Citrix or a virtual private network (VPN). If network access isn't set up yet, submit a C2 request to have it configured before you use MDClone.

Before your access request can be approved, you must:

- Have written approval from your principal investigator. Every team member who requests access needs it.
- Review and sign the MDClone Data and User Use Agreement, which sets the terms for data access, privacy and acceptable use of the platform.
- Complete the first three modules of the MDClone Academy, MDClone's training platform. These introductory modules are mandatory for all users.

When you meet the eligibility criteria and have completed the prerequisites, submit your request through the official [MDClone Access Request form](https://forms.office.com/pages/responsepage.aspx?id=KP7hBotfdUC_bK4kvhp5koBH7agR8YRInTq4ci_I_T5UQUVDRjJONFlVN0lFOEo4SkQzSlZETFhTSiQlQCN0PWcu&route=shorturl). Every field of the form is mandatory, and incomplete submissions will not be processed. The form asks for:

- Your UNI number.
- Your full name, as it appears in institutional records.
- Your work email address, an active MUHC or affiliated institutional address.
- The research account number to be associated with the access.
- Your principal investigator's full name and email address, which every team member must give.

Access is activated only once all the required information and documents have been submitted and validated. Access is tied to your UNI number, so do not share your login with others.

For questions about eligibility, the training or the use agreement, ask your principal investigator or the [Data Warehouse team](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca).

### Move from MDClone to real patient data

Once you have finalized your cohort definition in MDClone and the study has the necessary approvals, submit a Data Warehouse request. For an approved research project, the Data Warehouse team can generate the real patient dataset that corresponds to the cohort definition you developed in MDClone, which reduces the need to recreate it. The request follows the Data Warehouse's [request process](#the-request-process) and its [requirements by type of data](#requirements-by-data-type).

## Request real data from the MUHC Data Warehouse

The MUHC Data Warehouse brings together health care data from many of the MUHC's clinical and administrative systems, organized mainly around each patient. Researchers use it to count patients when they plan a study, and to obtain patient data for research once the study is approved. The RI-MUHC [Data Warehouse team](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) runs the service for research and helps you define the data you need.

As of 2024, the Data Warehouse held data on 4.9 million patients and 28 million episodes of care, in more than 400 database tables with more than 6,000 columns, for more than 7.5 billion facts. Its data come from more than 18 MUHC hospital systems, such as those of the emergency departments, the operating rooms, outpatient clinics, imaging, the laboratory, the pharmacy, the cancer registry and the medical records archives. The data are updated within 24 hours.

### What the Data Warehouse team offers

The team offers two services, both requested through its Request Center:

- **Consultation.** The team checks whether a project is feasible, counts patients and variables, helps prepare the data specifications and advises on completing the privacy impact assessment (EFVP) or the data access section of the Nagano submission.
- **Data extraction.** Research analysts and programmers extract aggregate data, anonymized data, de-identified data and identifying data for research projects.

### When to use the Data Warehouse

The [Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) serves research, including large retrospective analyses of patient data across MUHC sites: admissions, emergency visits, laboratory results, imaging reports, pharmacy dispensations, surgical records and more. Many of its data go back to 2008. The Data Warehouse provides data for research projects, not for quality improvement projects. If you aren't sure which your project is, use the Centre for Applied Ethics' [Quality Improvement (QI) vs Research Screening tool](https://muhc.ca/cae/templates-consent-forms).

<Note>
The Data Warehouse team recommends a consultation before you finalize your protocol and submit it in <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's electronic study submission and management platform. All studies requiring MUHC Authorization are submitted through Nagano." headline="Nagano (MUHC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#nagano">Nagano</Tooltip>. The team can help define the patient selection criteria, the sample size and the variables. If these are not clearly defined in Nagano, they can cause unnecessary delays.
</Note>

The Data Warehouse holds data from these MUHC sites: the Montreal General Hospital (MGH), the Royal Victoria Hospital (RVH), the Montreal Children's Hospital (MCH), the Lachine Hospital (LAC) and the Montreal Neurological Hospital (MNH). Not every kind of data is available for every site and every period. When you plan the protocol, check the [Data Summary](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/MUHC2.download_my_file?p_file=65514749312768742) (PDF) and the [Data Dictionary tool](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=132:1).

The main kinds of data available are:

- Patient demographics and identifiers.
- Hospitalization and emergency episodes.
- Laboratory results, from 2020.
- Medical imaging reports, from 2000.
- Surgical records, from 2003.
- Pharmacy prescriptions and dispensations.
- The cancer tumour registry.
- Infection control data.
- Blood bank records.
- The activities of allied health professionals.
- Outpatient appointments.

The Data Warehouse doesn't hold medical image files (PACS), scanned documents or outpatient clinical notes. Some of these may be available through other systems, such as PACS or OACIS, and the team can advise you, since similar information may still be in the Data Warehouse.

### The request process

Every consultation and data request goes through the [RI-MUHC Data Warehouse Request Center](https://rimuhc.atlassian.net/servicedesk/customer/user/login?destination=portals), a separate site where you sign in or create an account. The Data Warehouse page on the RI-MUHC Portal links to it. Do not submit patients' medical record numbers (MRNs) or any other sensitive information through the Request Center.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Explore the Data Summary and the Data Dictionary">
    Check which data are available, from which source systems and from what dates. Use the Data Dictionary tool to check variable definitions, data types and the sites where each variable is available before you settle the protocol's design.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Request a consultation">
    Through the Request Center, discuss the patient selection criteria, the variables available and the sample size with the team, before you finalize the protocol.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Finalize the protocol and submit it in Nagano">
    Define the data access section of the Nagano submission clearly. The team can advise you on completing it, and on completing the EFVP if the study needs one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit a data request">
    Once the Research Ethics Board (REB) has approved the study and it has <Tooltip tip="The MUHC's final authorization for a study to begin, issued by the Personne mandatée once the scientific, ethics and institutional feasibility reviews are favourable and, where the study needs it, Health Canada has authorized it." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/foundations/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>, and you meet the other [requirements for your type of data](#requirements-by-data-type), submit a data request through the Request Center.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive the data through NextCloud">
    The data come to you through the MUHC's NextCloud platform. A NextCloud account is required for every type of data except aggregate data. [Store and share the data you receive](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security) as their type requires.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Aggregate data can be requested at any time, for example for feasibility checks or sample size calculations, before the study is approved.

Besides consultations and data requests, the Request Center takes feedback: suggestions to improve the service, reports of a data issue and other questions. Every communication and file about your request is visible to you in the Request Center, and you receive an email for each update. Reply to the email or add a comment to the request to respond. To check a request's status, select **Requests** under your name, in the top right corner.

### Request aggregate data

Even with no names or medical record numbers, aggregate results can identify patients, so the [Data Warehouse team](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) applies privacy rules to aggregate requests, set out in its [*Guidelines on Aggregate Data Requests*](https://portal.rimuhc.ca/pls/apex/f?p=106:360):

- The Data Warehouse doesn't release aggregate cells that hold fewer than 5 patients.
- A request that is acceptable on its own can become risky when combined with other requests or with outside data, through small changes between similar requests, or through repeated requests over time.
- The team keeps a log of requests by project and by population, and may review earlier requests with a new one. The team may deny, restrict, change or restructure requests whose overall pattern presents an unacceptable privacy risk.

Simple extractions, such as the number of patients in a cohort or its age or sex distribution, are generally provided at no cost. Complex extractions, such as a cohort broken down by several variables, or means and trends for specific variables, may involve costs or other approvals.

When you request aggregate data, you should:

- Plan your analyses in advance, rather than refining your requests step by step.
- Disclose earlier or parallel projects on similar populations or data sets.
- Avoid unnecessary breakdowns, or breakdowns finer than you need.
- Flag any result that looks as if it could identify someone.
- Consult the team when you are unsure about a privacy risk.

### Requirements by data type

What you need before the team releases data depends on the type of data you request. The [Data Warehouse](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) sorts data into four types, from least to most identifying: aggregate, anonymized, de-identified (coded) and identifying. [What each type is](/kb/conduct/data-documentation/data-security#know-how-identifiable-your-data-are) decides how you must store and share it.

| Requirement | Aggregate | Anonymized | De-identified | Identifying |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Data Use Agreement | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Valid Human Research Privileges or Researcher Status, and standard operating procedure (SOP) training | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| NextCloud account | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Most recently REB-approved protocol | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| REB approval letter | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| MUHC Authorization letter | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| REB renewal letter, if applicable | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Privacy impact assessment (EFVP) | No | No | Without consent only | Without consent only |

For de-identified or identifying data, the EFVP depends on consent:

- If the study accesses personal health information without the participants' consent, to screen candidates, it needs the short EFVP (EFVP-FT), on form F17.
- If the study accesses personal health information without the participants' consent for any other research purpose, it needs the EFVP, on form F18.
- If the participants have consented to this use of their information, no EFVP is required.

[How to complete the EFVP](/kb/governance/privacy#get-authorization-before-using-health-records-without-consent) is on the privacy page.

Consider requesting the least identifying type of data that answers your research question, since each more identifying type needs more documents before the team releases it.

<Warning>
De-identified data are not anonymized. Their direct identifiers have been replaced by a code, and a key can link the code back to the patients, so the data can still identify people. The requirements for EFVPs and for security differ between the two types. The RI-MUHC's *Researcher's Guide to Data Security* gives only the principal investigator access to the key that links de-identified data to the patient list.
</Warning>

### Acknowledge the Data Warehouse in publications

If your research used data from the MUHC Data Warehouse, the [Data Warehouse team](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca) asks you to acknowledge the MUHC Data Warehouse and the RI-MUHC in the Methods, Data Sources or Acknowledgements section of your publication, as appropriate. If you aren't sure of the citation format for your study design or your journal, write to the team.

The team keeps a record of the publications that used Data Warehouse data. If your article is missing from it, the team asks you to [let it know](mailto:info.ridw@muhc.mcgill.ca).
