Before agreeing to conduct a study at The Institute, the site and research team must assess whether it is feasible — scientifically, operationally, and financially. Feasibility is cheapest to evaluate at the design stage, before the protocol is finalized and before contracts or budgets are negotiated. A study that passes ethics review but cannot enrol participants, or requires equipment the site does not have, will stall at activation.

<Tip>
  If you haven't yet connected with the Research Facilitator about your planned study, complete the [Clinical Research Intake questionnaire](/kb/planning-overview) first. It produces a personalized startup checklist and connects you with the right support.
</Tip>

## Before the Site Feasibility Visit

When a Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator approaches the site with a study, the first step is to sign a confidentiality agreement before receiving any confidential study documents, including the protocol. Legal review of the agreement is often required before signing.

Once you have the protocol, conduct a preliminary self-assessment before the Sponsor's Site Feasibility Visit (SFV). The main questions to consider are:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Science and ethics" icon="microscope">
    Is the protocol scientifically and ethically sound, and compatible with local healthcare practice?
  </Card>
  <Card title="Patient population" icon="users">
    Does The Institute have access to the target patient population within the required timeline?
  </Card>
  <Card title="Team capacity" icon="user-group">
    Does the team have the time, qualifications, and experience to conduct this study?
  </Card>
  <Card title="Facilities and equipment" icon="building">
    Does the site have the required infrastructure, storage, and equipment?
  </Card>
  <Card title="Financial viability" icon="dollar-sign">
    Are the proposed budget and payment terms acceptable to the institution?
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  Any electronic software used in the study must be validated and compliant with 21 CFR Part 11. Contact IT via "IT rescue" on the RI-MUHC Portal to evaluate study-specific software before the SFV.
</Warning>

Use the **Study Feasibility Checklist** (Appendix 1 of [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004)) to structure this assessment and bring it to the SFV.

## The Site Feasibility Visit

The Sponsor or Sponsor-Investigator conducts the Site Feasibility Visit (SFV) to evaluate whether the site has the right population, facilities, equipment, and team to meet the study's requirements within the required timeline.

The <Tooltip tip="The team member who handles day-to-day administration and coordination of the clinical trial." headline="Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#crc">Clinical Research Coordinator</Tooltip> (or delegate) is responsible for compiling the information requested by the Sponsor, assisting the <Tooltip tip="The physician responsible to the sponsor for the conduct of a clinical trial at the site. One QI per study per Canadian site." headline="Qualified Investigator (QI)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#qi">QI</Tooltip>/PI with completing the Feasibility Checklist, facilitating the SFV meeting, and ensuring all follow-up requirements are met.

During the SFV, the Sponsor will typically assess:

- QI/PI and Sub-Investigator qualifications and experience
- Team knowledge of <Tooltip tip="International standard for design, conduct, monitoring, recording, and reporting of clinical studies." headline="Good Clinical Practice (GCP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#gcp">GCP</Tooltip>, Health Canada regulations, and TCPS2
- Availability of team members and supporting departments
- Patient population available for screening and enrolment
- Ability to meet study timelines
- Secure storage for records and investigational products

<Note>
  Before the SFV, ensure secure, temperature-controlled storage for investigational products has been identified, and that a qualified person responsible for <Tooltip tip="A drug, medical device, or natural health product being tested or used as a reference in a clinical trial." headline="Investigational Product (IP)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ip">IP</Tooltip> management is named.
</Note>

## Feasibility checklist

The Study Feasibility Checklist (Appendix 1 of [SOP-CR-004](/sops/cr-004)) must be completed before any feasibility assessment. It covers protocol review, site and team capacity, ethical considerations, and financial terms.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Scientific and ethical review">
    Is the protocol sound? Are there any ethical concerns with the design, population, or procedures?
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Patient population">
    Are eligible patients accessible at this site within the required enrolment timeline?
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Team capacity">
    Is there a qualified QI/PI? Are CRCs and sub-investigators available with the right experience?
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Facilities and equipment">
    Does the site have the required infrastructure, storage, and equipment to meet protocol requirements?
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Financial viability">
    Are the proposed budget and payment terms acceptable? Have <Tooltip tip="The ethics committee that reviews and approves research involving human participants. Also called CER at French-language institutions." headline="Research Ethics Board (REB)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#reb">REB</Tooltip> billing fees been accounted for?
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Budget and financial agreements

The budget negotiation happens after the feasibility assessment and before the site agreement is signed. The Finance team at RI-MUHC must review and approve the budget before any agreement is executed.

<Note>
  Health Canada Division 5 and ICH E6(R3) require that financial arrangements and any payments to investigators be documented in the study agreement and disclosed to the REB as required.
</Note>

**REB billing fees are a financial viability consideration.** Under Directive ministérielle 2023-016, REB review and authorization fees are billed to the Sponsor for any study funded by a private enterprise — a category that now includes foundation funding (such as the MUHC Foundation or other private foundations), not just industry. If your study falls into this category, the Sponsor's budget must account for these fees before the site agreement is signed. See [Contracts, Budgets and Agreements](/kb/budgets-contracts) for the full fee schedule and what to confirm with your Sponsor before submitting.

## What happens next

Once feasibility is confirmed and the site has agreed to participate, the study moves into initiation. The sequence below is governed by [SOP-CR-005](/sops/cr-005).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Sign the Site Agreement">
    The Clinical Trial Agreement (or equivalent) is negotiated and executed between the Sponsor and the institution. Finance must review and approve the final budget before signing.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete the Nagano submission">
    The study is submitted via Nagano for REB review and <Tooltip tip="Institutional authorization allowing a study to proceed at the RI-MUHC. Issued after ethics approval and feasibility review." headline="MUHC Authorization" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#muhc-auth">MUHC Authorization</Tooltip>. Health Canada submission (<Tooltip tip="Application to Health Canada for authorization to conduct a drug trial under Division 5 of the Food and Drug Regulations." headline="Clinical Trial Application (CTA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#cta">CTA</Tooltip> or <Tooltip tip="Health Canada authorization to conduct a clinical investigation of a medical device. The device equivalent of a CTA." headline="Investigational Testing Authorization (ITA)" cta="Full definition" href="/kb/glossary#ita">ITA</Tooltip>) runs in parallel if required for the study type.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Prepare for the Site Initiation Visit">
    Once all approvals are in place, the Sponsor schedules the Site Initiation Visit (SIV) to train the team, confirm site readiness, and authorize the start of enrolment.
  </Step>
</Steps>
