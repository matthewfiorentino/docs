Developing a clinical trial budget with an industry sponsor can be one of the most unfamiliar parts of the process for investigators. This guide covers what to prepare, what line items to include, how to negotiate the exhibit (payment terms), and common mistakes to avoid — with Canadian and Quebec-specific context throughout.

<Note>
  The CIM Budget Specialists are available to help any RI-MUHC researcher negotiate an industry-sponsored budget. Engage them early — during feasibility, before your Site Agreement is signed. See the [CIM advisory service](#cim-budget-advisory-service) at the bottom of this page.
</Note>

## Before you negotiate

Before sitting down with a sponsor, gather these documents. Missing any of them slows the process and leads to incomplete budgets.

| Document | Why you need it |
|---|---|
| **Protocol** (draft or final) | The table of assessments is the foundation — every procedure listed must have a corresponding budget line |
| **Informed Consent Form (ICF)** | Contains visit details, patient compensation commitments, and procedures described to participants |
| **Sponsor's proposed budget** | The starting point for negotiation — compare against the protocol's table of assessments to identify gaps |
| **Exhibit / financial appendix** | The payment terms — payment schedules, holdback, invoicing instructions, currency, and archiving language |
| **Third-party quotes** | Quotes from departments or external vendors your study will use — nursing, pharmacy, ophthalmology, pathology, imaging, laboratory services |

## Key cost categories

Every industry-sponsored trial budget should account for four categories of costs. Use this as a checklist when reviewing a sponsor's proposed budget.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Site costs">
    - **Startup fees** — REB submission, pharmacy setup, regulatory document preparation, site qualification and initiation visit preparation. These are non-negotiable and non-refundable.
    - **REB fees** — At the RI-MUHC, REB fees apply to industry-sponsored studies. Across Canadian institutions, REB fees typically range from $2,000 to $5,000 per site.
    - **Pharmacy fees** — Dispensing, drug accountability, storage, on-call pharmacist services, and annual maintenance.
    - **Overhead** — The RI-MUHC charges 30% on direct costs for industry-sponsored studies, excluding startup, REB, and pharmacy fees. This is consistent with the Canadian institutional standard (most major centres charge 25–30%).
    - **Archiving** — Document retention is 15 years for drug trials and 7 years for most other studies. Storage, security, and retrieval costs over that period add up — negotiate this before study start, not after.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Site management costs">
    - **Pre-study and qualification visits** — Site staff time for sponsor visits during feasibility
    - **Monitoring visits** — On-site and remote monitoring visit preparation, hosting, and follow-up
    - **Protocol amendments** — Re-training, re-consenting, revised source documents, and REB submissions per amendment. Average cost is approximately $2,500 per amendment — yet this line item is often absent from initial budgets.
    - **Annual REB renewals** — Submission preparation and fees for each renewal year
    - **Document translation** — For non-English or non-French speaking participants, or for Quebec regulatory requirements
    - **Close-out visit** — Preparation, hosting, data cleaning, query resolution, and final regulatory submissions
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Patient costs (per visit)">
    - **Screening visits** — Including informed consent process and eligibility assessments
    - **Treatment and follow-up visits** — All protocol-mandated assessments and procedures
    - **Screen failures** — Reimbursement for completed screening procedures on participants who do not qualify
    - **Re-screening** — When the protocol allows re-screening of previously failed participants
    - **Unscheduled visits** — Often underpriced or missing entirely from sponsor budgets
    - **Non-standard-of-care tests** — Procedures performed solely for research purposes
    - **Third-party costs** — Imaging, central and local lab work, pathology, ophthalmology
    - **Overnight stays** — For Phase I or intensive monitoring protocols
    - **Patient reimbursement** — Transportation, parking, meals, and any stipends committed in the ICF
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Patient administration costs">
    - **Principal Investigator fees** — Per-visit or per-patient PI oversight, medical review, and sign-off
    - **Study Coordinator fees** — Time for every patient-facing and administrative task: data entry, query resolution, source document verification, monitoring visit preparation
    - **Research Nurse fees** — For protocol procedures requiring nursing staff
    - **Data entry fees** — CRF completion, EDC data entry, and query resolution
    - **Pharmacy administration** — Per-visit dispensing and accountability
    - **SAE reporting** — Coordinator and PI time for preparing, submitting, and following up on serious adverse event reports. This is frequently overlooked.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Negotiating the budget

### What is non-negotiable

Certain costs must be in every industry-sponsored budget. These are institutional requirements — they are not optional and cannot be waived.

- **Startup, REB, and pharmacy fees** — Non-refundable. Must be paid before study activities begin, ideally upon fully executed Clinical Trial Agreement.
- **Overhead (30%)** — Applied to direct costs. Excludes startup, REB, and pharmacy fees. This is the RI-MUHC institutional rate, consistent with the national standard.

### What to always include

These line items should appear in every budget. If the sponsor's template does not include them, add them.

- **Study Coordinator and PI fees on every patient visit** — Not just treatment visits. Screening, re-screening, unscheduled visits, and follow-up visits all require coordinator and PI time.
- **Re-consenting** — When protocol amendments require participants to sign a new ICF, the time to conduct re-consent must be budgeted.
- **Local labs, pathology, imaging** — Any protocol procedures performed by MUHC departments outside the research team. Get departmental quotes early.
- **Archiving and close-out visit** — These happen at the end, but must be budgeted at the start.
- **Patient stipends and reimbursement** — As described in the ICF.

### Optional but recommended

These items are not always required but are common sources of unbudgeted cost. Including them upfront avoids budget amendments later.

| Line item | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Lead site submission fees | If your site is leading a multi-site Canadian submission |
| On-site and remote monitoring fees | Staff time to prepare for and host monitoring visits |
| Amendment processing fees | ~$2,500 per amendment for re-training, re-consent, and REB submission |
| SAE reporting fees | Coordinator and PI time per event — adds up in large trials |
| Screen failure and re-screening fees | See [negotiating screen failures](#screen-failures) below |
| Unscheduled visit fees | Include PI, coordinator, and patient stipend components |
| Advertising and recruitment costs | If the site is expected to recruit actively |
| Invoice uploading to sponsor portal | Administrative time for sponsor-specific invoicing systems |

## Negotiating the exhibit

The exhibit (financial appendix) governs how and when you get paid. This is where most of the actual negotiation happens.

### Payment timing

- **Startup fees** — Should be payable upon fully executed CTA, not upon first patient enrolled.
- **Payment schedule** — Clarify whether payments are automatic (triggered by milestones) or invoiceable (you must submit an invoice). If invoiceable, confirm the submission process and address.
- **Payment terms** — Standard is 30–60 days from receipt of correct invoice. Push back on anything longer than 60 days — 23% of sites report payment delays of 90 days or longer.
- **Currency** — Confirm whether the budget is in CAD or USD. This affects every line item and must be clear before signing.

### Holdback

Many sponsors withhold a percentage of earned payments until data cleaning or database lock — typically 10–14%.

- **Push to reduce or eliminate holdback.** The practice originated when data was physically transported; it is less justified in the era of electronic data capture.
- If holdback cannot be removed, negotiate it down to 5% and request release on a per-subject basis as each participant's data is cleaned — not withheld until study end.

### Screen failures

Sponsors often cap screen failure reimbursement at a fixed ratio — for example, paying for 1 screen failure per 3 participants randomized (1:3 ratio).

- **Negotiate to remove ratios entirely.** Every screen failure consumes real site resources regardless of how many participants are eventually randomized.
- If ratios cannot be removed, negotiate based on realistic failure rates for your patient population. Some therapeutic areas (e.g., Alzheimer's, oncology) have screen failure rates of 50–80%.
- Ensure screen failure payments include all completed procedures — not just a flat fee.

### Tax status

The RI-MUHC is a non-profit, tax-exempt organization. **GST/HST is not charged** on payments from pharmaceutical sponsors for exempt research services. This is standard for Canadian hospital research institutes and should be reflected in the exhibit — do not inadvertently include tax in your budget.

## Finalizing the budget

Once negotiations are complete:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the final documents">
    The sponsor will send clean final copies of the budget and exhibit. Review them carefully to confirm they match what you agreed to — not a prior draft.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Forward to your Contract Officer">
    Reply to the sponsor and copy your Contract Officer at the Research Agreements Office. Include the approved documents as the final version and reference your OCC file number.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Final CTA review">
    The Contract Officer conducts a last review of the full CTA, which includes your final budget, before the signature process begins.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify before signing">
    Before the agreement is signed, review the attached budget one last time to confirm it is the version you approved. Errors at this stage are costly to correct.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Common mistakes

<Warning>
  The most common source of budget problems is late engagement. If CIM services, departmental resources, or third-party vendors are part of your protocol, their costs must be in the initial budget — not added after the Site Agreement is signed.
</Warning>

- **Underestimating coordinator time** — Every study procedure requires coordinator effort: screening, consenting, data entry, query resolution, monitoring visit prep, SAE reporting, and regulatory maintenance. Budget for all of it.
- **Accepting flat visit payments** — A 3-hour screening visit should not pay the same as a 30-minute follow-up. Differentiate by visit complexity.
- **Missing amendment costs** — The average industry trial undergoes multiple amendments. Each one costs time and money. Budget for at least 2–3 amendments.
- **Forgetting ancillary department fees** — Pharmacy dispensing, lab processing, imaging, dry ice, specimen shipping, and patient transportation are frequently omitted.
- **Weak unscheduled visit terms** — Vague or missing language on unscheduled visit compensation leaves sites absorbing real costs.
- **Not addressing cash flow** — Accepting 90-day payment terms and high holdback percentages creates cash flow problems, especially for sites with multiple concurrent trials.
- **Overlooking archiving** — 15–25 years of document storage is a real cost that must be negotiated upfront.
