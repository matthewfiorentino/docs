## Key points

- Qualified Investigators are responsible for reviewing all SUSARs distributed by the sponsor and taking any required actions.
- SUSAR review must be documented — the signed training record form confirms the investigator has read and understood each report.
- Unblinding decisions following a SUSAR must follow the protocol-specified procedure and be documented in the study records.
- All SUSAR communications and investigator responses must be retained in the Investigator Site File (ISF).
- Investigators must assess whether a SUSAR affects the risk-benefit assessment for ongoing participants and determine if protocol or consent form amendments are required.

---

## Summary

**WI-CIM-CR002** governs how Qualified Investigators at the CIM review and process Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reactions (SUSARs) — external safety reports distributed by sponsors during the conduct of a clinical study. Key requirements include:

- **Review obligation**: The QI must review all SUSARs received from the sponsor in a timely manner and document the review using the associated Training Record Form.
- **Risk-benefit assessment**: Following each SUSAR, the QI must assess whether the new safety information affects the ongoing risk-benefit profile for enrolled participants.
- **Regulatory and REB reporting**: If the SUSAR triggers a reportable event obligation (e.g., requires REB notification or protocol amendment), the QI must act in accordance with SOP-CR-026 and applicable CIM procedures.
- **ISF filing**: All SUSAR communications, the investigator's review acknowledgement, and any resulting actions must be filed in the Investigator Site File (ISF) per WI-CIM-CR007.
- **Unblinding**: Any unblinding decisions prompted by a SUSAR must follow the protocol-specified emergency unblinding procedure and be fully documented.
